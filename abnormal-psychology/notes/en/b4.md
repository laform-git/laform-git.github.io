# Pihl — Abnormal Psychology Part II, Ch.4: Reducing Risk
*How suicide is predicted, classified, theorized, and prevented: attempters vs. completers, the post-discharge danger window, Durkheim's three types, genetics and biology, and the evidence that doing something (and above all social support) lowers risk.*

*Source: Dr. Robert O. Pihl, Peterson Academy course "Introduction to Abnormal Psychology Part II," Lecture 4 (transcript). Continues mid-topic from Lecture 3 (attempters vs. completers). Sensitive topic: this note is a study summary, not clinical guidance.*

## Why This Matters
This is the prevention half of the depression-and-suicide arc (Ch.1 Rise in Depression, Ch.2 Inside Depression, Ch.3 Recognizing Risk). The lecture's practical payload is short: risk factors predict groups, not individuals; the highest-risk window is right after hospital discharge; almost any structured intervention helps; and social support is the single most protective factor Pihl names. It is also useful for anyone who wants to be a steady presence for someone else.

## Attempters vs. Completers
Only about **10 to 13% of attempters go on to complete suicide**, which is why prediction is so hard. Pihl's point: a gesture can be **social communication (an operant)**, a message to someone, not necessarily a wish to die. He gives two cases at the extremes: a teenager whose low-lethality gesture was meant to move her parents, and a woman whose message to her husband went fatally wrong when a timing assumption failed. ✅ Lesson: intent and outcome can diverge, so classification is genuinely hard.

| Feature | Attempters | Completers |
|---|---|---|
| **Sex** | Mostly women | Mostly men |
| **Age** | Younger | Older |
| **Method lethality** | Low (e.g., pills, cutting) | High (e.g., firearms) |
| **Setting** | Chance of rescue | Low chance of rescue |
| **Typical diagnoses** | Borderline personality disorder | Mood disorders, schizophrenia, substance abuse |

✅ Pihl's anchoring phrase: suicide is **"a permanent solution to a temporary problem"**; he adds "can be" when pressed, since many cases (especially terminal illness) differ.

## Aftermath and Liability
- **Survivors:** in one survey, 8% felt relief; **67% of people close to the person became depressed** and another **25% developed health problems**. ✅
- Suicide is no longer a crime in the US, but Pihl says failure to predict and prevent it is a leading reason psychiatrists get sued. 🟡 His claim; not sourced in the lecture.

## Predictors and Warning Signs
- **Common predictors:** mental illness, depression, alcoholism, drug abuse, suicidal thoughts or talk, preparation, prior attempts, lethal methods, living alone without social support.
- **Single predictors:** older white men, family modeling and genetics, work or financial pressure, marital problems, stress and life events, anger/aggression, low serotonin, physical illness.
- **Beck Hopelessness Scale:** true/false items such as "things just won't work out the way I want them to." Low optimism is itself a risk marker. ✅
- **Shneidman's warning categories:** verbal (direct or indirect, e.g., asking how assets pass after death), behavioral (a trial run, or an unusual sudden long trip), situational (a major diagnosis such as cancer or a neurological disease). For most people the thought is fleeting; for some it becomes a focus.
- **Assessment checklist:** thoughts, plan, means, proximity of others, hopelessness, past attempts, related loss, substance use, poor judgment and impulse control.

## The Post-Discharge Danger Window
A Finnish follow-up of roughly **91,000 treated depressives** (Pihl first says "almost 10,000" then corrects to 91,000) showed suicides cluster **very close to hospital discharge, especially the first week**, then drop sharply. ✅ Exception: people with **alcohol or substance use disorders** did not show this tight clustering. Pihl's gloss: it is as if patients use the hospital and treatment in their planning. 🟡 interpretive.

## Five Classes of US Suicide (300,000+ cases)
1. Mental health and substance problems
2. Mental health problems only
3. Alcohol-related and intimate partner problems
4. **Physical health problems** (largest group; the sick and elderly; consistently high 2003 to 2020)
5. **Poly-substance problems** (rising since about 2012)

Mental-health-only and relationship-related classes are declining as predictors. ✅ Pihl's caution: **most high-risk patients never attempt**, correlations describe groups not individuals, and the base rate is very low. He cites Pavese: "Everyone has a good reason for suicide," yet very few act.

## Theories of Suicide

| Theory | Core idea | Pihl's take |
|---|---|---|
| **Durkheim: egoistic** | No belonging, no glue to relationships | Isolation as risk |
| **Durkheim: altruistic** | Individual absorbed by the group (Jonestown, Hale-Bopp, and ideological "murdercide" such as the Al-Qaeda members he mentions, who were often educated, married, middle or upper class) | Groupthink overrides normal controls |
| **Durkheim: anomic** | Change too sudden to cope with (lottery winners, sudden fame) | Rapid change is a stressor even when positive |
| **Freud: thanatos** | Death drive; unconscious it shows as risk-taking and self-harm | Circular and uninformative |
| **Other** | Magical rebirth or world-destruction fantasy (some schizophrenia), despair and loss, expiating guilt | Descriptive categories |

**Integrated model:** genes, family history, biology, and personality interact; enough risk plus precipitating events crosses a threshold. Inhibitory factors sit in the middle: **cognitive flexibility, strong social support, no recent losses, hopefulness, treatment.** ✅

## Biology and Genetics
- **Self-injury in rare genetic syndromes** (Lesch-Nyhan, Cornelia de Lange, familial dysautonomia, extreme autism), seen mostly in institutionalized children. Pihl's hypothesis: head-banging or cutting may release **endorphins**, and **naltrexone** (an opioid blocker) reduced the behavior in a graph he shows. 🟡 The pressure-point explanation is his speculation. Historic cattle-prod punishment worked but was abused and is now illegal.
- **Family patterns:** four Amish families (16% of the population) accounted for **73%** of suicides; adoptees with depression had 3 to 4 times more suicide than adoptive relatives; more concordance in identical than fraternal twins. ✅
- **Serotonin:** linked to impulsivity, aggression, depression; the metabolite **5-HIAA is low in CSF** of attempters. Pihl frames the link to suicide as indirect, via depression. 🟡
- **Genes:** a study of **600,000 US veterans** localized genes for an estrogen receptor (also implicated in depression and PTSD), a dopamine receptor, synaptic plasticity, and lithium function. ✅
- **Brain imaging:** depressed people who attempted showed **no structural difference** (frontotemporal, amygdala) from depressed people who did not. ✅ Useful null result.
- **NHL enforcers (331 players, 50+ fights, 1967 to 2022)** died about 10 years earlier, with fewer tumors but more suicides and overdoses than matched players. Cause unclear: pre-existing traits or neurological damage. 🟡

## Prevention: Philosophy and Evidence
- **Szasz vs. Shneidman:** Szasz (*The Myth of Mental Illness*) saw suicide as a right and hotline intervention as intrusion; Shneidman saw calls as cries for help and the "**congress of the mind**" (competing votes) as something a professional should tip toward life. Pihl says they argued about different things.
- **988 Lifeline (launched 2022):** early survey of about 5,000 people; among seriously distressed respondents only **29% said they would use it again**. Pihl reads this as "not that functional." 🟡 Willingness to reuse is not the same as effectiveness, and the counts as spoken are garbled in the transcript.
- **Any approach helps:** a forest plot showed many interventions reduce attempts, with no single theory winning. "It's doing something." ✅
- **Medication data:** benzodiazepines, and surprisingly antipsychotics and antidepressants, were associated with **higher** risk; ADHD stimulants with lower. Pihl: "You're better off talking to the person than giving them a drug." 🟡 Observational associations (confounding by severity is likely); not a reason to change any prescription without a clinician.
- **Therapies:** dialectical behavior therapy (borderline personality disorder) reduces attempts; an internet emotion-regulation program reduced non-suicidal self-injury. ✅
- **Media:** after Netflix's *13 Reasons Why* (2017), suicides among 10 to 19 year olds rose 12% in males and 27% in females. Pihl says media should not sensationalize or normalize suicide. ✅ (supports safe-messaging guidelines)
- **Social support:** each standard deviation of social support lowered odds of severe depression by **47%**, anxiety by **22%**, and reduced ideation and attempts (ages 19 to 20). Support means standing behind someone, which Pihl distinguishes from granting autonomy. ✅
- **Public-health framing:** treat suicide as a public health problem, with accessible affordable services and **limiting lethal means**.

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 4)
- **"A permanent solution to a temporary problem."**
- **Only 10 to 13% of attempters complete.**
- **The first week after discharge is the peak-risk window** (Finland, about 91,000 patients).
- **67% of those close to a suicide become depressed.**
- **Depressed attempters and depressed non-attempters look the same on brain structure.**
- **Each standard deviation of social support: 47% lower odds of severe depression.**
- **"It doesn't matter what theory you follow"**: doing something beats doing nothing.
- **Bridge-jump survivors**: a very small percent ever try again (Q&A).

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 Assuming an attempt equals intent to die: gestures can be communication, and only 10 to 13% of attempters complete.
- 🔴 Assuming risk is highest while someone is hospitalized: it peaks right after discharge.
- 🔴 Assuming brain scans or a single risk factor can identify who will act: most high-risk people never attempt, and imaging shows no attempter-specific difference among depressed patients.
- 🔴 Assuming one therapy school is the answer: any structured approach helps; social support is the standout protective factor.

## Practical Takeaway
Stay connected, notice verbal, behavioral, and situational warnings, ask directly, and keep support in place through transitions such as discharge. If you or someone you know is in crisis in the US, call or text **988**. Limiting access to lethal means is part of prevention. Do not use this note to change any medication.

*Why C: low base rate plus group-level correlations is Pihl's explicit reason for weak individual prediction.*

*Why A: the curve peaks near discharge and then drops sharply; people with alcohol or substance use disorders were the exception.*

**3. Which pairing best matches Durkheim's "altruistic" type?**
A. Someone who belongs to no group
B. \* Someone whose individuality is absorbed by a group, as in Jonestown or Hale-Bopp
C. Someone overwhelmed by sudden wealth or fame
*Why B: egoistic is isolation, anomic is sudden change, altruistic is over-integration into the group.*

**4. What does the lecture identify as the strongest practical protective factor, and what is the evidence?**

*Why C: the medication data actually showed higher risk associations, and the forest plot showed no therapy beating the others; social support had the clearest quantified effect.*
