# Substances — 02 Cannabis, the Real Risks
*Legal in many places and marketed as medicine, cannabis carries real risks, especially at today's high potency, for teenagers, and for daily users: dependence, psychosis, impaired driving, and a vomiting syndrome many people have never heard of.*

> **Educational, not medical advice.** SAMHSA Helpline: 1-800-662-4357.

## Core Insight

The cannabis studied decades ago was a different product. Today's THC levels are several times higher, edibles and concentrates are common, and use is often daily. The mechanism notes in this series (Endocannabinoid System, THC & Neurotransmitters) explain *how* THC works; this note lists *what can go wrong*.

## The Main Risks

### Dependence
- ✅ **CDC:** about 3 in 10 people who use cannabis have cannabis use disorder; the risk is higher for those who start young or use daily.
- 🟢 Withdrawal is real: irritability, insomnia, vivid dreams, low appetite and craving, peaking in the first week and fading over about a month.

### Psychosis and mental health
- 🟢 Daily use of high-potency cannabis was linked to about **five times** the odds of a psychotic disorder in a large European study (Di Forti et al., *Lancet Psychiatry*, 2019).
- 🟢 Cannabis can trigger or worsen schizophrenia and bipolar disorder in vulnerable people, and is linked to earlier onset.
- 🟡 Links to depression, anxiety and suicidal thinking exist, especially in teens; cause and effect are still debated.

### The teenage brain
- 🟢 The brain develops until the mid-20s. Regular adolescent use is linked to poorer memory, attention and school outcomes; Pihl cites imaging showing frontal tissue loss in heavy users years later.
- ✅ Starting young sharply raises the risk of dependence.

### Driving and accidents
- ✅ THC slows reaction time and impairs coordination and judgment; combined with alcohol the risk multiplies.

### Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome
- 🟢 Long-term heavy users can develop cycles of severe nausea and vomiting, often relieved only by hot showers, and only cured by stopping. Emergency visits for it have risen where cannabis is legal.

### Heart and lungs
- 🟢 Smoking irritates the lungs (cough, bronchitis). Cannabis use is linked to higher risk of heart attack and stroke, especially in younger users 🟡 (research growing).

### Pregnancy
- 🟢 Linked to lower birth weight and possible developmental effects; medical groups advise no use in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

### The street supply
- ✅ Unknown potency, contaminated vapes (the 2019 EVALI outbreak), and edibles that act late and are easy to overdose on (see note 00).

## Medical Use in Context

✅ FDA-approved cannabinoid medicines exist for narrow uses: dronabinol and nabilone (chemotherapy nausea, appetite in AIDS) and Epidiolex, a CBD drug for certain severe childhood epilepsies. This is a long way from recreational or self-medicating use (see THC — Benefits & Costs).

## Key Corrections

- ❌ "Weed isn't addictive." ✅ About 3 in 10 users develop a use disorder.
- ❌ "It's natural and legal, so it's harmless." ✅ Legal products can be very potent; harm depends on dose, age and frequency.
- ❌ "It helps me sleep." ✅ It suppresses REM and leads to rebound insomnia and vivid dreams when stopped.

## Practical Takeaway

The risks rise with potency, frequency and younger age. For teenagers, people with a family history of psychosis, and pregnant women, the safest amount is none.

> If the product today is several times stronger than the one older studies examined, what does "we used it in college and were fine" really prove?
