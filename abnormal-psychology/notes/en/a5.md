# Pihl — Abnormal Psychology Part I, Ch.5: Influences on Mental Health
*Explaining abnormal behavior requires genes, biochemistry, brain and environment modeled together: epigenetics, the twin-vs-molecular gap, gene-by-environment interaction, drug-derived neurotransmitter theories, and ADHD as the worked example.*

*Source: Dr. Robert O. Pihl, Peterson Academy course "Introduction to Abnormal Psychology Part I," Lecture 5 (transcript). The lecture covers genetic and biochemical factors and closes on ADHD; the "brain factors" it promises at the start are largely deferred to Lecture 6.*

## Why This Matters
This is the lecture where Pihl stops defining and classifying abnormal behavior and starts asking what causes it. His answer is deliberately uncomfortable: no single level (a gene, a molecule, a brain region) explains behavior, and treatments that work do not prove the cause they imply. It sets up the "Chemical Imbalance Myth" notes you already have, and gives the genetics and pharmacology scaffolding for Lecture 6 (brain health) and for Part II (depression, addiction).

## The Core Claim — A Multi-Level Model
- To explain a behavior you need **genetic, biochemical, brain and environmental factors and their interactions** — not one of them. ✅
- Pihl's complaint: science is getting so narrow (one brain region, one molecule) that nobody holds the whole picture. ✅
- Until the causes (etiology) are understood at every level, explanations stay partial. ✅

## Nature vs. Nurture — "A Stupid Debate"
- Arguing everything is genes (or everything is environment) means arguing organisms exist in a vacuum. ✅
- People resist genetic explanations because of the wish that "all individuals are created equal" — Pihl calls that "a generalization of wish," since people differ enormously. ✅
- Second misperception: **inherited does not mean untreatable.** Gene editing is hyped but, per Pihl, mostly mice and rats so far; he mentions a CRISPR attempt in South Korea whose researcher is in jail and a six-patient trial for a fatal disorder (name not clear in transcript). 🟡 Dated, anecdotal framing.

## Genetic Overlap Between Disorders

| Pair | What the genome-wide data show | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Schizophrenia ↔ bipolar** | Highest genetic correlation; the same genes turn up in both | Clinically treated as separate disorders |
| **Anxiety ↔ depression** | Also highly correlated | Pihl links this to the poor diagnostician reliability from Lecture 4 |
| **OCD ↔ anorexia** | Significant correlation | Same story: genetically related, clinically separate |

✅ Core point: the diagnostic categories do not carve genetics at its joints.

## Epigenetics — The Environment as a Dimmer Switch
- Genes shape biological systems, **and behavior and environment in turn change gene activity** — onset and degree of expression. ✅ Pihl's analogy: a light switch with a dimmer, graded rather than on/off.
- **Bullied mice:** constant bullying raised expression of ~309 genes and lowered 17; lasting social aversion, altered dopamine pathway and BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) — and, he says, reversible. ✅
- **Dean Ornish study:** 30 men with mild prostate cancer, three months of nutrition plus lots of walking; RNA before/after showed 48 genes up and 453 down, in processes tied to tumor development. ✅ Pihl uses it to argue gene activity can be manipulated even in cancer. 🟡 Small, uncontrolled as described; his reading, not a treatment claim.
- **Socioeconomic status (SES) and early mental ability:** at 10 months, three SES groups look similar; by two years, genetic influence is visible in high SES but in low SES "it's all environment" — a poor environment suppresses the expression of genetic potential. ✅

## Ways to Study Genetics — And Their Limits

| Method | What it does | Pihl's caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Family pedigrees** | Trace a disorder through a family (Tennyson family, probable bipolar depression) | Families also share interests and professions; you can draw the same chart for lawyers or doctors |
| **Twin studies** | Identical (MZ) vs fraternal (DZ) correlation; difference estimates heredity | Not strictly valid: MZ twins differ even in the womb. Over-optimistic about genes |
| **Molecular / GWAS** | Look for actual risk genes | Explains far less: schizophrenia ~23% by genes vs ~81% by twin data |
| **Cytogenetics** | Karyotype maps of chromosomes (Down, Fragile X) | Works for chromosomal conditions only |
| **Animal models** | Manipulate genes, test behavior | Show breadth of effect, not direct human answers |

- **The missing-heritability gap** (23% vs 81%) means "something else is going on." Same pattern for bipolar, depression, autism and ADHD. ✅
- "One gene, one disease" essentially does not exist in psychopathology. ✅
- Proposed way forward: **endophenotypes** (intermediate traits such as working memory, information gating, glutamatergic mechanisms, glial abnormalities, eye-movement control in schizophrenia). Genes shape endophenotypes, which interact with environment over development. Problem: the huge studies rarely measure them well. ✅

## Single-Gene and Chromosomal Conditions
- **Huntington's disease:** autosomal dominant, chromosome 4, 50% risk if a parent has it; symptoms usually after childbearing years. Pihl says normal is well under 27 repeats, suspicious above 27, diagnostic above 42. He stresses it is really a neurological condition, not psychopathology. ✅ 🟡 He describes the repeat as "glutamate" causing excess stimulation; the repeated unit is, as far as I know, a CAG codon (glutamine) — verify before citing.
- **Predictive testing dilemma:** after a test was developed (Nancy Wexler's work), many who tested positive suffered severely, and most at-risk people now decline testing. Pihl's lesson: for many conditions you may be better off not letting a distant prediction run your life. ✅ (Sensitive topic; the lecture's framing, not advice.)
- **Down syndrome:** trisomy of chromosome 21 in over 90%, ~1 in 650 births, arises in meiosis (genetic but not inherited). Pihl shows an accomplished young woman with Down syndrome to counter low expectations. ✅
- **Parental age:** women over 40 account for 4% of births but 40% of Down syndrome. Fathers matter too: older fathers carry more risk for schizophrenia, autism spectrum, bipolar, epilepsy, OCD. Reason: egg precursors undergo about 22 divisions, sperm precursors ~150 by age 20 and ~840 by age 50, so more chances for mutations. ✅
- **Fragile X:** most frequent inherited intellectual disability; X chromosome break; far more common in males (one X); gene isolated 1991; normal 6 to 54 repeats vs over 200; "anticipation" (worsening across generations); reduced penetrance — which Pihl himself calls a circular explanation. ✅

## Gene by Environment — Interactions, Not Additions
- **MAOA:** the gene for monoamine oxidase, the enzyme that breaks down dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine and serotonin. In Pihl's data, high-activity MAOA carriers show less conduct disorder, especially when not maltreated. ✅ 🟡 Transcript wording is garbled on the direction of the effect.
- **Serotonin transporter alleles:** people with the "SS" version who are severely maltreated are much more likely to be depressed. ✅
- **Salt-sensitive rats:** rats bred to be salt-sensitive, put in a food-versus-shock conflict (salt-free food), showed severe blood-pressure changes and some died. Sensitivity to one thing made them vulnerable to something unrelated. ✅
- Pihl's summary: not just a gene, not just biochemistry, but "what's happening to you."

## Biochemistry — Where Genes Do Their Work
- Neurons communicate chemically across synapses: neurotransmitters are **synthesized, stored, released, degraded and taken back up**; most psychiatric drugs act at these steps. ✅
- Scale (Pihl's figures): cortex ~25 billion neurons and 300 trillion synapses; whole brain 86 billion neurons and ~600 trillion synapses. ✅
- **Glial cells** (three types) are far more than packing: they prune unused synapses, insulate, tune signals and regulate synaptic flow; recent autism work looks at them. ✅

## Drug-Derived Theories — The Logic and Its Gap
- **Serotonin and depression:** reserpine (from Rauvolfia) lowered serotonin/norepinephrine and caused depression in some blood-pressure patients; antidepressants (amphetamine, MAO inhibitors, tricyclics, SSRIs such as Prozac, which blocks serotonin reuptake) raise it. So the theory "comes from purely pharmacological data." ✅
- **Schizophrenia and dopamine:** phenothiazine/chlorpromazine, developed from antihistamine research, calmed psychosis in early-1950s studies (dates in the transcript are muddled 🟡); by 1960 about 50 million people had used it; US institutionalized patients fell from ~700,000 (1950) to ~150,000. ✅
- Drugs reduced positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions); amphetamine worsens them; heavy amphetamine use (5-6 days) can induce psychosis. Side effects: extrapyramidal symptoms and tardive dyskinesia from D2 blockade; newer atypicals target other receptors (D4 per Pihl). ✅
- **The caveat:** a meta-analysis of 28 first-episode studies found mean recovery of only ~20%, unaffected by intervention type; few people respond to antidepressants. Pihl's inference: **treatments that work are not telling us what causes the disorder.** ✅

## Dopamine and ADHD — The Worked Example
- Dopamine is ~80% of brain catecholamines but not abundant. Sources: substantia nigra and the medial forebrain bundle, projecting to frontal lobes and limbic regions (amygdala, hippocampus, nucleus accumbens). Made from tyrosine via DOPA. Ritalin, cocaine and amphetamines block reuptake — the "high" in drug abuse. ✅
- **Diagnosis:** six or more symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity, present in at least two settings (so parents must confirm what teachers see). Most diagnoses begin with teachers, which Pihl warns can reflect kids not fitting in. ✅ 🟡 Transcript mislabels the subtypes.
- **Comorbidity:** oppositional defiant, conduct disorder, depression ~30%, bipolar ~10%, anxiety ~25%, learning disabilities ~20%. 33-year follow-up of kids diagnosed at 8: worse education, work, economic and social outcomes, ongoing ADHD, more antisocial personality and substance problems (he guesses ~40% for the latter), hospitalizations, incarceration and divorce. ✅
- **Genetics:** meta-analysis of 38,000 ADHD cases and 186,000 controls implicated early brain development, dopamine neurons and cognition genes; 84-98 of the variants are shared with other disorders, explaining comorbidity. The DAT1 allele ("480 DT" in transcript) raises risk 3-5x; DRD4 is also linked. ✅ 🟡
- **Dopamine mechanism:** about 80% respond to Ritalin; frontal dopamine lesions cause hyperactivity in animals; imaging shows deficits in striatum and frontal lobes; adults with ADHD show higher dopamine transporter density. 🟡 Pihl says this leaves dopamine "staying longer" in the synapse, which seems reversed (more transporters would clear it faster); flagged, not resolved.
- **Behavioral treatment case:** two boys scheduled for amygdala surgery were managed with an operant points-for-sitting system and frustration tolerance training, generalized to school. ✅ Pihl's point: behavioral management can address what was to be handled physiologically.

- **Schizophrenia: 23% explained by molecular genetics vs 81% by twin data.**
- **"One gene, one disease" does not exist outside Huntington's — and that is neurology.**

- **Tolstoy: "All healthy families resemble each other; each unhealthy family is unhealthy in its own way."**

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "Genes versus environment": the real unit is interaction; genes change with behavior and environment, and risk alleles often matter only given adversity.
- 🔴 "Heritable means fixed": epigenetic data, plus treatability, say otherwise.
- 🔴 "Twin heritability equals the genes": molecular studies find far less, so twin estimates run optimistic.
- 🔴 "Our diagnoses mirror biology": schizophrenia/bipolar and anxiety/depression share genes, yet are treated as separate illnesses.
- 🔴 "A drug works, therefore we know the cause": reserpine-to-serotonin and phenothiazine-to-dopamine are inferences from pharmacology, and ~20% recovery shows the explanation is incomplete.

## Practical Takeaway
When you read a claim about a "gene for" or a "chemical imbalance in" a disorder, ask three questions: which level is being studied, what environment was it crossed with, and is the evidence causal or inferred from how a drug behaves? Also treat risk information (genetic tests included) as probability, not destiny.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Twin data attribute ~81% of schizophrenia to heredity, while molecular studies explain ~23%. What is the best reading?**
A. Twin studies are correct and molecular studies are broken
B. \* Twin estimates run optimistic (identical twins also differ, and shared environment is hard to separate), and molecular methods have not yet found the missing sources, so "something else is going on"
C. Schizophrenia is not genetic at all
*Why B: Pihl treats both as informative but flawed and calls the gap a sign that the story is incomplete.*

**2. In the bullied-mouse study, what does the change in 309 genes up and 17 down demonstrate?**
A. Mice are born with genes that cause social aversion
B. Gene changes are permanent once triggered
C. \* Experience changes gene expression in a graded, potentially reversible way — the dimmer-switch idea
*Why C: Pihl says the aversion and altered dopamine/BDNF function can be reversed, which is why he uses a dimmer rather than an on/off switch.*

**3. Why does Pihl argue that "drugs that work" do not settle the cause of a disorder?**
A. \* The serotonin and dopamine theories were inferred from drug effects, and recovery rates (~20% in first-episode schizophrenia, few antidepressant responders) show those drugs do not fully explain the disorder
B. Because drugs have no effect on the brain
C. Because placebo is always stronger
*Why A: the lecture's own examples (reserpine, phenothiazines) are reasoning backward from pharmacology.*

**4. A child shows ADHD-type symptoms only in the classroom. What does the lecture's diagnostic rule imply?**
A. Diagnose from the teacher's report alone
B. The child cannot have ADHD
C. \* Symptoms must appear in at least two settings, so parents' observations must corroborate before diagnosis
*Why C: Pihl notes teachers make most referrals, which is why the multi-setting requirement matters.*
