# Pihl — Abnormal Psychology Part II, Ch.1: A Rise in Depression
*Opens Pihl's Part II with the case that depression is many disorders, plus the prevalence, recurrence, symptom-cluster and youth-trend data that frame the course.*

*Source: Dr. Robert O. Pihl, Peterson Academy course "Introduction to Abnormal Psychology Part II," Lecture 1 (transcript). Opens Part II (depression, suicide/self-harm, addiction) after a recap of Part I: definitions, assessment and theories of abnormality all "found wanting."*

## Why This Matters
This lecture sets the frame for the whole course: depression is not one thing, our definitions and diagnostic tools are loose, and the numbers (prevalence, prescriptions, youth distress) are large and in some groups rising. It is a psychology study note, not a clinical one. The practical value is vocabulary: what counts as a major depressive episode, how recurrence works, and why "depression" in the singular hides several disorders with different causes.

## Recap of Part I — Why Definitions Come First
- **Definitions come before prediction and control.** Pihl: you have to define something before you can predict or control it. ✅
- **DSM inflation:** 128 disorders in 1952 versus 543 in 2022. Pihl predicts a DSM-6 and DSM-7 in our lifetimes and "a thousand mental illnesses." ✅ (his prediction, not a finding)
- **Names are bestowed, not found.** "Schizophrenia" is a word describing behaviors; it does not explain them. ✅
- **Assessment is mostly the clinical interview.** Almost no biological measures appear in the 543 definitions, so assessment is open to bias and manipulation; Pihl says his lab has tricked clinicians into wrong conclusions and wrong prescriptions. Treatment is often trial and error, "more art than science." ✅
- **Bottom line for Part II:** there are multitudes of depressions with different etiologies, most unknown, yet we treat depression as one ubiquitous thing and so lack differential treatments. ✅

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 1)
- **"That's my definition of inflation"** — 128 DSM disorders (1952) to 543 (2022).
- **Lifetime diagnosability at age 100: about 85% for women, about 75% for men** (read off a graph Pihl could barely see, so approximate).
- **About 79% of women will receive an antidepressant at some point; 60-something percent an anxiolytic** — "prescribed like candy."
- **20% of U.S. people (50 million) have a mental illness; 28 million are never treated.**
- **If you have untreated depression, "time" often does the work:** typical untreated episodes last six to nine months.
- **Antidepressant use is up 64% in young teens and young adults since 2020**; 1 in 10 Americans use them.
- **A random GP is wrong about 25% of the time** (versus a psychiatrist/psychologist "gold standard") when about 20% of patients are truly depressed.
- **Kay Jamison**, author of *An Unquiet Mind*, loved her manic highs but took lithium after repeated suicide attempts in her lows.

| Statistic | Figure given |
|---|---|

| Adults with a substance use disorder | 15% |

- **Age pattern:** mental illness and unintentional injury are the only burdens spread evenly across life; other major diseases skew older. In the 15 to 24 range the dominant problems are depression, anxiety and drug abuse. ✅
- **British survey (50,000 adults, 2020-2022):** distress was stable over time, slightly under 10% severe, highest at ages 18 to 24. ✅
- **COVID:** in-person treatment collapsed, was replaced by telehealth, and now both add together so total people treated is rising. ✅

## Pihl's Theoretical Frame for the Course
- **Genetics always interact:** every disorder has a genetic component, almost none is purely genetic. ✅
- **Layers to examine:** family history, genetics, biochemistry, brain structure, and temperament.
- **Two temperament pathways:** *externalizing* (antisocial) versus *internalizing* (anxiety, depression), "totally separate problems with separate pathways." ✅

## The Student's Letter — What Depression Feels Like
Pihl read a former student's account (major depressive disorder, diagnosed two years earlier). Features worth keeping as symptom illustrations:
- Happy childhood and no classic risk factors, yet onset in second year of university; **family history of depression** learned only afterward. ✅
- Exhaustion with daytime napping, **insomnia and 4 to 5 a.m. waking**, no appetite, irritability, withdrawal, poor focus, a lump in the throat, crying about once a day.
- She hoped for a physical illness (mono) instead; blood work was normal.
- Recovery took psychotherapy and several medications; "neutral was as good as it got" for a long time. Her point: she is not a statistic, and knowing more about depression earlier might have helped her catch it. 🟢

## Happiness Data
- Compared with depressed people, happy people are less self-focused, less hostile and less susceptible to disease. ✅
- **World Happiness Report (as cited):** Finland 1, Denmark 2, Canada 15, USA 23, but for under-30s Canada ranks 58th and the USA 62nd. The youth gap, atypical of the rest of the world, drags scores down. ✅
- In the U.S.: unmarried are sadder than married, teen girls sadder than teen boys, liberals sadder than conservatives and more likely to report a diagnosis. Pihl declines to interpret ("too rancorous"). 🟡 (as stated, source details not given)

## Treatment Numbers and Diagnostic Accuracy
- **Antidepressant use:** 1 in 10 Americans; 23% of women in their 40s or 50s; fewer than a third had seen a mental health professional. A "400% rise" figure is one Pihl himself distrusts (start date unknown). 🟡
- **GP accuracy study:** most psychotropic prescriptions come from GPs. Against a psychiatrist/psychologist gold standard, false positives and false negatives together reach about 25% when 20% of attendees are depressed. Pharma checklists exist but most GPs still do not use them. ✅

## Prevalence, Costs and Consequences
| Measure | Women | Men |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime, community sample | 10-25% | 5-12% |
| Point prevalence | 5-9% | 2-3% |

- About **80% go untreated.** ✅
- **Suicide:** 70% of suicides were clinically depressed; risk with a mood disorder is about 15%, "15 times" general population. 🟡 (both numbers as stated)
- **DALYs (2020):** unipolar depression second after heart disease, **first for women.** Days in bed: second to heart disease. ✅
- **Cost per year:** about $27 billion Canada, $326 billion U.S. ✅

## The Youth and Young-Women Trend
- Across 17 countries, suicide rates since 2003 rose most for **teen girls**, then women in their 20s and 30s. ✅
- **U.S. high-school survey (17,232):** girls scored worse on nearly all measures: 57% more sadness or hopelessness, 30% had considered suicide, 18% experienced sexual violence. 🟡 (the non-heterosexual subgroup figures were garbled in the transcript)
- Pihl asks whether lack of acceptance fully explains the subgroup statistic: "partially true, but how much does it explain?" and notes the resulting social division.
- **2022:** 2.3 million U.S. youth (about 10%) had a major depressive episode with severe impairment.
- **Trajectories:** two matched samples (U.S. and Great Britain) show the same pattern. Kids depressed at 10 may be a parabolic group (peak about 13, then fall), an early-high group that recovers by 14 to 16, or an **increasing** group, the one to worry about. A single-time assessment cannot tell them apart. ✅
- Only people aged 18 to 25 show a rising depression line in the graph he showed. 🟡 (graph not legible)

## Screening and Course of Depression
- **USPSTF logic:** screen only if early detection helps and harms (labeling, drug side effects) do not outweigh benefits. They concluded screening adults, including pregnant and postpartum persons, is worthwhile. ✅
- **Episodic or chronic?** Untreated episodes last about 6 to 9 months (range two weeks to years); natural remission occurs.
- **Recurrence odds:**

| Prior episodes | Chance of another |
|---|---|
| 1 | 50-60% |
| 2 | about 70% |
| 3 | about 90% |

## Symptom Clusters and Diagnosis
| Cluster | Features |
|---|---|
| Physical | Sleep (too little or too much), appetite and weight (usually gain), sex drive; correlate least with other clusters |
| Affective | Misery, hopelessness, guilt, loneliness; "the essence of depression" |
| Cognitive | Negative expectations, indecision, poor concentration, distortion |
| Motivational | Suicidality, underestimating reward, overestimating negative feedback, low self-efficacy |

- **Major depressive disorder:** five or more symptoms in the same two weeks, representing a change in functioning, including depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure. Others: weight change, insomnia/hypersomnia, psychomotor change, fatigue, worthlessness or guilt, poor concentration, thoughts of death; with distress and no manic episode. ✅
- **Depressive realism:** depressives notice subtle negative interactions that others miss; most people survive on rose-colored glasses. Distortion is not always negative. 🟢
- **Mania:** at least three of inflated self-esteem, decreased need for sleep, talkativeness, flight of ideas, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity over a week; Pihl's ski-hill loan anecdote illustrates impulsivity.

## Depressive Disorders in the DSM
- **Disruptive mood dysregulation disorder:** severe temper outbursts, wild emotional swings.
- **Major depressive disorder.**
- **Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia):** "major depression lite."
- **Premenstrual dysphoric disorder.**
- **Substance/medication-induced depressive disorder.**
- **Seasonal pattern:** about four times likelier in women; responds to bright light (about 2,500 lux, five times office lighting, a couple of hours each morning). ✅
- **Peripartum/postpartum:** about 9% during pregnancy, 7% in the first 12 months; can be severe and affect mother-child attachment. ✅
- 🟡 Pihl said the DSM lists a set of these ("1, 2, 3, 4, 7"); the count was garbled, so the list above is what he actually named.

## Screens, Peers and Development
- Pihl: perhaps 90% of pathology has roots in childhood, interacting with later stressors. 🟡 (his estimate)
- Depression rises sharply from age 15, much more for girls; the older graph predates handheld devices.
- Since about 2011, girls aged 12 to 15 show an upswing in self-poisoning, depressive episodes and suicide. Screen-time proponents cite less face-to-face contact, cyberbullying, sleep loss and exposure to suicide methods. **Pihl: partially right, not totally.** Declines in hanging out with friends and dating began in the 1990s, before smartphones (about 2012). ✅
- **South Australia (50,000-70,000 students, grades 4-9, 2017-2022):** lower satisfaction, optimism, happiness, emotion regulation and perseverance; more worry and sadness.
- **6,595 adolescents:** media time rises with internalizing and externalizing problems; Seattle has sued major platforms. 🟢

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 Treating "depression" as one disease: Pihl's repeated point is several disorders with different, mostly unknown causes, so one-size treatment fails.
- 🔴 Assuming a diagnosis reflects a biological test: the 543 DSM disorders rest almost entirely on interview and symptoms.
- 🔴 Blaming smartphones alone: the decline in teen socializing predates them; Pihl calls the screen theory partly right.
- 🔴 Assuming one episode is the end: recurrence odds climb to about 90% after three episodes.
- 🔴 Assuming a depressive child will stay depressed: trajectories differ, and only the increasing group is the worry.

## Practical Takeaway
For study purposes: know the four symptom clusters and the five-symptom, two-week rule, remember that recurrence risk climbs with each episode, and treat prevalence and prescription figures as approximate numbers read from slides. If this material resonates personally, it is a reason to talk to a clinician, not to self-diagnose from a lecture note.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Pihl says DSM disorders grew from 128 (1952) to 543 (2022) and that diagnosis rests mostly on interviews. What is the best reading of that point?**
A. Biological tests now confirm most DSM diagnoses, so growth reflects discovery
B. The count grew because disorders were found in blood and imaging
C. \* Definitions are bestowed, not found, and diagnosis depends largely on interview and symptoms, so the number reflects classification choices as much as new biology
*Why C: Pihl notes that almost none of the 543 definitions include biological measures and that names are bestowed, not found.*

**2. A person has had two depressive episodes. According to the lecture, what is the approximate chance of a third?**
A. \* About 70%
B. About 20%
C. About 50% regardless of history
*Why A: recurrence odds rise with each episode: 50-60% after one, 70% after two, 90% after three.*

**3. Which cluster of depressive symptoms does Pihl call the essence of depression, and which correlates least with the rest?**
A. Cognitive is the essence; motivational correlates least
B. \* Affective is the essence; physical symptoms (sleep, appetite, sex drive) correlate least
C. Physical is the essence; affective correlates least
*Why B: he says affective or emotional symptoms are the essence, while physical symptoms correlate least with the other three clusters.*

**4. Why does Pihl resist blaming the rise in youth depression on smartphones alone?**
A. Because teen distress has not risen at all
B. Because media use is unrelated to mental health in any dataset
C. \* Because declines in time with friends and dating began in the 1990s, before smartphones, so screens are partly but not wholly responsible
*Why C: he says the media link to mental health is real but the curves were already turning in the early 1990s.*
