# Substances — 00 Street Supply, the Hidden Danger
*Alcohol, THC and psilocybin mushrooms are studied as medicines or sold legally in some places, but most real-world use is not medical, not supervised, and often from illegal or unregulated sources where the dose and contents cannot be known.*

> **Educational, not medical advice.** If someone is in danger after using a substance, call 911 (U.S.) or local emergency services. For help with substance use: SAMHSA National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish).

## Why This Note Comes First

News about "medical cannabis" or "psilocybin therapy" can make these substances sound safe. In a clinical trial, a patient receives a measured, pure dose, is screened for risk, and is supervised by trained staff. **Almost none of that is true for ordinary use.** The gap between the lab and the street is where most of the harm happens.

## Medical Use vs. Real-World Use

| | Medical or research setting | Typical real-world use |
|---|---|---|
| **Dose** | Measured precisely (e.g., 25 mg synthetic psilocybin) | Unknown; varies by batch, plant, mushroom or drink |
| **Purity** | Pharmaceutical grade, tested | Can be mislabeled, contaminated or replaced with something else |
| **Screening** | People with risk factors (psychosis, bipolar disorder, heart disease) excluded | No one checks |
| **Supervision** | Trained staff present, medical help ready | Often alone, at a party, or driving |
| **Follow-up** | Planned, with therapy support | None |
| **Legal status** | Authorized | Often illegal; no recourse if something goes wrong |

## The Hidden Dangers, Substance by Substance

### Cannabis (THC)
- 🟢 **Potency has multiplied.** Seized U.S. cannabis averaged roughly 4% THC in the mid-1990s; many products today are 15–30% for flower and 60–90% for concentrates (vapes, "dabs"). Older research on "marijuana" does not describe today's products.
- 🟢 **Edibles** take 30 minutes to 2 hours to act, so people take more, then get far more than intended. Emergency visits for children eating edibles have risen sharply.
- 🟢 **Illegal vape cartridges** caused the 2019 lung-injury outbreak (EVALI), largely linked to vitamin E acetate added to THC products; dozens died.
- 🟢 High-potency daily use is linked to higher rates of psychosis (see the Cannabis risks note).

### Psilocybin mushrooms
- 🟢 **Potency varies widely** between species, mushrooms, and even parts of the same mushroom, so a "dose" is a guess.
- ✅ **Misidentification can kill.** Some wild mushrooms that look similar are deadly poisonous (for example, *Amanita* and *Galerina* species), causing liver failure.
- ✅ **"Mushroom" products may not contain mushrooms at all.** In 2024 the FDA recalled Diamond Shruumz "microdosing" chocolates and gummies after more than 100 illnesses across many states, including seizures and hospitalizations, and deaths were investigated. FDA tests found undisclosed substances such as muscimol.

### Alcohol
- ✅ Alcohol is legal and labeled, but **legal is not the same as safe** (see the Alcohol note): it is a leading cause of preventable death and cancer.
- 🟢 **Counterfeit or homemade alcohol** can contain methanol, which causes blindness and death; outbreaks happen regularly worldwide, including in tourist areas.
- 🟢 **Drinks at parties** often have unknown strength and can be spiked.

### Any street drug
- ✅ **Fentanyl contamination:** illegal pills and powders can contain fentanyl, which is deadly in tiny amounts. Overdose deaths in the U.S. have been driven mainly by fentanyl; many victims did not know they were taking it.

## Key Corrections

- ❌ "It's natural, so it's safe." ✅ Many deadly poisons are natural; natural products are often the hardest to dose.
- ❌ "It's used in medicine, so it's fine." ✅ Morphine is used in medicine too. The setting, dose and supervision are what make medical use safer.
- ❌ "It's legal now." ✅ Legal products still carry real risks, and much of the supply is still illegal or untested.

## Practical Takeaway

The safest choice is not to use. For anyone who does, the street supply adds a layer of danger that no amount of research on medical use can remove: **you cannot know the dose, you cannot know what is in it, and no one is there to help if it goes wrong.**

## Socratic Question

> If a medicine is only considered safe with a measured dose, screening and supervision, what is left of that safety when all three are removed?
