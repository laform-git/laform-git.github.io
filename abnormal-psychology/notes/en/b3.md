## Why This Matters
This is the hinge lecture of Part II: it finishes the "what treats depression" question and opens the "how do we recognize risk" question. Pihl's central message is humbling and useful: there are *multiple depressions* with different pathways, suicide is statistically common enough to matter hugely at the societal level, yet no screening tool or prediction model works well enough to be used. Understanding why prediction fails is the real lesson, and it protects against both overconfidence and myths.

## Part 1 — Treatments Compared (Closing the Depression Section)

| Treatment | Pihl's point | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **ECT** (applied to non-dominant hemisphere) | Dose-response: highest dosage gives biggest effect. Clinically far more effective than magnetic seizure therapy, but less palatable to patients. Seizure appears required for the effect. More effective than ketamine, but the benefit does not last. | ✅ As stated in lecture |
| **TMS** (magnetic stimulation) | No seizure. Effect in older adults is "not that dramatic," but it is used more in practice because it is "in and out" and does not require an MD. | ✅ |
| **Ketamine** | Weaker than ECT in the forest plot shown. Durability unknown: "just hasn't been done long enough." | 🟡 Open question |
| **CBT** | No physical side effects, no interference with medication, often preferred by patients, teaches coping tools. Relapse lower after acute CBT than after drugs, and lower for combined treatment than drugs alone. | ✅ |
| **Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy** | Adds to CBT's relapse prevention by teaching people to handle depressing thoughts rather than avoid them. | ✅ |
| **Internet and app programs** | Results vary "dramatically"; some programs make people worse; no way to know which is effective or whether a single finding is spurious. | 🟡 Heterogeneous evidence |
| **Exercise** (walking, jogging, yoga, aerobic, strength, tai chi) | Each reduces depression in the summary shown. Pihl's clinical note: getting a depressed patient active is the most useful goal and also the hardest. | ✅ / 🟢 |
| **Drug plus psychotherapy combinations** | Forest plot shows only a "minimal effect." | 🟡 |

### Two kinds of depressed patients
- A study of 1,000+ patients given a cognitive battery (heavy on frontal-lobe function) found two groups; one is markedly deficient. That group is about **28%** of depressives. ✅
- Imaging pointed to the **prefrontal cortex and cingulate cortex**. ✅
- Pihl's inference: for this cognitively impaired subgroup, standard CBT is a poor fit ("they're not functioning that well cognitively"). 🟡 This is his reading of the figure, not a stated trial result.

### CBT's logic (Beck)
Depression involves a maladaptive way of processing the world. CBT identifies the thinking errors and re-frames them; if you change how someone thinks, you change how they interact with their environment, which is why the effect endures. Relapse figure quoted: combined treatment reduced relapse "72% to 46%." 🟡 The direction and comparison group are not fully clear in the transcript.

### The Rejoyn point
The FDA-approved app (transcribed "Rejoin") is designed to stimulate the amygdala and prefrontal cortex. Pihl says the FDA approved it because it "doesn't hurt anybody," but the data show it does not work, so "I'd argue it hurts somebody." 🟡 His opinion; he also mentions Woebot, whose published data he describes as scattered.

## Part 2 — Why Suicide Matters Societally
- **Golden Gate Bridge net:** about 30 deaths a year; survival under 5%. The steel mesh net was budgeted around $140M and is reportedly past $400M (Pihl's figures). Debate: that money versus general mental health spending. ✅ as his illustration, 🟡 on the exact costs.
- **Method asymmetry:** only ~2% of suicides involve jumping while ~50% involve firearms, yet nets are not placed around firearms. His point: where we spend prevention effort is not proportional to where the deaths are.
- **MAID (medical assistance in dying) in Canada:** Pihl cites 44,958 deaths from June 2016 to end of 2022, 4.4% of all deaths nationally and 7% in Quebec, with the number projected to reach 65,000 per year. He uses strong wording and links MAID to organ-donation questions. Mental illness was excluded until March 17, 2023, which he reads as an admission that "we don't know what it is." 🟡 Contested political and ethical territory; figures are the lecturer's, not verified here.

## Part 3 — Who Is at Risk (the Epidemiology)
All rates in the lecture are per 100,000 population.
- **Sex and age:** Canadian male peak at ages 50-64; in the US, male suicides are far higher but peak over 75. MAID recipients mostly over 70. ✅
- **Self-inflicted injury:** hospitalizations are dominated by girls aged 10-19, the mirror of the male lead in completed suicide. ✅
- **Race and group:** high in American Indian/Alaska Native and white populations; Aboriginal males aged 15-24 show a very large increase in Canadian data; daily stress levels are far higher in American Indian/Alaska Native respondents. ✅
- **Alcohol:** alcohol use disorders correlate strongly with suicide mortality; drug poisoning, alcohol deaths and suicide map together geographically (Pihl corrected himself: drug poisoning clusters in the old Rust Belt, suicide is more prevalent in the West). ✅
- **US ideation funnel:** 12.2 million adults a year have suicidal thoughts, 3.5 million make plans, 1.7 million attempt. ✅
- **Method:** about 57-58% of men use guns; women more often use poisoning. Suicide is thought about often but is still frequently an **impulsive act**; outcome depends heavily on method. ✅
- **Occupation:** highest by work status for oil roughnecks and miners; among professionals, physicians, dentists, police, veterinarians. Pihl's explanation: high responsibility with low control (time limits, paperwork), lost status, aversive work. 🟡 Interpretation, not a tested mechanism.
- **Autism:** autistic girls are more prone to suicidal thoughts and behavior than autistic boys, the reverse of the non-autistic pattern; mothers often report bullying. The study did not separate deaths from self-harm. 🟡
- **Sexual orientation:** in a survey of ~190,000 US adults, ideation rises for gay, lesbian and especially bisexual respondents regardless of metropolitan status. Pihl notes prejudice and isolation likely explain part but "I don't know if it explains everything." ✅ / 🟡
- **Cyberbullying:** both victims and perpetrators report more suicidal thoughts. ✅
- **Country:** Lesotho highest (72.4 per 100,000), tied to illness, men returning from collapsing South African mines with no status; Guyana second (~40) mostly pesticide overdose among farmers, with statistics that are not comparable year to year. Pihl stresses that national figures were historically unreliable (Catholic Ireland and Italy under-reported because of church burial rules). ✅ / 🟡
- **Accidents:** suicide and accidents both rose. In his Transport Canada record review, single-driver crashes often involved intoxication and a discrete problem that week, leading him to suspect hidden suicides. 🟡 Informal review, not a rigorous psychological autopsy.

## Part 4 — Why Prediction and Screening Fail
- **Trajectories matter:** of childhood symptom trajectories (withdrawn, somatic complaints, anxious/depressed, social, thought, attention, delinquent, aggressive), the ones that predict suicide are **withdrawn behavior** and **somatic complaints**: people who are psychologically alone and cannot test their thoughts against others. ✅
- **Diagnoses are weak predictors:** suicidal behavior appears across mood, psychotic, anxiety, substance, and eating disorders, but only a small percentage of people with each disorder die by suicide (anorexia ~2%, bipolar ~5%, schizophrenia ~5%, substance use ~3.5% in women). Present in all, predictive in none. ✅
- **USPSTF:** screening is not recommended because there is no adequate screening instrument. ✅
- **DSM proposal:** "suicidal behavior disorder" requires an attempt within 24 months, not non-suicidal self-injury, not during delirium, and not solely for political or religious aims. ✅
- **Machine learning:** only marginally successful; top predictors in women were socioeconomic status, father's age, mother's education, positive interactions, gestational age. Pihl's verdict: "add enough variables together, you can predict anything, but it's meaningless." ✅
- What does correlate in the study shown: the Scale for Suicidal Ideation and family history. ✅

## Part 5 — Fallacies About Suicide

| Fallacy | Pihl's correction |
|---|---|
| It is about young love (Romeo and Juliet) | Young people are great *attempters* and seldom completers; completed suicide skews elderly. |
| It is an act of passion | Elderly men dominate the statistics. (Anecdote: cut hands suggest someone changed their mind after a jump.) |
| Bad weather causes it | No winter correlation; the peak is in **spring**, with a smaller bump near Christmas: a **contrast effect** when everyone else feels better. |
| It is a national habit | Historic national statistics were unreliable and politically or religiously shaped. |
| Those who threaten never do | About **75%** give some warning (maybe more); roughly **10-13%** of attempters later complete; repeating may become easier. |
| Notes explain everything | Only about **one-third** leave notes, and Shneidman found they often do not state reasons. (Five categories begun: thesis, antithesis, no viewpoint, new insight, conflicted/empathic. Transcript ends here.) |

Historical aside: Pihl recommends A. Alvarez, *The Savage God*, for the history (punishment of suicide with gibbeting and crossroads burial), which contrasts with modern attitudes. ✅

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 3)
- **"There are multiple depressions"** with probably different pathways, so treatment will improve once "differential etiologies" are identified.
- **About 28% of depressives** form a cognitively deficient subgroup, per the study shown; Pihl would not use standard CBT with them.
- **"Add enough variables together, you can predict anything, but it's meaningless."**
- **~2% of suicides are jumps; ~50% are firearms; the bridge got the net.**
- **The spring peak:** it is contrast, not gloom, that raises risk.
- **Withdrawn and somatic trajectories predict**, not aggression or delinquency.
- **"The secret to being miserable is to have the leisure to bother about whether you're happy or not."** (George Bernard Shaw, as quoted)

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "Winter and dark weather drive suicide": the lecture reports no winter correlation and a spring peak.
- 🔴 "Someone who talks about it won't act": roughly three-quarters of suicides give some warning.
- 🔴 "Suicide is mainly a young person's romantic act": youth attempt more, older men complete more.
- 🔴 "A diagnosis tells you who is at risk": most people with each disorder never die by suicide, which is why screening fails.
- 🔴 "A suicide note will explain why": most people leave none, and notes often do not explain reasons.
- 🔴 "One depression, one treatment": at least two cognitive subtypes, and CBT may suit them differently.

## Practical Takeaway
For study purposes: treat depression as a family of conditions; take withdrawal and bodily complaints seriously as signals; do not rely on diagnosis alone; and favor connection (Pihl's prevention suggestion: regular, undistracted family meals, started young, because teens cannot be dragged to the table later). For anyone who needs support right now, call or text 988 (US and Canada) or local emergency services; this note is not a substitute for professional care.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Pihl argues that machine-learning models predicting suicide are "meaningless" even when marginally successful. What is the reasoning?**
A. Machine learning is always less accurate than clinical judgment
B. Suicide is too rare for any statistical method to study
C. \* The strongest predictors found (father's age, gestational age, mother's education) lack a plausible causal story; with enough variables you can predict almost anything, so prediction without mechanism is not usable
*Why C: Pihl's objection is about meaningless variable sets, not about machine learning per se. He also expects to "learn a lot from AI" later.*

**2. Which explanation does the lecture give for the spring peak in suicide rates?**
A. \* A contrast effect: when most people feel better, a person in turmoil is more sharply set apart
B. Longer daylight directly lowers mood
C. Winter depression delays its effect by several months
*Why A: Pihl rejects the weather fallacy and cites the same contrast logic for the small Christmas bump.*

**3. Why does the US Preventive Services Task Force decline to recommend suicide screening?**
A. Early detection would not help anyone
B. \* No adequate screening instrument exists, so the benefit of early detection cannot be realized
C. Screening is too costly compared with firearm regulation
*Why B: the lecture states that early detection would be helpful in principle, but without validated instruments, screening is not worthwhile at present.*

**4. A depressed subgroup (about 28%) performs poorly on frontal-lobe cognitive tests. What does Pihl suggest follows?**
A. \* Standard CBT is likely a poor fit for them and they deserve individual attention
B. They should be treated with ketamine only
C. They are not truly depressed
*Why A: he says "don't do cognitive behavior therapy with them," while still crediting CBT's benefits for others. 🟡 This is his inference from the figure.*
