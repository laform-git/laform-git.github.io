# Pihl — Abnormal Psychology Part I, Ch.6: Brain Health and Mental Illness
*Pihl's closing lecture of Part I: dopamine and addiction, the limbic system and frontal lobes through famous brain cases, then schizophrenia as the "poster child" of the medical model, and his admission that we still do not understand etiology.*

*Source: Dr. Robert O. Pihl, Peterson Academy course "Introduction to Abnormal Psychology Part I," Lecture 6 (transcript, includes a closing Q&A). Last chapter of a six-lecture series.*

## Why This Matters
This lecture is where the course's "brain" thread lands. Pihl moves from dopamine as the common denominator of drug abuse, through what specific brain regions do (amygdala, hippocampus, frontal lobes), to schizophrenia, the disorder he calls the backbone of the medical model. The punchline is deliberately humbling: treatments are "trial and error" because cause is not understood. Useful both as a map of brain systems and as a lesson in how far case studies and group differences can be pushed.

## Dopamine and Drug Abuse
- Dopamine is central in schizophrenia and in "darn near every drug that tends to be abused." ✅
- **Ritalin example:** it acts on the dopamine system and is easily abused if inhaled or injected, but not as a pill, because the pill reaches the system much more slowly. ✅
- Blocking D1/D5 receptors reduced cocaine craving in human studies; reduced dopamine transmission reduces alcohol consumption; naltrexone was used to blunt the dopamine response to alcohol. ✅ (as stated by Pihl)
- Self-administration of drugs increases dopamine activity in the **mesolimbic system** (nucleus accumbens), areas that evolved to reinforce survival, sex and food. Destroying those neurons stops self-administration. ✅
- Pihl's own lab study showed specific dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens in people at risk for alcoholism. ✅
- 🟡 The transcript says "dopamine agonist drugs, drugs which block dopamine" in one breath; agonists stimulate and antagonists block, so this is likely a slip or transcription garble. Not carried forward.
- Brain health is shaped from outside the skull: nutrition, exercise, drug abuse, education, social connections, even pollution. ✅

## The Limbic System and the Amygdala
- Limbic = "ring": hippocampus, amygdala, fornix, corpus callosum (as listed by Pihl). Basic behaviors, the **four Fs**: feeding, fighting, fleeing, mating. ✅
- **Amygdala default:** it labels stimuli as fearful unless another region, immediately the hippocampus, says "we've seen this before, don't worry." ✅
- Pairing a neutral light with a shock conditions the light to trigger the amygdala response, a model for learned fear and avoidance. ✅
- Lesioning the amygdala: the person no longer recognizes or responds to fear. ✅
- Animal finding: attentive mothers increase amygdala functioning by developing extra inhibitory receptors, which reduces fear. ✅
- **Antelope video:** anxiety "steals away any productive response"; the panicked antelope runs straight into the sleeping lions. ✅

## Case Studies — What One Brain Can Show

| Case | What happened | What Pihl draws from it |
|---|---|---|
| **Heath's septal patient (Tulane, 1960s)** | 24-year-old with suspected temporal lobe epilepsy had electrodes implanted and a belt device to self-stimulate; obsessively stimulated the septal area; later developed attraction to women and his sexual behavior changed | Sexuality is far more malleable than the "fixed" stereotype |
| **Charles Whitman (Texas Tower)** | IQ 138, engineer; killed 13, wounded 34, plus his mother and wife; autopsy (which he requested) found a walnut-sized tumor pressed on the amygdala | The tumor is felt to explain the marked shift in behavior |
| **Deep brain stimulation, subgenual cingulate** | 38-year-old depressed 20 years, every treatment failed; stimulation brought calm, interest, depression vanished | Large trials built on this one case did not work: it helps a few and "we don't know with who" |
| **Phineas Gage** | Tamping rod through the frontal lobe, survived; from upright leader to hard-to-get-along reprobate | Poster boy for frontal lobe damage |
| **Lorber's hydrocephalus scans** | ~600 scans; student with IQ 126 had a 1 mm cortex vs ~4.5 cm normal; 10% had ventricles filling 95% of the cranium; half had IQ of at least 100 | "Is your brain really necessary?" (joke); shows surprising reserve |

✅ Pihl presents these as case-level illustrations, and explicitly warns about generalizing one case into a population-wide treatment.

## Stress, Cortisol and the Brain
- Stress runs through the amygdala, hypothalamus, sympathetic system and adrenals, which secrete cortisol. ✅
- Pihl showed graphs where this stress pathway is linked to **reduced hippocampal and brain volume** in depression, Cushing's disease and PTSD. ✅

## Frontal Lobes — What Makes Us Human
- Organization, planning, problem solving, inhibition, foresight, impulse control, social appropriateness. ✅
- 🟡 Pihl says our frontal lobes are "102%" larger and later "120%" bigger than other primates'. Inconsistent in the transcript; treat as "substantially larger" only.
- **Concussion and head impact:** NFL players cited as 19 times more likely to have memory problems; ~144,000 ER visits per year for concussions under age 18 in the US; chronic traumatic encephalopathy; soccer players showed memory and planning deterioration; depression scores rise with concussion scores. ✅ 🟡 (figures quoted from slides, not sourced in the lecture)
- **Alcohol as a frontal lobe drug:** in a lab study (with Jordan), two doses (0.66 and 1.32 ml/kg) produced dose-related deficits on frontal tasks. Alcohol "washes the whole brain" but hits the functions that make us human; Pihl says it is involved in at least 50% of murders, assaults and rapes, for both perpetrator and victim. ✅

## Schizophrenia — Poster Child of the Medical Model
**Scale:** about 0.4% to 1% of the population, yet a leading contributor to disability and economic burden; add 4-7% of genetic relatives with sub-threshold features (psychotic-like experiences, magical thinking, social disinterest). ✅

**Brain differences listed:** delayed/reduced P300, eye tracking problems, lateral ventricle enlargement, smaller frontal lobes, enlarged sulci, left hippocampal dysfunction, prefrontal hypofunction, increased basal ganglia activity. ✅

**Meta-analysis:** 2,833 people with schizophrenia vs controls showed reduced tissue in thalamus, amygdala, hippocampus and nucleus accumbens. ✅ A UK biobank study found people at high polygenic risk without the disorder already had smaller gray matter. ✅

🟡 **Caution:** one study distinguished patients with 83% accuracy from brain structure, but Pihl warns that with enough variables you can statistically separate any groups, so it does not prove the disorder "is" the structure.

**Competing models (Pihl: "common models, many others to come")**

| Model | Core idea |
|---|---|
| Cognitive | Hypofrontality: frontal area cannot handle input, so the person assigns idiosyncratic meaning (delusions, hallucinations) |
| Neurobiological | Working memory deficit leading to dissociation and a need to make sense of things |
| Psychobiological | Attention deficit starts the chain |
| Thalamic filtering | Excess dopamine and an "open tube" thalamus sends too much information to the cortex; supported by phenothiazines lowering dopamine (and causing extrapyramidal symptoms) |
| Cognitive dysmetria | Cortico-cerebellar-thalamic-cortical circuit inefficiency |

✅ Andreasen (1999): the biggest problem is not finding the gene but "defining what schizophrenia is" at the clinical level (perceptual, thought, or affective disorder?).

**Louis Wain:** cat painter who ended in an institution; his late paintings are said to depict psychosis. 🟡 Pihl himself says that is hard to confirm.

## Developmental Factors in Schizophrenia
- **Genetics:** twin studies show monozygotic twins of affected parents at highest risk. Adoption study: children of mothers with schizophrenia who were adopted away developed it at about 10% vs about 1% for controls, regardless of the rearing environment. ✅ 🟡 (the transcript's counts, 164 vs 155 vs 185, do not line up cleanly)
- **Schizophrenia spectrum:** carriers who are not diagnosable show antisocial behavior, impulsivity, isolation, anxiety, panic, alcohol and drug use. ✅
- **"Can you catch it?"** Winter births were over-represented in a New York institutionalized sample; in the Helsinki flu epidemic, second-trimester exposure raised risk. ✅
- **Dutch Hunger Winter (1944-45):** about a twofold increase in risk in the exposed birth cohort. ✅
- **Fetal hypoxia:** predicted reduced temporal gray matter, ventricle enlargement and higher risk in birth record plus MRI studies. ✅
- **Prediction problem (attenuated psychosis syndrome):** intact reality testing, mild symptoms at least weekly in the past month. False positive rates quoted: 57, 57, 59, 50, 78 and 65 percent. ✅ The ethical question: what happens to someone labeled pre-psychotic who never converts?

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 6)
- **"Darn near every drug that tends to be abused is a drug which markedly increases dopaminergic levels."**
- **The amygdala is the default emergency label;** the hippocampus is the "it's okay, we've seen this" brake.
- **Gage, Whitman, the DBS patient and Lorber's students:** four cases, four different lessons; none should be generalized alone.
- **"If I have enough variables, I'll be able to separate every group, just statistically."**
- **False positive rates of 50-78%** in psychosis-risk prediction.
- **"Our treatments are trial and error,"** because we do not understand cause.
- **Closing answer on children's mental health:** "A stable family at home. Without a doubt."

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 Sexual behavior and the brain are "fixed": Heath's single 1960s case is Pihl's evidence of malleability (one case, so treat as suggestive, not proof).
- 🔴 A dramatic single-case treatment success proves the treatment: the DBS case did not scale; it works for a few and we cannot say whom.
- 🔴 Group brain differences equal the disorder: with enough variables any groups separate, and 83% classification accuracy does not show causation.
- 🔴 Schizophrenia is purely genetic: adoption data show about 10% vs 1% genetic effect, but infection, famine, hypoxia and birth complications also matter.
- 🔴 Early identification of psychosis is solved: false positive rates of 50-78% mean labeling has real costs.
- 🔴 Antidepressant success proves the chemical model: Pihl notes a large placebo effect plus the passage of time (effects take about a month).

## Practical Takeaway
Hold brain explanations loosely. The lecture's practical thread is protection: concussions and head impacts matter, alcohol hits frontal control, chronic stress is linked to hippocampal shrinkage, and Pihl's own closing advice is social: a stable family, active kids (sports help, open classrooms did not), and combining approaches rather than either-or. Study notes only; not medical advice.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Why does Pihl say the amygdala keeps responding to a light that was paired with a shock?**
A. The light physically damages the amygdala
B. The hippocampus permanently shuts the amygdala down after a shock
C. \* The amygdala treats stimuli as threatening by default, and pairing conditions the neutral light to trigger that fear response
*Why C: the amygdala labels stimuli fearful unless the hippocampus signals safety; conditioning attaches the response to the light.*

**2. A brain scan model separates schizophrenia patients from controls with 83% accuracy. What is Pihl's caution?**
A. \* With enough variables you can statistically separate any groups, so separation does not prove the structure is the cause
B. Brain scans are never useful in psychiatry
C. 83% is too low to be meaningful in any research
*Why A: he explicitly says including obvious variables can separate groups, so it needs careful interpretation.*

**3. What did the single successful deep brain stimulation case teach about generalizing?**
A. One success proves the treatment works for all depressed people
B. \* It worked in a few people and we do not know in whom, so the large-scale trial failed to confirm it
C. Deep brain stimulation only works in the frontal cortex
*Why B: Pihl uses it to warn against turning one case into a population-wide intervention.*

**4. Why are psychosis-risk (prodromal) labels a problem?**
A. They are always accurate in the first episode
B. They only apply to schizophrenia in adults over 60
C. \* False positive rates of about 50-78% mean many labeled people never develop the disorder, with unknown consequences of being labeled
*Why C: the lecture lists false positive rates of 57, 57, 59, 50, 78 and 65 percent and asks what happens to the mislabeled.*
