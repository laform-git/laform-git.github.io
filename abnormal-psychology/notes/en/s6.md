# Substances — 03 Psilocybin, Evidence and Risks
*Psilocybin, the active compound in "magic mushrooms," is being tested as a supervised treatment for severe depression, with real but modest results. That research says almost nothing about the safety of taking mushrooms on your own.*

> **Educational, not medical advice.** Psilocybin is illegal under U.S. federal law (Schedule I). It is not FDA-approved. SAMHSA Helpline: 1-800-662-4357.

## Core Insight

In trials, patients receive one or two measured doses of synthetic psilocybin after careful screening, in a quiet room with trained therapists present for hours, plus follow-up. Street mushrooms come with none of this: unknown potency, possible misidentification, no screening and no help if a "bad trip" turns dangerous.

## What the Research Shows

- 🟢 **Treatment-resistant depression:** a 2022 *New England Journal of Medicine* trial (Goodwin et al., 233 patients) found a single 25 mg dose reduced depression more than a 1 mg dose at 3 weeks, but the benefit faded by 12 weeks, and some patients had suicidal thoughts or self-harm.
- 🟢 **Versus a standard antidepressant:** a 2021 NEJM trial (Carhart-Harris et al., 59 patients) found no significant difference from escitalopram on the main measure.
- 🟢 **Phase 3 trials (Compass Pathways, COMP005 and COMP006, 2025–2026):** synthetic psilocybin (COMP360) met its main goal; in COMP006 the difference from a tiny dose was about 3.8 points on a 60-point depression scale at 6 weeks. The company is submitting an application to the FDA; **as of late 2026 it is not approved.**
- 🟡 **Blinding problem:** most participants can tell whether they received the real drug, which can inflate results in any psychedelic trial.
- 🟡 Smaller studies in addiction, end-of-life anxiety and PTSD are promising but early.
- 🟢 Some U.S. states (Oregon from 2023, Colorado from 2025) allow supervised psilocybin services under state law; these are not FDA-approved treatments.

## The Risks

### In any setting
- 🟢 **Acute anxiety, panic and paranoia** ("bad trips"), which can lead to dangerous behavior, falls or accidents.
- 🟢 **Raised heart rate and blood pressure** during the experience.
- ✅ **Psychosis or mania** in people with a personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder; trials exclude them.
- 🟢 **Persistent perception changes** (HPPD) in a small number of users.
- 🟡 **Suicidal thoughts** were reported in some trial patients with severe depression.

### Outside the clinic (most use)
- ✅ **Potency varies** between mushrooms and batches, so the dose is a guess.
- ✅ **Misidentification:** some look-alike wild mushrooms are deadly (liver failure).
- ✅ **Fake products:** the 2024 FDA recall of Diamond Shruumz "microdosing" candies followed more than 100 illnesses and deaths under investigation; FDA tests found undisclosed substances such as muscimol.
- 🟢 **No screening and no help:** the people most at risk (history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, heart disease, or on certain medications such as lithium) are exactly those who would be excluded from a trial.
- 🟡 **"Microdosing":** controlled studies have mostly found effects no better than placebo.

## Key Corrections

- ❌ "Mushrooms are proven to cure depression." ✅ The best trials show a modest effect, in severe cases, under supervision; not approved yet.
- ❌ "It's natural, so it's safe." ✅ Wild mushrooms include deadly species, and dose is unpredictable.
- ❌ "Microdosing is harmless self-care." ✅ Products are unregulated, and benefits beyond placebo are unproven.

## Practical Takeaway

Promising research is not permission. Psilocybin may become a supervised treatment for a narrow group of patients; that is entirely different from taking mushrooms bought or picked without knowing what or how much they contain.
