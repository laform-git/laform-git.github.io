# JW-02 They Deny That the Holy Spirit Is God

> **Error 2 of 10.** They take the Third Person of the Trinity and turn him into a thing.

## What they teach

The Holy Spirit is not a person. He is God's "active force," an impersonal power God sends out the way a man uses electricity. That's why their Bible writes "holy spirit" in small letters and calls the Spirit "it."

**Where it came from.** This is the second half of the Arian error. After Nicaea, a group called the **Pneumatomachians** (the "Spirit-fighters") taught that the Spirit was a creature or a mere power. The Church answered them at **Constantinople in 381**. Russell's movement simply picked the old error back up.

## Why it is an error

- **A force has no mind and no will, but the Spirit does both.** He teaches, speaks, decides, grieves and prays for us. Things don't do that. Persons do.
- **It breaks the baptismal formula.** Jesus commands baptism in the one *name* of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. You don't put a force in the same name as two Persons.
- **It empties the Christian life.** If the Spirit is only power, then no one dwells in your soul, consoles you, or prays in you. You have a battery instead of a Guest.

## Response

### Scripture ✅

- **He can be lied to.** *"Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit? … You have not lied to men but to God"* (Acts 5:3–4). Peter calls the Spirit God in the same breath.
- **He speaks and chooses.** *"The Holy Spirit said, 'Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul'"* (Acts 13:2). Notice: *for me*.
- **He decides.** *"It has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us"* (Acts 15:28).
- **He teaches.** *"The Counselor, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things"* (Jn 14:26).
- **He grieves.** *"Do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God"* (Eph 4:30). You can't hurt the feelings of electricity.
- **He prays for us.** *"The Spirit himself intercedes for us with sighs too deep for words"* (Rom 8:26).
- **He wills.** He gives gifts *"to each one individually as he wills"* (1 Cor 12:11).

### Catechism ✅

- CCC 243–245: the Spirit is a divine Person, revealed by Jesus as another Paraclete, one in being with the Father and the Son.
- CCC 685: to believe in the Holy Spirit is to profess that he is one of the Persons of the Holy Trinity, consubstantial with the Father and the Son.
- CCC 687–688: the Spirit speaks through the prophets and is known in Scripture, Tradition, the sacraments and prayer.

### Councils and Encyclicals ✅

- **Constantinople (381):** *"I believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the giver of life … who with the Father and the Son is adored and glorified."* No one adores a force.
- **Leo XIII,** ***Divinum Illud Munus*** (1897): the Holy Spirit is a divine Person who dwells in the souls of the just as in a temple.
- **John Paul II,** ***Dominum et Vivificantem*** (1986): the Holy Spirit is the Person-Love and Person-Gift of the Trinity.

### Doctors 🟢

- **St. Basil the Great** wrote *On the Holy Spirit* (c. 375) against the Spirit-fighters. He insisted that the Church gives the Spirit the same glory as the Father and the Son, because he shares the same divine nature.
- **St. Gregory of Nazianzus**, in his *Fifth Theological Oration*, answered those who called the Spirit a mere power: if he is God, he is to be worshipped.

### Saints 🟢

- The **Church's oldest prayer**, the *Glory Be*, gives equal glory to Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Every Christian who prays it is confessing that the Spirit is God.

## One line at the door

> *"Peter told Ananias he lied to the Holy Spirit, and then said he lied to God. Can you lie to electricity?"*

🕯 **Reflect:** The Spirit dwells in me as in a temple. Do I ever speak to him as a Person, or only think about him as a power?

### The Holy Spirit
**Catholic teaching:** ✅ The Spirit is the third divine Person (CCC 687–688). Besides teaching, interceding, grieving and being lied to (above), he sends (Acts 13:4). A force does none of these.
