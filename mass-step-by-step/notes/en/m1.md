# The Mass, Return Home — 1 — Coming In

## Holy water at the door

**What happens:** Just inside the door there's a small bowl of water. You dip your fingers and make the Sign of the Cross.

**Why:** It's a reminder of your Baptism, the day God made you his child. Every time you walk into church, you're saying: *I belong here. This is my family's house.*

## The bow before you sit

**What happens:** Before you step into the pew, you genuflect (touch your right knee to the floor) toward the tabernacle. If you can't kneel, a deep bow is fine.

**Why:** Jesus is there. You'd greet a friend when you walk into his home. This is how we greet the King.

## The entrance

**What happens:** Everyone stands. A procession comes up the aisle: a cross held high, candles, the readers, and last of all the priest. Often a hymn is sung. The priest kisses the altar.

**Why:** We stand because something great is starting. The cross leads the way because everything that happens today flows from it. And the kiss? The altar stands for Christ himself. The priest greets him first.

## The Sign of the Cross

**What happens:** The priest begins: "In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." Everyone makes the Sign of the Cross and answers: **"Amen."**

**Why:** It's the first prayer you ever learned, and it says everything. One God, three Persons. Saved by the Cross. You trace it on your own body: *I belong to him, head to heart, shoulder to shoulder.*

## "The Lord be with you"

**What happens:** The priest says, "The Lord be with you." You answer: **"And with your spirit."**

**Why:** This is more than "good morning." It is a blessing straight out of the Bible, and it means: *God is here, with us, right now.* "And with your spirit" honors the priest's ordination, the gift of the Holy Spirit he received to stand at this altar.

## "I confess": starting with honesty

**What happens:** Right at the beginning, before anything else, we stop and say together:

*"I confess to almighty God and to you, my brothers and sisters, that I have greatly sinned…"*

**Who goes first? Everyone.** The priest says it too. Nobody in that church is pretending to be perfect.

**Who do you say it to? God, and the people around you.** Your sins don't stay private. They touch your family, your friends, your neighbors. So we say sorry to all of them at once.

**Then we strike our chest:** "through my fault, through my fault, through my most grievous fault." It's an old gesture that means: *the fault is mine, and no one else's.*

**And then comes the best part:** we ask Mary, the angels and the saints, and each other, to pray for us. Then the priest asks God to have mercy on us all.

> 🟢 Prayed with real sorrow, this prayer obtains God's forgiveness for everyday (venial) sins. It does not replace Confession, where serious (mortal) sins are forgiven (Catechism 1436, 1458, 1446; General Instruction of the Roman Missal 51).

If you've been carrying something for years, this is where you start putting it down.

## "Lord, have mercy"

**What happens:** We pray three times: "Lord, have mercy. Christ, have mercy. Lord, have mercy." Sometimes it's sung in Greek: *Kyrie, eleison.*

**Why:** It's the oldest prayer of the poor. The blind man on the road cried it. The tax collector at the back of the temple prayed it. It is not groveling. It is the honest cry of someone who knows God is good and wants to come close.

## The Gloria: the angels' song

**What happens:** On Sundays (except in Advent and Lent), everyone sings or says: *"Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to people of good will…"*

**Why:** These are the words the angels sang over the fields of Bethlehem on the night Jesus was born. You just admitted your sins and asked for mercy. Now the Church bursts into joy, because mercy has been given. First confess, then rejoice: that is the Christian life in two minutes.

## The opening prayer

**What happens:** The priest says, "Let us pray." There's a short silence. Then he prays one prayer out loud, with his arms open, and everyone answers **"Amen."**

**Why:** That silence is for you. Bring whatever you walked in with: worries, a sick parent, a broken marriage, a job you lost. Lay it down there. Then the priest gathers everyone's silent prayers into one and offers them to the Father. Your "Amen" means: *Yes. That's my prayer too.*
