# The Mass, Return Home — 3 — The Eucharist

*Now the Mass moves from the ambo to the altar. Everything so far has been getting us ready for this.*

## The gifts are brought up

**What happens:** Everyone sits. People from the parish carry bread and wine up the aisle to the priest. The collection is taken. The priest prepares the altar, pours a drop of water into the wine, and washes his hands.

**Why:** The bread and wine stand for us: our work, our week, our whole life. Put yours on that plate in your mind: your job, your family, your worries, even your failures. Everything that goes up to the altar will be offered to God. That drop of water mixed into the wine? It's us, joined to Christ.

## "Pray, brethren"

**What happens:** The priest asks everyone to pray that "my sacrifice and yours" be acceptable to God. Everyone stands and answers: **"May the Lord accept the sacrifice at your hands, for the praise and glory of his name, for our good and the good of all his holy Church."**

**Why:** Did you catch that? *My sacrifice and yours.* You're not watching. You're offering your life along with Christ's.

## "Lift up your hearts"

**What happens:** The priest says, "Lift up your hearts." You answer: **"We lift them up to the Lord."** Then he gives thanks to God in a prayer called the Preface.

**Why:** Leave the parking lot, the to-do list, the argument in the car. For the next few minutes, your heart belongs up there.

## "With all the angels and saints": who else is here?

**What happens:** At the end of that prayer, the priest says we now join "the Angels and Archangels" and "all the hosts and Powers of heaven" to sing God's praise. Then everyone sings: **"Holy, Holy, Holy Lord God of hosts. Heaven and earth are full of your glory…"**

**Why:** These aren't our words. They're the song the angels sing in heaven. The prophet Isaiah heard it in a vision (Isaiah 6:3), and so did St. John in the Book of Revelation (Revelation 4:8). At this moment, heaven and earth sing the same song together.

Look around the church. You see the people in the pews, but they're the smallest part of who's there. Around the altar are the angels, the saints, Our Lady, and the people we love who have died in Christ.

> ✅ Church teaching: the liturgy on earth shares in the liturgy of heaven (Catechism 1090; *Sacrosanctum Concilium* 8).

So when you sing "Holy, Holy, Holy," **you're singing with all of heaven.** Then we kneel.

## The Eucharistic Prayer

**What happens:** Everyone kneels. The priest prays the great prayer of the Mass. He holds his hands over the bread and wine and asks the Holy Spirit to come down on them.

**Why:** We kneel because we are about to be in the presence of God himself. This is the heart of the Mass. Stay with it, even if your mind wanders. Gently bring it back.

## The Consecration: "This is my Body"

**What happens:** The priest takes the bread and says the words Jesus spoke at the Last Supper, the night before he died: *"Take this, all of you, and eat of it, for this is my Body, which will be given up for you."* Then he does the same with the wine: *"…this is the chalice of my Blood…"*

After each, he lifts it up for everyone to see. Bells may ring.

**Why:** At that moment, the bread and wine become Jesus himself. Not a symbol. Not a reminder. **Him.**

The priest isn't speaking in his own name. He's acting *in the person of Christ*: Jesus is the one saying "This is my Body," through the priest's voice.

When the Host is lifted up, look at it. Many Catholics silently pray the words of the Apostle Thomas: ***"My Lord and my God."***

> ✅ Defined teaching: Council of Trent (1551); Catechism 1374–1376.

## Transubstantiation: what is it?

It's a big word for something simple and amazing. Here's how it works.

**Every thing has two sides: what it looks like, and what it really is.**

**What stays the same: the appearances.** After the Consecration, it still looks like bread. It still tastes like bread, feels like bread, weighs the same as bread. The wine still looks, smells and tastes like wine. Your senses aren't wrong: those appearances really remain.

**What changes: what it really is.** Underneath those appearances, it isn't bread or wine anymore. The whole reality of the bread becomes the Body of Christ. The whole reality of the wine becomes his Blood. And because Jesus is alive, he is there whole and entire: **his Body, Blood, soul and divinity.**

That's what the word means: ***trans*** (a change) of the ***substance*** (what a thing truly is).

**Two things it is NOT:**

- **Not a symbol.** A photo of your mother is a symbol. This is not a picture of Jesus. It is Jesus.
- **Not a chemical change.** You couldn't find it under a microscope. The change happens at a deeper level than anything a lab can measure.

**How can that be?** Because of who said it. Jesus said, "This is my Body." He calmed storms with a word and raised the dead with a word. If he says it, it is.

> ✅ Defined teaching: Council of Trent, Session XIII (1551); Catechism 1374–1377. "Substance" and "appearances" (also called "accidents" or "species") are the Church's own terms.

## "The mystery of faith"

**What happens:** The priest says, "The mystery of faith." Everyone answers, for example: **"We proclaim your Death, O Lord, and profess your Resurrection until you come again."**

**Why:** Jesus is on the altar. Everything we believe is right here: his death, his rising, his coming again. We say it out loud, to him.

## The Great Amen

**What happens:** The priest lifts up the Host and the chalice together and sings: "Through him, and with him, and in him…" Everyone answers: **"Amen!"** (often sung, often more than once).

**Why:** This is the most important Amen of the whole Mass. It means: *Yes. All of this is true, and I'm in.* Say it like you mean it. Sing it loud.

## The Our Father: the prayer you already know by heart

**What happens:** Everyone stands and prays the Our Father together.

You learned it as a child. Maybe it's the one prayer you never forgot. Now hear it again, standing at the altar.

**Jesus gave us these words himself.** When his friends asked, "Lord, teach us to pray," this is what he taught them. So here you're not inventing a prayer: you're praying with Jesus's own voice.

**And look at the first word: *Our.*** Not "my." Every person in that church is God's child, and you're one of them. You belong here.

**"Give us this day our daily bread."** In a few minutes, the bread he gives is himself.

**"Forgive us, as we forgive."** Before we come to his table, we let go of our grudges.

If you've been away, this is the moment to come home: **same words, same Father, still waiting for you.**

## The sign of peace

**What happens:** The priest may invite everyone to offer each other a sign of peace. You shake hands with the people around you (or nod) and say: **"Peace be with you."**

**Why:** It's not "nice to meet you." It's the peace of the risen Jesus, who said to his frightened friends, "Peace be with you." Before we receive him, we make peace with each other. If there's someone you need to forgive, this is a good moment to decide to.

## "Lamb of God"

**What happens:** Everyone sings or says: **"Lamb of God, you take away the sins of the world, have mercy on us…"** Meanwhile, the priest breaks the large Host.

**Why:** John the Baptist pointed at Jesus and said, "Behold, the Lamb of God" (John 1:29). In the Old Testament, a lamb was sacrificed so God's people could be saved. Jesus is that Lamb. The Host is broken because his Body was broken for us, and because one bread is shared by many.

## "Lord, I am not worthy"

**What happens:** The priest holds up the Host and says: "Behold the Lamb of God… Blessed are those called to the supper of the Lamb." Everyone kneels and answers:

***"Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word and my soul shall be healed."***

**Why:** These are almost the exact words of a Roman soldier (Matthew 8:8). His servant was dying. He asked Jesus to heal him, but felt too unworthy to have Jesus come into his house. Jesus was amazed by his faith, and healed the servant with a word.

That's us. None of us is worthy. That's not the point. **He comes anyway, and he heals.**

## Holy Communion

**What happens:** People go forward in a line. The priest or minister holds up the Host and says: **"The Body of Christ."** You answer: **"Amen."** Then you receive, either on the tongue or in your hands (left hand over right, then you place the Host in your mouth with your right hand, right there).

**Why:** This is it: the reason for everything. Jesus gives himself to you, not just near you but *into* you. The God who made the stars comes to live in you. Your "Amen" is your yes: *I believe this is you, Lord.*

**Who can receive?** Catholics who believe it is truly Jesus, who are not aware of any serious (mortal) sin, and who have fasted for one hour (water and medicine are fine).

> ✅ Church law and teaching: Catechism 1385, 1415; Code of Canon Law 916, 919.

**If you've been away a long time:** that's okay. You're not the first, and you won't be the last. The way back is Confession. It's quick, it's private, and every priest has heard it all before. Until then, you can still go up in line with your arms crossed over your chest, and the priest will give you a blessing. Or stay in the pew and make a **spiritual communion**: *"Jesus, I can't receive you today, but come into my heart anyway."* He hears that prayer.

> 🟡 The blessing with arms crossed is a common custom in many parishes, not universal law. If unsure, a spiritual communion from the pew is always fine.

## The silence after Communion

**What happens:** Back in your pew, you kneel or sit in silence.

**Why:** For a few minutes, Jesus is physically present inside you. The saints called these the most precious minutes of the week. Don't waste them looking at your phone or the exit. Talk to him. Thank him. Tell him what's going on. Or just stay quiet with him.
