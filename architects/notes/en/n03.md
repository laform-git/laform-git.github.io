# Architects of the Culture of Death — Note 3: The Eugenic Evolutionists

## Core Idea
Human beings are treated as biological products of natural selection, and society is invited to take over the job of selection. The key distinction for this note: **Darwin's biology is science; the social program built on it is ideology.** The Church itself makes this distinction (see below), and it is what makes the critique credible to a scientific reader.

---
## The Architects
**Charles Darwin (1809–1882, English)**
- *Core claim:* Species arise through natural selection (*On the Origin of Species*, 1859), and man descends from earlier forms (*The Descent of Man*, 1871). In *Descent*, he worried aloud that civilized societies, by caring for the weak, check the process of elimination.
- *Cultural footprint:* The foundation of modern biology — and, in the hands of others, the claimed scientific license for ranking human worth by fitness.

**Francis Galton (1822–1911, English)**
- *Core claim:* Talent and character are inherited, so society should breed better humans. Darwin's half-cousin; wrote *Hereditary Genius* (1869) and coined the word "eugenics" (1883).
- *Cultural footprint:* Founded the eugenics movement that spread across Britain, America, Scandinavia, and Germany in the early 20th century.

**Ernst Haeckel (1834–1919, German)**
- *Core claim:* Monism — matter is all there is, God and soul are illusions — combined with a ranking of human races from higher to lower. Founded the German Monist League (1906).
- *Cultural footprint:* Darwin's chief promoter in Germany. His famous embryo drawings were criticized as inaccurate even in his lifetime. His link to Nazi racial policy is a live historical debate (see [[Architects 09 Political Outcomes Map|Note 9]]).

## From Philosophy to Institutions
- **Education:** Eugenics was taught as settled science in American schools. The biology textbook at the center of the 1925 Scopes trial, Hunter's *A Civic Biology* (1914), included a section endorsing eugenics.
- **Medicine:** Physicians carried out forced sterilizations. The U.S. total over the 20th century is commonly estimated at more than 60,000. Nazi Germany's 1933 sterilization law, partly modeled on American statutes, sterilized roughly 400,000 people, and the Aktion T4 program (1939–1941) killed disabled patients in hospitals.
- **Law:** Virginia's 1924 sterilization statute was upheld in *Buck v. Bell* (1927), where Justice Holmes wrote the notorious line about "three generations of imbeciles." About 30 states had similar laws. Eugenic testimony also shaped the restrictive U.S. Immigration Act of 1924. Nazi defendants at Nuremberg cited the American precedent.

## The Secular Case (no faith required)
- **Genetics disproved eugenics from the inside.** The Hardy–Weinberg principle (1908), worked out by a mathematician and a physician, showed that sterilizing people who display a rare recessive trait barely changes its frequency, because most carriers look normal. Later genetics showed most traits are shaped by many genes plus environment, so breeding programs cannot deliver what eugenicists promised.
- **Race ranking fails the data.** Population genetics (Richard Lewontin, 1972, and much work since) found that most human genetic variation exists within groups, not between them.
- **The flagship case was false.** Historian Paul Lombardo's research (*Three Generations, No Imbeciles*, 2008) showed that Carrie Buck was an ordinary student, and her pregnancy resulted from rape by a relative of her foster family. The "science" in *Buck v. Bell* was fabricated to fit a conclusion.
- **Public repudiation:** Virginia's governor formally apologized for the sterilization program in 2002.

## Truth's Defenders in Their Own Era
- **G.K. Chesterton, *Eugenics and Other Evils* (1922):** Written while eugenics was fashionable among the educated elite, he attacked it as tyranny dressed as science.
- **Justice Pierce Butler (1927):** The only dissenting vote in *Buck v. Bell* — and the only Catholic on the Court.
- **Pius XI, *Casti Connubii* (1930):** Condemned state sterilization by name, teaching that the state has no direct power over the bodies of innocent citizens. Catholic members withdrew from eugenics societies as a result.
- **Pius XI, *Mit brennender Sorge* (1937):** Smuggled into Germany and read from pulpits, it condemned the Nazi idolization of race and blood.
- **Bishop Clemens von Galen of Münster (1941):** Preached publicly against the T4 killing of the disabled. The public outcry that followed helped force the official halt of the program in August 1941. Beatified 2005.

## The Church's Defense — Worth Is Not Fitness
The aspect of human dignity at stake is **equal, inherent worth regardless of health, intelligence, or usefulness**. The Church did not condemn evolutionary biology: Pius XII's *Humani Generis* (1950) left the origin of the human body open to scientific inquiry while teaching that each soul is directly created by God. What she condemned was the claim that biology decides who deserves to live and reproduce. The Catechism rejects attempts to produce human beings selected according to predetermined qualities as contrary to personal dignity (CCC 2275) and affirms the equal dignity of all persons (CCC 1934–1935).

The Church steps in here because eugenics turns the weakest members of the human family into problems to be solved. The Gospel teaches the reverse: the least of these are where Christ himself is found (Matthew 25:40).
