# Architects of the Culture of Death — Note 2: The Will Worshippers

## Core Idea
The human will replaces God, reason, and nature as the source of meaning and value. If there is no given order, the self must invent one. Every later category in this series borrows this move.

---
## The Architects
**Arthur Schopenhauer (1788–1860, German)**
- *Core claim:* Reality at bottom is a blind, striving "will" with no purpose; life is suffering, and the best response is to deny the will (*The World as Will and Representation*, 1818).
- *Cultural footprint:* Introduced atheistic pessimism into mainstream European thought and directly formed the young Nietzsche, Wagner, and later Freud's idea of blind drives.

**Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900, German)**
- *Core claim:* "God is dead" (*The Gay Science*, 1882); Christian morality is a "slave morality" of the weak, and the strong must create their own values through the will to power.
- *Cultural footprint:* The philosophical hinge of 20th-century secularism. Read by Bolsheviks and appropriated by the Nazis (see [[Architects 09 Political Outcomes Map|Note 9]]), and the main ancestor of postmodern thought (Foucault's analysis of all truth as power).

**Ayn Rand (1905–1982, Russian-born American)**
- *Core claim:* Rational self-interest is the highest virtue; altruism and self-sacrifice are immoral (*The Fountainhead*, 1943; *Atlas Shrugged*, 1957; *The Virtue of Selfishness*, 1964). She was an outspoken atheist and a firm supporter of legal abortion.
- *Cultural footprint:* Shaped American libertarianism and entrepreneurial culture. Her books still sell widely, especially among young adults.

---
## From Philosophy to Institutions
- **Education:** Nietzsche's claim that truth is a disguise for power passed through Foucault and Derrida into university humanities departments, where it underlies much of today's critical theory.
- **Medicine:** Modern bioethics made *autonomy* its dominant principle. Where the patient's will once had to answer to the good of the patient, the will increasingly becomes the good.
- **Law:** The clearest legal statement of will worship is the U.S. Supreme Court's 1992 *Planned Parenthood v. Casey* decision, which described liberty as the right to define one's own concept of existence and meaning. That is Nietzsche's program written into constitutional law.

---
## The Secular Case (no faith required)
- **The logical problem:** If values are only what a will creates, there is no standard by which to condemn a stronger will that creates cruel values. Will worship cannot, on its own terms, call the Nazis wrong — only different.
- **The data on self-focus:** The Harvard Study of Adult Development, running since 1938, found that the quality of close relationships is the strongest predictor of health and happiness in old age — not achievement or self-assertion. Rand's anthropology fails the longest-running study of human flourishing.
- **Rising narcissism:** Psychologist Jean Twenge's research documented measurable increases in narcissistic traits among American young adults from the 1980s onward, tracking the cultural shift toward self-definition, alongside rising anxiety and depression.
- **Nietzsche's own politics:** Both the Bolsheviks and the Nazis found usable material in him. A philosophy that can be picked up with equal ease by two totalitarian systems has no internal brake.

---
## Truth's Defenders in Their Own Era
- **G.K. Chesterton, *Orthodoxy* (1908):** Argued that worshipping the will leads to paralysis, because a will that refuses every limit can choose nothing. Written while Nietzsche's influence was peaking in England.
- **C.S. Lewis, *The Abolition of Man* (1943):** Warned that once objective moral order is denied, a small group of "conditioners" will impose their own will on everyone else. Written during the war against a regime that had claimed Nietzsche.
- **Whittaker Chambers, review of *Atlas Shrugged* in *National Review* (1957):** A conservative ex-communist who saw in Rand's system the same coldness toward the weak he had seen in Marxism.

---
## The Church's Defense — Freedom Ordered to Truth
The aspect of human dignity at stake here is **freedom itself**. The Church does not oppose freedom; she denies that freedom creates truth. John Paul II's *Veritatis Splendor* (1993) teaches that freedom depends on truth and is fulfilled by choosing the good, drawing on Christ's words that the truth will make us free (John 8:32). The Catechism states that the more one does what is good, the freer one becomes (CCC 1733). *Fides et Ratio* (1998) named nihilism as the end point of philosophies that deny any truth beyond the self.

The Church steps in because will worship destroys the very person it claims to exalt: a will with nothing above it becomes a tyrant over others and a slave to its own passions. Real dignity is receiving one's nature and growing into it, not inventing it.
