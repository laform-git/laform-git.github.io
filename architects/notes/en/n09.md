# Architects of the Culture of Death — Note 9: Political Outcomes Map

## Purpose
Maps each thinker to concrete political events and marks the outcome as **Success** (achieved its aims and endured), **Collapse** (failed or was repudiated), or **Contested** (mixed or still disputed). Claims historians still debate are marked as such.

---
## Marx — The Largest Experiment
| Where | What happened | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Russia, 1917 | Bolshevik revolution; USSR founded 1922; famine of 1932–33; Gulag system | **Collapse** — USSR dissolved 1991 after economic stagnation and the arms race |
| Eastern Europe, 1945–1989 | Soviet-imposed communist states | **Collapse** — regimes fell in 1989 |
| China, 1949 | Mao's revolution; Great Leap Forward famine (tens of millions dead, est. 15–45 million); Cultural Revolution | **Contested** — became an economic power only after Deng Xiaoping's market reforms (1978); still a one-party state |
| Vietnam, 1975 | Unified under communist rule | **Contested** — steady growth only after market reforms (Đổi Mới, 1986) |
| Cuba, 1959; North Korea, 1948 | Communist dictatorships | **Collapse in all but name** — isolated and impoverished |
| Soviet abortion law, 1920 | First country to legalize abortion, rooted in Engels' theory of the family | **Contested** — Stalin re-banned it 1936; re-legalized 1955 |

**Pattern:** Every Marxist economy that succeeded did so by abandoning Marx's economics while keeping the party's power.

## Nietzsche — The Strangest Case
- **Bolshevik readers:** Some early Bolshevik intellectuals, including Alexander Bogdanov, blended Nietzsche's will to power with Marxism.
- **Mao:** Encountered Nietzsche before Marx, through late Qing and early Republican reformers who had absorbed him in Japan and Germany. *(Single detailed source: Palladium Magazine — credible but not widely repeated.)*
- **Nazis:** His sister Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche, a Nazi sympathizer, compiled his unpublished notes into *The Will to Power*, and the regime celebrated him. **Contested:** scholars still debate how much distortion was hers and how much the Nazis added on their own.
- **Outcome:** **Contested** — claimed by both totalitarian movements of the century.

---
## Haeckel — Racial Biology
- His monism and ranking of races gave German eugenics a scientific veneer.
- **Live historical debate:** Historian Daniel Gasman argued Haeckel's ideas directly fed Nazi racial ideology. Historian Robert Richards argues the link is overstated and that the Nazis themselves rejected Haeckel's monism. Write this as an open question, not a settled fact.
- **Outcome:** **Collapse** — racial biology was discredited after 1945.

---
## Galton and the American Eugenicists
- Virginia 1924 → *Buck v. Bell* (1927) → about 30 states with sterilization laws → Germany's 1933 sterilization law, partly modeled on U.S. statutes, cited by Nazi defendants at Nuremberg.
- **Outcome:** **Collapse** — repudiated; Virginia apologized in 2002. *Buck v. Bell* has never been formally overruled.

---
## Sanger, Kinsey, and the Sexual Revolution
- The Pill (1960) → *Griswold* (1965) → *Roe* (1973) → sexual revolution reshaping family life and law across the West.
- *Roe* overturned by *Dobbs* (2022), returning abortion to the states.
- **Outcome:** **Success** in transforming the culture; **Contested** in law and in its social results (see Note 7).

---
## Sartre and Beauvoir
- Signed the Manifesto of the 121 (1960) backing Algerian independence; Sartre prefaced Frantz Fanon's *The Wretched of the Earth* (1961), giving anti-colonial violence a Western platform. Algeria became independent in 1962 under a one-party state. **Contested.**
- Beauvoir's Manifesto of the 343 (1971) → France's Veil Law (1975). **Success** in its aims. France wrote abortion into its constitution in 2024.

---
## Comte
- Brazil's republic (1889) adopted positivist ideas and Comte's motto "Order and Progress" for its flag. Mexico's liberal and Porfirian elites used positivism to push the Church out of public life (see Note 10). **Contested.**

---
## Humphry, Kevorkian, Singer
- Oregon (1997) → spread to other U.S. states, Canada (2016), and parts of Europe; Groningen Protocol (2004) for newborns. **Success** in legal terms and still expanding; **Contested** on ethical and medical grounds (see Note 8).

---
## Summary Pattern
The economic utopias (Marx, Comte) mostly **collapsed** on contact with reality. The racial and eugenic programs **collapsed** in disgrace after the Holocaust. The ideas about sex, the family, and death **succeeded** — they won quietly, through courts, medicine, and schools, rather than through revolution. That is why the book's warning is most urgent where the victories are most recent.
