# Architects of the Culture of Death — Note 8: The Death Peddlers

## Core Idea
Death becomes a personal choice, a medical service, and a calculation about which lives are worth living. This is where all six earlier categories converge: the sovereign will (Note 2), the ranking of lives by fitness (Note 3), and the person as a means (Note 4).

---
## The Architects
**Derek Humphry (1930–2025, English)**
- *Core claim:* Rational people have a right to end their lives with help. After assisting his terminally ill wife's death in 1975, he wrote *Jean's Way* (1978), founded the Hemlock Society (1980), and published the suicide manual *Final Exit* (1991), a bestseller.
- *Cultural footprint:* Co-authored the first U.S. model assisted-dying law (1986), the template for Oregon's 1997 Death with Dignity Act.

**Jack Kevorkian (1928–2011, American)**
- *Core claim:* Physicians should help anyone who asks to die. Built a "suicide machine" and took part in about 130 deaths (1990–1998).
- *Cultural footprint:* Made assisted death a national television issue. After broadcasting a lethal injection on *60 Minutes* (1998), he was convicted of second-degree murder in 1999 and served eight years.

**Peter Singer (b. 1946, Australian)**
- *Core claim:* Moral worth depends on capacities such as self-awareness, not on being human; some disabled newborns may be killed, and some animals have greater moral status than some humans (*Practical Ethics*, 1979; *Should the Baby Live?*, 1985).
- *Cultural footprint:* Appointed to a Princeton bioethics chair in 1999 over protests from disability groups; the most influential living utilitarian philosopher.

## From Philosophy to Institutions
- **Law:** Oregon (1997) was followed by 12 more states and D.C. — **13 states plus D.C. as of September 2026** (Montana by court ruling; New York's law took effect 5 August 2026 and Illinois's 12 September 2026). The Netherlands and Belgium legalized euthanasia in 2002; Belgium extended it to children in 2014. Canada legalized "medical assistance in dying" (MAID) in 2016 and expanded it in 2021 to people who are not terminally ill.
- **Medicine:** Death reclassified as a medical treatment. The Dutch Groningen Protocol (2004) set criteria for ending the lives of severely ill newborns — the policy Singer's arguments had defended in principle. Health-economics tools that weigh the value of a life-year shape rationing decisions.
- **Education:** University bioethics programs, in which Singer's framework is standard, now train the people who write hospital policy.

## The Secular Case (no faith required)
- **It is not mainly about pain.** Oregon's own annual reports consistently show that the most common reasons patients give are loss of autonomy, inability to enjoy activities, and loss of dignity. Fear of being a burden on family is frequently cited. Uncontrolled pain ranks much lower.
- **Kevorkian's patients were often not dying.** A 2000 study in the *New England Journal of Medicine* of his cases in Oakland County, Michigan, found most were not terminally ill, and several showed no anatomical evidence of disease at autopsy.
- **The limits keep moving.** Canada went from terminal illness only (2016) to non-terminal conditions (2021) within five years, and MAID has since become a significant share of all Canadian deaths. The Netherlands and Belgium now permit euthanasia for psychiatric conditions.
- **Disability advocates object on secular grounds.** The group Not Dead Yet (founded 1996) and lawyer Harriet McBryde Johnson, who debated Singer in person (*New York Times Magazine*, 2003), argue that these laws tell disabled people their lives are less worth living.
- **History already ran this experiment.** Psychiatrist Leo Alexander, a medical adviser at the Nuremberg trials, wrote in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (1949) that Nazi medical killing began with a small shift in attitude: the idea that some lives are not worth living.

## Truth's Defenders in Their Own Era
- **Bishop von Galen (1941):** Publicly denounced the Nazi killing of disabled patients and helped halt the T4 program.
- **Dame Cicely Saunders:** A Christian physician who founded the modern hospice movement with St Christopher's Hospice, London (1967) — the positive answer: kill the pain, not the patient.
- **CDF, *Declaration on Euthanasia* (1980):** Issued the same year Humphry founded the Hemlock Society.
- **Elizabeth Anscombe, "Modern Moral Philosophy" (1958):** The Catholic philosopher who coined the term "consequentialism" and argued that it can justify any act, including killing the innocent — the logic Singer later made systematic.

## The Church's Defense — Life Does Not Depend on Quality or Usefulness
The aspect of human dignity at stake is **the value of every life from conception to natural death, regardless of capacity, suffering, or cost**. John Paul II's *Evangelium Vitae* (1995, §64–67) condemned euthanasia as a grave violation of God's law, written as Kevorkian and Oregon's campaign were at their peak. The Catechism forbids euthanasia while permitting patients to refuse burdensome, disproportionate treatment and encouraging palliative care (CCC 2276–2279). *Samaritanus Bonus* (2020) called for accompanying the dying instead of abandoning them to death.

The Church steps in because the suffering person is not a problem to be removed but a neighbor to be accompanied. A society that offers death as care has decided that some of its members are finished.
