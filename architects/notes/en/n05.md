# Architects of the Culture of Death — Note 5: The Atheistic Existentialists

## Core Idea
There is no human nature — only freedom. Man exists first and defines himself afterward. This is the direct philosophical root of today's claim that identity, including sex and motherhood, is chosen rather than given (see [[Architects 11 Gender Ideology|Note 11]]).

---
## The Architects
**Jean-Paul Sartre (1905–1980, French)**
- *Core claim:* "Existence precedes essence" (*Existentialism Is a Humanism*, 1945). Since there is no God to design man, there is no human nature; each person is "condemned to be free" and creates his own values (*Being and Nothingness*, 1943).
- *Cultural footprint:* The public face of postwar atheism. Also a longtime apologist for the Soviet Union and later for Maoism.

**Simone de Beauvoir (1908–1986, French)**
- *Core claim:* "One is not born, but becomes, a woman" (*The Second Sex*, 1949). Womanhood is a social construction; motherhood and marriage are forms of confinement.
- *Cultural footprint:* Founding text of second-wave feminism and the seed of the later sex/gender split. Wrote the 1971 Manifesto of the 343 that led to France's 1975 abortion law.

**Elisabeth Badinter (b. 1944, French)**
- *Core claim:* Maternal love is not a natural instinct but a historical and social product (*Mother Love: Myth and Reality*, 1980). Later criticized the return of "natural" motherhood and breastfeeding as a threat to women's freedom (*The Conflict*, 2010).
- *Cultural footprint:* A leading French public intellectual carrying Beauvoir's line into the present.

---
## From Philosophy to Institutions
- **Education:** *The Second Sex* became the foundational text of women's studies, later gender studies, departments. The idea that human nature is a construct now shapes much of the humanities and social sciences.
- **Medicine:** Abortion reclassified as a component of women's health care rather than an exception to it.
- **Law:** Beauvoir's Manifesto of the 343 (1971) created the public pressure behind France's Veil Law (1975) legalizing abortion. Sartre and Beauvoir also signed the 1960 Manifesto of the 121 supporting Algerian independence, and Sartre prefaced Frantz Fanon's *The Wretched of the Earth* (1961) (see [[Architects 09 Political Outcomes Map|Note 9]]).

---
## The Secular Case (no faith required)
- **Human nature is measurable.** Twin and adoption studies consistently find that a large share of the variation in personality and many behaviors is heritable. Harvard psychologist Steven Pinker's *The Blank Slate* (2002), written from a fully secular standpoint, argues that denying human nature is one of the great errors of modern intellectual life.
- **Human universals exist.** Anthropologist Donald Brown documented hundreds of traits found in every known culture (*Human Universals*, 1991), including mother–infant bonding and distinctions between the sexes.
- **Maternal bonding has biology.** Research on oxytocin and maternal brain changes during pregnancy and nursing shows that attachment between mother and child has a physiological basis, directly contrary to Badinter's thesis.
- **Sartre's political judgment failed.** Albert Camus, himself an atheist, broke with Sartre in 1952 over Sartre's refusal to face the truth about Soviet labor camps. A philosophy that denies fixed truth struggled to recognize the Gulag.

---
## Truth's Defenders in Their Own Era
- **Gabriel Marcel:** A Catholic existentialist and Sartre's contemporary who argued that the person is fulfilled through presence, fidelity, and hope — receiving being as a gift rather than inventing it.
- **Pius XII, *Humani Generis* (1950):** Named existentialism among the modern errors that deny unchangeable essences, one year after *The Second Sex*.
- **Edith Stein (St. Teresa Benedicta of the Cross):** A philosopher trained in phenomenology whose lectures on women in the 1930s defended a real feminine nature with its own gifts. Killed at Auschwitz in 1942; canonized 1998.

---
## The Church's Defense — Human Nature as Gift
The aspect of human dignity at stake is **a given human nature, including the nature of man and woman**. *Gaudium et Spes* (1965, §19–21) addressed modern atheism directly, teaching that the denial of God leaves the deepest questions of human life unanswered. John Paul II's *Mulieris Dignitatem* (1988) and *Letter to Women* (1995) answered Beauvoir by affirming woman's equal dignity together with the unique gift of motherhood, spiritual and physical. The Catechism teaches that each person should acknowledge and accept his or her sexual identity (CCC 2333).

The Church steps in because a person told he has no nature is left with nothing to become. Freedom is real, but it is the freedom to grow into what one is, not to erase it.
