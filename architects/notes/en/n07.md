# Architects of the Culture of Death — Note 7: The Sex Planners

## Core Idea
Sexuality and reproduction are detached from marriage and then managed by experts through research, contraception, sterilization, and abortion. The most institutionally successful of the seven categories.

---
## The Architects
**Margaret Mead (1901–1978, American)**
- *Core claim:* Samoan adolescents enjoyed guilt-free premarital sex without the turmoil of Western youth, proving sexual morality is merely cultural (*Coming of Age in Samoa*, 1928).
- *Cultural footprint:* The most widely read anthropology book of the century; gave "scientific" cover to sexual permissiveness.

**Alfred Kinsey (1894–1956, American)**
- *Core claim:* Americans' actual sexual behavior was far more varied than public morality admitted, so the morality should change (*Sexual Behavior in the Human Male*, 1948; *Female*, 1953).
- *Cultural footprint:* The statistical foundation of the sexual revolution and modern sex education. His institute still operates at Indiana University.

**Margaret Sanger (1879–1966, American)**
- *Core claim:* Women must control fertility, and society should reduce births among the "unfit." Coined "birth control" (1914), opened America's first birth-control clinic (1916), and founded the organization that became Planned Parenthood. An open supporter of eugenics (*The Pivot of Civilization*, 1922).
- *Cultural footprint:* Planned Parenthood is today the largest abortion provider in the United States.

**Clarence Gamble (1894–1966, American)**
- *Core claim:* Birth control and sterilization should be spread among the poor and "unfit." A Procter & Gamble heir who funded contraception and sterilization programs, including in North Carolina, and founded the Pathfinder Fund (1957) for overseas programs.
- *Cultural footprint:* The model of philanthropy-funded population control later scaled up in international aid.

**Alan Guttmacher (1898–1974, American)**
- *Core claim:* Abortion and contraception are standard medicine and should be legal and widely available. President of Planned Parenthood (1962–1974).
- *Cultural footprint:* The Guttmacher Institute, named after him, is the main research source cited in abortion policy debates worldwide.

## From Philosophy to Institutions
- **Education:** SIECUS (Sex Information and Education Council of the U.S.) was founded in 1964 by Mary Calderone, a former Planned Parenthood medical director, building on Kinsey's framework. It shaped modern sex-education curricula. *(Claims of deeper Kinsey–SIECUS ties come mainly from critics and are contested.)*
- **Medicine:** FDA approval of the Pill (1960); abortion incorporated into obstetrics; federal family-planning funding under Title X (1970).
- **Law:** *Griswold v. Connecticut* (1965) created a constitutional right to privacy for contraception, which led to *Eisenstadt* (1972) and *Roe v. Wade* (1973). *Roe* was overturned by *Dobbs* (2022), returning abortion law to the states. Kinsey's data was also cited in mid-century sex-offense law reform debates *(moderate confidence on specific statutes)*.

## The Secular Case (no faith required)
- **Mead's Samoa was wrong.** Anthropologist Derek Freeman showed that Samoan culture prized female virginity and had high rates of adolescent conflict (*Margaret Mead and Samoa*, 1983). One of Mead's original informants later said she and a friend had been joking with her.
- **Kinsey's numbers were unreliable.** A 1954 review commissioned for the American Statistical Association found his samples were not random and over-represented prisoners and sex offenders. Some of his child-sexuality data came from the records of adult abusers, as reported by the Associated Press in 1995.
- **Sanger's own heirs disowned her eugenics.** Planned Parenthood of Greater New York removed her name from its Manhattan clinic in 2020 because of her eugenic views.
- **The social outcomes are documented.** U.S. births outside marriage rose from about 5% in 1960 to about 40% by the 2010s. Nobel economist George Akerlof, writing from a secular standpoint, argued (1996) that the Pill and legal abortion undermined the social norms that once protected women and children, contributing to the rise in single-parent families. Sociologists Sara McLanahan and Gary Sandefur (*Growing Up with a Single Parent*, 1994) found children raised outside two-parent families face worse outcomes on average.
- **Population control became coercion.** China's one-child policy (1980–2015) and India's forced sterilization campaign during the 1975–1977 Emergency show where state-managed reproduction leads.

## Truth's Defenders in Their Own Era
- **Pius XI, *Casti Connubii* (1930):** Issued months after the Anglican Lambeth Conference became the first major Christian body to accept contraception. It reaffirmed the link between marital love and new life.
- **Paul VI, *Humanae Vitae* (1968):** Written at the height of the sexual revolution. It predicted (§17) that widespread contraception would bring more infidelity, lower respect for women, and governments imposing birth control — all later observable.
- **Jérôme Lejeune:** French geneticist who discovered the cause of Down syndrome (1959) and then spent his career opposing the use of his discovery to abort children with the condition. Declared Venerable 2021.

---
## The Church's Defense — The Child as Gift, the Union of Love and Life
The aspect of human dignity at stake is **the child as a gift to be received, not a product to be planned or prevented**, and the dignity of women and marriage. *Humanae Vitae* teaches that the unitive and procreative meanings of the marital act are inseparable. *Familiaris Consortio* (1981) defended the family against population-control programs. The Pontifical Council for the Family's *The Truth and Meaning of Human Sexuality* (1995) taught that parents, not the state or outside organizations, are the primary educators of their children in sexuality.

The Church steps in because when sex is separated from children, children become optional, then inconvenient, then disposable.
