# How to Plan Your Life — S6 — A Higher Vision

**Source:** Jordan Peterson, *How to Plan Your Life*, Session 6: "A Higher Vision" (six-hour course) · [Peterson Academy](https://petersonacademy.com/courses/how-to-plan-your-life)

## Pocket Version

If you do not know what you want, start with what every person needs: a partner, family, friends, meaningful work, good use of free time, care of body and mind, a plan against your own temptation, and service to the community. Picture each one at its best five years out, then build a daily schedule that works as an invitation, not a prison. Faith is putting yourself on the line, staking your life on the belief that facing chaos truthfully produces good order. Every person who refuses to lie and does his part tilts the world toward heaven.

## The Insight

If you don't know what you want, start with what every person needs:

- an intimate relationship
- family
- friends
- meaningful work
- good use of time outside work
- care of body and mind
- a plan against your particular temptation
- service to your community

Picture each one five years from now at its best, then make it concrete in a schedule designed as an invitation, not a prison. Underneath it all: face chaos truthfully, aiming high, and the order you create will be good. You either stake your life on that or on its opposite; there is no third option. Each person who lives well tilts the world toward heaven.

## Why It Matters

It turns the whole course into a practical checklist. It separates being depressed from simply not having a life. It defines faith as the willingness to put yourself on the line, not belief in childish things. And it gives each person a responsibility (to speak up, to serve, not to lie) that keeps a free society from rotting.

## Key Points

### 1. Depressed, or no life?

In clinical interviews, Peterson separated two cases. Some people are physically depressed: good feelings shut down, bad feelings turned up. Antidepressants may help if they respond. Others simply "don't have a life," which is a more serious problem.

His checklist: Do you have an intimate relationship? Family, and do you get along? Friends? A job? Education? Good use of time outside work? Habits that could drag you down? You may not need all of these, "but the probability that you need none of them is zero." The cynic who says none of it is for him is claiming to be different from every human being who ever lived.

> 🟢 Isolation and lack of meaningful roles are strong risk factors for low mood. 🔴 The "depressed vs. no life" split is a clinical rule of thumb.

### 2. Relationship: ask the right question

Five years from now, what relationship would you want? Drop the fantasies; seven movie stars in a trailer would be hell. Peterson once saw Hugh Hefner, at 75, touring Europe with young women and repeating the same empty conversations: "Hugh Hefner hell. Be careful what you hope for."

On tour he was asked again and again, "How do I find the person who's right for me?" That's the wrong question. It imagines someone hiding under a rock whose whole aim is to suit you exactly as you are now. The right question: "How can I be the person that would attract the person that I would want to be with?" Doing that doesn't guarantee Prince Charming; not doing it nearly guarantees he won't come. To men who blame women: if all women reject you, either it's all women or it's you. Assume it's you, because then you can do something about it. A man who has his life together at 27, and lets people know, has a good chance of finding a partner.

Be detailed: romance, children (how many, and when), what kind of parent your partner would be. Temperament matters; a feisty, disagreeable person may want someone who pushes back. Men who split women into the "pure mother" and the "seductress" make their wife untouchable and then stray. Be honest about what you want, even if you're ashamed of it, and bring it into the marriage wisely; she may have her own hidden desires to share. That might make you less drawn to pornography. Honest desire stays healthier than buried desire.

> 🔴 Advice. 🟢 Freud's "madonna–whore complex" is a recognized concept. Catholic note: the Church teaches that sexual union belongs within marriage, ordered to unity and children (CCC 2360–2363); Peterson's point about honesty is best read within those bounds.

### 3. Family: who else will want you so much?

"I don't want kids." Then what will you do from 50 to 90, possibly alone? Plans to stay childless often assume you'll be 25 for 75 years. Backpacking for six months is fine at 25, not at 75 and alone. Maybe you don't want to be a parent, but would you want to be a grandparent?

Your child is "the only person in your whole life who wants nothing more, from the time they're born, than to have the best possible relationship they could have with you." Four of them, thrilled when you walk in, keep loneliness and gloom away. Trading that for the freedom to chase pleasure is not treating yourself well.

> 🟡 Research on parenthood and wellbeing is mixed, but family ties show strong benefits in old age.

### 4. Friends: the good-news test

A real friend is happy when you share good news, and doesn't top it with their own or put you down. A real friend hears your bad news without competing with their own misery and without secretly enjoying your fall. The same goes for family. Surround yourself with people who are aiming high.

Agreeable people must learn this: you don't have to sacrifice yourself to rescue people who don't want rescuing. Enabling can be crueler than walking away: "I'll just go do my thing until you're a little more clear-headed." Help those who will accept help. "Do not throw your pearls before swine" (Mt 7:6). The psychologist Carl Rogers held that the client must decide he would rather be better. Peterson's one court-ordered client, a grandfather who had abused two grandchildren under five, came only to act out the lie; seeing himself truly might have destroyed him. If you've said it three times and they don't listen, look elsewhere.

> ✅ Mt 7:6. 🟢 Shelly Gable's research shows that how people respond to your good news predicts the quality of the relationship. 🟡 Client motivation is central to Rogers's work but is a widely shared principle, not his alone.

### 5. Work: there are no trivial jobs

From 14 to about 25, Peterson held some 30 jobs, including busboy and dishwasher. No job is simple: getting along with the servers, learning to cook from the cooks, running a kitchen. Scotty Kyle, a former alcoholic missing teeth from fights, kept the restaurant lively with jokes and treated 14-year-old Peterson like an adult. The best waiter in a small restaurant makes the place thrive and learns people and communication.

Educated people who look down on tradesmen are foolish. A bad plumber is a disaster. A good, honest, punctual plumber who keeps his books, treats customers well and charges fairly, or even a premium, will be swamped with work. "Try it for a year"; thousands have told him it changed everything. Excellent work in an entry-level job doesn't stay entry-level. Dead-end jobs exist under bosses who don't reward good work; then leave. He praises America as the country that most admires success, especially among working people.

> 🔴 From experience. 🟡 His claim that Canadians earn about 60% of what Americans do points to a real gap in income per person, but the figure is closer to 70–80% depending on how it's measured.

### 6. Time outside work, health and temptation

Time outside work isn't "leisure"; it's your life. Curious, creative people may want art or ideas. Caring people may need someone to look after. Dutiful people may want politics or a community club. Plan three or four things to learn over the next five years: singing, piano, wrestling, woodturning, coding (a 10-week bootcamp), public speaking. A skilled tradesman who learns to communicate would be unstoppable.

**Body and mind.** Maybe the gym once a week is a start, but you need to know *why*, or you won't go. That's what the pictures of heaven and hell are for.

**Temptation.** Drinking even twice a week can keep you on a rebound cycle. As alcohol wears off, the brain's calming system (GABA) is thrown off balance, which raises anxiety and disturbs sleep. Peterson quit drinking at 27 for 23 years, started again around 50, and quit again a year later: "just as stupid when I was 50 as I was when I was 27." He stopped because drinking was the only time he did things he regretted, and it got in the way of hard work. "If you can't control your temptations, what you're aiming at is probably not good enough." If you slip, get back on track tomorrow.

> 🟢 Rebound anxiety and poor sleep after drinking are well documented. 🟡 His "three days hungover" after one evening overstates it for moderate drinking.

### 7. Community: step back and a tyrant steps in

Everyone carries some civic responsibility, or someone else will take it, usually people who want power. "Every bit of necessary social responsibility you leave on the table will be taken up by a king-like figure who will be a tyrant," and you become more of a servant.

It's also an opportunity. Political parties, churches and service clubs are starving for reliable people. Two young women he met in Belgium and the Netherlands had political careers at 24 because they showed up and did the entry-level work. Capable people are often busy elsewhere, so who ends up on the school board or city council? You'll learn public speaking, sharpen your arguments and see how a city runs.

> 🔴 Interpretation, consistent with the Catholic principle of subsidiarity (CCC 1883–1885). His aside about one party's government hires is partisan opinion 🔴.

### 8. A schedule should be an invitation

Creative, less organized people think schedules kill freedom. Wrong: a schedule should be an invitation. "Sufficient unto the day" (Mt 6:34): the day is the right unit. When you wake up, you face the day's possibilities, "an unformed realm of bounded possibility." If you wake up worried, something may need doing. Peterson says Nietzsche, on waking, asked what unpleasant duty he owed that day and did it first.

Make your bed. It is the eternal story in miniature: facing chaos and rescuing something of value. Your mind, like the sun, has passed through the night and returned. The first act of the day is to put a little order where there was chaos. At his new house north of Toronto, Peterson spends 10 minutes every morning putting something in order, the garage or a room. In a year, the whole house is in order, and your mind isn't cluttered with undone tasks.

> ✅ Mt 6:34. ⚠️ The Nietzsche morning habit is unverified; no source found. 🟢 Small daily habits add up (habit research).

### 9. The Word brings order out of chaos

In Genesis, God brings the world out of chaos by his Word. St. George slaying the dragon is the image. When you explore chaos and tell the truth, you use the Word to bring order: "that's the part of you that's made in the image of God."

His second claim: if you face chaos aiming high and truthfully, the order you create will be good. "You either believe that and act it out or you don't… There's no third option." Either way, you stake your life on it.

Dostoevsky taught that you are responsible not only for what you do, but for everyone and everything. You don't know how much better the world would be if you were all you could be.

> ✅ Gen 1:1–3; John 1:1–3. ✅ Dostoevsky, *The Brothers Karamazov*: Father Zosima's brother Markel says "each of us is guilty before all for all." Catholic link: humans made in God's image (Gen 1:27; CCC 356–357).

### 10. Ten righteous people: speak when it's your turn

Abraham bargains with God over Sodom: will you spare the city for the sake of the righteous? God agrees to fewer and fewer, down to 10. Peterson: if a corrupt society still has 10 people willing to say what's true, there's hope, and you could be one of them. At work, when an injustice happens and everyone is watching: "Hey, it's your turn to say something."

Societies became Nazi Germany or the Soviet Union when ordinary people, prompted by conscience, didn't speak. Each step back makes the next more likely, until everyone lies about everything.

Theodore Dalrymple visited North Korea and described a showcase department store full of fake goods, with people paid to ride the escalators pretending to shop. He bought a pen. A crowd gathered. The box was flimsy cardboard, the pen wouldn't write, and the clip broke. A whole country of lies: "That's hell." How do you stay out of it? Don't lie.

> ✅ Genesis 18:22–33: Abraham starts at 50 (Peterson says 40) and ends at 10. ⚠️ Dalrymple's account is in *The Wilder Shores of Marx* (1991), from a visit around 1989, not "15 years ago" or under the title Peterson gives.

### 11. What would bring you joy? A great challenge

"Enthusiastic" comes from the Greek for "filled with God." What would it take for you to feel joy in a harsh world? Not comfort and plenty; you'd smash that out of spite. You need a challenge big enough to satisfy you. Your restless dissatisfaction may be a longing for something great.

God offers Abraham the chance to be the father of nations, a pattern so good it carries down the generations. A stay-at-home mother who truly raises her children may start a line of people who change the future. "You do something well… you tilt the world towards heaven." Prosperous societies exist because many people do their part. By historical standards, the plumber who fixes your pipes and doesn't rob you is "a bloody miracle."

> ✅ "Enthusiasm" comes from the Greek *entheos* ("god within"). ✅ Gen 12:2; 17:4–5.

### 12. Resist tyranny in your own life

Jung studied Nazi Germany more deeply than anyone Peterson has read, and analyzed Hitler for American intelligence during the war. Jung's view, shared by Solzhenitsyn, Viktor Frankl and Václav Havel, is that totalitarianism is a psychological catastrophe, everyone accepting the lie, and that you resist it by refusing the lie in your own life.

You'll meet tyranny and slavery in miniature, as the one doing it or the one it's done to. You know how much trouble you can cause; so how much good could you do? Maybe you're 5% in. If you threw yourself in wholeheartedly, what could you accomplish? "Jung believed… the problems of the modern world will not be solved on the political side."

> ✅ Jung worked with Allen Dulles of the OSS, the wartime forerunner of the CIA. ✅ Havel's essay "The Power of the Powerless" (the greengrocer who puts up the party sign) makes the same point.

### 13. Faith means putting yourself on the line

Faith moves mountains (Mt 17:20). Faith isn't believing childish things; it's being willing to put yourself on the line. Everyone who marries does it in faith; they don't really know who their partner is, and neither does the partner. Starting a new job or business is the same; by the odds alone, you'd never start one. You become the knight on the horse: find a dragon the right size, win, and get ready for a bigger one. Faith in yourself is faith in your ability to learn; exposure therapy simply rediscovered that people can learn.

His three-year-old daughter on a 10-foot climbing frame: first her foot halfway to the first rung, then resting on it, then her weight on it. Five rungs the first day, "climbing up Jacob's ladder, one little step at a time," playing at the edge of what she could do. Don't run out crying "be careful," secretly hoping to be the hero who rescues her. Say instead, "A little danger, but you're handling it well." Wish that for yourself, maybe as much danger as you can handle. "You justify your miserable existence by your bravery."

> ✅ Mt 17:20. 🟡 His claim that over 50% of new businesses fail in the first year is wrong: U.S. data show about 20% fail in year one and about half by year five. His point, that you act without guarantees, still stands.

### 14. Summing up: vision and plan

- **What's the vision?** How would you set the world right around you? Break it down: relationships, conduct. Ask yourself; don't order yourself around like a tyrannical father.
- **Is your morality too timid?** "Maybe you should be 10 times as aggressive as you are, if it was channeled in the right direction."
- **How could it go terribly wrong**, and how do you avoid that?
- **How could it go wonderfully right**, not as a childish dream? A client wanted to retire at 50 to a Caribbean beach with a cocktail. Day one: 10 drinks, sunburned, hungover in the hotel for a week. "That's your life."
- **Take yourself seriously**, because you could be a force for destruction. What would make your life worth it, so you could drop nihilism, cynicism, bitterness, resentment, hostility and pride?

Peterson quotes: "The Kingdom of God is spread upon the earth, but men do not see it." You glimpse it when you're in the right place at the right time, as when you fall in love, and then it closes. Get good at opening it. Don't hide your light under a basket (Mt 5:15). You have 50 years to find out how much good you could do.

> ⚠️ The "Kingdom spread upon the earth" line is from the *Gospel of Thomas* (saying 113), a non-biblical text, not one of the four Gospels. The closest biblical parallel is "the kingdom of God is in the midst of you" (Lk 17:21). ✅ Mt 5:15.
