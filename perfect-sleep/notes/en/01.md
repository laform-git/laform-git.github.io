# PERFECT SLEEP — 01 Sleep Architecture

*Foundation note. Words like "fragmentation" and "depth" are meaningless without this. Every other note in the series refers back to it.*

## Sleep is not one state

Sleep is four distinct states doing different jobs. Losing one is not compensated by more of another. This is the single most important idea in the series, because it means **total hours asleep is a poor measure of a night.**

## The stages

**N1 — the doorway.** A few minutes. Muscle tone drops, hypnic jerks happen here. Woken from N1, people often deny they were asleep. Roughly 5% of the night. Its importance is not restorative — it is that **every return to sleep passes through it.**

**N2 — the bulk.** Around 45–55% of total sleep. Marked by two electrical signatures: *sleep spindles*, brief bursts associated with memory consolidation and with gating sensory input away from the sleeping brain, and *K-complexes*, large single waves that appear to suppress arousal from external stimuli. N2 is not filler — the spindle machinery is part of what decides whether a noise or a bladder signal breaks through.

**N3 — slow-wave sleep, the deep stage.** 15–25% of the night in a healthy adult, declining with age. Large synchronised delta waves. Growth hormone is released in pulses here. Glymphatic clearance runs here ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 03 Glymphatic Clearance]]). Arousal threshold is at its highest — this is the stage where a signal has to be loud to break through. Being woken from N3 produces severe disorientation.

**REM — rapid eye movement.** 20–25%. Brain activity approaches waking levels while skeletal muscle is paralysed (atonia). Vivid narrative dreaming. Emotional memory processing and consolidation. REM is actively switched on by cholinergic neurons in the pons (PPT and LDT) firing PGO waves — the eye movements, the cortical activation and the atonia are three outputs of one signal. The eye movements are a readout of that process, not a participant in consolidation. Full mechanism in [[PERFECT SLEEP — 08A Sedative Mechanisms: THC, Alcohol and CBD]]. One overlooked detail: **thermoregulation is largely suspended during REM** — the body stops defending its temperature, which is part of why a hot or cold room disrupts the back half of the night more than the front.

## Cycle structure — the asymmetry that matters

Cycles run roughly 90 minutes, though 70–120 is normal, four to six times a night. But the cycles are **not identical**:

- **N3 is front-loaded.** Most deep sleep occurs in the first two cycles — the first three hours or so. By the second half of the night there is very little N3 left to have.
- **REM is back-loaded.** REM periods are short early and lengthen progressively. The longest REM period of the night is usually the last one before waking.

The consequence is direct: **when sleep is lost determines what is lost.** Going to bed two hours late costs deep sleep. Waking two hours early costs REM. They are not interchangeable losses.

## The two-process model

Sleep timing is governed by two independent systems (Borbély, 1982):

- **Process S — homeostatic pressure.** Adenosine accumulates in the brain every waking hour and is cleared during sleep. The longer awake, the stronger the drive.
- **Process C — circadian alerting.** The clock produces a wakefulness signal that *rises* through the day, counteracting the growing adenosine pressure, then drops sharply in the evening.

Sleep happens in the gap where the alerting signal falls away and pressure remains high. This model explains things that otherwise seem contradictory: the late-evening "second wind" (a final circadian alerting push before it falls), why a nap can make bedtime harder (it discharges Process S), and why lying in on a weekend both reduces pressure and shifts the clock.

## What trackers actually measure

Consumer devices infer stages from movement and heart-rate variability. They do not measure brain activity.

- Sleep versus wake detection: reasonably good, often around 90% agreement with laboratory polysomnography.
- Four-stage classification: substantially weaker — commonly cited around 70%, with wide variation between devices and between nights.

Practical reading: **trust the trends, not the single night.** A tracker reporting 42 minutes of deep sleep on one particular Tuesday is not a measurement. A tracker showing deep sleep declining across three weeks may be.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Four stages, different jobs, not interchangeable.
- N3 lives in the first half of the night; REM lives in the second.
- Every return to sleep restarts at N1 — sleep never resumes where it left off.
- Arousal threshold is highest in N3 and lowest in N2 and REM, which decides whether a signal breaks through at all.
- Thermoregulation is suspended in REM, so room temperature hits the back half of the night hardest.
- Total hours is the weakest measure of a night. Structure is the real one.
