# PERFECT SLEEP — 10 Stress and Cortisol

*The other major output of the clock, and a leading cause of the 3am alert waking.*

## The normal curve

Cortisol is not a stress hormone that happens to have a rhythm. It is **a circadian hormone that also responds to stress**, and the rhythm is the larger part of the story.

- **Nadir around midnight.** Cortisol should be at its lowest at sleep onset. This is what permits sleep onset and depth.
- **Rises through the second half of the night**, preparing the body for waking.
- **Cortisol awakening response** — a sharp 50–75% rise in the 30–45 minutes after waking. This is a distinct, measurable event and it is what makes waking feel like waking.
- **Declines across the day** to the midnight nadir again.

The important property is the **slope**: a steep drop from morning peak to midnight trough. A steep slope is the healthy pattern. Flattening is the problem, regardless of the average level.

## What goes wrong

**Chronic stress flattens the curve.** Not simply "raises cortisol" — the characteristic pattern is a blunted morning rise and an elevated evening level. Less amplitude, not more hormone. The result is feeling unrefreshed on waking and wired at bedtime. **Tired and wired is a slope problem.**

**Evening elevation blocks sleep onset.** Cortisol is alerting. If it has not fallen by bedtime, sleep pressure and clock signal are fighting an active wake signal.

**The 3am waking.** A middle-of-the-night waking — fully alert, sometimes with a racing heart, and unable to return to sleep — is frequently HPA-axis activation — a cortisol pulse arriving too early and too large. It feels like anxiety because the chemistry is the same. It is often misread as a psychological event when the driver is physiological timing.

Two other things produce the same 3am signature and are worth separating: **counter-regulatory adrenaline after a sugary dinner** ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 07 Food]]) and **alcohol metabolising out** ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 07 Food]]). All three feel identical from inside.

## The bidirectional loop

Sleep loss raises cortisol. Elevated cortisol impairs sleep. This is one of the tighter self-sustaining loops in the series.

It also connects directly to the metabolic picture ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 12 Metabolic Status and Sleep]]): cortisol drives gluconeogenesis, raises blood glucose, worsens insulin resistance and promotes visceral fat deposition. Where insulin resistance is already the upstream driver of the wider health trajectory, the sleep–cortisol loop is not a side issue.

## Cortisol and night urine

A less-known link. Cortisol influences renal water and sodium handling and contributes to overnight urine production independently of vasopressin. A flattened curve with elevated night cortisol therefore adds to nocturia by a route separate from everything in [[PERFECT SLEEP — 05 Night Fluid]].

## What actually lowers evening cortisol

**Morning light.** The cortisol awakening response is anchored by the same clock that light sets ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 04 The Clock]]). A sharp morning signal produces a sharp morning peak, which produces a steeper decline into the evening. **Morning light is an evening-cortisol intervention.**

**A genuine wind-down.** Cortisol needs a runway to fall. Sixty minutes of low-stimulation before bed does more than any supplement in [[PERFECT SLEEP — 08 Substances and Stack]]. This is not a soft recommendation — the descent is a physiological process with a duration.

**Offloading rumination.** Lying in bed rehearsing the day is a cortisol stimulus. Writing the day down, or the Examen ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 16 Contemplative Practice]]), moves the processing out of the bed and out of the dark.

**Slow breathing.** Extended exhalation increases vagal tone and lowers sympathetic drive, measurable within minutes. Around six breaths per minute is the resonance point ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 16 Contemplative Practice]]).

**Exercise — with timing caveats** ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 13 Exercise, Sleep Pressure and Naps]]). Regular training lowers baseline cortisol reactivity. A hard session late in the evening raises it acutely.

**Not: late-evening problem-solving, news, email, or confrontational content.** These are cortisol inputs regardless of screen brightness ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 17 External Environment]]).

## Where this note is deliberately incomplete

Cortisol values, DHEA-S, and any lab interpretation fall outside this series. This note covers only the sleep-facing mechanism.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Cortisol is a circadian hormone first. The **slope** matters more than the level.
- "Tired and wired" is a flattened curve: blunted morning rise, elevated evening.
- The 3am alert waking is often an early cortisol pulse. Sugary dinners and alcohol produce an identical feeling by different routes.
- Cortisol contributes to night urine independently of vasopressin.
- Morning light sharpens the morning peak, which steepens the evening fall. It is a cortisol intervention, not just a clock one.
- Cortisol needs a runway. Sixty quiet minutes before bed is a physiological requirement, not a luxury.
- Get the day out of the head before bed — on paper or in prayer, not in the dark.
