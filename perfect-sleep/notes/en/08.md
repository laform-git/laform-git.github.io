## THC — the two-night cost

**Why it feels like it helps.** THC shortens sleep onset and suppresses REM. Less REM means fewer remembered dreams and a subjective impression of deeper, cleaner sleep. The impression is real; the restoration is not.

**Night one — the dose night.** REM is suppressed directly. Sleep looks deep but delivers far less REM than normal. The next day brings yawning and an unrestored state, partly from the missing REM and partly because THC has a long half-life with active metabolites — mild intoxication carries into the following day.

**Night two — the rebound.** The brain tracks the REM debt and pays it back. The second night runs REM-heavy: vivid dreams, lighter sleep, easier waking, more fragmentation.

**So one use costs a flat night and then a fragile one.** This is the detail almost nobody knows, because the bad next day gets noticed and the night after it never gets connected.

**Why the second night matters especially here.** REM-heavy sleep is shallow sleep, and from [[PERFECT SLEEP — 02 Nocturia and Broken Sleep]], arousal threshold is lowest in REM. Each bladder signal breaks through more easily. In someone already waking several times a night, the rebound night is where the damage compounds.

**Tolerance masks it.** With frequent use, receptors downregulate and REM suppression becomes the steady state — the contrast disappears. After months off, those receptors resensitise. The same dose lands on a naive system and hits far harder than it did during regular use.

**The blunt conclusion: once sensitivity is restored, occasional use is worse than regular use was.** There is no such thing as an isolated dose — each one carries a two-night bill.

**Stopping.** Vivid dreams in the weeks after quitting regular use are REM rebound at scale — the same mechanism as above, stretched across weeks after years of suppression.

## The stacking lesson

A lesson that generalises well beyond magnesium.

The generalisable rule: **when several products contain the same active compound under different names, the dose is the sum, not the label.** Magnesium glycinate is magnesium bound to glycine — the glycine comes along whether or not it appears on the front of the bottle.

## Magnesium forms — what actually differs

Once absorbed, magnesium is magnesium. The forms differ in absorption and in what they are bound to.

- **Magtein (magnesium L-threonate)** — the distinguishing feature is that it crosses into the brain more readily. This is the form with a specific cognitive and sleep rationale.
- **Magnesium glycinate** — well absorbed and gentle on the gut, but its sleep effect is substantially *the glycine*, not the magnesium.
- **Magnesium taurate** — taurine is mildly calming; same principle as glycinate.
- **Magnesium malate** — malate feeds the energy cycle and is generally considered the more **activating** form. Usually recommended for daytime. **At bedtime it is the odd one out.**

**Honest note on evidence:** magnesium supplementation for sleep in people who are not deficient has modest support. It is not a strong intervention. The forms are marketed with more confidence than the data supports.

## Inositol

Myo-inositol, used at gram doses. The best-supported item in a typical bedtime stack for anxiety and sleep quality, though the strongest evidence base sits in other conditions. Importantly, **it is not a GABA-A compound** — it works through a different signalling pathway and does not add to the inhibitory load.

## Nicotine

Included because it is commonly mis-filed. Nicotine is stimulant and arousing, suppresses REM, and — contrary to its grouping with caffeine — **stimulates vasopressin release**, making it antidiuretic rather than diuretic ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 05 Night Fluid]]).

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- One THC dose costs two nights: a REM-suppressed night, then a REM-rebound night that is shallow and easily interrupted.
- Tolerance hides this. After months off, sensitivity is restored and occasional use hits harder than regular use did.
- The dose is the sum across products, not what each label says. Magnesium glycinate is glycine.
- Malate is activating — wrong form for bedtime.
- Magnesium for sleep is a modest intervention. Do not expect much from form selection.
- Incidental glycine in a magnesium pill is not a glycine dose; the research uses about 3g.
