## Often-missed factors

- **Reflux at night.** Night reflux (stomach acid coming up into the food pipe) and broken sleep often go together, but which one causes the other is debated. In a study that measured acid and sleep at the same time all night, almost every night reflux event came just *after* a brief waking, not before it (Shepherd 2020) 🟡. Having no heartburn does not rule reflux out. The first steps cost nothing and help in other ways too. Sleep on the left side: it gives less acid exposure and faster clearing than the right side or the back (Schuitenmaker 2022) 🟢. Raise the head of the bed 6–8 inches, with blocks under the bed legs or a foam wedge under the head and upper back (NIDDK; recommended for night symptoms by the ACG guideline, Katz 2022) 🟢. Leave 3 hours between the last bite and bed (the ACG guideline asks for at least 2–3 hours) 🟢. If sleep is still broken, ask the doctor about acid exposure at night. [[PERFECT SLEEP — 15 GERD and Sleep]].
- **Sound in the bedroom.** A bed partner's snoring or sleep apnea (breathing that stops and starts during sleep) can wake you, and nothing else in this series deals with it. Snoring, then silence, then a sudden loud restart is noise that comes and goes. Noise that comes and goes wakes the brain more than steady noise of the same average loudness: what wakes the brain is a sound that rises quickly above the background. The brain gets used to it only partly. The brief brain wakings fade over several nights, but the heart-rate jumps do not (Basner 2011) 🟢. Try steady white noise or a fan. It works by raising the background, not by being louder. The idea makes sense, but the trial evidence that steady noise improves sleep is very low quality (Riedy 2021) 🟡. A mattress that does not pass on movement can help with the shifting at the end of each event. If a partner has apnea, getting it properly treated is the biggest help of all, and that is for their health first. [[PERFECT SLEEP — 14 Sleep-Disordered Breathing]] and [[PERFECT SLEEP — 17 External Environment]].
- **The two measurements most often skipped.** First: do the night wakings come from making too much urine, or from a bladder that holds too little? This is settled by a 3-day bladder diary (writing down each time you urinate and how much), the ICS standard of care, not by guessing (Hashim 2019) ✅. Second: how long it takes to fall back asleep after each waking. This series argues that matters more than the number of wakings 🔴. [[PERFECT SLEEP — 19 Measuring It]].

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Low energy, low testosterone, food cravings and lost exercise are not four separate problems. They are one sleep problem showing up in four places, and they improve together when the nights are fixed. 🔴 This way of seeing it is the series' own reading; the testosterone link has direct support: one week of 5-hour nights cut daytime testosterone by 10–15% in 10 young men (Leproult 2011).
- The surprising detail is the point of these notes.
- Measure before adding fixes. The skipped measurements usually tell you which problem to work on first.

**The point to remember: good sleep is deep sleep that is not broken, so measure what is waking you before you try to fix it.**
