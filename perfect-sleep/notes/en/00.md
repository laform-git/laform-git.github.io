## Often-missed factors

- **Nocturnal reflux.** Night reflux and broken sleep travel together, but which drives which is debated: in a study combining overnight pH monitoring with polysomnography, almost every night reflux event came just *after* an arousal rather than causing one (Shepherd 2020) 🟡. Having no heartburn does not rule reflux out. First moves cost nothing and stack with other benefits: sleep on the left side, which gives less acid exposure and faster clearance than the right side or the back (Schuitenmaker 2022) 🟢; raise the head of the bed 6–8 inches with blocks under the legs or a foam wedge under the head and upper back (NIDDK; recommended for night symptoms by the ACG guideline, Katz 2022) 🟢; keep 3 hours from last bite to bed (the ACG guideline asks for at least 2–3 hours) 🟢. If fragmentation persists, ask the physician about nocturnal acid exposure. [[PERFECT SLEEP — 15 GERD and Sleep]].
- **Bedroom sound.** A bed partner's snoring or apnea is an external arousal source not addressed by anything else in this series. Snore → silence → sudden loud resumption is intermittent noise, and intermittent noise is more arousing than steady noise of the same average level: what wakes the brain is a sound event rising quickly above the background. The brain habituates only partly — cortical arousals fade over successive nights, heart-rate responses do not (Basner 2011) 🟢. Test continuous white noise or a fan (it works by raising the floor, not by being louder) — the masking logic is sound, but trial evidence that continuous noise improves sleep is very low quality (Riedy 2021) 🟡; consider mattress motion isolation for the movement at the end of each event. If a partner has apnea, getting it properly treated is the largest lever — and that is for their health first. [[PERFECT SLEEP — 14 Sleep-Disordered Breathing]] and [[PERFECT SLEEP — 17 External Environment]].
- **The two measurements most often skipped.** Whether wakings come from polyuria (too much urine made) or capacity (too little room) — settled by a 3-day bladder diary (frequency–volume chart), the ICS standard of care, not by inference (Hashim 2019) ✅. And return-to-sleep latency after each waking, which this series argues matters more than the raw count 🔴. [[PERFECT SLEEP — 19 Measuring It]].

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Sleep is not one problem expressing itself in four places — low energy, low testosterone, food craving, lost exercise. It is one problem, and it runs in reverse when the nights are restored. 🔴 This framing is the series' interpretation; the testosterone link has direct support — one week of 5-hour nights cut daytime testosterone by 10–15% in 10 young men (Leproult 2011).
- The counterintuitive detail is the point of these notes.
- Measure before adding interventions. The skipped measurements usually decide which tap to work on.
