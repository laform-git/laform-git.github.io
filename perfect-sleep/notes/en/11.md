# PERFECT SLEEP — 11 Hormones and Sleep

*Hormones as sleep factors only. Lab values, dosing and interpretation fall outside this series.*

**In plain words:** several hormones are made or set during sleep. Testosterone rises during the first hours of sleep, and growth hormone comes in a big burst in the first deep sleep, so broken nights lower both. Short sleep also raises hunger and makes the body handle sugar worse. Testosterone therapy can worsen sleep apnea, thyroid problems can disturb sleep, and age reduces deep sleep but does not explain a sudden change.

**Words in this note:**
- **REM**: rapid eye movement sleep, the dreaming stage
- **GH**: growth hormone
- **DHEA**: a hormone made by the adrenal glands that the body turns into sex hormones
- **DHEA-S**: the stored, blood-test form of DHEA

## Testosterone is made during sleep

The detail that changes this whole topic: **testosterone release depends on sleep; it does not just happen alongside it.** The main rise happens during sleep and needs about three hours of normally structured sleep, peaking in the early morning (Wittert 2014) ✅. Corrected: the rise is tied to the first hours of sleep and to the start of the first REM (dream sleep) period, not to deep slow-wave sleep (Luboshitzky 2001).

The result is direct. **Broken sleep is not just a time when testosterone happens to be low. It is a cause of it.** 🟡 In healthy men, breaking up their sleep on purpose delayed and weakened the night-time rise, mainly in those who lost their REM periods (Luboshitzky 2001).

The research is less clear-cut than people often say. In one well-known study, limiting 10 healthy young men to five hours a night for one week lowered daytime testosterone by 10–15% (Leproult 2011) 🟢. But a study pooling 18 studies found that a whole night or more without sleep (24 hours or more) clearly lowers testosterone, while short periods of partly reduced sleep had no clear effect (Su 2021) 🟡. That years of broken nights add up to a much bigger total effect is 🔴 an educated guess, not measured.

And it works both ways. Low testosterone itself goes with poorer sleep quality, which replacement doses can improve (Wittert 2014) 🟡. Another loop that keeps itself going.

**In practice:** a testosterone result from blood taken during a period of broken sleep is partly reporting on the sleep, not only on the testes and the hormones that control them. This matters when reading any blood test taken during such a time.

## Growth hormone

Growth hormone (GH) is released in bursts, closely tied to deep slow-wave sleep. The biggest burst of the day comes in the first N3 (deep sleep) period of the night ✅. At any age, the amount of GH released follows the amount of slow-wave sleep (Van Cauter 2000).

Since deep sleep (N3) comes early in the night ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 01 Sleep Architecture]]), **the first unbroken stretch carries the growth hormone burst**. A 1:00am waking can cut into it 🟡 (believable from the timing; not measured directly). This is a second, separate reason, along with the brain's nightly cleaning ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 03 Glymphatic Clearance]]), to protect the first sleep period in particular.

## The full hormonal cascade of short sleep

Short sleep produces a fairly consistent pattern, though not every link is equally solid:

- **Ghrelin up**: the hunger hormone rises.
- **Leptin down**: the fullness signal falls. 🟡 Shown in a small, tightly controlled study (ghrelin +28%, leptin −18%: Spiegel 2004), but a study pooling 41 randomised trials found no strong evidence of a change in average leptin or ghrelin (Zhu 2019).
- Overall: more hunger and more eating ✅, about 250–385 extra kcal a day when sleep is partly cut (Zhu 2019; Al Khatib 2017). Appetite for high-calorie, carbohydrate-rich food rose by 33–45% in the small study (Spiegel 2004), but the extra eating measured came mainly from fat, not carbohydrate (Al Khatib 2017).
- **The body responds less to insulin** ✅ (Zhu 2019) ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 12 Metabolic Status and Sleep]]).
- **The cortisol curve flattens** ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 10 Stress and Cortisol]]).
- **Testosterone falls** 🟡 (clear with a whole night lost; inconsistent when sleep is only partly cut).

This is the chain in concrete terms: **poor sleep → low energy → low testosterone → food craving → no exercise → worse metabolism → worse sleep.** It is not several separate problems. It is one problem showing up in several places, and it runs in reverse when the nights are fixed. Testosterone, appetite control, the body's response to insulin and energy all improve without being targeted one by one. 🔴 The chain is an interpretation linking separate findings. The reversal has some support: sleeping about 1.2 hours more a night cut eating by about 270 kcal a day in adults with overweight (Tasali 2022) 🟢.

## Testosterone therapy and sleep

Testosterone taken as a medicine has a truly two-sided relationship with sleep, and both sides should be known.

- **It can worsen breathing problems during sleep.** Testosterone therapy is linked to worsening or bringing out obstructive sleep apnea (breathing that stops and starts during sleep) in some men, especially at higher doses. High-dose injections in older men shortened sleep by about an hour and added about seven breathing events per hour (Liu 2003) 🟢; at normal replacement doses the effect seems small and often temporary (Wittert 2014). The Endocrine Society lists untreated severe sleep apnea as a reason not to start therapy (Bhasin 2018) ✅ ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 14 Sleep-Disordered Breathing]]). This matters even where there is no apnea now.
- **Night sweats** can break up sleep. 🟡 Corrected: hot flushes are listed but uncommon (about 1%) on testosterone gel labels; flushing and sweats are more typical of *low or falling* testosterone, for example late in the gap between doses ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 06 Heat and Timing]]).
- **Taking it the same way every day matters when reading results.** A gel applied irregularly gives swinging values that can look like a real change in the body but are really caused by gaps in dosing; a steady daily morning dose fixes that.

## DHEA

DHEA (a hormone made by the adrenal glands) is commonly taken in the morning for low DHEA-S (its main form in the blood). Only the points that matter for sleep:

- It is a starting material the body turns into both male and female hormones (androgens and estrogens), so its effects are indirect and vary.
- Some reports of insomnia or vivid dreams, usually at higher doses 🟡 (little evidence).
- Taking it in the morning makes sense: it follows the body's own DHEA rhythm, which peaks early. An evening dose would be the wrong timing 🔴 (logical, not tested).
- The evidence that DHEA improves sleep directly is weak. When it is taken, it is for the blood value, not for sleep.

## Thyroid

Worth keeping in mind even when nothing points to it. Both directions disturb sleep: an overactive thyroid causes insomnia and night sweats; an underactive one causes too much sleepiness, weight gain and tiredness that looks like poor sleep. Tiredness that does not go away when sleep improves is a reason to check thyroid function.

## Age — stated plainly

This belongs in the series so the goal is realistic, rather than the sleep of a twenty-year-old.

- **Deep slow-wave sleep falls a lot with age** ✅. In men the steepest fall is from early adulthood to midlife, from about 19% of the night at ages 16–25 to about 3% at 36–50. After that it levels off, while time awake at night rises by about 28 minutes every ten years (Van Cauter 2000). Corrected: earlier versions said the fall continues steadily. Total sleep time falls far less than deep sleep does (Ohayon 2004).
- **The night-time rise of vasopressin (the hormone that holds back urine) flattens with age** 🟢. Most healthy older adults in one study lacked the normal night-time rise (Asplund 1991). That is a separate cause of nocturia (waking to urinate) that has nothing to do with the prostate ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 05 Night Fluid]]).
- **The body clock's daily swing gets smaller**, and its timing tends to move earlier 🟢.
- **It gets easier to wake**: older people's sleep is more easily disturbed by the same noise or signal ✅. The loudness needed to wake someone falls from early adulthood onward, already by the 40s in deep and N2 sleep (Zepelin 1984).

What this does *not* mean: that poor sleep is simply age and must be accepted. Age sets the limit. It does not explain a sharp decline over months.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Testosterone is made during sleep, especially in the first hours. Broken nights weaken the rise; one week of short sleep cut it 10–15% in one small study of young men, though results for partly reduced sleep are inconsistent across studies.
- The biggest growth hormone burst comes with the first N3 period. Another reason to protect the first unbroken stretch.
- Short sleep raises hunger and adds roughly 250–385 kcal a day of eating. The craving comes from the body, not a lack of willpower, though the leptin/ghrelin part is less consistent than often claimed.
- One problem, four ways of showing up, and it reverses the same way it broke.
- Testosterone therapy can worsen breathing problems in sleep and cause night sweats. Both are sleep issues, not just hormone ones.
- Age lowers deep sleep and flattens the vasopressin rise. It sets the limit; it does not explain a sudden change.
- A testosterone result from blood taken during a period of broken sleep is partly measuring the sleep.

**The point to remember: protect the first hours of sleep, because that is when testosterone and growth hormone are made.**
