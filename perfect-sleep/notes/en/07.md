# PERFECT SLEEP — 07 Food

*Dinner composition and timing, caffeine, alcohol — judged only by effect on the night.*

## The 3am waking that is not indigestion

🔴 A hypothesis, not an established mechanism. A sugary or refined-carbohydrate dinner produces a glucose rise, then an insulin response, and in some people a **dip** a few hours later. The body corrects a real dip by releasing adrenaline and cortisol.

That adrenaline release is a waking signal: ✅ when glucose is pushed low during sleep in experiments, most healthy people wake, and waking tracks the adrenaline rise (Schultes 2007). If a dinner-driven dip occurs, it could produce the classic **3am waking — alert, sometimes with a racing heart, and unable to get back to sleep** — and it feels like anxiety because physiologically it is the same chemistry.

People attribute this to stress or to something they ate disagreeing with them. 🟡 Whether an ordinary sweet dinner drops glucose far enough to wake a non-diabetic sleeper has not been shown. The wider evidence is mixed: in a large cohort of postmenopausal women, high-glycemic-index diets were linked to more insomnia (Gangwisch 2020), while in a small lab study a high-glycemic rice dinner four hours before bed shortened the time to fall asleep (Afaghi 2007). If the mechanism is real, it matters more in someone with existing insulin resistance ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 12 Metabolic Status and Sleep]]), because the glucose swings are larger.

## What loads the night, in rough order of impact

**1. Salt.** 🟢 The best-documented dietary contributor to overnight urine: cutting salt reduced nocturia in an uncontrolled study (Matsuo 2019). The ranking in this list is a judgement, not a measured order. Every gram of sodium must leave and takes water with it; eaten at dinner, that excretion lands overnight. There is also a slower skin-storage and release effect, so the weekly pattern matters, not just one meal.

**2. A large protein serving.** Protein produces urea, and urea is excreted with water — an osmotic obligation. For someone on a high daily protein target, the lever is **distribution, not reduction**. Move the heavier serving to lunch.

**3. Total meal volume.** A large meal keeps digestion active and raises core temperature at the hour it should be falling ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 04 The Clock]]). The thermic effect of food is real and works against the descending curve.

**4. High fat and heavily spiced food.** Slows gastric emptying and raises reflux risk; the ACG guideline suggests avoiding individual trigger foods (Katz 2022). In someone with GERD ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 15 GERD and Sleep]]), this fragments sleep even on nights when it does not cause a full waking.

**5. Sugar and refined carbohydrate.** The counter-regulatory mechanism above.

## Timing

Roughly three hours from the end of dinner to bed. 🟢 The ACG reflux guideline advises avoiding meals two to three hours before lying down (Katz 2022), and in a case-control study a dinner-to-bed time under three hours was linked to about sevenfold higher odds of reflux disease (Fujiwara 2005). This serves three separate purposes at once: gastric emptying for reflux, the fluid runway for night urine, and letting the thermic effect finish before the temperature curve needs to fall.

A further point that is often missed: **late eating shifts peripheral clocks.** The liver and gut have their own circadian oscillators, entrained largely by feeding time rather than light. Eating late pulls those out of alignment with the master clock. 🟢 In humans, shifting meals five hours later delayed the blood-glucose rhythm by about 5.7 hours and a fat-tissue clock gene by about 1 hour, while melatonin, the master-clock marker, did not move (Wehrens 2017). The sleep consequences of this are less well established than the light effects, but the mechanism is solid and it is one more argument for a consistent, earlier dinner.

## What actually helps

- A **moderate** meal, finished three hours before bed.
- Salt and the heavier protein serving moved earlier in the day.
- Not going to bed hungry — 🟡 an empty stomach may fragment sleep in its own right, and low-calorie dieting has been shown to raise cortisol (Tomiyama 2010).
- Carbohydrate is not the enemy at dinner; *refined* carbohydrate producing a sharp swing is. A moderate portion of slower carbohydrate alongside protein and fat blunts the curve.

## Caffeine — the honest version

**The morning delay advice.** The common recommendation, popularised by podcasts rather than trials, is to wait 90 minutes to two hours after waking. 🔴 No trial has tested whether delaying the first coffee improves anything. The two rationales are not equally plausible:

- **The cortisol argument** — that caffeine during the natural morning cortisol rise blunts it and causes a crash — is popular but thin. Largely extrapolated rather than demonstrated. Caffeine does raise cortisol, but the morning response is largely abolished in daily users (Lovallo 2005).
- **The adenosine argument is better grounded, but still untested as advice.** ✅ Adenosine builds while awake and clears during sleep; on waking some residual remains. Caffeine does not remove it, it blocks the receptor. Drinking immediately masks leftover grogginess rather than letting it clear, and it returns as the caffeine fades. 🔴 That a delay prevents an afternoon crash is an inference.

**The evening rule matters far more.** ✅ Half-life averages about five hours but ranges from roughly 1.5 to 9.5 hours between people (IOM 2001). At five hours, a 2pm coffee still has roughly a quarter of its dose active at midnight, lightening sleep even if falling asleep is easy. 🟢 400 mg taken six hours before bed still disrupted sleep significantly (Drake 2013), and a meta-analysis of 24 studies advises a standard coffee at least 8.8 hours before bed (Gardiner 2023).

**A practical override:** if waiting 90 minutes means skipping the morning light, drink the coffee outside and stop optimising. The light is worth more than the delay.

## Alcohol — the most sleep-destructive item on the list

Stated in full because understanding why it is out is worth as much as the fact that it is out.

**On the hormone:** ✅ it directly suppresses vasopressin release. Not indirect, not via the clock — it blocks the signal. The valve opens, the kidney stops concentrating, and dilute urine follows; the suppression is strongest while blood alcohol is rising and fades after (Eisenhofer 1982). In someone already dealing with nocturia this stacks straight onto the existing problem.

**On architecture:** ✅ it is a sedative, so onset is faster and the first half of the night is more consolidated, with more deep sleep. Then as it metabolises — roughly one drink per hour — sleep is disrupted in the second half of the night (Ebrahim 2013). The first REM period is delayed and total REM falls at moderate and high doses, sleep fragments, heart rate stays elevated, and waking at 3 or 4am feeling wired is typical. 🟢 In over 4,000 people monitored at home, even low doses cut overnight heart-rate-variability recovery by about 9%, and high doses by about 39% (Pietilä 2018). **Total time asleep can look fine while the quality is destroyed.**

**On temperature:** it dilates skin vessels, so the skin feels warm while the normal overnight core curve is disturbed.

**🟢 There is no dose that improves overall sleep quality.** 🔴 Three hours before bed is a rule of thumb, not a tested cutoff, if drinking at all; closer to zero in the evening is better.

## Hydration is not simply "drink less"

🟡 Short sleep and poor hydration go together: in US and Chinese adults, sleeping 6 hours was linked to higher odds of inadequate hydration than sleeping 8 (Rosinger 2019). The direction may run from short sleep to dehydration, via the vasopressin rhythm, rather than the reverse. The principle is **same total water, earlier**: drink freely until late afternoon, taper over the final three hours. Restricting total intake to avoid nocturia trades one problem for another.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- The 3am alert waking after a sweet dinner is counter-regulatory adrenaline, not indigestion.
- Salt is the single biggest dietary contributor to overnight urine. Protein via urea is second.
- Move salt and the heavy protein serving to lunch. Do not reduce total protein.
- Three hours from last bite to bed serves reflux, fluid and temperature simultaneously.
- Caffeine's evening tail matters far more than the morning delay ritual.
- Alcohol: no dose improves sleep. Zero is the target.
- Do not fix nocturia by drinking less water. Fix it by drinking the same water earlier.
