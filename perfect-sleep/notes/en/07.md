# PERFECT SLEEP — 07 Food

*Dinner composition and timing, caffeine, alcohol — judged only by effect on the night.*

## The 3am waking that is not indigestion

The most useful thing in this note. A sugary or refined-carbohydrate dinner produces a glucose rise, then an insulin response, then a **counter-regulatory dip** a few hours later. The body corrects that dip by releasing adrenaline and cortisol.

That adrenaline release is a waking signal. It produces the classic **3am waking — alert, sometimes with a racing heart, and unable to get back to sleep** — and it feels like anxiety because physiologically it is the same chemistry.

People attribute this to stress or to something they ate disagreeing with them. The actual mechanism is glycemic. It matters more in someone with existing insulin resistance ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 12 Metabolic Status and Sleep]]), because the glucose swings are larger.

## What loads the night, in order of impact

**1. Salt.** The largest dietary contributor to overnight urine. Every gram of sodium must leave and takes water with it; eaten at dinner, that excretion lands overnight. There is also a slower skin-storage and release effect, so the weekly pattern matters, not just one meal.

**2. A large protein serving.** Protein produces urea, and urea is excreted with water — an osmotic obligation. For someone on a high daily protein target, the lever is **distribution, not reduction**. Move the heavier serving to lunch.

**3. Total meal volume.** A large meal keeps digestion active and raises core temperature at the hour it should be falling ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 04 The Clock]]). The thermic effect of food is real and works against the descending curve.

**4. High fat and heavily spiced food.** Slows gastric emptying and raises reflux risk. In someone with GERD ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 15 GERD and Sleep]]), this fragments sleep even on nights when it does not cause a full waking.

**5. Sugar and refined carbohydrate.** The counter-regulatory mechanism above.

## Timing

Roughly three hours from the end of dinner to bed. This serves three separate purposes at once: gastric emptying for reflux, the fluid runway for night urine, and letting the thermic effect finish before the temperature curve needs to fall.

A further point that is often missed: **late eating shifts peripheral clocks.** The liver and gut have their own circadian oscillators, entrained largely by feeding time rather than light. Eating late pulls those out of alignment with the master clock. The sleep consequences of this are less well established than the light effects, but the mechanism is solid and it is one more argument for a consistent, earlier dinner.

## What actually helps

- A **moderate** meal, finished three hours before bed.
- Salt and the heavier protein serving moved earlier in the day.
- Not going to bed hungry — an empty stomach can fragment sleep in its own right, and severe restriction raises cortisol.
- Carbohydrate is not the enemy at dinner; *refined* carbohydrate producing a sharp swing is. A moderate portion of slower carbohydrate alongside protein and fat blunts the curve.

## Caffeine — the honest version

**The morning delay advice.** The common recommendation is to wait 90 minutes to two hours after waking. The two rationales are not equally supported:

- **The cortisol argument** — that caffeine during the natural morning cortisol rise blunts it and causes a crash — is popular but thin. Largely extrapolated rather than demonstrated.
- **The adenosine argument is solid.** Adenosine builds while awake and clears during sleep; on waking some residual remains. Caffeine does not remove it, it blocks the receptor. Drinking immediately masks leftover grogginess rather than letting it clear, and it returns as the caffeine fades.

**The evening rule matters far more.** Half-life is five to six hours. A 2pm coffee still has roughly a quarter of its dose active at midnight, lightening sleep even if falling asleep is easy.

**A practical override:** if waiting 90 minutes means skipping the morning light, drink the coffee outside and stop optimising. The light is worth more than the delay.

## Alcohol — the most sleep-destructive item on the list

Stated in full because understanding why it is out is worth as much as the fact that it is out.

**On the hormone:** it directly suppresses vasopressin release at the pituitary. Not indirect, not via the clock — it blocks the signal. The valve opens, the kidney stops concentrating, and dilute urine is produced all night. In someone already dealing with nocturia this stacks straight onto the existing problem.

**On architecture:** it is a sedative, so onset is faster and the first couple of hours look deep. Then as it metabolises — roughly one drink per hour — there is a rebound in the second half of the night. REM is suppressed early and crowds back late, sleep fragments, heart rate stays elevated, and waking at 3 or 4am feeling wired is typical. **Total time asleep can look fine while the quality is destroyed.**

**On temperature:** it dilates skin vessels, so the skin feels warm while the normal overnight core curve is lost.

**There is no dose that improves sleep.** Three hours before bed is the minimum if drinking at all; closer to zero in the evening is better.

## Hydration is not simply "drink less"

Dehydration fragments sleep in its own right and is associated with shorter sleep duration. The principle is **same total water, earlier**: drink freely until late afternoon, taper over the final three hours. Restricting total intake to avoid nocturia trades one problem for another.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- The 3am alert waking after a sweet dinner is counter-regulatory adrenaline, not indigestion.
- Salt is the single biggest dietary contributor to overnight urine. Protein via urea is second.
- Move salt and the heavy protein serving to lunch. Do not reduce total protein.
- Three hours from last bite to bed serves reflux, fluid and temperature simultaneously.
- Caffeine's evening tail matters far more than the morning delay ritual.
- Alcohol: no dose improves sleep. Zero is the target.
- Do not fix nocturia by drinking less water. Fix it by drinking the same water earlier.
