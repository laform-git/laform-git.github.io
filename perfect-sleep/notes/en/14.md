# PERFECT SLEEP — 14 Sleep-Disordered Breathing

*Included for two reasons even where the sleeper does not have it: it overrides everything in [[PERFECT SLEEP — 05 Night Fluid]] when present, and a bed partner may have it.*

## Why this note matters even where apnea is ruled out

Two separate reasons, and the second is the one that usually gets missed.

1. **Apnea overrides the vasopressin mechanism entirely.** If it were present, nothing in [[PERFECT SLEEP — 05 Night Fluid]] would hold, and all the fluid work would fail for a reason that has nothing to do with fluid.
2. **A bed partner's apnea fragments the other person's sleep.** This is a real and measurable external arousal source, and it is not addressed by anything else in this series.

## What obstructive apnea does

The upper airway collapses during sleep. Breathing stops or becomes severely reduced, oxygen falls, carbon dioxide rises, and the brain produces a brief **arousal** to reopen the airway. Breathing resumes and sleep continues.

The arousal usually does not reach conscious waking. That is the defining feature: **someone can have hundreds of arousals a night and report sleeping through.** What they report instead is unrefreshing sleep and daytime fatigue with no obvious cause.

The cost to architecture is severe — repeated arousals prevent the descent into N3 and truncate REM, so the night delivers very little of either ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 01 Sleep Architecture]]).

## The nocturia connection — the part almost nobody knows

This is the most important thing in the note.

During an obstructive event, the person strains to breathe against a closed airway. That generates large negative pressure in the chest, which **stretches the atria of the heart**. Stretched atria release **atrial natriuretic peptide**, a hormone whose job is to make the kidney excrete sodium and water.

So apnea drives overnight urine production **directly, by a hormonal route that overrides vasopressin entirely.** No amount of fluid timing, salt reduction or leg elevation will counter it.

The clinical consequence is striking: **nocturia is one of the strongest predictors of undiagnosed obstructive sleep apnea in men** — in some series a better predictor than snoring. And treating apnea with CPAP reduces nocturia substantially, often within weeks.

This is why apnea must be excluded before concluding that a nocturia problem is prostatic or hormonal. **It is the first fork in the road, not a footnote.**

## Risk factors and why they matter here

- Male sex, increasing age
- Central and neck adiposity — links to [[PERFECT SLEEP — 12 Metabolic Status and Sleep]]
- **Testosterone therapy can worsen or unmask apnea**, particularly at higher doses ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 11 Hormones and Sleep]]). Relevant for anyone on testosterone gel or injections.
- Alcohol and sedatives relax the airway. Both benzodiazepines and antihistamines worsen apnea ([[PERFECT SLEEP — 09 Sleep Medications and Aids]]).
- Supine sleeping position worsens it — gravity closes the airway more readily on the back.
- Nasal obstruction and mouth breathing.

## Signs worth watching for, even after apnea has been excluded

Stated because risk factors change, and testosterone therapy is one of them.

- Loud snoring, or witnessed pauses in breathing
- Waking gasping or choking
- Morning headache, dry mouth on waking
- Daytime sleepiness disproportionate to hours slept
- Nocturia that **does not improve** as the prostate issue resolves

That last one is the specific tripwire for someone being treated for BPH. If wakings plateau or fail to follow the expected improvement after a prostate procedure, apnea is the first thing to re-examine — not the last.

## Central sleep apnea

A different condition: the airway is open but the brain intermittently fails to signal a breath. Associated with heart failure, opioid use and high altitude. Rarer, and it does not respond to the same treatments. Mentioned only for completeness.

## Upper airway resistance syndrome

Worth knowing as the gap in the diagnostic net. Airflow is reduced enough to cause repeated arousals, but not enough to register as apneas or hypopneas on a standard scoring threshold. A sleep study can come back "normal" while the person is being fragmented all night. If unexplained fragmentation persists with a normal study, this is the thing to ask about.

## The shared bed

The dimension this series would otherwise miss entirely. **When one partner has apnea and the other does not**, the unaffected partner's sleep is disrupted through three distinct routes:

**1. Snoring and gasping as noise.** Apneic snoring is not steady — it is loud, then silent, then a sudden loud resumption. **Intermittent and unpredictable noise is far more arousing than continuous noise**, because the brain habituates to steady sound and cannot habituate to irregular sound. This makes apneic snoring disproportionately disruptive compared with its decibel level.

**2. Movement.** Arousals at the end of each event come with body movement and position shifts. In a shared bed these transmit directly, and **mattress choice matters more than it seems** — motion isolation is a genuine intervention here.

**3. CPAP, if in use.** Modern machines are quiet, around 30 decibels, but mask leak produces irregular hissing, which falls under the same intermittent-noise problem above. Worth resolving as a mask-fit issue rather than tolerating.

**Interventions, in order of impact:**

- The affected partner's apnea being properly treated is the largest single lever — for that partner's health first, not only the other's sleep.
- **Continuous masking noise** — a fan or white noise — works by raising the floor so that intermittent sounds are less distinct against it. This is the standard fix and it is effective.
- Earplugs.
- Motion-isolating mattress.
- Separate beds or rooms. Worth naming plainly rather than treating as a failure — the research on sleeping apart is more favourable than the cultural framing suggests, and it is a legitimate option when one partner has a treated or untreated sleep disorder.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Apnea arousals usually do not reach consciousness. Someone can be fragmented hundreds of times and report sleeping through.
- Apnea drives nocturia via atrial stretch and natriuretic peptide — a route that overrides vasopressin completely. Fluid measures cannot beat it.
- Nocturia is one of the strongest predictors of undiagnosed apnea in men.
- Testosterone therapy can unmask it. If wakings fail to improve as the prostate resolves, re-examine apnea first.
- A normal sleep study does not exclude upper airway resistance syndrome.
- A partner's apnea fragments the other person's sleep. Intermittent noise is far more arousing than steady noise — which is exactly why continuous white noise works as the fix.
- Sleeping apart is a legitimate option, not a failure.
