# Q137 · Are Catholics morally obliged to vote for pro-life candidates?

**Short answer**
Catholics must form their conscience well and vote to promote the common good, giving special weight to the protection of innocent life. The Church does not endorse parties or candidates; voters must weigh the issues prudently.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Citizens have a duty to take part in public life and to vote responsibly (CCC 2239–2240).
- ✅ The U.S. bishops' *Forming Consciences for Faithful Citizenship* calls the threat of abortion a "preeminent priority" while insisting other grave issues also matter (racism, poverty, immigration, euthanasia, the death penalty).
- 🟡 A Catholic may not vote for a candidate *because* of their support for an intrinsic evil like abortion; voting for that candidate for other proportionate reasons is discussed among moral theologians (Cardinal Ratzinger, memorandum, 2004).

**Why it matters**
Faith shapes the whole of life, including the voting booth, but parties rarely match Catholic teaching fully.

**For class**
How would you weigh several issues when no candidate fully agrees with Church teaching?
