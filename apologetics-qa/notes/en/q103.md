# Q103 · Why did the Church torture and burn heretics in the Inquisitions?

**Short answer**
The Inquisitions were real and caused real injustice, which the Church has repented of. But modern research shows they were smaller and more regulated than the legend, and they reflected a time when heresy was treated as a crime against the state.

**Facts**
- 🟢 There were several inquisitions: medieval (from the 1180s–1230s), Spanish (1478–1834, run largely under the Spanish crown) and Roman (from 1542).
- 🟢 Historians estimate about 3,000 to 5,000 executions by the Spanish Inquisition over about 350 years, far fewer than older claims of millions 🟡.
- 🟢 Torture was used, as in all European courts then, but under rules that often made it rarer than in secular courts.
- 🟢 Executions were carried out by the civil authorities.

**What the Church says**
✅ In 2000 the Church asked forgiveness for "the use of violence in the service of truth". Religious freedom is now taught as a right rooted in human dignity (CCC 2106–2109).

**Why it matters**
Telling the truth includes admitting sin and correcting exaggerations.
