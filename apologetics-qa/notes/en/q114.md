# Q114 · What are the basic principles of objective morality?

**Short answer**
An act is good when three things are good: the act itself (its object), the intention behind it, and the circumstances. A few simple rules follow from this.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ The three sources of morality: object, intention, circumstances (CCC 1750–1756).
- ✅ A good intention cannot make an evil act good: "the end does not justify the means" (CCC 1753, 1756).
- ✅ Some acts are always wrong in themselves, whatever the intention, such as murder, adultery or blasphemy (CCC 1756).

**Rules to remember**
- ✅ One may never do evil so that good may come of it (Rom 3:8; CCC 1789).
- ✅ The Golden Rule: "Whatever you wish that men would do to you, do so to them" (Mt 7:12; CCC 1789).
- ✅ Charity always respects one's neighbor and their conscience (CCC 1789).
- 🟢 Double effect: an act with a good and a bad effect can be allowed if the act itself is good, the bad effect is not intended, and there is a serious reason (example: self-defense, CCC 2263).
