# Q109 · Is it ever okay to lie?

**Short answer**
Lying is always wrong in itself. How serious it is depends on what is at stake, and in rare extreme cases a person's guilt is greatly reduced.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ A lie is saying something false in order to deceive someone who has a right to the truth (CCC 2482–2483).
- ✅ Most lies are venial; a lie becomes grave when it seriously harms justice or charity (CCC 2484).
- ✅ The duty to share the truth is not absolute: no one is obliged to reveal the truth to someone who has no right to know it; silence and discretion are often right (CCC 2488–2489).

**The hard case**
🟡 A family hides Jews from the Nazis, and soldiers ask at the door. Catholic moralists differ: some say a false answer is still a lie, though with very little guilt; others say the soldiers have no right to the truth, so deceiving them is not a lie in the strict sense. All agree the family must protect the innocent.

**Why it matters**
Speech exists to share what is true. Every lie weakens the trust that families and societies depend on.

**For class**
Is staying silent ever better than speaking? When is keeping a secret a duty rather than a deception?
