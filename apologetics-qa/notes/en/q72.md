# Q72 · Why do Catholics have to get married in a church? Why so many rules?

**Short answer**
Marriage between Catholics is a sacrament, a public act of the Church, not just a private event. Marrying before the Church's minister and witnesses makes the covenant clear, public and protected.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Catholics are normally required to marry in the presence of a priest or deacon and two witnesses (CCC 1631).
- ✅ Reasons: marriage is a liturgical act, it creates rights and duties in the Church, it requires certainty about who is married, and it calls for public witness (CCC 1631).

**Exceptions**
🟢 A bishop can grant a dispensation, for example for a Catholic marrying a non-Catholic in another church. A beach wedding is not forbidden in itself; it requires the bishop's permission, which is often not given.

**Why it matters**
The rules are like guardrails on a mountain road: they protect something precious, not restrict it for no reason.
