# Q58 · Why does the Church baptize infants?

**Short answer**
Because baptism is a gift of grace, not a reward for understanding. Parents give children life, food and love before they can ask; the Church gives them the life of grace the same way, and the parents promise to raise them in the faith.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Infants are born with original sin and need the new birth of baptism; the Church and parents would deny them a priceless grace by delaying (CCC 1250).
- ✅ Infant baptism has been practiced "from the most ancient times" (CCC 1252).

**In Scripture and history**
- 🟢 Whole "households" were baptized (Acts 16:15, 33; 1 Cor 1:16).
- 🟢 "The promise is to you and to your children" (Acts 2:39).
- 🟢 Baptism replaces circumcision, which was given to babies at eight days (Col 2:11–12).
- 🟢 Early writers (Hippolytus, c. 215; Origen, Cyprian) describe infant baptism as apostolic practice.

**Why it matters**
God's love comes first. Faith is later embraced personally, especially at Confirmation.
