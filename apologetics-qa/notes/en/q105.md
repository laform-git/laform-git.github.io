# Q105 · Why did the Church support the slave trade? Did Columbus and Catholic missionaries forcibly convert indigenous peoples?

**Short answer**
The record is mixed and painful. Many Catholics, including clergy, took part in slavery and abuses. At the same time popes and missionaries repeatedly condemned the enslavement of indigenous peoples and forced conversions, though often they were ignored.

**Condemnations**
- 🟢 Pope Eugene IV (*Sicut Dudum*, 1435) ordered the freeing of enslaved Canary Islanders.
- 🟢 Pope Paul III (*Sublimis Deus*, 1537) declared that indigenous peoples are true humans who must not be enslaved, and must be evangelized by preaching and good example, not force.
- 🟢 Bartolomé de las Casas and Antonio de Montesinos denounced abuses; the Valladolid debate (1550–51) argued their case.
- 🟢 Gregory XVI (*In Supremo*, 1839) condemned the slave trade.

**Failures**
🟡 Some papal documents (like *Dum Diversas*, 1452) allowed Portuguese conquest and enslavement; many Catholic institutions owned slaves.

**What the Church teaches**
✅ Enslaving people is a grave sin against human dignity (CCC 2414). Faith must be free (CCC 160).
