# Q83 · What is required for a sacrament to be given and received validly?

**Short answer**
Three things: the right **matter** (the material sign, such as water or bread), the right **form** (the essential words), and a proper **minister** who intends to do what the Church does. The recipient must also be capable of receiving it.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Sacraments work by Christ's power, not the minister's holiness (*ex opere operato*) (CCC 1128).
- ✅ Their fruit in us also depends on our disposition (CCC 1128, 1131).

**Examples**
- 🟢 Baptism: matter, water; form, "I baptize you in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"; ordinary minister, a bishop, priest or deacon, but in an emergency anyone can baptize (CCC 1256).
- 🟢 Eucharist: matter, wheat bread and grape wine; form, the words of consecration; minister, a validly ordained priest.

**Validity and fruitfulness**
🟢 A sacrament can be valid but not fruitful, for example Communion received in serious sin.

**Why it matters**
These rules guarantee that when the Church celebrates, Christ truly acts.
