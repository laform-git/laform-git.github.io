# Q144 · Why is the Church against euthanasia if it reduces suffering? Isn't it more charitable?

**Short answer**
Compassion means "suffering with", not eliminating the sufferer. The Church supports every effort to relieve pain, including strong painkillers and stopping burdensome treatments, but never intentionally causing death.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Direct euthanasia, an act or omission that intends to cause death to end suffering, is morally unacceptable (CCC 2277).
- ✅ Stopping medical procedures that are burdensome, dangerous, extraordinary or disproportionate is not euthanasia; it accepts that death cannot be prevented (CCC 2278).
- ✅ Painkillers may be used even if they may shorten life, as long as death is not intended (CCC 2279).
- ✅ Ordinary care, including food and water, should normally be continued (CCC 2279).

**Concerns**
🟢 Where euthanasia is legal, eligibility has often expanded over time, and people with disabilities and the elderly report pressure to see themselves as burdens.

**Why it matters**
Palliative and hospice care show a better way: no one should die alone or in uncontrolled pain.
