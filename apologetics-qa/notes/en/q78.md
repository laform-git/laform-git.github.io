# Q78 · Does the Church ever permit divorce or separation? Does it ever dissolve a valid marriage?

**Short answer**
Separation is permitted for serious reasons, and a civil divorce may be tolerated for legal protection, without either spouse being free to remarry. A valid sacramental and consummated marriage is never dissolved. A few non-sacramental or unconsummated marriages can be dissolved in specific cases.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Separation can be legitimate, for example in cases of abuse or danger (CCC 1649, 2383).
- ✅ "A ratified and consummated marriage cannot be dissolved by any human power or for any reason other than death" (canon 1141; CCC 2382).

**Specific cases**
- 🟢 Pauline privilege: a marriage between two unbaptized persons may be dissolved when one is baptized and the other departs (1 Cor 7:15; canons 1143–1147).
- 🟢 A marriage never consummated can be dissolved by the Pope for a just reason (canon 1142).
- 🟢 The "Petrine privilege" for some marriages involving a non-baptized person.

**Why it matters**
No one is required to stay in danger. The Church protects both persons and the bond.
