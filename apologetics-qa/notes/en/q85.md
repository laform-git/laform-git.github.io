# Q85 · Why does the Church presume to grant indulgences?

**Short answer**
An indulgence is not forgiveness of sin, and it is not for sale. It is the remission of temporal punishment for sins already forgiven, granted by the Church using the "keys" Christ gave her, from the treasury of Christ's and the saints' merits.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Sin has two consequences: eternal punishment (removed by forgiveness) and "temporal" punishment, the disorder and attachment that remain (CCC 1472–1473).
- ✅ The Church, by Christ's authority to "bind and loose" (Mt 16:19), can apply the merits of Christ and the saints (CCC 1471, 1478).
- ✅ Indulgences can be applied to oneself or to the dead (CCC 1479).

**History**
🟢 In the late Middle Ages some preachers sold indulgences, a real abuse that helped spark the Reformation. The Council of Trent banned the sale of indulgences in 1562.

**Why it matters**
Indulgences remind us that sin has effects, that the Church is one family who help each other, and that God's mercy is generous.
