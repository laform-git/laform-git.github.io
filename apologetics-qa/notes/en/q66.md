# Q66 · Is Jesus really present in the Eucharist? How do we know "This is my body" is literal, when Jesus also called Himself a "gate" and a "vine"?

**Short answer**
Yes. Jesus is present "body, blood, soul and divinity" under the appearances of bread and wine. When Jesus called Himself a gate or vine, no one was confused and no one left. When He said to eat His flesh, many walked away and He let them.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ The whole Christ is "truly, really and substantially contained" in the Eucharist (CCC 1374, Council of Trent).
- ✅ By consecration the substance of bread and wine becomes Christ's Body and Blood: transubstantiation (CCC 1376).

**Why literal**
- 🟢 Context: "gate" and "vine" are clear images in their passages; John 6 insists, repeats and loses followers.
- 🟢 Paul: eating unworthily makes one "guilty of the body and blood" (1 Cor 11:27); one cannot be guilty against a symbol.
- 🟢 The early Church believed it: Ignatius of Antioch (c. 107) wrote that the Eucharist "is the flesh of our Savior Jesus Christ".

**Why it matters**
Holy Communion is meeting the living Lord. That is why Catholics kneel, fast before Communion and keep the tabernacle lamp lit.
