# Q73 · What's wrong with same-sex marriage? If two people love each other, shouldn't they be able to marry? How does it hurt anyone?

**Short answer**
The Church teaches that marriage is not only love between two people, but a specific union of a man and a woman, ordered to their mutual good and to children. Same-sex couples can truly love each other as persons, but their union is not a marriage in this sense. The Church also insists that persons with same-sex attraction be treated with respect, compassion and sensitivity.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Marriage was established by the Creator between a man and a woman (CCC 1601–1605; Gen 2:24; Mt 19:4–6).
- ✅ Homosexual acts cannot be approved; the inclination itself is not sinful (CCC 2357–2358).
- ✅ "Every sign of unjust discrimination in their regard should be avoided" (CCC 2358).

**Common arguments**
- 🟢 Love is necessary for marriage but does not define it; many loving relationships (friends, siblings) are not marriages.
- 🟡 On public law, Catholics argue that the state recognizes marriage because of its link to children; others disagree, and this remains a contested civic debate.

**Why it matters**
Catholics are asked to hold both truth and charity: clear teaching and real friendship.
