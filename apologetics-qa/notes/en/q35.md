# Q35 · Where are cardinals and papal elections in the Bible?

**Short answer**
They are not in the Bible, and they don't need to be. Cardinals and conclaves are Church practices that developed to choose Peter's successor in an orderly way. What is from Christ is the office; how the person is chosen is a matter of Church discipline.

**History**
- 🟢 Early on, the bishop of Rome was chosen by the Roman clergy and people. "Cardinals" were originally the chief clergy of Rome's churches.
- 🟢 In 1059, Pope Nicholas II reserved the election to the cardinals, to protect it from emperors and noble families.
- 🟢 The rules today are set by papal law (*Universi Dominici Gregis*, 1996, later amended).

**Biblical roots**
🟢 The apostles chose Matthias to replace Judas, with prayer and casting lots (Acts 1:15–26): an orderly way of filling an apostolic office.

**Why it matters**
Not everything in Church life must be spelled out in Scripture. The Church, guided by the Spirit, organizes how to carry out what Christ instituted.
