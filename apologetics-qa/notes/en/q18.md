# Q18 · Is the Bible historically accurate? Should it be read literally or figuratively?

**Short answer**
Both, depending on the passage. Each book must be read according to its literary form: history as history, poetry as poetry, parable as parable. The Bible is true in what it intends to teach.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ To understand Scripture, we must look for what the human authors truly meant, paying attention to "literary genres" (CCC 109–110).
- ✅ The literal sense is what the words actually mean; spiritual senses build on it (CCC 115–117).
- ✅ The Gospels have real historical character; they faithfully hand on what Jesus did and taught (CCC 126).

**Examples**
- 🟢 The Psalms are poetry; "the trees clap their hands" is not a botanical claim.
- 🟢 Genesis 1–3 uses figurative language to teach real truths: God created everything, creation is good, humans fell (CCC 337, 390).
- 🟢 Archaeology has confirmed many places, rulers and customs named in Scripture; some details remain debated 🟡.

**Why it matters**
Reading every line the same way leads either to wooden literalism or to dismissing the Bible. The Church asks for intelligent reading in faith.

**For class**
Pick a verse that is clearly poetic and one that is clearly historical. What clues tell you which is which?
