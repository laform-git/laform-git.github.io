# Q125 · The unborn fetus is not a person. How can it be a person if it cannot feel or survive on its own?

**Short answer**
From conception, the embryo is a living, distinct, whole human organism, directing its own development. What it lacks is size, development and independence, but none of those decide whether someone is a human being with rights.

**What the Church teaches**
✅ "The human being is to be respected and treated as a person from the moment of conception" (CCC 2270; *Donum Vitae* I,1).

**The "SLED" test**
The differences between an unborn child and us are:
- 🟢 **Size:** toddlers are smaller than teens; size doesn't decide worth.
- 🟢 **Level of development:** newborns can't reason yet either.
- 🟢 **Environment:** moving eight inches down the birth canal doesn't change who you are.
- 🟢 **Degree of dependency:** people on insulin or ventilators are still persons.

**About feeling**
🟡 When the fetus can feel pain is debated, but a person's right to life doesn't depend on feeling pain; a person under anesthesia keeps their rights.
