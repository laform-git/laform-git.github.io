# Q104 · Why did the Church persecute Jews?

**Short answer**
Many Christians, including some Church leaders, mistreated Jews over centuries, through accusations, restrictions, expulsions and violence. This contradicted the Gospel. The Church has firmly rejected the idea that Jews are collectively guilty of Jesus' death and has asked forgiveness.

**History**
- 🟢 Medieval laws required distinctive dress (Fourth Lateran Council, 1215); papal Rome confined Jews to a ghetto from 1555.
- 🟢 Popes also at times protected Jews: several condemned the "blood libel" and forced baptisms.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Jews are not collectively responsible for Jesus' death; all sinners share responsibility (CCC 597–598; *Nostra Aetate* 4, 1965).
- ✅ God's covenant with the Jewish people "has never been revoked" (CCC 121; Rom 11:29).
- ✅ The Church "deplores" antisemitism (*Nostra Aetate* 4). In 1998 it published *We Remember: A Reflection on the Shoah*, and in 2000 John Paul II prayed at the Western Wall asking forgiveness.

**Why it matters**
Jesus, Mary and the apostles were Jews. Christians call the Jewish people their "elder brothers" in faith.
