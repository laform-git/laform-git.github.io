# Q75 · How are annulments different from divorce?

**Short answer**
A divorce claims to end a real marriage. An annulment, properly a "declaration of nullity", is the Church's finding, after investigation, that a valid marriage never existed from the start, because something essential was missing.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ The Church can declare a marriage null when, for example, consent was not free or another essential requirement was lacking (CCC 1629).
- ✅ After a declaration of nullity, both parties are free to marry (CCC 1629).

**Examples of grounds**
🟢 Force or grave fear; deception about something essential; serious psychological incapacity to assume marriage's obligations; exclusion of children or permanence; lack of proper form.

**Why it matters**
An annulment is not "Catholic divorce". It recognizes a truth about the past; it cannot dissolve a real bond.
