# Q101 · Did the Church persecute Galileo for not taking the Bible literally?

**Short answer**
The Galileo affair was a real mistake by Church authorities, but the popular version is exaggerated. Galileo was not tortured or executed, and the case mixed science, Scripture interpretation, politics and personalities.

**What happened**
- 🟢 1616: the Holy Office declared heliocentrism contrary to Scripture; Galileo was told not to defend it as proven.
- 🟢 1632: his *Dialogue* appeared to mock Pope Urban VIII, a former supporter.
- 🟢 1633: he was tried, forced to recant, and placed under house arrest in his villa, where he kept working until his death in 1642.
- 🟡 The science was not settled then: Galileo's proofs (like tides) were wrong, and the decisive evidence came later.

**What the Church said later**
✅ In 1992 Saint John Paul II acknowledged the theologians' errors and said the case showed a "tragic mutual incomprehension".

**Why it matters**
One case does not show the Church was against science (see Q99). It does show leaders can err in matters outside defined faith.
