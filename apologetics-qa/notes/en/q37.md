# Q37 · Why did some Church Fathers seem to teach sola scriptura?

**Short answer**
The Fathers praised Scripture as the supreme written word of God and the test of doctrine. But they always read it within the Church's living Tradition and under the bishops' authority. That is not *sola scriptura* in the Protestant sense.

**What they held**
- 🟢 Athanasius and Cyril of Jerusalem said doctrine must be shown from Scripture. Yet both defended the Creed of Nicaea, the authority of councils and the Church's interpretation.
- 🟢 Irenaeus refuted heretics both from Scripture and from the apostolic tradition kept in churches with successive bishops.
- 🟢 Augustine wrote: "I would not believe the Gospel unless moved by the authority of the Catholic Church."
- 🟢 Basil the Great defended unwritten apostolic traditions, such as the sign of the cross and prayer facing east.

**What the Church teaches**
✅ Scripture and Tradition "flow from the same divine wellspring" and are interpreted by the Magisterium (CCC 80–85).

**Why it matters**
Quoting a Father in isolation can mislead. Read in full, the Fathers show Scripture and Church working together.
