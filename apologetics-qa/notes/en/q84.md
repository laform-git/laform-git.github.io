# Q84 · Where is Purgatory in the Bible, and why do Catholics pray for the dead?

**Short answer**
Purgatory is the final purification of those who die in God's friendship but are not yet fully holy. The word is not in the Bible, like "Trinity", but the reality is: Scripture shows prayer for the dead and a cleansing "as through fire".

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ Those who die in grace but imperfectly purified undergo purification to reach the holiness needed for heaven (CCC 1030–1031).
- ✅ From the beginning the Church has prayed and offered the Eucharist for the dead (CCC 1032).

**In Scripture**
- 🟢 Judas Maccabeus "made atonement for the dead, that they might be delivered from their sin" (2 Macc 12:43–46).
- 🟢 A person's work is tested by fire; "he will be saved, but only as through fire" (1 Cor 3:13–15).
- 🟢 Some sins are forgiven "in the age to come" (Mt 12:32).
- 🟢 "Nothing unclean shall enter" heaven (Rev 21:27).

**Why it matters**
Love does not end at the grave. Praying for the dead is a work of mercy.
