# Q108 · Why does the Church distinguish between mortal and venial sin? What is the difference?

**Short answer**
Not all sins are equally serious. A mortal sin is a grave choice against God that destroys charity in the heart; a venial sin wounds charity but does not destroy it. Scripture itself makes the distinction.

**What the Church teaches**
- ✅ A sin is mortal when three conditions are met: grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent (CCC 1857–1859).
- ✅ Mortal sin cuts us off from God's grace; unrepented, it leads to separation from God. It is forgiven in confession (CCC 1861, 1856).
- ✅ Venial sin weakens charity and should be fought, but it does not break our friendship with God (CCC 1862–1863).

**In Scripture**
🟢 "There is sin which is mortal… All wrongdoing is sin, but there is sin which is not mortal" (1 Jn 5:16–17).

**Why it matters**
The distinction protects us from two errors: thinking nothing is serious, and thinking every slip ends our friendship with God.
