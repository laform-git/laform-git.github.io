# Q30 · Was St. Paul a misogynist? He seems to belittle women in several of his writings.

**Short answer**
No. Paul wrote within his culture, but he also taught that "there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (Gal 3:28), and he worked closely with women leaders.

**What the record shows**
- 🟢 Paul names women as co-workers: Phoebe, a deacon (Rom 16:1), Prisca, who taught Apollos (Acts 18:26), Junia, "outstanding among the apostles" (Rom 16:7).
- ✅ In Ephesians 5, wives are asked to submit, but first all are told to "be subject to one another" (5:21), and husbands must love their wives "as Christ loved the Church and gave Himself up for her" (5:25), a demand more radical for men of his time.
- ✅ Saint John Paul II taught that the submission in Ephesians must be understood as mutual (*Mulieris Dignitatem* 24).

**Hard verses**
🟡 Passages like 1 Cor 14:34 ("women should keep silent") are debated: they likely address specific disorders in worship, and the same letter assumes women pray and prophesy (1 Cor 11:5).

**Why it matters**
Christianity raised the dignity of women in the ancient world, and Paul helped lead that change.

**For class**
Read Ephesians 5:21–33 aloud. Who is asked to sacrifice more: the husband or the wife?
