# Carbohydrates — Master Note

*What carbohydrates are, how chain length decides how fast they hit your blood, and why fructose — not glucose — is the sugar most tied to liver fat and ApoB.*

## Why This Matters

Refined carbs and liquid sugar are the main dietary drivers of insulin resistance and of a high-ApoB, low-HDL lipid pattern. Knowing the carb families lets you read a label or a plate and predict its metabolic effect, instead of memorizing "good" and "bad" foods.

## 1 · What a Carbohydrate Is

- Built from carbon, hydrogen and oxygen — literally "hydrated carbon." ✅
- The building block is a **saccharide** (sugar unit). Carbs are classified by **how many units are linked**. ✅
- Gut enzymes can only absorb **single units**. Everything longer must be cut down first — and how easy that cutting is decides speed. ✅

## 2 · Simple Carbohydrates

![](media/carbs_1_simple_sugars.png)

### Monosaccharides — one unit

| Sugar | Where | Key fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glucose | Blood, starch breakdown | The body's universal fuel; triggers insulin ✅ |
| Fructose | Fruit, honey, sugar, HFCS | 5-sided ring; handled by gut wall + liver, no insulin needed ✅ |
| Galactose | Milk (inside lactose) | Converted to glucose in the liver ✅ |

### Disaccharides — two units joined

| Sugar | = | Enzyme that splits it | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sucrose | glucose + fructose | sucrase | Table sugar, cane, beet ✅ |
| Lactose | glucose + galactose | lactase | Milk ✅ |
| Maltose | glucose + glucose | maltase | Malted grains, starch breakdown ✅ |

- **Lactose intolerance** = lactase production drops after childhood in most adults worldwide. Undigested lactose reaches the colon, bacteria ferment it → gas, bloating. ✅
- **High-fructose corn syrup** (HFCS) is free glucose + free fructose (~45/55), no bond to split — metabolically close to sucrose. ✅

## 3 · Complex Carbohydrates

### Oligosaccharides (3–10 units)

- Found in beans, onions, garlic, leeks, chicory. Mostly **not digested** by us — fermented by colon bacteria (the "beans → gas" link). Many act as **prebiotics**. ✅

### Starch (hundreds to thousands of glucose units)

- Plants' energy storage. Two forms: **amylose** (straight chain, digests slower) and **amylopectin** (branched, many ends for enzymes to attack → digests faster). ✅
- **Resistant starch** escapes small-intestine digestion and feeds colon bacteria. Cooking then cooling potato, rice or pasta increases it (retrogradation). ✅ Mechanism solid; size of glucose benefit modest. 🟡

### Glycogen — your own stored carbs

- Animals' version of starch, highly branched. Stored in **liver** (refills blood glucose between meals) and **muscle** (fuels muscle only). ✅
- When glycogen is full, extra carbs are more likely to be turned into fat. ✅

### Fiber — chains we cannot cut

| Type | What it does | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soluble / viscous | Forms gel, slows sugar absorption, binds bile acids → lowers LDL | Oats, psyllium, legumes, chia ✅ |
| Insoluble | Bulk, transit speed | Bran, vegetable skins ✅ |
| Fermentable | Bacteria turn it into short-chain fatty acids (butyrate) | Many plants, resistant starch ✅ |

## 4 · Digestion — Where Each Gets Cut

1. **Mouth:** salivary amylase starts on starch. ✅
2. **Stomach:** acid halts amylase; little carb digestion. ✅
3. **Small intestine:** pancreatic amylase → maltose; wall enzymes (sucrase, lactase, maltase) → single units → absorbed. ✅
4. **Colon:** fiber, resistant starch, oligosaccharides, leftover lactose → fermented by bacteria. ✅

## 5 · Glucose vs Fructose — The Key Split

![](media/carbs_3_glucose_vs_fructose.png)

- **Glucose:** raises blood sugar, triggers insulin, used everywhere, excess stored as glycogen. ✅
- **Fructose:** cleared by the small-intestine wall at low doses; larger or faster doses spill to the **liver**, which converts the excess into fat (**de novo lipogenesis**, DNL). ✅
- Liver fat → more **VLDL** particles exported → higher triglycerides and **ApoB**; over time, worse liver insulin sensitivity. ✅ Mechanism well supported; strongest in overfeeding studies. 🟡 for normal-intake effect size.
- **Dose and speed matter.** Whole fruit = small, slow, fiber-wrapped dose. Soda, juice, sweetened coffee = large, fast dose that overwhelms the gut's buffer. ✅

## 6 · Speed — Glycemic Index and Load

- **Glycemic index (GI):** how fast a food raises blood glucose vs pure glucose (100). ✅
- **Glycemic load (GL):** GI × grams of carb per serving — the real-world number. Watermelon: high GI, low GL. ✅
- Lowers the spike: fiber, protein, fat, acid, eating carbs **last** in a meal, cooled starches, a walk after eating. ✅ / 🟡 (food-order and walk effects are consistent but modest)
- Fructose has a **low GI** precisely because it doesn't raise blood glucose — low GI does not mean metabolically harmless. ✅

## 7 · Insulin Resistance Link

- Repeated large glucose loads → repeated insulin peaks → cells respond less over time. ✅
- Fructose-driven liver fat adds **hepatic** insulin resistance: the liver keeps making glucose and fat even when insulin says stop. ✅
- Result: higher fasting glucose/HbA1c, high triglycerides, low HDL, small dense LDL ✅

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward

- 🔴 "Sugar is sugar" → glucose and fructose take different routes; fructose drives liver fat.
- 🔴 "Low GI = healthy" → fructose and some processed foods are low GI and still harmful in quantity.
- 🔴 "Fruit is as bad as soda" → same sugar, different dose, speed and fiber.
- 🔴 "Complex carbs are all slow" → white bread and white rice are complex (starch) but digest fast.
- 🔴 "Fiber is just roughage" → soluble fiber lowers LDL; fermentable fiber feeds the microbiome.

## Questions to Learn From

**1. Why does fruit juice raise liver fat more than the whole fruit it came from?**

A. Juice contains a different kind of sugar

B. \* Juice delivers fructose fast and without fiber, overwhelming the gut's buffer so more reaches the liver

C. Whole fruit has no fructose

**2. White rice is a complex carbohydrate. Why does it still spike blood sugar quickly?**

A. \* Its starch is mostly highly branched and unprotected by fiber, so amylase cuts it to glucose fast

B. It contains sucrose

C. Complex carbs never spike blood sugar

**3. Someone gets bloated from milk but not from hard cheese. Why?**

A. Cheese has more lactase

B. \* Most lactose is removed with the whey or fermented away during cheese making

C. Cheese has no galactose bond
