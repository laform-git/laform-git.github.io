# Insulin Resistance

## What Insulin Is
After you eat, sugar (glucose) fills your blood. Your body wants that sugar *inside* cells where it gets burned for energy. **Insulin is the key** that unlocks your cells so glucose can enter.
- Produced by the pancreas in response to rising blood sugar
- Without insulin working, glucose stays trapped in the bloodstream
- ✅ Rock solid: textbook mechanism, zero dispute

---
## What Insulin Resistance Means
Over time, cells stop responding well to insulin. The lock stops working properly.
- Glucose can't enter cells easily, so it piles up in the blood
- Body's fix: produce *more* insulin to force the doors open
- This compensates for years — blood sugar can look normal while resistance is already serious
- **Fasting insulin is an earlier warning signal than HbA1c or fasting glucose**
- 🟢 Strong evidence: fasting insulin as earlier marker than standard blood sugar tests

### The Sneaky Timeline
- Resistance begins → Insulin rises → Blood sugar stays normal (hidden phase)
- Years pass → Insulin can no longer compensate → Blood sugar finally rises
- Prediabetes shows on tests — but resistance started much earlier

---
## Why Cells Become Resistant — 3 Mechanisms
### 1. The Cell Is Already Full
- Cells overfull with fuel deliberately downregulate insulin response — self-protection, not a defect
- 🟢 Strong evidence: the cellular overflow model

### 2. Fat in the Wrong Place (The Most Important One)
- Fat cells overfill → fat leaks into muscle and liver cells
- That misplaced fat produces two damaging molecules:
    - **DAG (diacylglycerol)** → activates PKCθ/ε → jams the insulin signal at IRS-1
    - **Ceramide** → activates PP2A → strips Akt of its activation tags
- These break the insulin signaling chain at its two most critical relay points
- 🟢 Strong evidence: DAG-PKC and ceramide pathways

### 3. Too Much Insulin, Too Often
- Constant high insulin wears out the cell's response
- Creates a vicious cycle: more insulin → cells listen less → body makes even more insulin
- 🟢 Strong evidence for the cycle itself

### Where Experts Disagree
- **Bikman:** Mechanism 3 (hyperinsulinemia) is the primary driver → 🔴 Advocacy
- **Mainstream:** Mechanisms 1 & 2 (energy excess, ectopic fat) dominate → 🟡 Contested
- **Most balanced:** All three interact → 🟢 Strong evidence

## Why It Matters Beyond Blood Sugar
Chronically high insulin: stores fat, drives liver triglyceride production, wrecks cholesterol — all simultaneously.

## Key Correction
> "Blood sugar tests catch insulin resistance early" — **FALSE**
> Blood sugar can stay normal for years while resistance is already significant. Fasting insulin and TG/HDL ratio are far earlier signals.

---
## Practical Takeaway
If fasting insulin is elevated, insulin resistance is already present — even if every blood sugar number looks normal.

---
## Measuring It: HOMA-IR (Worked Example)
Formula: (fasting glucose mg/dL × fasting insulin uIU/mL) / 405
- **Under 1.0:** optimal — Bikman's tighter target. 🔴 Advocacy-tier target, not a diagnostic consensus cutoff.
- **1.0–1.9:** mild, by looser clinical convention. 🟡 Contested — cutoffs vary by lab and study.
- **≥1.9–2.5+:** significant resistance by most commonly cited thresholds. 🟡 Contested — no single universal cutoff exists.

**Limitation:** one fasting draw is a snapshot, not a trend — real day-to-day noise exists.

## Systemic Reach — Organ by Organ
Insulin resistance is not a blood-sugar-only condition; chronically elevated insulin signals reach essentially every tissue with an insulin receptor.
- **Liver:** drives de novo lipogenesis → fatty liver, feeds back into worse hepatic insulin resistance. 🟢 Strong evidence. See [[Sugar vs. Cholesterol Number — Why Insulin Resistance Drives Heart Disease Risk]].
- **Fat tissue:** promotes storage, disrupts the leptin/adiponectin signaling balance. 🟢 Strong evidence.
- **Heart & vessels:** elevated insulin promotes vascular inflammation and plaque formation independent of LDL level alone. 🟢 Strong evidence.
- **Kidneys:** insulin drives sodium reabsorption, raising blood pressure and long-term kidney strain. 🟢 Strong evidence (mechanism below).
- **Skin:** acanthosis nigricans (dark, velvety skin patches, usually neck/armpits) is a visible marker of significantly elevated insulin. ✅ Rock solid, well-documented clinical sign.
- **Reproductive/hormonal:** in women, insulin resistance impairs aromatase activity, disrupting ovulation (PCOS mechanism). In men, the pathway is indirect — elevated insulin and adiposity lower SHBG (sex hormone binding globulin) and increase aromatization of testosterone to estradiol in fat tissue; this is a fat-tissue and SHBG effect, not a direct pancreatic or testicular one. 🟢 Strong evidence, corrected framing (see Key Corrections below).

---
## Insulin Resistance and the Brain
- Insulin resistance impairs glucose uptake in brain tissue, reducing available cellular energy in affected regions. 🟢 Strong evidence for the glucose-uptake mechanism itself.
- The label **"type 3 diabetes"** for Alzheimer's disease is a researcher-coined framing term describing this overlap — it is not a formal diagnostic category and the causal strength (mechanism vs. correlation vs. shared risk factor) remains actively debated. 🟡 Contested — treat the mechanism as plausible and evidence-supported, the "type 3 diabetes" label itself as shorthand, not settled fact.
- Normal age-related slowing (word-finding, processing speed) is expected and distinct from this pathway — this mechanism describes an added risk factor, not a claim that all memory change is insulin-driven.

---
## Blood Pressure: Two Additive Levers, Not Competing Ones
- **Sodium/fluid mechanism:** elevated insulin directly increases kidney sodium reabsorption, raising blood volume. 🟢 Strong evidence.
- **Vascular stiffness mechanism:** chronic hyperinsulinemia promotes vascular smooth muscle changes that reduce vessel flexibility. 🟢 Strong evidence.
- **Correction:** dietary salt and insulin resistance are not alternative explanations for the same blood pressure — both raise blood volume through sodium retention via different pathways, and their effects are additive. Someone can have normal salt intake and still show insulin-driven sodium retention, or have both mechanisms compounding.

---
## Fatty Liver: The Progression Ladder
Simple fat accumulation (steatosis) → NASH (steatohepatitis: inflammation + active cell damage) → fibrosis (scarring) → cirrhosis (functional liver failure). 🟢 Strong evidence for the sequence; 🟡 contested how reliably/quickly any one person progresses through it. Cross-link: [[Sugar vs. Cholesterol Number — Why Insulin Resistance Drives Heart Disease Risk]].

---
## Periodontal Disease — A Third Feed Into the Same Inflammatory Marker
Gum disease bacteria entering the bloodstream through inflamed gum tissue trigger vascular inflammation through a distinct pathway from insulin resistance — but both converge on the same downstream marker. 🟢 Strong evidence for the periodontal-cardiovascular inflammatory link.
