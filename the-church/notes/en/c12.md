# CHURCH-12 The Source - Christ, the Mass and the Seven Sacraments

> **Closing catechesis** · **Series:** THE CHURCH

---

## Where Everything Came From

Eleven chapters have asked one question: *why* did these people build schools, hospitals, universities and cathedrals, and why did they free slaves, ransom captives and die for strangers? Their answer was never "because we are good people." It was: **because Christ is alive, and we received his life.**

This chapter explains that answer: who Christ is, how his life reaches people today, and how each sacrament fed the history you have read.

---

## Part 1 · The Life of Christ in Brief

- ✅ **God became man.** "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (Jn 1:14). The eternal Son of God took a human body and soul from the Virgin Mary (CCC 456–463). From that moment, the human body, the poor, the sick and the child became holy ground.
- ✅ **He lived among us.** Thirty years of ordinary work in Nazareth, then three years of preaching, healing, forgiving sinners, feeding crowds and choosing twelve apostles.
- ✅ **He gave himself for us.** At the Last Supper he took bread and said, "This is my body which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me" (Lk 22:19). The next day he died on the Cross for the sins of the world (CCC 613–617).
- ✅ **He rose from the dead** on the third day and appeared to many witnesses (1 Cor 15:3–8).
- ✅ **He ascended and sent the Spirit.** At Pentecost the Holy Spirit came upon the apostles and the Church was born (Acts 2).

🟢 Christ did not leave only memories and teachings. He left a **Body**, the Church (1 Cor 12:27), and he acts through it. "What was visible in our Savior has passed over into his mysteries" (St. Leo the Great, quoted in CCC 1115).

---

## Part 2 · What a Sacrament Is

✅ A sacrament is "an efficacious sign of grace, instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church, by which divine life is dispensed to us" (CCC 1131). In plain words:

- **A sign** you can see, touch and hear: water, oil, bread, words.
- **That really does what it signifies:** it does not just remind you of grace; it gives it (CCC 1127–1128).
- **Because Christ himself acts in it.** When the priest baptizes, it is Christ who baptizes (CCC 1088, 1127).

🟢 The fruit depends on how we receive it: the sacraments are not magic, and they bear fruit in those who receive them with faith and openness (CCC 1128).

✅ Three sacraments leave an **indelible character** on the soul and are never repeated: **Baptism, Confirmation and Holy Orders** (CCC 1121; Council of Trent, Session 7, canon 9).

---

## Part 3 · The Mass at the Center

✅ "The Eucharist is 'the source and summit of the Christian life'" (CCC 1324; *Lumen Gentium* 11). Every other sacrament is ordered to it.

- **It is a sacrifice.** The Mass makes present, not repeats, the one sacrifice of the Cross (CCC 1366–1367; Trent, Session 22).
- **It is Christ himself.** ✅ After the consecration, the bread and wine become the Body, Blood, soul and divinity of Christ, truly, really and substantially present (CCC 1374, 1376; Trent, Session 13). "He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life" (Jn 6:54).
- **It makes the Church.** Those who share one bread become one body (1 Cor 10:17; CCC 1396).
- **It sends us out.** "The Eucharist commits us to the poor" (CCC 1397).

**In history:** the Mass fed the charity of **CHURCH-05** and inspired the beauty of **CHURCH-10**. Justin Martyr's Sunday Mass in 155 already ended with a collection for orphans, widows, the sick and prisoners.

## Part 4 · The Seven Sacraments and What They Built

### 1. Baptism · *the door*

✅ Forgives all sin, makes us new creatures and **adopted children of God**, members of Christ and his Church, and seals us with an indelible mark (CCC 1262–1274). We were already made in God's image (Gen 1:27); Baptism raises that image to the dignity of a child of the Father. It is the gateway to every other sacrament (CCC 1213).
**In history → CHURCH-06:** if a slave can be a child of God, slavery cannot stand. Gregory of Nyssa, Montesinos, Las Casas and Peter Claver all argued from this.

### 2. Confirmation · *the strength*

✅ Completes Baptism with the gift of the Holy Spirit, seals with an indelible character, and gives strength to witness to Christ by word and action (CCC 1285, 1303–1304).
**In history → CHURCH-09:** missionaries and martyrs, from Polycarp to Kolbe and Romero.

### 3. The Eucharist · *the summit*

See Part 3. **In history → CHURCH-05, CHURCH-10.**

### 4. Penance (Confession) · *the second chance*

✅ Christ gave the apostles power to forgive sins (Jn 20:22–23). Through the priest's absolution, God forgives sins committed after Baptism and reconciles us with him and the Church (CCC 1440–1449, 1468).
**In history → CHURCH-07:** the Peace of God, the penitentiary that reforms, the conviction that no one is beyond redemption.

### 5. Anointing of the Sick · *Christ in suffering*

✅ Strengthens, consoles and forgives the seriously ill and the dying, unites their suffering to Christ's, and sometimes restores health if God wills (Jas 5:14–15; CCC 1520–1523).
**In history → CHURCH-04:** the Basileias, the Hôtel-Dieu, Damien of Molokai, and 5,000 Catholic hospitals today.

### 6. Holy Orders · *Christ the teacher and shepherd*

✅ Bishops, priests and deacons are configured to Christ to act *in persona Christi*, in the person of Christ the Head, to teach, sanctify and govern (CCC 1536, 1548, 1581–1584). It leaves an indelible character.
**In history → CHURCH-01, CHURCH-02:** the monk-scribes and the clergy who founded the cathedral schools and universities. (The consecrated life of monks and nuns, though not itself a sacrament, grows from the same total gift to God.)

### 7. Matrimony · *Christ in the home*

✅ A man and a woman give themselves to each other for life by free consent, and Christ gives them grace to love as he loves the Church (Eph 5:25–32; CCC 1638–1642).
**In history → CHURCH-08:** consent, the dignity of women, the stable family.

## Honest Footnote

- **The sacraments do not make Catholics automatically good.** CHURCH-11 proves it. Grace is offered; it must be received and cooperated with (CCC 1128, 2001–2002).
- **Other Christians** (Orthodox, Protestants) also built hospitals, schools and charities. The Orthodox share the seven sacraments; most Protestants recognize two. Christ's grace is not limited to the Catholic Church's visible boundaries (CCC 819, 838), though the Church holds that the fullness of the means of salvation subsists in her (*Lumen Gentium* 8).
- **History cannot prove the faith.** It can show the fruits. The faith itself is a gift received freely (CCC 153–155).

## For You: How to Begin

Every chapter in this series ended with a question. Here is where the answers lead.

- **If you are not baptized:** contact a Catholic parish and ask about **OCIA** (Order of Christian Initiation of Adults). You will be welcomed, taught, and, when ready, receive Baptism, Confirmation and the Eucharist together, usually at the Easter Vigil.
- **If you are baptized Catholic but far away:** go to **Confession**. Tell the priest how long it has been. That is the whole entrance requirement. Then go to Mass.
- **If you are a practicing Catholic:** you already hold the source of everything in this series. The question is whether you let it send you out.

The monks, nurses, teachers and martyrs of the last 2,000 years were not a different kind of human being. They received the same sacraments offered to you this Sunday.

## Closing Reflection · From the Cross to Today

### The Most Tragic Story Ever Told

No one could write a more tragic story, because it gathers into one death everything people fear most.

Crucifixion was the cruellest death Rome had. It was reserved for slaves and rebels, a slow death from exhaustion, exposure and, most likely, suffocation.

And look at everything that came with it:

- He knew it was coming.
- A close friend betrayed him.
- The crowd that had cheered him turned against him.
- A governor who asked "What is truth?" gave him up to keep the peace.
- They chose a known criminal over an innocent man.
- He was young, and all he had done was heal and teach.

Yet he was not only a victim. "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord" (Jn 10:18). He walked into the worst thing in the world on purpose, out of love.

At the foot of the Cross stood his mother. Her courage was not in holding him back. It was in saying yes, and staying.

When we pray the Stations of the Cross, we find ourselves in the story. We are Pilate, going along with the crowd. We are the crowd. We are Peter, who denied him, and the thief, who asked to be remembered. The Cross shows us who we are.

But the story does not end there. Look at the worst, and keep looking, and on the third day the tomb is empty.

### One Tree

Everything in this series grew from there.

In the beginning there was a tree in a garden. A man reached for its fruit to make himself a god, and death came into the world.

On a hill outside Jerusalem there was another tree: rough wood, nails, a dying man. Here God reached down to become a man, and the fruit that hung on that tree was life.

Nobody looking at it that Friday would have seen a seed, only a corpse and a failure. The friends had run, the cause was finished, and the soldiers were throwing dice for his clothes. But a seed looks like death too, buried in the dark, until it breaks the ground.

From that one tree the branches spread.

A monk copies a book by candlelight while an empire burns, so a later age will remember how to read. A nun washes the sores of a leper nobody will touch, because she sees in his face the face on the Cross. A teacher opens a school in a village that has never had one. A slave and his master kneel at the same altar, and for that moment neither is above the other. A missionary walks into a country that will kill him, and walks anyway. A builder sets stone on stone for two hundred years, for a cathedral he will never see finished, so that the poorest woman in the city can stand inside light.

Hospitals, universities, orphanages, hospices. Chant, glass, marble and fresco. Martyrs in the arena and in the camps. Saints in palaces and in slums. They are two thousand years of fruit on one tree, still growing.

"Every sound tree bears good fruit" (Mt 7:17). That is the test this series began with. You have now seen the fruit.

At the end of the Bible the tree appears one last time, standing beside the river of the water of life, "and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations" (Rev 22:2). Every hospital was a leaf, and every act of mercy was a leaf.

The tree is still standing, and the fruit is still offered at every altar, every day, on every continent. It is the same Body that hung on the wood, given now as bread.

The last question of this series is not about history. It is about you. You have seen what grew from that tree. Will you stand under it as a spectator, admiring the branches? Or will you come close enough to taste the fruit?

### The Open Door

*In the style of the lament attributed to Lübeck Cathedral (attribution disputed).*

He brought us light, and we don't look for it.
He offers forgiveness, and we don't ask for it.
He gave us his Body, and we don't come to receive it.
He paid our debt, and we keep running up new ones.
He calls us by name, and we don't answer.
He opened the door, and we stand outside.
He died so we could live, and we choose to stay dead.

He has done everything.
The door is open.
If you are lost, it isn't his fault.
