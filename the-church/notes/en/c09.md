# CHURCH-09 Confirmation - Mission and Martyrs

> **Sacramental root:** Confirmation · **Series:** THE CHURCH

---

## The Scene

Auschwitz, the end of July 1941. A prisoner has escaped, and the commander picks ten men to be starved to death in reprisal. One of them, Franciszek Gajowniczek, cries out for his wife and children. A small prisoner steps out of line, which can itself mean death. "I am a Catholic priest," says Maximilian Kolbe. "I want to die for that man. He has a wife and children." The exchange is accepted. In the starvation cell Kolbe leads the others in prayers and hymns. After two weeks he is the last one still conscious, and on 14 August the guards kill him with an injection. Gajowniczek lived to be ninety-three, and he was in St. Peter's Square the day Kolbe was declared a saint. [Documented]

Where does a man find the strength to step out of that line? This chapter is about the answer.

---

## The Myth

"Missionaries were the religious arm of colonialism. They destroyed native cultures and forced people to convert."

---

## The Root

✅ Before he ascended, Christ promised: "you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria and to the end of the earth" (Acts 1:8). At Pentecost the Spirit came (Acts 2), and the apostles, who had hidden in fear, went out to preach and to die.

✅ **Confirmation** completes Baptism with that same gift of the Spirit. It seals the Christian with an indelible character and gives "special strength of the Holy Spirit to spread and defend the faith by word and action as true witnesses of Christ" (CCC 1303; also 1285, 1304). 🟢 The word *martyr* means *witness*.

---

## The Story

**1. The witness of blood.** [Documented]

- **St. Polycarp**, bishop of Smyrna, burned c. 155 at 86 years old, refused to curse Christ: "Eighty-six years I have served him, and he never did me any wrong. How can I blaspheme my King who saved me?" (*Martyrdom of Polycarp* 9).
- **Sts. Perpetua and Felicity**, a young noblewoman and her slave, died together in Carthage in 203, recorded partly in Perpetua's own prison diary.
- Tertullian wrote around 197 that "the blood of Christians is seed" (*Apology* 50).

**2. Missionaries who gave peoples their written languages.** [Consensus]

- **Sts. Cyril and Methodius** (9th c.) created the Glagolitic alphabet to bring the liturgy and Scripture to the Slavs in their own tongue; Cyrillic script, used by hundreds of millions today, grew from their school.
- Missionaries wrote the first grammars and dictionaries of many languages. Fray Alonso de Molina's Nahuatl dictionary (1555) and Domingo de Santo Tomás's Quechua grammar (1560) remain key sources for these languages.
- **St. Patrick** (5th c.) brought the faith to Ireland without a single martyr among his converts, and Irish monks later re-evangelized Europe (CHURCH-01).

**3. Meeting cultures with respect.** [Documented]

- **Matteo Ricci SJ** (d. 1610) learned classical Chinese, dressed as a Confucian scholar, translated Euclid into Chinese, and drew a world map (1602) that introduced China to the wider world.
- The **Jesuit Reductions** of Paraguay (17th–18th c.) gathered Guaraní communities into self-governing towns that protected them from slave raiders, kept their language, and developed music and crafts.

**4. Modern martyrs.** [Documented]

- **St. Maximilian Kolbe** volunteered to die in place of a stranger, a husband and father, at Auschwitz (1941).
- **St. Óscar Romero**, Archbishop of San Salvador, defended the poor against violence and was shot while celebrating Mass (24 March 1980). Canonized 2018.
- In the 20th century, Christians suffered under Nazi, Soviet and other regimes in enormous numbers; John Paul II called it the century of martyrs.

> *Root named:* Confirmation is the sacrament of courage. The same Spirit who turned frightened fishermen into apostles gave Kolbe and Romero the strength to give their lives for others.

## Honest Footnote

- **Mission was entangled with conquest.** Spanish and Portuguese missionaries traveled with conquerors, and colonial powers used religion to justify domination. [Consensus]
- **Forced and mass conversions** happened at times, against the Church's own teaching that faith must be free (Vatican II, *Dignitatis Humanae* 2, 10). [Documented]
- **Destruction of cultures.** In 1562 the Franciscan Diego de Landa burned Maya codices and religious objects in Yucatán; only a handful of codices survive. [Documented]
- **St. Junípero Serra** (California missions, 18th c.) is honored as a saint and as the founder of missions that became Los Angeles-area communities, and criticized by many Native Californians for the harsh mission system and the deaths from disease and forced labor. Both perspectives deserve a hearing. [Contested]
- **Residential schools** in Canada and elsewhere are treated in CHURCH-11.

## For You

Confirmation is not graduation from religion class. It is the Holy Spirit given as power: power to speak about Christ, to stand up for the weak, and, if it comes to that, to suffer for the truth. If you were confirmed, that strength is still in you. If you were not, it is still offered. Who in your life needs a witness right now?

**The world has plenty of opinions. It is starving for witnesses.**
