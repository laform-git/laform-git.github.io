# CHURCH-08 Matrimony - Marriage, Family and the Dignity of Women

> **Sacramental root:** Matrimony · **Series:** THE CHURCH

---

## The Myth

"The Church has always oppressed women and controlled people's private lives through marriage rules."

---

## The Root

✅ Christ restored marriage to God's original plan: "Have you not read that he who made them from the beginning made them male and female... What therefore God has joined together, let not man put asunder" (Mt 19:4–6). St. Paul calls marriage "a great mystery... Christ and the church" (Eph 5:32), and commands husbands: "love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (Eph 5:25).

✅ For baptized Christians, marriage is a **sacrament**: the spouses themselves give it to each other, and Christ gives them grace to love faithfully for life (CCC 1601, 1623, 1638–1642). 🟢 A sacrament made by **free consent** of a man and a woman, equal in dignity, changed how the West thought about women, choice and the family.

---

## The Story

**1. The Roman family.** [Consensus] Roman law gave the father (*paterfamilias*) great power over his household. Girls were often married very young, divorce was easy for men, infanticide of girls was common, and a husband's adultery was rarely punished.

**2. Christianity was attractive to women.** [Consensus, with debate on details] Sociologist Rodney Stark argues that early Christian communities had more women than men, partly because Christianity banned infanticide and abortion, condemned male adultery and divorce, and honored widows. Christian girls, he argues, married later and with more say (*The Rise of Christianity*, ch. 5). Some historians dispute his numbers but not the overall picture.

**3. Consent makes the marriage (12th c.).** [Consensus] Medieval canon law, developed under Pope Alexander III, held that a marriage is made by the **free consent** of the couple themselves, not by their parents, lords or a payment. A forced marriage was not a marriage. Historians (Charles Donahue, James Brundage) note that this gave young people, including women, a legal protection against arranged marriages that few other societies offered.

**4. A secular scholar's surprising finding.** [Contested, but influential] Harvard evolutionary biologist **Joseph Henrich**, writing with no religious agenda, argues in *The WEIRDest People in the World* (2020) that the Western Church's marriage rules (banning cousin marriage and polygamy, requiring consent, favoring independent households) broke up powerful clans and helped produce the individualism, trust and institutions of the modern West.

**5. Women who led.** [Documented]

- **Abbesses** ruled great monasteries and sometimes double houses of men and women: St. Hilda of Whitby hosted the Synod of Whitby (664).
- **St. Hildegard of Bingen** (d. 1179): abbess, composer, writer on medicine and natural science, adviser to popes and emperors, declared a **Doctor of the Church** in 2012.
- **St. Catherine of Siena** (d. 1380) persuaded the Pope to return from Avignon to Rome; Doctor of the Church (1970).
- **St. Teresa of Ávila** (d. 1582) reformed an entire religious order and wrote spiritual classics; the first woman named Doctor of the Church (1970).
- **Elena Cornaro Piscopia** became the first woman in the world to earn a doctorate, at the University of Padua (1678). **Laura Bassi** became a professor of physics at Bologna (1732) with the support of Pope Benedict XIV.
- For centuries, **women religious** ran much of the Church's schools and hospitals (CHURCH-02, CHURCH-04) as founders, administrators and professionals, long before most professions opened to women.

> *Root named:* the Church honors no human being more than a woman, Mary, the Mother of God. 🟡 Many historians and theologians see Marian devotion as shaping the medieval ideal of the dignity of women.

---

## Honest Footnote

- **Medieval law and theology also subordinated wives to husbands** in practice, and some canonists allowed "moderate correction" of a wife. The ideal of Ephesians 5 (sacrificial love) was often not the reality. [Consensus]
- **Women cannot be ordained priests.** The Church teaches this is not a judgment on women's dignity but fidelity to Christ's choice of the Twelve, and that she has no authority to change it (CCC 1577; John Paul II, *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis*, 1994). Many critics see it as discrimination. Readers should know both arguments.
- **Annulments** are often misunderstood as "Catholic divorce." The Church holds that an annulment declares a marriage never validly existed; critics argue the process is sometimes too easy, others that it is too slow and painful.
- **Abuse of women** in some Church-run institutions (Ireland's Magdalene laundries) is treated in CHURCH-11.

## For You

In Matrimony, Christ does not just bless a couple; he moves in. He gives grace for the hardest thing two people try: to love each other for life. Whether you are married, hoping to be, wounded by a broken marriage, or the child of one, this sacrament says love that lasts is possible, because it is held by someone stronger than the two of you.
