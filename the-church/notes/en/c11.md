# CHURCH-11 The Hard Questions

> **Spiritual root:** A holy Church made of sinners · **Series:** THE CHURCH

---

## Why This Chapter Exists

A record that hides its failures is not believable. This chapter faces the four charges every skeptic raises: the Crusades, the Inquisition, the abuse crisis, and the treatment of Jews and native peoples. For each: what actually happened, what is myth, and what the Church herself has said.

---

## The Root

✅ The Church is holy because Christ, her Head, is holy and gives her the sacraments; her members are sinners (CCC 823, 827). Vatican II says the Church, "embracing sinners in her bosom, is at the same time holy and always in need of being purified" (*Lumen Gentium* 8).

🟢 This is not an excuse. It is the reason the Church has a sacrament of Confession at all (CHURCH-07): sin is named, confessed, and repaired. The failures below are failures *against* the Gospel, judged by the Gospel.

---

## 1. The Crusades

**What happened.** [Consensus] In 1095 Pope Urban II called for an armed pilgrimage after Byzantine Emperor Alexius I asked for help against the Seljuk Turks, who had conquered most of Asia Minor after the battle of Manzikert (1071), and after reports of attacks on pilgrims. Crusades continued for two centuries.

**The myth.** That the Crusades were unprovoked colonial wars for land and loot. Historians such as Jonathan Riley-Smith (Cambridge) show most crusaders were motivated by penance and piety, often ruined themselves financially, and saw the wars as defensive. [Consensus]

**The failures.** [Documented]

- Crusading mobs massacred Jewish communities in the Rhineland (1096); some bishops tried to shelter them.
- The capture of Jerusalem (1099) ended in a massacre of its inhabitants.
- The Fourth Crusade sacked Christian Constantinople (1204). Pope Innocent III condemned it. In 2001 John Paul II asked forgiveness of the Orthodox for it.

---

## 2. The Inquisition

**What happened.** [Documented] Church tribunals investigated heresy from the 13th century. The **Spanish Inquisition** (1478–1834) was established at the request of the Spanish crown and largely controlled by it.

**The myth.** That it killed millions. Modern historians working from the tribunals' own records estimate the Spanish Inquisition tried roughly **125,000–150,000** people over three and a half centuries and executed roughly **3,000–5,000** (Henry Kamen; Gustav Henningsen and Jaime Contreras; Joseph Pérez). [Consensus on range] Most witch-hunt executions (some 40,000–60,000 across Europe) were carried out by secular courts, especially in German lands; the Spanish Inquisition was notably skeptical of witchcraft accusations (Henningsen, *The Witches' Advocate*). [Consensus]

**The failures.** [Documented] The tribunals used torture, punished people for their beliefs, and handed thousands over to be burned. Converted Jews and Muslims (*conversos*, *moriscos*) were special targets of suspicion. Smaller numbers do not make this right.

---

## 3. The Abuse Crisis

**What happened.** [Documented] Priests sexually abused children and vulnerable adults, and many bishops covered it up, moving abusers to new parishes. The U.S. John Jay College report (2004) counted about **10,667** allegations against **4,392** priests from 1950 to 2002, about 4% of priests serving in that period. Similar findings emerged in Ireland, Germany, France, Australia, Chile and elsewhere.

**No myth to correct here.** Abuse happens in every institution that works with children, but the Church claims to speak for Christ, and the cover-ups by those in authority made it worse. This is a grave sin and a grave crime.

**The response.** [Documented] Zero-tolerance policies, mandatory reporting, background checks and safe-environment training in many countries; Pope Francis's *Vos estis lux mundi* (2019, made permanent 2023) set rules for reporting abuse and holding bishops accountable. Critics argue it remains incomplete.

---

## 4. Jews and Native Peoples

**Jews.** [Documented] Christian teaching of contempt, forced sermons, ghettos and expulsions marked centuries of history. Vatican II's *Nostra Aetate* (1965) rejected collective Jewish guilt for Christ's death and condemned antisemitism. On the Day of Pardon (12 March 2000), John Paul II asked forgiveness for sins against the Jewish people and placed a prayer of repentance in the Western Wall.

**Residential schools.** [Documented] In Canada, Catholic religious orders operated a majority of the government-funded residential schools that took Indigenous children from their families; Canada's Truth and Reconciliation Commission (2015) documented abuse, cultural destruction and deaths. Pope Francis traveled to Canada in 2022 to apologize.

**Magdalene laundries.** [Documented] In Ireland, institutions run by religious orders (with state referrals) held thousands of women in harsh, unpaid labor from 1922 to 1996 (McAleese Report, 2013).

## Honest Footnote on This Chapter

- **The numbers are estimates.** Even careful historians disagree, especially on the Inquisition.
- **Comparison is not excuse.** Pointing out that others did worse can correct a myth, but it never justifies a single victim.
- **Readers should consult critics too**, not only Catholic historians. The sources below include both.

---

## For You

The Church's sins are real, and some of them were committed by people who should have been the most trustworthy. If you have been hurt by the Church, your anger is not unbelief; it is a sense of justice the Gospel itself gave you. Christ was betrayed by one of his own apostles and denied by the first pope. He did not abandon the Church. He came back and forgave Peter, and made him a shepherd again (Jn 21:15–17). The question is not whether Catholics have sinned. It is whether Christ is still present, still forgiving, and still worth following.
