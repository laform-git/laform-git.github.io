# CHURCH-02 Holy Orders - The School and the University

> **Sacramental root:** Holy Orders (the teaching office) · **Series:** THE CHURCH

---

## The Myth

"The Church wanted people ignorant so they would obey. Education spread only when secular states took it away from the clergy."

---

## The Root

🟢 Christ sent the apostles to "teach all nations" (Mt 28:19–20), and the Church has always counted teaching as part of her mission. The bishop and the priest receive in Holy Orders a share in Christ's office as **teacher**, along with priest and shepherd (CCC 1592; *Lumen Gentium* 25). A Church whose first command is to teach will build schools.

There is a deeper root too. Christians believe "the truth will make you free" (Jn 8:32), and that truth is one: the same God made the world and revealed himself, so honest study cannot finally contradict faith (CCC 159). That conviction is the soil the university grew in.

---

## The Story

**1. Cathedral schools and free teaching (9th–13th c.).** [Documented] Bishops ran schools at their cathedrals. In 1179 the Third Lateran Council ordered that every cathedral appoint a master to teach the clergy **and poor scholars free of charge**, and forbade charging money for a license to teach (canon 18). In 1215 the Fourth Lateran Council repeated the law and extended it to other churches with means (canon 11). These are among the earliest laws anywhere requiring free schooling for the poor.

**2. The university is born (c. 1088–1250).** [Consensus] The university as we know it, a self-governing corporation of masters and students granting recognized degrees, appeared first in medieval Christian Europe: Bologna (traditionally 1088), Paris (late 12th c.), Oxford, then Salamanca, Padua, Cambridge and dozens more. Popes protected them. In the bull *Parens scientiarum* (1231), Gregory IX confirmed the University of Paris's right to govern itself and even to strike. A papal charter let graduates teach anywhere (*ius ubique docendi*), the start of a recognized international degree.

> *Root named:* the teaching office of the Church created a demand for trained minds, and the conviction that truth is one gave masters the freedom to argue, debate and test ideas. The medieval *disputatio* (state the opposing argument at its strongest, then answer it) is the method behind Aquinas's *Summa*.

**3. What the university taught.** [Consensus] Historian Edward Grant (Indiana University) shows that medieval universities made logic and natural philosophy (early science) a required part of the curriculum for every arts student. That, he argues, laid foundations for the Scientific Revolution. (See CHURCH-03.)

**4. Schools for everyone (16th–19th c.).** [Consensus]

- **The Jesuits** (founded 1540) built hundreds of free colleges across Europe, Latin America and Asia within two centuries, one of the largest school networks of the early modern world (John O'Malley, *The First Jesuits*).
- **St. Angela Merici** founded the Ursulines (1535), a pioneering order dedicated to educating girls.
- **St. John Baptist de La Salle** (1651–1719) founded the Brothers of the Christian Schools to teach poor boys for free, taught in the local language instead of Latin, grouped students by level, and trained teachers in what were among the first teacher-training schools. He is the patron of teachers.
- **Fe y Alegría** (Venezuela, 1955), a Jesuit movement, now runs schools for the poorest families across Latin America and beyond.

**5. Today: the largest non-government school system on earth.** [Documented] According to the Church's own statistics, released by the Vatican agency Fides in October 2025, the Catholic Church runs:

- **74,550** kindergartens (7.6 million children)
- **102,455** primary schools (36.2 million pupils)
- **52,085** secondary schools (20.7 million students)
- plus about **4.5 million** students in Catholic universities

Independent economist Quentin Wodon, a World Bank analyst who leads the *Global Catholic Education Report*, uses this data to show the network's size and its large share of enrollment in Africa, where it often reaches the poor that states do not. The description of the Church as **the world's largest non-governmental provider of education** is widely used, including by Wikipedia and the UK Embassy to the Holy See. [Consensus — no rival network of comparable size is known]

## Honest Footnote

- **Early schooling served mainly future clergy and the better-off.** Mass literacy came only in the 19th century, driven by states as well as churches. Protestant countries, with their emphasis on reading the Bible, often led in popular literacy. [Consensus]
- **The Index of Forbidden Books** (1559–1966) restricted what Catholics could read, including some scientific and philosophical works. It was abolished in 1966. [Documented]
- **Restrictions on vernacular Bibles** in some places and periods limited ordinary people's direct access to Scripture. [Documented]
- **Abuse in some Catholic schools and residential schools** is a grave failure, treated in CHURCH-11.
- **The statistics come from the Church herself.** They are compiled from diocesan reports and could contain errors; Wodon notes this openly.

## For You

Every Catholic school began with someone who believed a poor child's mind was worth forming because that child was made for God. The Church teaches because she believes you were made for truth, not just for a job. Are you still learning about the things that matter most, or did your education about God stop in childhood?
