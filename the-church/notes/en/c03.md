# CHURCH-03 The Word - Faith and Science

> **Sacramental root:** Creation by the Word (*Logos*), received in Baptism and the Mass · **Series:** THE CHURCH

---

## The Myth

"Religion and science are at war. The Church burned scientists, jailed Galileo, and only lost its grip when reason won."

---

## The Root

✅ Christians believe the world was made by a rational God through his Word (Jn 1:1–3), and that it is ordered: God "arranged all things by measure and number and weight" (Wis 11:20). 🟢 If the world is made by Reason, it can be understood by reason. That belief gave natural science its confidence that nature follows laws worth discovering.

The Church teaches that faith and reason cannot truly conflict, "because the things of the world and the things of faith derive from the same God" (CCC 159; Vatican I, *Dei Filius*, ch. 4). For the Christian scientist, research is a way of reading the mind of the Creator.

---

## The Story

**1. Historians have abandoned the "warfare" story.** [Consensus] The idea of permanent war between science and religion was popularized by two 19th-century books (Draper, 1874; White, 1896). Modern historians of science, many not religious, reject it. Ronald Numbers (ed.), *Galileo Goes to Jail and Other Myths about Science and Religion* (Harvard UP, 2009), dismantles the myths one by one, including the claim that medieval Christians thought the Earth was flat. They did not.

**2. The medieval foundations.** [Consensus] Edward Grant and David Lindberg show that medieval universities made natural philosophy a core subject. Churchmen led the work:

- **Robert Grosseteste** (d. 1253), Bishop of Lincoln, wrote on optics and stressed experiment and mathematics.
- **Jean Buridan** (d. c. 1360) developed the theory of *impetus*, a forerunner of the idea of inertia.
- **Nicole Oresme** (d. 1382), Bishop of Lisieux, used graphs to represent motion and argued the Earth's daily rotation could not be disproved.

**3. The Church as patron of astronomy.** [Consensus] Historian J. L. Heilbron (UC Berkeley, not a Catholic apologist) wrote that "the Roman Catholic Church gave more financial and social support to the study of astronomy for over six centuries, from the recovery of ancient learning during the late Middle Ages into the Enlightenment, than any other, and, probably, all other, institutions" (*The Sun in the Church*, Harvard UP, 1999). Cathedrals in Bologna, Florence, Paris and Rome were built with meridian lines that made them precise solar observatories. The need to calculate Easter drove the work, and produced the **Gregorian calendar** (1582), designed with the Jesuit mathematician Christopher Clavius and used by the whole world today.

**4. Catholic scientists who changed everything.** [Documented]

- **Nicolaus Copernicus** (d. 1543), a canon of Frombork Cathedral, proposed the sun-centered system; his book was dedicated to Pope Paul III.
- **Nicolas Steno** (d. 1686), a founder of geology and stratigraphy, later became a Catholic bishop and was beatified.
- **Gregor Mendel** (d. 1884), an Augustinian friar and abbot, discovered the laws of heredity in his monastery garden: the father of genetics.
- **Jesuit scientists** ran observatories worldwide; many lunar craters bear Jesuit names (Clavius, Riccioli, Grimaldi). The Vatican Observatory still does professional research today.

**5. Georges Lemaître: the priest of the Big Bang.** [Documented / Consensus]
A Belgian Catholic priest and physicist (1894–1966), Lemaître proposed in **1927** that the universe is expanding and gave the first estimate of the rate later called Hubble's constant, two years before Hubble. In 2018 the International Astronomical Union voted to recommend renaming Hubble's law the **Hubble–Lemaître law**. In **1931** he proposed that the universe began from a "primeval atom," the theory later nicknamed the "Big Bang" (a mocking phrase by Fred Hoyle in 1949).

- Einstein first rejected the idea. Lemaître recalled him saying at the 1927 Solvay Conference: *"Your calculations are correct, but your physics is abominable."* [Documented — Lemaître's own account]
- In January 1933, after Lemaître lectured at Caltech in Pasadena, newspapers reported Einstein praising it as "the most beautiful and satisfactory explanation of creation to which I have ever listened." [Contested] The quote is widely repeated, but historians note disagreement about the press reports, and Einstein may have been praising one part of the talk (on cosmic rays) rather than the whole theory. Use it with this caveat.
- In 1951 Pope Pius XII spoke as if the Big Bang confirmed creation. Lemaître privately urged against linking the two: he held that science and faith use different methods, and the Pope did not repeat the claim. [Consensus]

> *Root named:* Lemaître said there were two ways to the truth and he followed both. A priest who prayed the Mass every morning also wrote the equations of an expanding universe. For him there was no war.

---

## Honest Footnote

- **Galileo (1633).** [Documented] Galileo was tried by the Roman Inquisition, forced to renounce heliocentrism, and spent his last years under house arrest. He was not tortured, nor executed, and the case was tangled with politics, personality and the still-incomplete evidence of his day. But the Church was wrong, and said so: in 1992 Pope John Paul II formally acknowledged the error of Galileo's judges.
- **Giordano Bruno** was burned in 1600 mainly for theological heresies (denying the Trinity, the divinity of Christ), not for astronomy. The execution was still an injustice. [Consensus]
- **The Index** listed Copernicus's book (with corrections) from 1616 until 1758. [Documented]
- **Popular Catholic books sometimes overclaim.** Not every scientist who happened to be Catholic proves the Church caused his discoveries.

## For You

The universe is intelligible, and you can understand it. That is not obvious; it is a kind of faith, and modern science grew up inside the Christian version of it. The question is whether the Mind behind the order can also be known. Lemaître thought he could be met in the Mass every morning. Have you ever looked for him there?
