# GBH-1 God Beyond Being

> **Series:** GBH · Lecture 1 of 6 · Hub: [[GBH-0 God, the Bible and Humanity (Series Hub)]]

## 1 · Anchor

**God is not one being among many. He is Being itself, so he cannot be escaped and he cannot be controlled.**

*One image to carry:* a bush on fire that is not burned up. The closer the true God comes, the more fully alive we become.

## 2 · Pocket Version (15-second re-read)

At the burning bush Moses asks God's name. God answers "I AM WHO AM," which means: I am not an item you can file. Aquinas calls this Ipsum Esse, being itself. So God is closer to me than I am to myself and higher than anything I can imagine. Do not run from him, do not try to manage him. Fall in love with him.

## 3 · Scripture text (Douay-Rheims)

**Exodus 3:14.** "God said to Moses: I AM WHO AM. He said: Thus shalt thou say to the children of Israel: HE WHO IS, hath sent me to you."

**Exodus 3:2-5 (summary of the scene).** The Lord appears to Moses in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush. The bush is on fire and is not burnt. Moses goes to see. God calls him by name and tells him to remove his shoes, for the ground is holy.

**Deuteronomy 6:4.** "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God is one Lord."

**Psalm 139.** "Lord, thou hast proved me, and known me… Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy face? If I ascend into heaven, thou art there: if I descend into hell, thou art present."

*Scripture quoted from the Douay-Rheims and checked against it: Exodus 3:14 at [drbo.org](http://drbo.org), [Deuteronomy 6:4](https://biblehub.com/drb/deuteronomy/6.htm) and [Psalm 139:1, 7-8](https://biblehub.com/drb/psalms/139.htm). The Douay numbers this psalm 138; most Bibles today number it 139.*

## 4 · Synthesis: Text and Setting

Barron opens with three rules for reading the Bible. First, it is a library of genres, so read each one on its own terms. Second, not everything that is in the Bible is what the Bible teaches, so look for the great themes and trajectory. Third, read inside the tradition, which is the Church. He then takes thirteen biblical passages across the course. Exodus 3:14 is the first and the foundation.

The setting matters. Moses was raised a prince of Egypt. His first recorded act is killing an Egyptian and running away. Barron reads him as proud, self-important and unready. Years of desert stripped him down before God appeared. Barron's point: you must be rightly ordered to see God, and the Bible shows its great figures purified first. Joseph's long road through slavery and prison before he rules Egypt is the same pattern.

## 5 · The Passage Explained, by Beat

**Beat 1 — Moses in the desert.** The desert is where control is taken away. Weather, mountains and distance are out of our hands. Holy people have always gone there for that reason.

🕯 **Reflect:** Where in your life has God removed your control, and did you resist it or use it?

**Beat 2 — The bush burns and is not consumed.** Barron contrasts this with Greek and Roman myth, where gods who enter human affairs destroy people because the gods compete with the world. The true God does not compete. The nearer he is, the more the creature shines. He quotes Irenaeus: the glory of God is a human being fully alive.

🕯 **Reflect:** Do you fear that more of God will mean less of you? What does this image say back?

**Beat 3 — "Remove your sandals."** God says, come close, and also, do not take charge. Shoes are how we walk with confidence over any ground. Taking them off is the posture of receiving. Barron names the rhythm: invitation and distance, intimacy without control.

🕯 **Reflect:** In prayer, are you receiving or managing?

**Beat 4 — Moses asks the name.** Knowing a name gives a measure of control, as a contact in a phone does. Moses is asking, which god are you among the many gods of places, rivers and mountains?

🕯 **Reflect:** Where do you try to hold God by defining him?

**Beat 5 — "I AM WHO AM."** Barron reads this first as a gentle refusal of the question. God is not a kind of thing and cannot be sorted into a category. Read rightly, it also says what God is: the one whose very nature is to be.

🕯 **Reflect:** What changes in your prayer if God is not an object but the ground of everything that exists?

## 6 · Meditations

**Meditation A — The ground you stand on.** Picture Moses with his sandals in his hand, the rock warm under bare feet. He has nothing left to hold but the fact that he is standing, and that he is called by name. Stand there. Now notice that you also did not make the floor under you, or the breath you are drawing. Each breath is a gift held out and held up. Stay with the one fact: right now you are being given to be.

**Meditation B — The singer and the song.** Barron borrows Herbert McCabe: God sustains the world the way a singer sustains a song. Listen for the song. You are one note. So is the person who annoys you, and so is the sun. If the singer stopped, the note would not fall silent. It would never have been. Ask to sing in tune, and to let the singer sing through you.

## 7 · Doctrine (evidence-labeled)

**1. God's name reveals that God alone IS.** ✅ Dogma-level teaching of the Church's faith, stated in the Catechism. CCC 213: "The revelation of the ineffable name 'I AM WHO AM' contains then the truth that God alone IS. The Greek Septuagint translation of the Hebrew Scriptures, and following it the Church's Tradition, understood the divine name in this sense: God is the fullness of Being and of every perfection, without origin and without end. All creatures receive all that they are and have from him; but he alone is his very being, and he is of himself everything that he is." *(Source: [CCC 213 on vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P16.HTM).)*

🕯 **Reflect:** Everything I have is received. What do I treat as owned?

**2. In God, essence and existence are identical (divine simplicity).** 🟢 Common doctrine, developed by Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I, q.3, a.4: "it is impossible that in God His existence should differ from His essence." This is the technical form of "I AM WHO AM." Every creature has a nature and also receives existence. God alone is existence itself.

🕯 **Reflect:** If God has no parts, what does that do to the idea that I can understand him piece by piece?

**3. God is in all things by essence, presence and power.** 🟢 Aquinas, ST I, q.8, a.3: "God is in all things by His power, inasmuch as all things are subject to His power; He is by His presence in all things, as all things are bare and open to His eyes; He is in all things by His essence, inasmuch as He is present to all as the cause of their being." Barron adds Aquinas's word "most intimately." It comes two articles earlier, in ST I, q.8, a.1: "God is in all things, and innermostly" ([New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1008.htm)).

🕯 **Reflect:** Is there any place I think God is absent from?

**4. God is closer than I am to myself and higher than my highest.** 🟢 Augustine, Confessions III.6.11: "tu autem eras interior intimo meo et superior summo meo" (you were more inward than my inmost self and higher than my highest). Verified in the Latin text. Barron uses it to hold two truths together: transcendence and immanence. Deny the first and you get pantheism (God is just the world). Deny the second and you get deism (a God who made the world and then left it alone).

🕯 **Reflect:** Which error do I lean toward, a God who is too distant, or a God who is only my feelings?

**5. God is one.** ✅ Dogma. Deuteronomy 6:4 and the whole Creed. Barron's argument: two infinities would limit each other, so there can be only one unconditioned source of existence.

**6. Creation from nothing.** ✅ Dogma (Fourth Lateran Council, 1215; reaffirmed Vatican I). There is no rival to God and no raw material. Barron's spiritual point: nothing stands between you and God. Thomas Merton's definition of contemplation, as Barron cites it, is finding the place in you where you are here and now being created by God.

**7. The argument from contingency.** 🟡 Theological and philosophical argument, not itself a defined dogma, though the Church teaches that God can be known by reason (Vatican I, Dei Filius). Barron's version: things that did not have to exist (contingent things) need causes. A chain of dependent causes cannot go on forever without explaining anything. So something must exist whose nature is to be. Aquinas: "this is what all men call God."

**8. Barron's gloss on omniscience, omnipotence, omnipresence.** 🟡 Aquinas's teaching, put in modern speech. God is not the smartest or strongest being. He knows the world into being as an author knows a novel, and he is not a character in it.

## 8 · Key Corrections

- **"Do you read the Bible literally?"** is the wrong question. Ask what kind of text it is and what it teaches.
- **God is not the highest item in the universe.** The Yeti question, whether he exists like Bigfoot, misses what God is. Barron says Feuerbach and Hitchens share this mistake.
- **"Beyond" does not mean "far."** God is beyond as source, not beyond as distance.
- **Aquinas's "no real relation to the world"** does not mean God does not care. It means God does not depend on the world. Barron says this is good news.
- **"I AM WHO AM" is not only a refusal.** It is also a revelation.
- **Barron's claim that polytheism "says something true"** about God's presence in all things is a distinctive and debatable remark. The Church does not teach it as doctrine, and the Bible itself treats polytheism as error.
- **A note on the Hebrew.** Scholars debate how to translate the Hebrew of Exodus 3:14 (I am who I am, I will be what I will be). The Church's tradition, following the Septuagint, reads it as a statement of being. Both readings have defenders.

## 9 · Why This Matters

If God is an object, he becomes optional, and then easy to dismiss. If God is the ground of being, then everything depends on him, and the real question is not whether he exists but how I am related to him. This is also why prayer is not a technique. It is a response to someone who is already holding you in being.

**Barron's point in one line: God is not the biggest thing in the room. He is the reason there is a room.**
