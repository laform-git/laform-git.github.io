# GBH-2 Right Praise and Sacrifice

> **Series:** GBH · Lecture 2 of 6 · Hub: [[GBH-0 God, the Bible and Humanity (Series Hub)]] · Previous: [[GBH-1 God Beyond Being]]

## 1 · Anchor

**God needs nothing, so God can only love. Creation is a non-violent act of speech, and the whole world is made to lead one chorus of praise.**

*One image to carry:* a rose window, every piece gathered around the one center.

## 2 · Pocket Version (15-second re-read)

God does not create out of need; he has no need. Because he needs nothing, he can only will our good, which is what love is. Genesis shows creation as an ordered, peaceful procession, with no violence, ending in the human being, who is meant to lead all things in praise. When we worship rightly, our life becomes integrated, like a rose window. When we worship something less than God instead, wealth, pleasure, power or honor, we disintegrate.

## 3 · Scripture text (Douay-Rheims)

**Genesis 1:1-4.** "In the beginning God created heaven and earth. And the earth was void and empty, and darkness was upon the face of the deep; and the spirit of God moved over the waters. And God said: Be light made. And light was made. And God saw the light that it was good; and he divided the light from the darkness."

**Genesis 2:16-17 (summary).** God permits Adam to eat of every tree in the garden, and forbids only the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

*Verified against [drbo.org](http://drbo.org). Scripture quoted from the Douay-Rheims.*

## 4 · Synthesis: Text and Setting

Lecture 1 established that God is Ipsum Esse, being itself, who needs nothing. Barron opens lecture 2 with the question this raises: if God lacks nothing, why create at all? The common wrong answer is that God creates out of need. The Catholic answer is the opposite: because God needs nothing, he is free to love purely, since love means willing the good of the other as other, not using the other for oneself.

Genesis 1 is read as a liturgical procession. Each element of the cosmos, sky, sea, sun, animals, comes forth from God in order, each one called good but never called God. The human being is created last, as the one who leads the procession in worship, the way a bishop or priest enters last at Mass. The things the ancient world worshiped, sky, sea, animals, are named here precisely so that Israel will not worship them.

## 5 · The Passage Explained, by Beat

**Beat 1 — Why create at all?** Barron rejects the idea that God creates to gain something. Vatican I teaches that God creates to manifest and communicate his glory, not to increase it. Because God is complete in himself, creation can only be gift.

🕯 **Reflect:** Do I ever treat my relationships as useful to me rather than as gifts to give?

**Beat 2 — Creation by speech, not violence.** Unlike the pagan myths, where the world is made from the slain body of a defeated god, Genesis has God speak the world into being out of love. Barron ties this to Jesus's later command to love enemies: it is not a new ethic invented in the New Testament, but the pattern of reality from the first page of the Bible.

🕯 **Reflect:** Where in my life do I assume force or control is needed to set things right?

**Beat 3 — The tohu wa-bohu.** The "formless void" and watery chaos stands, through the whole Bible, for resistance to God: the flood, the Red Sea, the storm Jesus walks on. God has lordship over it from the first verse.

🕯 **Reflect:** What feels like chaos in my life right now that I have not yet handed to God's lordship?

**Beat 4 — The liturgical procession.** All things come forth good, but only the human being comes last, to lead creation in praise. Barron: this is close to the master theme of the whole Bible. When humanity worships rightly, the world coheres; when worship goes wrong, moral and social breakdown follows.

🕯 **Reflect:** What do I treat as more fundamental than worship: my schedule, my opinions, my comfort?

**Beat 5 — The great permission, then the one prohibition.** God tells Adam and Eve to eat of every tree, meaning: be fully alive, pursue art, science, friendship, cities. The one forbidden tree is the knowledge of good and evil, which stands for the prerogative, belonging to God alone, of defining what is good.

🕯 **Reflect:** Where have I quietly made myself the judge of what is good, rather than receiving that from God?

## 6 · Meditations

**Meditation A — The rose window.** Picture the great rose window of a Gothic cathedral, Christ at the center, all the other medallions wheeling around him, connected to the center and to each other by spokes. Now see your own life as that window: your mind, will, passions, friendships, work, entertainment, all the medallions. Picture Christ moving to the center. Watch the spokes tighten, the whole thing come into order. Ask what, right now, still sits uncentered.

**Meditation B — The garden with two trees.** Walk the garden in your imagination. Every tree is open to you; take and eat, the voice says, be fully alive. Come to the one tree that is closed. Put your hand on its bark and ask yourself honestly: have I been eating from this one, deciding for myself what is good and evil, rather than receiving that from God? Stand there until you can answer honestly, then walk back toward the trees that are open to you.

## 7 · Doctrine (evidence-labeled)

**1. God creates freely, not from need, in order to manifest and share his goodness.** ✅ Dogma. Vatican I, Dei Filius: God creates "not for increasing his own beatitude, nor for attaining his perfection, but in order to manifest this perfection through the benefits which he bestows on creatures." CCC 293 and 295 restate this: "It is not the product of any necessity whatever, nor of blind fate or chance. We believe that it proceeds from God's free will."

🕯 **Reflect:** Does my own giving still expect something back?

**2. Love is to will the good of the other as other.** 🟢 A classical definition (rooted in Aquinas, ST I-II q.26 a.4, "to love is to will good to someone"), which Barron uses to distinguish real love from self-interested kindness.

**3. Creation is non-violent, out of nothing, by the Word.** ✅ Dogma (Fourth Lateran Council, 1215; Genesis 1). No rival power is defeated to make the world, unlike the pagan cosmogonies.

**4. The human person is made to lead creation in praise, not to dominate it.** 🟢 Consistent with Genesis 1:28 read alongside Genesis 2:15 ("to dress and keep" the garden) and the Church's teaching against exploitative dominion (e.g., Laudato Si' §67, which reads "dominion" as responsible stewardship, not domination). Barron's claim that creation is meant to be "led in praise" by humanity is his own synthesis of this material, not a verbatim conciliar formula.

**5. "Our heart is restless until it rests in you."** 🟢 Augustine, Confessions I.1.1, Latin verified: "quia fecisti nos ad te et inquietum est cor nostrum donec requiescat in te." Barron does not quote this directly in this lecture, but it underlies his claim that finite goods cannot satisfy the heart's longing; it is quoted directly in lecture 4 and is placed here as the classic statement of the same principle at work in the false-worship material.

**6. The forbidden tree stands for the claim to define good and evil for oneself.** 🟡 A patristic and traditional reading, not a dogmatic definition of the tree's meaning. Barron credits this broadly to the Church Fathers' reading of Genesis.

## 8 · Key Corrections

- **God is not "needy" in a human sense**, and saying "God has no need of the world" is not coldness; Barron argues it is the basis for the possibility of real love.
- **"Dominion" over creation does not mean exploitation.** Barron is explicit that the biblical vision is of service and right praise, not domination, anticipating modern ecological critiques of "dominion" theology.
- **The prohibition on the tree is not arbitrary cruelty.** It protects the truth that only God determines good and evil.
- **Barron's claim that "Israel always goes wrong when some form of false worship comes into play"** is a strong generalization worth testing against individual narratives rather than accepting as an exceptionless law; it is his reading of the pattern, not a quoted doctrine.

## 9 · Why This Matters

If creation is gift rather than necessity, then my existence is not owed to me, and is not earned. The proper response to a gift is gratitude expressed as praise, not possession. Disordered worship, making wealth, pleasure, power or honor into one's highest good, is not merely a moral lapse; Barron frames it as a metaphysical mistake with real consequences: disintegration of the self and discord around it.
