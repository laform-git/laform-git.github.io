El mundo en voz alta · Lesson 1

Notes for Mom and Dad

Don't see the voice you want? In Settings, search for "Voices", download an English one, close Safari completely and open this page again.

What accessibility is

It means designing things so everyone can use them, no matter how they see, hear or move. A ramp, a braille book or a traffic light that beeps are examples.

On the phone it's the same: a person with a visual disability doesn't need to see the screen, because the phone tells them out loud what is on it.

Where it is on the iPhone

It's all built in, in Settings → Accessibility. For her, what matters is in the Vision group:

  • VoiceOver. The screen reader. It reads out loud what she touches and is used with gestures.
  • Siri. Does things just by talking: call, send a message, ask the time.
  • Spoken Content. Reads a text or a whole screen out loud. That's where voices are downloaded: search for "Voices" in Settings.

First setting: the emergency exit

Go to Settings → Accessibility → Accessibility Shortcut and choose VoiceOver.

From then on, three quick clicks on the side button turn VoiceOver on or off. If someone gets lost with VoiceOver on, three clicks and it turns off.

How to move through the lesson with VoiceOver

  • Next item: swipe one finger to the right. To the left goes back.
  • Next screen: swipe three fingers up. It doesn't scroll little by little: it jumps a whole screen and says "page 2 of 4". Three fingers down goes back.
  • Jump from part to part: turn two fingers on the screen like a knob until you hear "Headings". Then one finger down jumps to the next part.
  • Back to the top: touch the very top, where the time is, and double-tap.
  • Read everything in a row: swipe two fingers down. A two-finger tap stops it.

How to support her

  • Open the lesson together with VoiceOver on. Her page has no speaker buttons: only VoiceOver talks.
  • Play the game. There are five questions. Let her answer on her own, even if she gets it wrong.
  • Do the challenge. Have her look around the house for things with sound or raised texture, and ask her how she recognizes them.
  • Help with words, not with your hands. The goal is for her to use the iPhone on her own.
