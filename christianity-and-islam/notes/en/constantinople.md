# Constantinople

*"The imperial city on the Bosphorus, refounded by Constantine in 330, and the patriarchal see and conciliar setting that shaped much of the Church's doctrinal and jurisdictional history."*

Four ecumenical councils were held there. The first, in 381, completed the Church's trinitarian confession by affirming the divinity of the Holy Spirit against those who denied it, and produced the creed the Church still recites at Mass, properly called the Niceno-Constantinopolitan. The second, in 553, addressed the christological controversies that followed Chalcedon in an attempt to reconcile the non-Chalcedonian churches. The third, in 680-681, condemned monothelitism and defined that Christ possesses two wills, human and divine, corresponding to his two natures — the necessary completion of Chalcedon, since a nature without a will is not a nature. The fourth, in 869-870, is counted ecumenical in the West and dealt with the Photian dispute.

The see itself was a persistent source of tension. The council of 381 assigned the bishop of Constantinople a place of honor after Rome, on the ground that it was the new imperial capital; Chalcedon's canon 28 pressed the claim further and was not accepted by Rome, which held that Roman primacy rested on Peter and not on imperial status. That disagreement about the basis of primacy runs through everything that follows.

The mutual excommunications of 1054 between the papal legates and the patriarch are the conventional marker of the East-West schism, though the estrangement was longer and the sack of the city by crusaders in 1204 did far more lasting damage. Constantinople fell to the Ottomans in 1453.
