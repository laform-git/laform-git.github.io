# Aquinas on Muhammad — The Argument from Signs (SCG I.6)
## The Text
> "On the other hand those who introduced the errors of the sects proceeded in contrary fashion, as instanced by Mohammed, who enticed peoples with the promise of carnal pleasures, to the desire of which the concupiscence of the flesh instigates. He also delivered commandments in keeping with his promises, by giving the reins to carnal pleasure, wherein it is easy for carnal men to obey: and the lessons of truth which he inculcated were only such as can be easily known to any man of average wisdom by his natural powers: yea rather the truths which he taught were mingled by him with many fables and most false doctrines. Nor did he add any signs of supernatural agency, which alone are a fitting witness to divine inspiration, since a visible work that can be from God alone, proves the teacher of truth to be invisibly inspired: but he asserted that he was sent in the power of arms, a sign that is not lacking even to robbers and tyrants. Again, those who believed in him from the outset were not wise men practised in things divine and human, but beastlike men who dwelt in the wilds, utterly ignorant of all divine teaching; and it was by a multitude of such men and the force of arms that he compelled others to submit to his law. Lastly, no divine oracles of prophets in a previous age bore witness to him; rather did he corrupt almost all the teaching of the Old and New Testaments by a narrative replete with fables, as one may see by a perusal of his law. Hence by a cunning device, he did not commit the reading of the Old and New Testament Books to his followers, lest he should thereby be convicted of falsehood. Thus it is evident that those who believe his words believe foolishly."

---
## Where This Sits in the Summa Contra Gentiles
Chapter 6 of Book I carries the title *That to give assent to the truths of faith is not foolishness though they are above reason.* The paragraph on Muhammad is its second half, and it only makes sense read against the first. Aquinas has just argued that Christianity's claims, though they exceed reason, are credible because God confirmed them with works only God can do: healings, the raising of the dead, and above all the greatest miracle of all, the conversion of the world. Multitudes, the learned as well as the simple, embraced a faith that teaches things beyond understanding, restrains the pleasures of the flesh, and asks contempt for worldly goods, and they did so under persecution, with no promise of earthly reward. And all of this was foretold by the prophets centuries before.

The Muhammad paragraph is built as a point-by-point inversion of that case. Where the Gospel promised spiritual goods, Aquinas sees a promise of carnal ones; where the Gospel's commands are hard, these are easy; where Christian teaching exceeds reason, this stays within what any sensible person already knows, mixed with fables; where Christ gave miracles, here the sign offered is military success; where the Church won the wise and the simple by persuasion under the sword of persecution, here the sword is on the other side; where Christ fulfilled prophecy, no prophet foretold this. The conclusion mirrors the chapter title: believing the Gospel is not foolish, but believing a revelation that lacks every one of these credentials is.

## The Durable Core — The Argument from Signs
Strip away the medieval polemic and a principle remains that the Church teaches as her own. ✅ Vatican I, *Dei Filius* ch. 3 (1870), teaches that God willed to join external proofs of his revelation to the interior help of the Holy Spirit, above all miracles and prophecies, which show forth God's omnipotence and infinite knowledge and are most certain signs of revelation suited to the understanding of all. CCC 156 restates it: the miracles of Christ and the saints, prophecies, the Church's growth and holiness, and her fruitfulness and stability are motives of credibility, showing that assent to faith is not a blind impulse of the mind.

This is Aquinas's real point. A claim to speak for God needs credentials that only God can supply. Success, power, and the ability to compel are not such credentials, because tyrants have them too. The argument is aimed at the founder's warrant, not at the sincerity or worth of any Muslim who prays.

The same test cuts in both directions. If force is no sign of God, then every Christian attempt to spread the faith by coercion was equally without warrant. 🟢 *Dignitatis Humanae* §10 (1965) teaches that the act of faith is by its very nature free and that no one may be forced to embrace the faith against his will. Benedict XVI made the same argument at Regensburg (12 September 2006): because God acts with *logos*, with reason, spreading faith through violence is contrary to God's nature. He framed this through a quotation from the Byzantine emperor Manuel II and afterward made clear the quoted words were not his own judgment on Islam.

## Key Corrections
- **This is not the Church's magisterial judgment on Islam.** Aquinas is a Doctor of the Church, but SCG I.6 is theological argument, not a magisterial act. The Magisterium's own statement is *Nostra Aetate* §3 (1965): the Church regards Muslims with esteem; they adore the one God, living and subsistent, merciful and almighty, Creator of heaven and earth; they revere Jesus as a prophet, honor Mary, and await the day of judgment; the Council urges that past quarrels be set aside and mutual understanding be sought. CCC 841 adds that Muslims, professing the faith of Abraham, adore with us the one merciful God. Anyone teaching from Aquinas's paragraph must hold both texts together.
- **Aquinas's picture of Islam was limited.** 🟡 He worked from secondhand Latin sources shaped by centuries of conflict. Some of his points have real textual grounding (the Qur'an's sensual depictions of paradise, the early military spread of Islam, the absence of claimed miracles in the Qur'an's own account of Muhammad). Others are polemical compression: "giving the reins to carnal pleasure" does not describe a religion of daily prayer, the Ramadan fast, and the prohibition of wine. "Beastlike men" is the language of medieval disputation and should never be repeated as a description of any people.
- **"He did not commit the reading of the Old and New Testament to his followers."** This touches a live question. Islam holds that earlier scriptures were corrupted (*tahrif*), which in practice sets the Qur'an above the Bible as its judge. The apologetic point is fair; the claim of a deliberate "cunning device" is Aquinas's inference, not something he could demonstrate.
- **Do not use the argument as a club.** Aquinas himself notes in SCG I.2 that Muslims do not accept the authority of Scripture, so with them one must argue from natural reason, which is why the whole Summa Contra Gentiles leans on philosophy. The method is respect for the listener's reason, not contempt.

🕯 **Reflect:** Can I hold the truth of Aquinas's argument and the charity of *Nostra Aetate* at once, without letting either one cancel the other?

## Quick Reference
### Signs
- **Claim:** A prophet's authority is shown by success, power, and the spread of his movement.
- **Response:** Only works God alone can do witness to divine inspiration; force is a sign "not lacking even to robbers and tyrants." ✅ Dei Filius ch. 3; CCC 156

### Content of the message
- **Claim:** A true revelation will be easy to accept.
- **Response:** The Gospel spread though it taught things above reason and demanded self-denial; ease of acceptance is no mark of God. 🟢 SCG I.6

### Prior prophecy
- **Claim:** Muhammad is the prophet foretold in earlier scripture.
- **Response:** No prophet of Israel bore witness to him, and his teaching contradicts the Scriptures it claims to complete. 🟢 SCG I.6

### Force and faith
- **Claim:** Aquinas's argument justifies Christian triumphalism.
- **Response:** The same test condemns every coerced conversion; faith must be free. ✅ Dignitatis Humanae §10

### Attitude to Muslims
- **Claim:** Aquinas's polemic licenses contempt for Muslims.
- **Response:** The Church regards Muslims with esteem as worshippers of the one God. ✅ Nostra Aetate §3; CCC 841
