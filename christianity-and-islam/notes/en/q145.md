# Q145 - Does the Church ever permit war and self-defense? What about "turning the other cheek"? (Matthew 5:39)

The Church does permit war and self-defense under strict conditions. "Turning the other cheek" (Matthew 5:39, [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+5:38-39)) addresses personal insult borne with humility, not the abandonment of the innocent to grave harm — Christ himself responded forcefully in driving the money-changers from the Temple (John 2:13-16).

St. Augustine developed the Just War framework in *The City of God*, refined by St. Thomas Aquinas in the *Summa Theologiae*. Its conditions for entering a war justly (*ius ad bellum*) require: a just cause (defense against a lasting, grave, certain danger); legitimate authority declaring it; right intention, meaning war as a genuine last resort after diplomacy fails, a reasonable chance of success, and proportionate means that do not create evils worse than the danger being addressed. A separate set of conditions (*ius in bello*) governs the just conduct of a war already underway, chiefly the protection of non-combatants, the wounded, and prisoners.

The Crusades are a useful test case: they largely satisfied the conditions for entering the conflict justly, but failed conspicuously on conduct within it, with documented massacres of non-combatants on both sides — atrocities the Church never condoned even when individual crusaders committed them. See the [Catechism, paragraphs 2263-2265 and 2308-2317](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ccc/index_en.htm) for the full statement of these principles.
