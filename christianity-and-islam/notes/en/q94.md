# Q94 - What do Muslims believe?

What do Muslims believe?

Islam was founded by Muhammad, who lived from 570-632 A.D. Muhammad grew up an orphan and was raised by his uncle Abu Talib, and later married a wealthy widow, Kadijah. In 612 he experienced a revelation in a cave near Mecca, where he received the direct dictation of the Koran. Although he gathered followers almost immediately, he also incurred the local merchants' wrath because he criticized their polytheistic beliefs.

After years of persecution by Meccans, Muhammad and his followers fled to Medina on Friday, July 16, 622. This event, called the Hijra, marks the beginning of the Islamic lunar calendar. (622 is Year 1 in the Muslim calendar.) In Medina many followed Muhammad, but the Jews there refused to recognize him as a prophet in line with their prophets.

The years 627-629 saw the siege and military conquest of Mecca, which then became the center of Islam. In retaliation for having been rejected by the Jews in Medina, Muhammad ordered two Jewish tribes there to be expelled, and a third to be slaughtered (about 600-700 perished).

Islam spread quickly in the 7th and 8th centuries by means of jihad, or holy war against the infidels, through Arabia, Jerusalem (conquered in 638), Egypt, Syria, Persia, North Africa, and Spain (conquered in 711). Many Jews and Christians died while trying to defend their cities from Muslim attack. However, contrary to popular belief, most Jews and Christians remaining in already-conquered cities were not converted at sword-point; rather, they were taxed heavily and denied civil rights.

There are Five Pillars of Islam, such that faithful Muslims can be assured of salvation by fulfilling these requirements:

Recitation of the Creed – "There is no God but Allah, and his prophet is Muhammad."

Daily Prayer – 5 times a day facing Mecca ("Allahu akbar" is the call to prayer).

Almsgiving – for the poor, orphans, beggars, and slaves.

The Ramadan fast – no food or drink from sunrise to sunset, during the lunar month of Ramadan.

Pilgrimage to Mecca – to be done at least once in a lifetime (although exceptions can be granted).
