# Glycemic Index — Good and Bad

*What the glycemic index and glycemic load mean, which everyday foods are high, medium and low, how to blunt a spike, and where the numbers mislead.*

**In plain words:** the glycemic index (GI) tells you how fast a food raises blood sugar compared with pure glucose. The glycemic load (GL) also counts how much carbohydrate is in a normal serving, so it is the more useful number. Low is usually better, but not always: some low-GI foods are junk, and some high-GI foods are fine in normal portions.

## 1 · The two numbers

- **GI** = speed. Pure glucose = 100. ✅
  - Low: 55 or less · Medium: 56–69 · High: 70 or more
- **GL** = speed × amount. GI × grams of digestible carbohydrate in a serving ÷ 100. ✅
  - Low: 10 or less · Medium: 11–19 · High: 20 or more
- Example: watermelon has a high GI (76) but a low GL (8) because a cup is mostly water. ✅

## 3 · Good and bad: the real rule

**Usually good choices (low GL, fiber, whole):** beans, lentils, barley, nuts, most whole fruit, non-starchy vegetables, intact whole grains. 🟢

**Usually poor choices (high GL, fast, refined):** potatoes (baked or mashed), white rice in big portions, white bread, rice cakes, breakfast cereals, pancakes, sweets and sugary drinks. 🟢

**Where the GI misleads:**
- 🔴 **Low GI is not automatically healthy.** Harvard notes a Snickers bar has a GI of 43 and Coca-Cola 63 — both are little more than sugar. Fructose, agave and ice cream are low GI too.
- 🔴 **High GI is not automatically bad.** Watermelon and carrots have a low GL in normal portions.
- 🔴 **"Whole wheat" bread is not low GI.** Finely milled whole-wheat flour (GI ~74) behaves almost like white flour (~75). Intact or coarse grains (pumpernickel 46, barley 28) are what make the difference.
- 🟡 **Cooking changes the number.** White spaghetti boiled 20 minutes is 58; the average (more al dente) is 46.
- 🟡 **The same food varies from person to person**, and with ripeness, cooking and what else is on the plate.

## 4 · How to blunt a spike

1. **Add fiber, protein or fat** to the meal — they slow emptying of the stomach. 🟢
2. **Eat the carbohydrate last** in the meal. 🟡 Consistent but modest effect.
3. **Choose intact over milled**: steel-cut oats over instant, whole beans over flour. 🟢
4. **Cook pasta al dente; cook and cool starches** (potato, rice, pasta) to form resistant starch. 🟢 mechanism · 🟡 size of benefit
5. **Add acid** (vinegar, lemon). 🟡
6. **Walk 10–15 minutes after eating.** 🟡 Consistent, modest.
7. **Keep sugar out of drinks.** 🟢 Liquid carbohydrate is the fastest dose.

## 5 · Why it matters

🟢 Diets with a high GI or GL are associated with type 2 diabetes. Repeated big spikes mean repeated big insulin peaks, one of the routes to insulin resistance — see [[Insulin Resistance — Complete Study Notes]] and [[Too Much Sugar — What It Does to the Body]].

## Key Corrections

- 🔴 "Low GI = healthy" → judge by GL, fiber and how processed the food is.
- 🔴 "Fruit is high sugar, avoid it" → most whole fruit is low GI and low GL.
- 🔴 "Brown bread is low GI" → only if the grain is intact or coarse.

**Look at portion and processing, not just the GI number: whole, fibrous foods in normal portions keep blood sugar steady.**
