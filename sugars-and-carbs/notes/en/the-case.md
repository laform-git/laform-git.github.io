# The Case Against Added Sugar

*Robert Lustig's argument against added sugar, what holds up, and where it goes too far.*

**In plain words:** your body runs on glucose, but it can make all the glucose it needs. Added sugar is optional. Table sugar is half glucose and half fructose. Glucose is used by every cell. Fructose is handled first by the gut, and big, fast doses (soda, juice) spill over to the liver, which turns them into fat. That is the heart of Dr. Robert Lustig's case. Most of it holds up; a few of his strongest words ("poison", "just like alcohol", "like a drug") go further than the evidence. Each claim below carries its evidence label.

## The argument

Dr. Robert Lustig, a children's hormone specialist at the University of California, San Francisco, has argued for two decades that added sugar, and above all **fructose**, harms the body slowly and in proportion to the dose. His books *Fat Chance* (2012) and *Metabolical* (2021) set out the case.

**His claim:** added sugar (not fruit, not all carbohydrates) is a main driver of the rise in obesity, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver, heart disease and high blood pressure.

## You need glucose, not sugar

- ✅ Every cell can burn glucose, and the brain alone uses about 120 g a day.
- ✅ The body makes its own glucose from protein and fat (the liver does this, called *gluconeogenesis*), releases stored glucose between meals, and can switch to fat-based fuel (*ketones*) when glucose is low.
- ✅ So there is **no need** to eat added sugar. Starchy foods, fruit and vegetables already provide glucose.

## Glucose vs fructose: two different roads

| | Glucose | Fructose |
|---|---|---|
| Who uses it | Every cell in the body | The small intestine first; large, fast doses reach the liver |
| Insulin | Raises insulin | Little direct insulin rise, so no blood-sugar "alarm" |
| At high, fast doses | Blood-sugar and insulin spikes | Liver fat, uric acid, liver insulin resistance |

![](media/carbs_3_glucose_vs_fructose.png)

🟢 Small doses of fructose are cleared by the small intestine; big, fast doses spill to the liver (Jang 2018, in mice). Older notes said fructose goes to the liver "only" and is "7 times more damaging, 100 times more oxidative stress": those figures had no source and were removed.

**Table sugar (sucrose)** is 50% glucose and 50% fructose, so it does both at once: the glucose half raises insulin, and the fructose half loads the liver.

**The danger is not fruit.** Whole fruit holds a small dose wrapped in fiber and water, so it arrives slowly. The problem is soda, juice, high-fructose corn syrup and processed food, where a large dose arrives fast.

## What the liver does with a fructose flood

When large, fast doses reach the liver:

1. **Makes fat** (called *de novo lipogenesis*, "making new fat"). ✅ In controlled studies, fructose-sweetened drinks raised liver fat-making; glucose drinks did not.
2. **Uses up cell energy.** The enzyme that processes fructose has no brake, so it burns through the cell's energy (ATP) and triggers inflammation signals. 🟡 Shown mostly in lab and animal studies.
3. **Makes uric acid.** Linked to gout 🟢 (Choi 2008) and possibly to blood pressure and heart risk 🟡.
4. **Raises triglycerides** (blood fats) → heart risk. 🟢
5. **Fatty liver** (now called MASLD) → the same road alcohol uses. 🟢
6. **Liver insulin resistance** → the liver stops answering insulin. 🟢

## The chain of disease

```
TOO MUCH ADDED SUGAR, ESPECIALLY DRINKS
        ↓
FAT IN THE LIVER
        ↓
LIVER STOPS ANSWERING INSULIN
        ↓
WHOLE-BODY INSULIN RESISTANCE + HIGH INSULIN
        ↓
METABOLIC SYNDROME
(high triglycerides · low HDL · small dense LDL · high blood pressure · belly fat)
        ↓
TYPE 2 DIABETES · HEART DISEASE · SOME CANCERS
```

🔴 Interpretation: insulin resistance has other causes too (genes, inactivity, too many calories overall). Sugar is a large driver, not the only one. The evidence for each illness is in [[Too Much Sugar — What It Does to the Body]].

## Sugar coats proteins (AGEs)

When extra glucose stays in the blood, it sticks to proteins and fats, forming **AGEs** (advanced glycation end products), a bit like rust from the inside. ✅ The HbA1c blood test measures exactly this: the share of red blood cells coated with sugar over about 3 months.

AGEs are linked to damage in blood vessels, kidneys, eyes, nerves, skin and brain. 🟢

## Why it is more than calories

Lustig's point is that sugar affects the hormones that control hunger and fat storage, not only the calorie count:

1. **Leptin** (the "I'm full" signal): he argues fructose and constant high insulin block leptin in the brain, so hunger stays on. 🟡 Hypothesis with partial support.
2. **Insulin:** high insulin all day keeps cells in storage mode and holds fat in place. 🟢
3. **Dopamine and habit:** sugar does switch on the brain's reward system ✅ and trains repeat eating. 🟡 "Addictive like drugs" is shown mostly in animals and is debated in people.

**Result:** you can take in more calories while feeling less full. A can of soda does not fill you the way the same calories of food do. 🟡 This is why many experts say the problem is not just willpower.

## Liquid sugar is the worst form

- No fiber, no chewing → the whole dose arrives at once.
- The brain does not count drink calories well, so you do not eat less later. 🟡
- Sugary cocktails add alcohol on top, both handled by the liver.
- 🟡 In about 120,000 people, more than one can a day of regular **or diet** soda went with more liver disease (observational study, 2025).

## The industry story

✅ Documented: in the 1960s the sugar industry paid scientists to point the blame at fat instead of sugar (Kearns 2016, JAMA Internal Medicine). Low-fat advice in the 1980s and 90s then filled processed food with sugar and refined starch.

🔴 Interpretation: obesity and metabolic disease rose in the same years. That timing fits Lustig's case but does not prove cause on its own.

## Fiber: the antidote

In whole foods (fruit, vegetables, beans), fiber:
- slows sugar down, so less reaches the liver per minute,
- feeds gut bacteria, which make butyrate, an anti-inflammatory fat,
- lowers the calories you absorb,
- raises GLP-1, a fullness hormone.

**Lustig's rule:** whole food brings the sugar and its antidote together; processed food removes the fiber and keeps the sugar.

## Practical targets

- ✅ **WHO:** free sugars under 10% of daily calories, ideally under 5% (about 25 g, 6 teaspoons).
- ✅ **American Heart Association:** under 36 g a day for men, under 25 g for women.
- **Lustig:** as close to zero *added* sugar as practical. Whole fruit is fine.

**Cut first:** sweetened drinks (biggest, fastest dose, no fiber), daily desserts, sweetened yogurt, breakfast cereals, sauces with high-fructose corn syrup.

## Sweeteners in brief

- ✅ **Allulose** and pure **monk fruit** do not raise blood sugar. Allulose is about 70% as sweet as sugar; the FDA lets it be left out of "Total" and "Added Sugars" and counts it at 0.4 kcal/g. Many monk fruit products add erythritol, so check labels.
- 🟡 **Honey** is still glucose plus fructose; use sparingly.
- 🔴 **Agave** is about 56–85% fructose, often more than high-fructose corn syrup (55%), despite its "natural" label.
- The complete list is in [[Every Sugar and Sweetener]].

## What holds up and what does not

- 🟢 Large, fast doses of fructose make liver fat, raise triglycerides and uric acid, and feed insulin resistance.
- 🟢 Fructose and alcohol share the same liver road and both cause fatty liver.
- 🔴 "Identical to alcohol" overstates it: alcohol is a proven cause of cancer (IARC Group 1); sugar is not.
- 🔴 "Poison" is advocacy. **The dose and the speed make the harm.**

**Your body never needs added sugar; cut the sugar you drink first.**
