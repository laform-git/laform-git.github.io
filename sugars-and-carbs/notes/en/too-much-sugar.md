# Too Much Sugar — What It Does to the Body

*The major illnesses linked to too much added sugar, how strong the evidence is for each, how sugar is thought to cause them, and where the claims go too far.*

**In plain words:** added sugar, especially in drinks, is linked to a long list of illnesses: diabetes, fatty liver, heart disease, high blood pressure, gout, tooth decay, some cancers and depression. The strongest evidence is for teeth, weight, diabetes and fatty liver. For cancer and depression the link is real but weaker, and part of it runs through weight and insulin. The dose and the speed make the harm: a soda is not the same as an apple.

> **Free / added sugars** = sugars added to food and drinks, plus honey, syrups and fruit juice. Not the sugar inside whole fruit, vegetables or plain milk (WHO).

---

## How much is too much?

- ✅ **WHO (2015):** less than 10% of daily calories from free sugars; less than 5% (about 25 g, 6 teaspoons) for extra benefit. One can of soda holds up to 40 g.
- 🟢 **BMJ umbrella review (Huang 2023):** about 6 teaspoons of added sugar a day and fewer than one sugary drink a week.
- ✅ **American Heart Association:** no added sugar under age 2; under 25 g a day for children and teens (Vos 2017).

## The big picture

🟢 A 2023 review of 73 meta-analyses found harmful links between sugar and **45 health outcomes**: 18 metabolic, 10 heart and blood vessel, 7 cancers and 10 others (mental health, teeth, liver, bone, allergy). No reliable benefit was found (Huang 2023).

---

## 1 · Insulin resistance — the common root

🟢 Repeated large sugar loads → repeated insulin peaks → cells answer insulin less. Big fast doses of fructose add liver fat, which makes the liver itself insulin resistant. Many of the conditions below grow from this root.
→ See [[Insulin Resistance — Complete Study Notes]] and [[The Glucose Journey]].

## 2 · Type 2 diabetes

🟢 Each daily serving of a sugary drink went with **18% more type 2 diabetes**, and still **13% more after adjusting for body weight** — so the harm is not only through weight gain (Imamura 2015, 17 cohorts). Fruit juice showed a small risk too.

## 3 · Fatty liver (MASLD)

🟢 Added sugars are linked to fat building up in the liver and muscle (Huang 2023, moderate quality).
- How: the small intestine handles small doses of fructose; big, fast doses spill over to the liver, which turns them into fat (Jang 2018, mice). ✅ mechanism
- Fatty liver is now seen in children too. 🟢
→ See [[Insulin Resistance — How It Develops]].

## 4 · Heart disease and cholesterol

🟢 US adults getting **25% or more of their calories from added sugar** had about **2.75 times** the risk of dying of heart disease compared with those under 10% (Yang 2014, observational).
- How: liver fat → more VLDL particles → higher triglycerides and ApoB, lower HDL, small dense LDL. 🟢
→ See [[Sugar vs. Cholesterol]] and [[Lipid Triad]].

## 5 · High blood pressure

🟢 People drinking the most sugary drinks had **12% more high blood pressure** (Jayalath 2015, 6 cohorts, 240,000 people). 🟡 The same link was **not** found for fruit or yogurt.
- How: high insulin makes the kidneys keep salt and water; fructose raises uric acid. 🟡 for the uric-acid route.
→ See [[Insulin Resistance — Complete Study Notes]] (section "Blood Pressure: Two Additive Levers").

## 6 · Cancer

**Lustig's argument:** refined carbohydrates drive insulin release and insulin resistance; insulin is a growth signal for cells, and insulin resistance is thought to sit under diabetes, heart disease and possibly many cancers. Cancer biologist Lewis Cantley adds that people with type 2 diabetes have higher rates of several cancers.

Three routes, three strengths of evidence:

1. **Indirect: sugar → weight gain and high insulin → cancer.** 🟢 Strong. The World Health Organization's cancer agency reviewed over 1,000 studies and found that avoiding excess body fat lowers the risk of **13 cancers**, including colon, post-menopause breast, liver, pancreas, kidney and uterus. Mechanisms include chronic inflammation and disturbed sex hormones (Lauby-Secretan 2016).
2. **Direct: sugar feeds the tumor.** 🟡 Promising, mostly animals. Mice prone to bowel tumors given high-fructose corn syrup daily grew larger, higher-grade tumors **without getting fat**; the tumors used the fructose to make fat for growth (Goncalves 2019). Not yet shown in people.
3. **Human population studies.** 🟡 About 101,000 French adults followed 5 years: each extra 100 mL a day of sugary drink went with **18% more cancer** overall; 100% fruit juice also; diet drinks did not (Chazelas 2019). The authors ask for confirmation. Low-quality evidence also links each 25 g a day of fructose with 22% more pancreatic cancer (Huang 2023).

🔴 **What it does not mean:** "sugar feeds cancer, so cancer patients must eat zero sugar" goes beyond the evidence. Every cell runs on glucose, and the body makes its own when you eat none. Treatment diets belong with the oncology team.

## 7 · Depression and the brain

🟡 **Moderate evidence, cause not proven.**
- Men in the top third of sugar intake (above about 67 g a day from sweets and drinks) had **23% higher odds** of depression or anxiety 5 years later; low mood did not make people eat more sugar (Knüppel 2017, Whitehall II). No link was found in women.
- Pooling 40 studies (1.2 million people): **21% higher risk** of depression (Xiong 2024). Risk rises clearly above about 2 cups of cola a day (Hu 2019).
- 🟢 **The "sugar rush" is a myth:** 31 experiments found no mood lift at any time after sugar, and more tiredness and less alertness in the first hour (Mantantzis 2019).
- How (🟡): inflammation, blood-sugar crashes, lower BDNF (a brain growth factor; shown in rats), dopamine reward habits, and weight stigma.
- Memory: diabetes raises dementia risk about 1.6–1.7 times. 🟢
→ See [[DEPRESSION — 03 Inflammation and Sickness Behaviour]], [[DEPRESSION — 04 Brain Energy Mitochondria and Insulin]], [[Insulin Resistance and Brain Decline]].

## 8 · PMOS — Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (formerly PCOS)

> **New name (May 2026):** PCOS is now **PMOS, Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome**. Spanish: **Síndrome Metabólico Poliendocrino de Ovario (PMOS)**, formerly *síndrome de ovario poliquístico (SOP)*. The new name rolls in over about 3 years. ✅

**What it is:** a hormonal and metabolic condition affecting about 1 in 8 women (more than 170 million worldwide). It affects weight, metabolism, mental health, skin and fertility. There is **no** increase in true ovarian cysts — that is why "polycystic" was dropped. ✅

**The sugar–insulin connection:** 🟢
- **Insulin resistance affects up to 85% of women with PMOS — including 75% of lean women** (BMI 25 or less). That is why "metabolic" is in the new name.
- High insulin tells the ovaries to make more male hormones (androgens) and lowers SHBG, the protein that keeps those hormones in check → irregular periods, acne, extra hair, harder ovulation. ✅ mechanism
- A diet high in sugar and refined carbohydrates keeps insulin high and feeds this loop. 🟡 Trials testing sugar alone are few; diet, activity and weight loss are first-line care. 🟢
- Long-term risks follow the same root: type 2 diabetes, fatty liver, heart disease, depression and anxiety. 🟢

→ Full note: [[PMOS (formerly PCOS)]].

## 9 · Hormones: insulin, leptin, ghrelin, uric acid

- **Insulin:** see section 1. 🟢
- **Leptin** (the "I'm full" signal): Lustig argues constant high insulin blocks leptin in the brain, so hunger persists. 🟡 Hypothesis with partial support. → [[Leptin]]
- **Ghrelin** (hunger hormone): sugary drinks do not fill you up the way food does. 🟡
- **Uric acid and gout:** men drinking 2 or more sugary sodas a day had **1.85 times** the gout risk; diet sodas showed no link (Choi 2008). 🟢 → [[Gout]]

## 10 · Children — the early years matter

- ✅ Tooth decay is the most common disease linked to free sugars (WHO).
- 🟢 Early sugary drinks → childhood weight gain, early insulin resistance and fatty liver.
- 🟡 Taste habits for sweetness form early.
- ✅ AHA: no added sugar under 2; under 25 g a day after that.

## 11 · Teeth, kidneys, skin

- **Teeth:** ✅ free sugars are the main cause of tooth decay; the WHO 10% limit is based mainly on dental evidence.
- **Kidney stones:** 🟡 fructose raises uric acid and some stone types. → [[Kidney Stones]]
- **Skin aging:** 🟡 sugar binds to collagen (glycation); evidence in people is limited.

---

## Box — Fructose and alcohol: same liver road, different strength

- 🟢 **What they share:** both are handled by the liver and turned into fat there; both cause fatty liver; both do most harm as drinks. Lustig built his case on this comparison.
- ✅ **The difference:** alcohol is a proven cause of cancer — **Group 1 carcinogen** (the highest group, with tobacco and asbestos), linked to at least 7 cancers and about 4% of all new cancers worldwide; WHO says **no amount is safe** for cancer. Sugar has no such classification.
- 🔴 "Sugar is exactly like alcohol" overstates it. "Same road, smaller dose of harm" is fair.
→ See [[Substances — 01 Alcohol, Risks and Dependence]].

## What this does NOT mean

- 🔴 Whole fruit is not the problem — fiber and water slow it down; WHO excludes it from free sugars.
- 🔴 One dessert does not cause disease — the risk tracks the daily habit, especially drinks.
- 🔴 Calling sugar "poison" is advocacy. "The dose and the speed make the harm" is what the evidence supports.

**Cut the sugar you drink first: it is the fastest, biggest dose, and it is tied to almost every condition on this page.**
