# Wheat and Gluten

*Five separate problems people blame on wheat, and how strong the evidence is for each.*

**In plain words:** "wheat is bad" mixes up five different things. Celiac disease is a real immune disease in about 1 in 100 people. "Gluten sensitivity" is real for some people, but the trigger is often a wheat carbohydrate (fructans), not gluten. Modern bread, white or whole wheat, raises blood sugar fast. Wheat bran holds back some minerals. And links to brain conditions exist but are not proven causes. For most people the strongest reason to eat less refined wheat is blood sugar, not gluten.

## Five separate problems

Modern wheat, especially finely ground flour, raises five different issues. They should not be lumped together:

1. **Celiac disease:** an immune reaction to gluten.
2. **Non-celiac gluten sensitivity:** symptoms without the immune markers of celiac disease.
3. **High glycemic load:** fast blood-sugar rise, gluten or not.
4. **Phytic acid:** a compound that holds back minerals.
5. **Brain and nerve links:** associations between gluten reactions and some brain conditions.

## 1 · Gluten and the immune system

**Celiac disease** 🟢
Affects about 1% of people. The immune system attacks gluten *and* the lining of the gut, because the two look alike to it (*molecular mimicry*). Untreated, it slowly damages the gut, blocks the absorption of nutrients and causes inflammation throughout the body.

**Non-celiac gluten sensitivity** 🟡
A real scientific debate. Some people without celiac disease feel unwell when they eat gluten. In a blinded trial of 59 people who believed they were gluten-sensitive, **fructans** (a fermentable carbohydrate in wheat), not gluten, caused the most bloating (Skodje 2018). For many "gluten-sensitive" people the trigger may be fructans rather than gluten.

**"Leaky gut"** 🟡
Early evidence suggests gluten raises *zonulin*, a protein that briefly loosens the seals between gut cells, so partly digested particles can slip into the blood. This may drive inflammation in sensitive people. Whether it matters in people without celiac disease is contested.

## 2 · Blood-sugar spikes

🟢 Whole wheat bread has a **glycemic index (GI) of about 74**, practically the same as white bread (about 71–75; Atkinson 2008). The GI is a 0–100 scale of how fast a food raises blood sugar compared with pure glucose.

Why "whole wheat" doesn't help much: when grain is milled into fine flour, the fiber is still there, but the particles are so small that the body digests them almost as fast as white flour.

**Why a high GI matters** 🟢
Eating high-GI foods day after day is linked to obesity, type 2 diabetes, heart disease and faster *glycation* (sugar sticking to proteins, which ages blood vessels and tissues).

## 3 · Phytic acid and minerals

🟢 Wheat, especially whole wheat, contains **phytic acid**, which binds calcium, zinc, iron and magnesium in the gut so less is absorbed. Eating whole wheat bread with a zinc supplement or a mineral-rich meal lowers how much of those minerals you take in.

## 4 · Brain and nerve links

**Gluten ataxia** 🟡
A movement and balance disorder (a type of *cerebellar ataxia*) in which gluten triggers an immune attack on the cerebellum, the part of the brain that coordinates movement. Several studies show the link, and a controlled trial showed improvement on a gluten-free diet.

**Schizophrenia** 🟡
A link has been reported between gluten sensitivity, celiac disease and schizophrenia. Many patients carry antibodies against gliadin (part of gluten). Some improve on a gluten-free diet, but not all, which suggests gluten adds to the problem in a susceptible group rather than causing it in general.

**Autism and epilepsy** 🟡
Links have been observed; cause is not established.

## Key corrections

- Whole wheat bread is **not** low-GI: milling removes the protection of the intact grain.
- Gluten sensitivity is real, but the exact trigger (gluten, fructans and other FODMAPs, or wheat germ agglutinin) is still debated.
- Phytic acid matters mostly for people who rely on wheat as a main source of calories, not occasional eaters.
- **Sourdough** fermentation lowers phytic acid and lowers the GI: if you eat wheat, sourdough is meaningfully better than ordinary bread.

## The 30-day test

The most practical test: stop all wheat and gluten for 30 days, then bring it back and watch for symptoms. Joint pain, brain fog, digestion, mood and energy are the signs to watch. Anyone who suspects celiac disease should be tested **before** stopping gluten, because the blood test needs gluten in the diet to work.

## Practical takeaway

For anyone working on blood sugar or insulin resistance, high-GI foods, including most modern wheat products, are a major dietary driver. The blood-sugar case for eating less refined wheat stands whether or not you are gluten-sensitive. Swapping refined wheat for intact grains (oats, quinoa), beans, sweet potatoes or vegetables improves metabolic health either way.

## Open questions

- Whether fructan sensitivity (not gluten) explains why some "gluten-sensitive" people tolerate sourdough.
- Whether wheat germ agglutinin (a lectin, a plant protein) interferes with insulin signaling.

**Eat less refined wheat for your blood sugar; test for celiac disease before cutting gluten.**
