# Every Sugar and Sweetener

*Every sugar and sweetener in one place: what it is, how sweet it is, what it does to blood sugar, the downsides of each, and why the best amount of added sugar is as close to zero as you can manage.*

**In plain words:** there are five families. Real sugars and syrups raise blood sugar and add calories. Sugar alcohols raise it a little or not at all. Allulose, a "rare sugar", barely counts. The very strong sweeteners (stevia, monk fruit, sucralose, aspartame) add sweetness with almost no calories. "Natural" on a label does not mean better: honey, agave and maple syrup are still sugar. This note exists to help you eat less sugar in every form. It is not a menu of "healthier sugars."

## The bottom line first

**Your body does not need added sugar. Not a gram.** ✅

- Your liver makes all the glucose you need from protein and fat. Added sugar supplies no nutrient you can't get elsewhere.
- Above small amounts it works against you: liver fat, high triglycerides and ApoB, uric acid, insulin resistance.

**Three rules**

1. **Drink zero sugar.** Soda, juice, sweet coffee and sports drinks are the biggest, fastest dose.
2. **"Natural" sugar is still sugar.** Honey, agave, maple, coconut, brown and raw sugar all count.
3. **Whole fruit is fine; juice is not.** Fiber and water slow fruit down.

> **GI** (glycemic index) = how fast a food raises blood sugar compared with pure glucose (100). Low is 55 or less, high is 70 or more. **Low GI does not mean safe** (see agave). See [[Glycemic Index — Good and Bad]].

## 1 · Real sugars: avoid as added sugar

| Sugar | Made of | Where | GI | Notes and negatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose | 1 unit | Blood, starch, corn syrup | 100 | The body's main fuel; triggers insulin. As added sugar it spikes blood sugar and insulin ✅ |
| Fructose | 1 unit | Fruit, honey, table sugar, HFCS | ~25 | Low GI only because it barely raises blood glucose. The gut handles small doses; big, fast doses spill to the liver and are turned into fat ✅ (Jang 2018). They also weaken the gut barrier (bacterial endotoxin leaks in) and raise uric acid, both driving inflammation and insulin resistance 🟢 (Bikman, lecture 171) |
| Galactose | 1 unit | Milk (inside lactose) | — | Turned into glucose by the liver ✅ |
| Sucrose (white, brown, raw, cane) | glucose + fructose, 50/50 | Cane, beet | ~63–65 | Same sugar whether white, brown, raw or "cane". Both harms at once: glucose raises insulin, fructose loads the liver. Tooth decay, weight gain ✅ |
| Lactose | glucose + galactose | Milk | ~46 | Natural in plain milk, not counted as added sugar. Many adults lose the enzyme (lactase) → bloating ✅ |
| Maltose | glucose + glucose | Malted grains, rice syrup | high | Digests to pure glucose; fast spike ✅ |

## 2 · Syrups and "natural" sugars: all still sugar

| Sweetener | What it really is | GI | Verdict and negatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agave syrup** | Mostly fructose: about 56% in blue agave, 70–85% in light syrups; more than soda's HFCS | 11–28 | 🔴 **Worst "natural" sugar.** Low GI only because it is mostly fructose. Maximum liver-fat and uric-acid load; sold as healthy |
| High-fructose corn syrup (HFCS) | Free glucose + fructose; 42% fructose in foods, 55% in sodas | ~ like sucrose | 🔴 Same harm as sugar, slightly more fructose; mostly in sodas and processed foods |
| Rice syrup | Mostly maltose and glucose | high | 🔴 Fast spike; can carry arsenic from rice (Jackson 2012) |
| Fruit juice / juice concentrate | Fruit sugar without the fiber | apple juice ~40 | 🔴 Soda-like fructose dose; linked to higher diabetes risk; WHO counts juice as free sugar |
| Honey | ~40% fructose, ~30% glucose, water, traces of antioxidants | 58 | 🟡 Full sugar calories; the antioxidants are too small to matter. Counted as free sugar by WHO |
| Maple syrup | Mostly sucrose, some minerals | 54 | 🟡 Same as sugar; the minerals don't offset it |
| Coconut sugar | About 70–80% sucrose | ~50 (few tests) | 🟡 Same as sugar; the "low GI 35" marketing claim was not confirmed |
| Brown sugar, raw, turbinado, muscovado | Sucrose + a little molasses | ~ like sucrose | 🟡 Identical to white sugar |
| Molasses | Leftover syrup from refining | — | 🟡 Some minerals, still sugar |
| Date sugar / date syrup | Ground or cooked dates | dried dates 62 | 🟡 Whole-date powder keeps some fiber; syrup loses it |

✅ WHO counts as "free sugars": all added sugars plus honey, syrups and fruit juice. It does **not** count sugar inside whole fruit, vegetables or plain milk (WHO 2015).

## 3 · Sugar alcohols (polyols): use with care

They are partly absorbed, so fewer calories and a smaller blood-sugar effect. FDA: they are 25–100% as sweet as sugar and do not cause tooth decay. **All cause gas, bloating and loose stools in larger amounts** ✅.

| Polyol | GI (Livesey 2003) | Sweetness vs sugar | Notes and negatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Erythritol | 0 | ~70% | 🟡 Highest blood levels went with about **2× the risk of heart attack, stroke or death** over 3 years (Witkowski 2023, observational). A 30 g erythritol drink made platelets clot more easily in healthy people (Witkowski 2024). The FDA said the 2023 study did not prove cause |
| Xylitol | 13 | ~100% | 🟡 Similar heart and clotting signal (Witkowski 2024). Protects teeth ✅. **Deadly to dogs** ✅. Laxative |
| Maltitol | 35 (syrups up to ~52) | ~90% | 🔴 Raises blood sugar the most of this group; strong laxative. The trap in "sugar-free" chocolate |
| Sorbitol | 9 | ~60% | Strongest laxative of the group; cramps |
| Mannitol | 0 | ~50–70% | Gas, diarrhea |
| Isomalt | 9 | ~50% | Hard candies; gas, bloating |
| Lactitol | 6 | ~35% | Gas, bloating |

## 4 · Rare sugars

| Sweetener | Notes | Negatives |
|---|---|---|
| Allulose | Found in tiny amounts in figs and raisins; ~70% as sweet as sugar. Absorbed but hardly used for energy. ✅ The FDA lets it be left out of "Total Sugars" and "Added Sugars" on labels and counts it as 0.4 calories per gram (sugar is 4). 🟢 Does not raise blood glucose or insulin | 🟡 Long-term human data are still limited. Stomach upset and bloating in large doses |
| Tagatose | Similar idea, rarely sold | 🟡 Little data; stomach upset |

## 5 · High-intensity sweeteners (FDA table)

| Sweetener | Type | Times sweeter than sugar | FDA ADI (mg/kg/day) | Notes and negatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stevia (high-purity steviol glycosides) | Plant extract, GRAS | 200–400 | 4 (as steviol) | 🟢 No blood-sugar effect. Bitter aftertaste; possible gut-bacteria effects (weak evidence). Only high-purity extracts are approved, not crude leaf |
| Monk fruit (luo han guo) | Plant extract, GRAS | 100–250 | not set | 🟢 No blood-sugar effect. Often mixed with erythritol — read the label — so it inherits erythritol's risks. Limited long-term data |
| Aspartame | Additive | 200 | 50 (JECFA: 40) | 🟡 IARC "possibly carcinogenic" (Group 2B, 2023, limited evidence); JECFA kept the safe limit. A 70 kg adult would need 9–14 cans of diet soda a day to pass it. Must be avoided with PKU ✅. Headaches reported by some; blinded trials mixed |
| Sucralose | Additive | 600 | 5 | 🟡 May disrupt gut bacteria. Breaks down under high heat; a 2023 lab study flagged a byproduct (sucralose-6-acetate, Schiffman 2023), so don't bake with it. Small, mixed studies suggest it may raise insulin in some people |
| Saccharin | Additive | 200–700 | 15 | Oldest. 🟡 Gut-bacteria disruption; bitter aftertaste. The old rat bladder-cancer scare was later cleared for humans |
| Acesulfame potassium (Ace-K) | Additive | 200 | 15 | Usually blended. 🟡 Possible gut effects; little independent long-term data |
| Neotame | Additive | 7,000–13,000 | 0.3 | 🟡 Very little independent long-term data |
| Advantame | Additive | 20,000 | 32.8 | 🟡 Very little independent long-term data |

✅ **Negatives of all non-sugar sweeteners — WHO 2023:** do not use them to control weight. This is a *conditional* recommendation based on low-certainty evidence. Swapping sugar for sweeteners did not reduce body fat long term, and long-term use may be linked to type 2 diabetes and heart disease, though that link may reflect who uses them. They may also keep sweet cravings alive. This does not apply to people who already have diabetes, and it does not cover sugar alcohols.

## How to choose: best to worst

1. **Best: no added sweetness.** Water, unsweetened coffee and tea, whole fruit. Your taste resets in a few weeks. ✅
2. **If you must sweeten:** a little stevia, pure monk fruit (no erythritol) or allulose; they have no blood-sugar effect. 🟢
3. **Use moderately:** erythritol and xylitol, until the heart questions are answered. 🟡
4. **Treat as sugar:** honey, maple, coconut, brown and raw sugar. Small amounts, rarely. 🟡
5. **Avoid:** agave, HFCS, rice syrup, juice, maltitol "sugar-free" sweets, and any sugar in a drink (soda, juice, sweet coffee). 🔴 Liquid sugar is the fastest, biggest dose.

## Key Corrections

- 🔴 "I need some sugar for energy" → your liver makes all the glucose you need. Added sugar is never required.
- 🔴 "Agave is healthier because it is low GI" → it is low GI because it is mostly fructose, which loads the liver.
- 🔴 "Raw, brown or coconut sugar is a health food" → still mostly sucrose.
- 🔴 "Honey is natural, so it's fine" → still free sugar, 40% fructose.
- 🔴 "Sugar-free means no effect on blood sugar" → maltitol raises it.
- 🔴 "Diet soda helps you lose weight" → WHO found no long-term weight benefit. Water beats both.

**Your body never needs added sugar. Cut the sugar you drink first, then the rest. Sweetness without sugar is possible, but the healthiest habit is simply less sweetness.**
