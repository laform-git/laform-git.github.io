# Every Sugar and Sweetener

*Every kind of sugar and sweetener in one place: what it is, how sweet it is, what it does to blood sugar, and what the evidence says about safety.*

**In plain words:** there are five families. Real sugars and syrups raise blood sugar and add calories. Sugar alcohols raise it a little or not at all. Allulose, a "rare sugar", barely counts. The very strong sweeteners (stevia, monk fruit, sucralose, aspartame) add sweetness with almost no calories. "Natural" on a label does not mean better: honey, agave and maple syrup are still sugar.

> **GI** (glycemic index) = how fast a food raises blood sugar compared with pure glucose (100). Low is 55 or less, high is 70 or more. See [[Glycemic Index — Good and Bad]].

---

## 1 · Real sugars (the building blocks)

| Sugar | Made of | Where | GI | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose | 1 unit | Blood, starch, corn syrup | 100 | The body's main fuel; triggers insulin ✅ |
| Fructose | 1 unit | Fruit, honey, table sugar, HFCS | ~25 | Low GI because it barely raises blood glucose; the gut handles small doses, big fast doses spill to the liver and are turned into fat ✅ (Jang 2018) |
| Galactose | 1 unit | Milk (inside lactose) | — | Turned into glucose by the liver ✅ |
| Sucrose (table sugar) | glucose + fructose, 50/50 | Cane, beet | ~63–65 | Same sugar whether white, brown, raw or "cane" ✅ |
| Lactose | glucose + galactose | Milk | ~46 | Many adults lose the enzyme → bloating ✅ |
| Maltose | glucose + glucose | Malted grains, rice syrup | high | Digests to pure glucose ✅ |

## 2 · Syrups and "natural" sugars

| Sweetener | What it really is | GI | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-fructose corn syrup (HFCS) | Free glucose + fructose; 42% fructose in foods, 55% in sodas | ~ like sucrose | 🔴 Mostly in sodas and processed foods |
| Agave syrup | Mostly fructose: about 56% in blue agave, 70–85% in light syrups | 11–28 | 🔴 Low GI only because it is mostly fructose; "natural" is marketing |
| Honey | ~40% fructose, ~30% glucose, water, traces of antioxidants | 58 | 🟡 Still sugar; counted as free sugar by WHO |
| Maple syrup | Mostly sucrose, some minerals | 54 | 🟡 Still sugar |
| Coconut sugar | About 70–80% sucrose | ~50 (few tests) | 🟡 The "low GI 35" claim was not confirmed |
| Brown sugar, raw, turbinado, muscovado | Sucrose + a little molasses | ~ like sucrose | 🟡 Same as white sugar |
| Molasses | Leftover syrup from refining | — | 🟡 Some minerals, still sugar |
| Date sugar / date syrup | Ground or cooked dates | dried dates 62 | 🟡 Whole-date powder keeps fiber; syrup does not |
| Rice syrup | Mostly maltose and glucose | high | 🔴 Fast spike |
| Fruit juice / juice concentrate | Fruit sugar without the fiber | apple juice ~40 | 🔴 WHO counts juice as free sugar |

✅ WHO counts as "free sugars": all added sugars plus honey, syrups and fruit juice. It does **not** count sugar inside whole fruit, vegetables or plain milk (WHO 2015).

## 3 · Sugar alcohols (polyols)

They are partly absorbed, so fewer calories and a smaller blood-sugar effect. Large amounts cause gas and loose stools ✅. FDA: they are 25–100% as sweet as sugar and do not cause tooth decay.

| Polyol | GI (Livesey 2003) | Sweetness vs sugar | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Erythritol | 0 | ~70% | 🟡 2023 study: highest blood levels went with about 2× heart attack and stroke risk over 3 years (Witkowski 2023). The FDA said this did not prove cause. |
| Xylitol | 13 | ~100% | 🟡 Similar 2024 signal (Witkowski 2024). Protects teeth ✅. **Very toxic to dogs** ✅ |
| Sorbitol | 9 | ~60% | Strong laxative effect |
| Mannitol | 0 | ~50–70% | |
| Isomalt | 9 | ~50% | Hard candies |
| Lactitol | 6 | ~35% | |
| Maltitol | 35 | ~90% | 🟡 Raises blood sugar the most of this group; common in "sugar-free" chocolate |

## 4 · Rare sugars

- **Allulose**: a sugar found in tiny amounts in figs and raisins. It is absorbed but hardly used for energy. ✅ The FDA lets it be left out of "Total Sugars" and "Added Sugars" on labels and counts it as 0.4 calories per gram (sugar is 4). 🟢 It does not raise blood glucose or insulin. 🟡 Long-term human data are still limited.
- **Tagatose**: similar idea, rarely sold.

## 5 · High-intensity sweeteners (FDA table)

| Sweetener | Type | Times sweeter than sugar | FDA ADI (mg/kg/day) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stevia (high-purity steviol glycosides) | Plant extract, GRAS | 200–400 | 4 (as steviol) | 🟢 No blood-sugar effect |
| Monk fruit (luo han guo) | Plant extract, GRAS | 100–250 | not set | 🟢 No blood-sugar effect; often mixed with erythritol — read the label |
| Aspartame | Additive | 200 | 50 (JECFA: 40) | 🟡 IARC "possibly carcinogenic" (Group 2B, 2023, limited evidence); JECFA kept the safe limit. A 70 kg adult would need 9–14 cans of diet soda a day to pass it. Avoid with PKU ✅ |
| Sucralose | Additive | 600 | 5 | 🟡 Gut-bacteria questions |
| Saccharin | Additive | 200–700 | 15 | Oldest; 🟡 gut-bacteria questions |
| Acesulfame potassium (Ace-K) | Additive | 200 | 15 | Usually blended |
| Neotame | Additive | 7,000–13,000 | 0.3 | |
| Advantame | Additive | 20,000 | 32.8 | |

✅ **WHO 2023:** do not use non-sugar sweeteners to control weight. Swapping sugar for them did not help weight long term, and long-term use may be linked to type 2 diabetes and heart disease. This does not apply to people who already have diabetes, and it does not cover sugar alcohols.

---

## How to choose

1. **Best:** less sweetness overall. Water, unsweetened coffee and tea, whole fruit. ✅
2. **If you sweeten:** small amounts of stevia, monk fruit or allulose have no blood-sugar effect. 🟢 Erythritol and xylitol: 🟡 use moderately until the heart questions are answered.
3. **Treat honey, maple, coconut sugar and brown sugar as sugar.** 🟢
4. **Avoid sugar in drinks** (soda, juice, sweet coffee) and agave. 🟢 Liquid sugar is the fastest, biggest dose.

## Key Corrections

- 🔴 "Agave is healthier because it is low GI" → it is low GI because it is mostly fructose.
- 🔴 "Raw, brown or coconut sugar is a health food" → still mostly sucrose.
- 🔴 "Sugar-free means no effect on blood sugar" → maltitol raises it.
- 🔴 "Diet soda helps you lose weight" → WHO found no long-term weight benefit.

**Sweetness without sugar is possible, but the healthiest habit is simply less sweetness.**
