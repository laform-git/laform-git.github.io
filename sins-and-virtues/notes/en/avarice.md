# Avarice & Generosity

*The fifth deadly sin, the grip of "more," and the lively virtue that opens the hand and frees the heart.*

**In plain words:** money and property aren't evil, and the Church even praises honest work, business and private property. Avarice (greed) is wanting riches **too much**, clinging to them out of fear, and forgetting that God gave them to us to share. What we clutch, we lose. The cure is **generosity**, because God Himself is generous: He gives everything away, and the more we give, the more alive we become.

## Where avarice sits

With avarice we reach the upper part of Dante's mountain. Bishop Barron explains that the last three sins (avarice, gluttony and lust) are **the least deadly**; things get lighter as we climb. 🟡 The tradition sees greed as a bridge: more serious than the bodily appetites because it can lead to injustice against others, yet less direct than the spiritual sins. St. Paul still warns: *"the love of money is the root of all kinds of evil"* (1 Timothy 6:10).

## What avarice is

### Wanting riches beyond reason

St. Thomas Aquinas defines avarice as **an unreasonable or immoderate desire for riches**. The Catechism condemns *"greed and the desire to amass earthly goods without limit"* (Catechism 2536).

### Wealth is not the problem

Barron is careful here. The popes' social teaching speaks well of private property (it goes with freedom), of the entrepreneurial spirit, and even of profit, a sign that a business is doing well. So when does wanting money become a sin?

### The key: the common good

Every modern pope quotes Aquinas: **owning** private property is fine, but in **using** it, we must always keep the common good first in mind. God made the world for everyone; we are His stewards. The Catechism calls this *"the universal destination of goods"* (Catechism 2402-2406).

- **St. Ambrose:** *"You have two shirts in your closet. One belongs to you; the other belongs to the man who has no shirt."*
- **St. Basil the Great:** *"The bread you store up belongs to the hungry; the cloak in your closet belongs to the naked."*
- **Pope Leo XIII:** once our needs are met and we live decently, *"the rest belongs to the poor."*
- **Cardinal Francis George** told a group of wealthy donors: *"The poor need you to draw them out of poverty, and you need the poor to keep you out of hell."* God gives us the poor so that, by serving them, we find our mission.

The real question for anyone blessed with money, says Barron, is: *Why did God give me this? How does He want me to use it for His purposes?* Greed refuses to ask.

### Greed is idolatry in disguise

Jesus put it plainly: *"You cannot serve God and money"* (Matthew 6:24). Greed doesn't just love money; it **serves** it, putting a created thing on God's throne. It makes people slaves of what they own. Aquinas lists what greed breeds: treachery, fraud, lying, restlessness, violence and a hard heart toward the poor.

### The prodigal son

*"Father, give me the share of the property that is coming to me"* (Luke 15:12). Barron points out how insulting this was: in effect, "I can't wait for you to die." Me, me, me. The son goes off to the "far country," in Greek *chōra makra*, **"the great empty place,"** and quickly wastes it all.

That's the spiritual law: when we cling out of fear, we lose. *"Whoever would save his life will lose it"* (Luke 9:24). God's life in us grows only when we **give it away**, as surely as a law of physics.

### The rich fool

Jesus told of a rich man who kept building bigger barns to store everything. God said to him: *"Fool! This night your soul is required of you"* (Luke 12:20). *"Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also"* (Matthew 6:21). And *"He who loves money will not be satisfied with money"* (Ecclesiastes 5:10).

### A money check-up

Barron suggests some honest questions:

- Does money play too big a role in my life? How much time do I spend **worrying** about it?
- Do I often **compare** what I have with what others have?
- How **painful** is it for me to let go of money?
- **Look at your bank statement as an examination of conscience.** Where did my money go last month? It says a lot about where my heart is.

🕯 **Reflect:** What am I holding too tightly: money, things, security? Like the rich young man, what would I be *unable* to give away? That's where greed is ruling.

## The cure: generosity

### Because God is generous

Bishop Barron calls the lively virtue **generosity**, and it starts with who God is. From all eternity, the Father pours Himself out to the Son, the Son gives Himself back to the Father, and the Holy Spirit is the love between them. Every time we make the Sign of the Cross, we name a **community of generous love**.

God didn't create the world because He needed anything. He created it, and keeps creating it this very moment, out of pure, generous love. Every virtue is a way of becoming like God, and generosity shows it most clearly.

### Jesus became poor for us

*"Though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich"* (2 Corinthians 8:9). To the rich young man He said: *"Go, sell what you possess and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven"* (Matthew 19:21).

### The widow's two coins

A poor widow put two tiny coins in the Temple treasury, *"everything she had,"* and Jesus said she gave more than all the rich (Mark 12:41-44). Generosity is measured not by the amount but by the **love and sacrifice**. Greed asks, *"How much can I keep?"* Generosity asks, *"How much can I give?"*

### Saints of the open hand

- **St. Francis of Assisi** married "Lady Poverty," giving up everything to be free for God and rich toward the poor. The emptier the hand, the fuller the heart.
- **St. Lawrence the Deacon**, ordered by the Roman authorities to hand over the Church's treasures, gathered the poor and sick and said: *"These are the treasures of the Church."*

### Freedom, not misery

The Church doesn't say everyone must be poor. What must go is not always the possession, but **the grip**. *"The precept of detachment from riches is obligatory for entrance into the Kingdom of heaven"* (Catechism 2544). Giving to the poor is *"a witness to fraternal charity"* and *"a work of justice pleasing to God"* (Catechism 2447).

### Mary in Bethlehem

Caesar Augustus lived in his palace on Rome's Palatine Hill. Mary gave birth to the King of kings in a dusty stable and laid Him in a manger (Luke 2:7), in a simplicity and poverty that came from her generous heart. Keep that picture in mind when you examine your heart about money.

🕯 **Reflect:** Can I give something away today that I'd rather keep (money, time, a possession) and feel the *freedom* in it?

## How to fight avarice

### Starve the sin

- **Give until it costs.** Not just leftovers; the widow gave what she would rather have kept.
- **Give first.** Set aside your giving off the top, before the money finds other uses.
- **Name your "bigger barns,"** the thing you're hoarding "just in case," and confess the grip (Catechism 2536).
- **Take a week off from buying** anything that isn't essential.
- **Stop comparing** your things with other people's.

### Grow the virtue

- **Give regularly, not just on impulse.** Every instinct and the whole culture say "hang on, make it bigger." Make it smaller.
- **Clean out your closet every month.** Remember Ambrose's two shirts, and give away something you actually *like*, not just the old thing you haven't worn in years.
- **"Buy the next one down."** Barron's favorite (and hardest) practice: find the car, couch or clothes you want and can afford, buy the next one down, and **give the difference to the poor**.
- **Put a "poor box" by your front door** and drop something in every time you leave the house, even a quarter, as a reminder that the poor are always with us.
- **Think of the common good** in every money decision: *How will this affect others?*
- **Give anonymously**, free from the reward of being noticed.
- **Serve the poor in person.** As St. Lawrence knew, they are the Church's treasure.
- **Pray each morning:** *"This is Yours, Lord, entrusted to me."*

🎯 **The one move that matters most:** when greed says *"keep it, you might need it,"* give that very thing away, *now*. Open the hand, and the heart follows.

**Greed clutches and ends up in the great empty place; generosity gives itself away and becomes rich like God.**
