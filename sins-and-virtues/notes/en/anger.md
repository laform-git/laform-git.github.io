# Anger & Forgiveness

*The third deadly sin, the burning wish to get even, and the brave, lively virtue that breaks the cycle.*

**In plain words:** feeling angry isn't always a sin. Jesus was angry at injustice too. Anger becomes a sin when it turns into **wanting to hurt someone back**. It blinds us and chokes our words. The cure is **forgiveness**, and it isn't weak. Forgiveness is the bravest thing a person can do: standing in the middle of the cycle of "you hurt me, I hurt you" and saying *stop*. Jesus did exactly that on the Cross, and He asks us to do it too, with gentleness (meekness) as its everyday form.

## Where anger sits

Anger is the **third** deadly sin on Dante's mountain. Bishop Barron jokes that there's enough anger in any room "to light up a whole city," because every one of us has been hurt.

🟡 St. Thomas Aquinas defines the sin as **an unreasonable desire for revenge**. The Catechism says the same: *"Anger is a desire for revenge"* (Catechism 2302).

## What anger is

### Good anger and bad anger

Not all anger is sinful. When Jesus drove the money changers out of the Temple (John 2:15), His anger was holy, directed at what was wrong. Aquinas says anger can even be praiseworthy *"when it is directed against sin and injustice, in accord with reason."*

The line is crossed when anger stops wanting **the wrong fixed** and starts wanting **the person hurt**. The Catechism warns: *"If anger reaches the point of a deliberate desire to kill or seriously wound a neighbor, it is gravely against charity; it is a mortal sin"* (Catechism 2302). And Jesus said: *"Everyone who is angry with his brother will be liable to judgment"* (Matthew 5:22).

### Anger grows

Aquinas lists what anger gives birth to: indignation, a "swelling" mind, shouting, insults, even blasphemy. It moves from the boiling heart, to the wounding word, to the violent hand. No sin destroys what love has built faster.

### Dante's picture: thick smoke

On Dante's mountain, the angry walk through thick, choking **smoke**, coughing and unable to see. That's what anger does:

- **It blinds us.** We can't see the situation clearly. (At how many family dinners has someone said, "Would you please talk to him? He's being so unreasonable!")
- **It chokes us.** We can't speak well; the words come out wrong.

### Peter and the servant's ear

When soldiers came to arrest Jesus, Peter pulled a sword and cut off the ear of the high priest's servant (John 18:10). The early Church Fathers saw a lesson here: anger **cuts off our ears**, so we stop listening to each other. And what did Jesus do? He immediately **healed the ear** (Luke 22:51). That's His work: reconnecting people.

> *"Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger."* — Ephesians 4:26
> *"A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."* — Proverbs 15:1
> *"Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God."* — James 1:19-20

🕯 **Reflect:** When I'm angry, do I want *the wrong made right*, or the person *hurt*? Which one am I really feeling?

## The cure: forgiveness and meekness

### Forgiveness is at the heart of the Gospel

Bishop Barron calls the lively virtue against anger **forgiveness**, and it's everywhere in Jesus' life:

- In the prayer He taught us: *"Forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us."*
- To the paralyzed man: *"Your sins are forgiven... get up and walk."*
- After rising from the dead, He breathed on His disciples and sent them to forgive sins (John 20:22-23).
- How often should we forgive? *"Not seven times, but seventy times seven"* (Matthew 18:22). In other words, always.

God's pattern is beautiful. Peter fell at Jesus' feet: *"Depart from me, for I am a sinful man"* (Luke 5:8). Isaiah cried, *"I am a man of unclean lips"* (Isaiah 6:5). Both times God answered, in effect: *Yes, I know you're a sinner. Now I have a job for you.* Forgiveness doesn't just wipe the slate clean. It sets us free for a mission. If forgiveness is God's way, it has to be ours.

### Forgiveness is brave, not wimpy

Forgiveness is more than "I'd like to get back at you, but I'll let it go." That's a good start. But real forgiveness, says Barron, is **active and courageous**: it steps right into the middle of the cycle of *you hurt me, I hurt you back, you hurt me again...* and breaks it. You let the other person's anger spend itself, and you **don't answer in kind**, and so the anger runs out.

### Three brave examples

- **Mahatma Gandhi**, as a young law student in England, read the Sermon on the Mount for the first time and was amazed. He felt Christians had this great teaching but didn't use it. He did: "love your enemy" became the heart of his peaceful struggle, first in South Africa, then in India.
- **Rev. Martin Luther King Jr.** knew Gandhi and Matthew 5 well. To end segregation, he sent people simply to sit at "whites only" lunch counters. They were spat on, cursed, hit and arrested, and they didn't run and didn't hit back. Then five more came, and five more, until the power of hatred wore itself out.
- **The Amish families of Nickel Mines, Pennsylvania** (2006) lost their children to a gunman who then took his own life. Their pain and anger must have been unimaginable. Yet almost immediately they went to the gunman's family to offer forgiveness and comfort. That's the courage that stops the cycle.

### Jesus on the Cross: the greatest forgiveness

Barron compares Jesus' teaching to the martial art **aikido**: when someone strikes, you don't run away and you don't give in. You stand your ground, step out of the line of the blow, and let its force fall harmlessly to the ground. Meet fire with fire, and the fire grows. Meet it with forgiveness, and it goes out.

On the Cross, all the hatred, violence and injustice of the world came roaring at Jesus. He could have called down legions of angels. Instead He let it all spend itself on Him and prayed, *"Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do"* (Luke 23:34). That's why we call Him the **Lamb of God who takes away the sins of the world**.

### Meekness: strength under control

The everyday face of forgiveness is **meekness**, which is not weakness. It is strength under God's control. Aquinas says meekness *"restrains the onslaught of anger"*: the meek person can still be angry at injustice, but their anger obeys.

The only time Jesus describes His own heart in the Gospels, He says: *"Learn from me, for I am gentle and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls"* (Matthew 11:29). The One who could command armies of angels stood silent before His accusers. And He promised: *"Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth"* (Matthew 5:5).

- **St. Francis de Sales**, the gentlest of saints: *"Nothing calms an angry elephant like the sight of a little lamb; nothing stops a cannonball like wool."* He also taught us to be gentle **with ourselves**. Raging at our own faults is just anger turned inward.
- **St. Thomas More**, about to be executed, kept his peace and even joked kindly with his executioner.

### Mary in the Temple

When Mary and Joseph lost the twelve-year-old Jesus for three days, then found Him in the Temple (Luke 2:48), any parent would have been frightened and angry. Mary's words were mild. As St. Paul says, speak only *"what is good for building up... that it may give grace to those who hear"* (Ephesians 4:29).

🕯 **Reflect:** Can I be *strong* without being *harsh*? When I'm wronged today, can I fix the wrong firmly, without wishing harm on the person?

## How to fight anger

### Starve the sin

- **Pause before you speak.** Anger lives in the instant reaction; one breath breaks its momentum.
- **Never send it while you're hot.** Wait on the text, the email, the confrontation.
- **Find the real wound.** Anger often covers hurt or fear underneath.
- **Confess anger by its fruits:** the harsh word, the cold silence, the slammed door (Catechism 2302).
- **Stop replaying the offense.** Rehearsing the grievance feeds the fire.

### Grow the virtue

- **Take one concrete step today to heal a broken relationship.** If family members aren't speaking, get in the way of that. Write the note, make the call.
- **Forgive quickly.** The Amish families' gift was speed; brooding lets anger grow. *"Do not let the sun go down on your anger."* If you come to Mass carrying a grudge, Jesus says go and reconcile first (Matthew 5:23-24).
- **Block gossip.** St. Augustine had a sign over his dinner table: *"No one at this table speaks ill of his brother,"* and he enforced it. When others start tearing someone down, say something good about them.
- **Answer softly on purpose** (Proverbs 15:1).
- **Pray for the person who angered you**, right away, by name.
- **Be gentle with yourself**, too.
- **Meditate on Matthew 11:29** until Jesus' gentle heart becomes yours.

🎯 **The one move that matters most:** in the hot moment, *say nothing and take one breath.* Anger needs the instant reaction; it dies in the pause.

**Anger keeps the fire of hurt burning from person to person; forgiveness steps into the fire and puts it out.**
