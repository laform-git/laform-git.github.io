# Sloth & Zeal

*The fourth deadly sin, boredom with God, and the lively virtue that sets the heart on fire for its mission.*

**In plain words:** sloth isn't just being lazy. A person can be busy all day long and still be slothful. Sloth is **boredom with God**: a heavy "why bother?" about prayer, Mass and the spiritual life. Bishop Barron's cure is **zeal for the mission**. Everyone God touches, He also sends. Each of us was made for a definite purpose, and when we find it, the heaviness lifts and life catches fire.

## Where sloth sits

Sloth is the **fourth** deadly sin. On Dante's mountain it sits exactly halfway up, which makes it the very center of his whole poem. 🟡 The tradition counts it among the serious "spiritual" sins, because it attacks our friendship with God directly. And it's the most misunderstood of the seven.

The great Protestant theologian Karl Barth even thought sloth, not pride, was the deadliest sin. Pride, envy and anger usually crash, and in the crash people often come back to God. Sloth just sits there, bored and indifferent: *"I couldn't care less."*

## What sloth is

### Not laziness: sadness about God

The Catechism says spiritual sloth (*acedia*) *"goes so far as to refuse the joy that comes from God and to be repelled by divine goodness"* (Catechism 2094). St. Thomas Aquinas calls it *"sorrow about spiritual good"*: the good things of God are right there, but they make us feel heavy and tired instead of glad.

That's why you can be full of energy for work, sports, business or family, and still be slothful. Sloth is boredom and heaviness **toward God**.

### The "noonday devil"

The early monks called it the **noonday devil** (from Psalm 91:6). Picture a hot afternoon after a big lunch. Bishop Barron thinks of his 1:15 p.m. class at the seminary, when he's ready to teach and everyone else is ready for a nap. The desert monks described a monk at midday, restless, staring at the sun, unable to pray, wanting to be anywhere but where God put him. Sloth is the voice that whispers *"Why bother?"*

### What sloth looks like today

Aquinas says sloth gives birth to despair, faint-heartedness, sluggishness about God's commandments, bitterness, and a mind that **wanders** after empty things. Today that often looks like endless scrolling, constant noise, and never being able to sit still with God. Sloth often wears the mask of busyness.

Scripture names its signature: **lukewarmness**. *"Because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will spit you out of my mouth"* (Revelation 3:16). Not hatred of God, not burning love, just a shrug.

### When "nothing is true," nothing matters

Barron points to a big cause of sloth today: **relativism**, the idea that "it's true for you, but not for me." Pope Benedict XVI warned about it often. If nobody can know what's really true and good, then *who cares?* And indifference sets in.

Barron noted that around 70% of American Catholics don't go to Mass regularly, even though the Church calls the Eucharist *"the source and summit"* of Christian life. There are many reasons, he admits, but he suspects much of it is this kind of spiritual indifference.

### Chesterton's walls

G. K. Chesterton imagined children playing football on a high cliff, protected by strong walls all around. They run and throw with joyful abandon, because the walls keep them safe. Then someone says, "Tear down those walls! Don't limit their freedom!" Now the children huddle in the middle, afraid to move. When we knock down the truth, we don't become freer; we become frozen.

🕯 **Reflect:** Where has my faith gone *lukewarm*? What good thing of God (prayer, Mass, Confession, serving others) do I now approach with a heavy "why bother"?

## The cure: zeal for the mission

### Everyone God touches, He sends

Bishop Barron calls the lively virtue against sloth **zeal for the mission**. In the Bible there's no exception: **nobody meets God without being sent.**

- **Moses** sees the burning bush: *Go, set my people free.*
- **Isaiah** sees God's glory and feels unworthy; his lips are cleansed: *Go.*
- **Jeremiah** says, "I'm too young!" God answers: *Don't say that. Go.*
- **Peter** says, "Leave me, I'm a sinner." *Yes, I know. Now come, I have a mission for you.*
- **Saul** is knocked to the ground on the road to Damascus and sent to the whole world as **St. Paul**.

Right now God is loving you into existence. That should free you to say, *"Here I am, Lord. Send me."*

### You were made for something definite

St. John Henry Newman wrote that each of us has been created *"to do Him some definite service."* No two of us are alike, because a personal God made each one for a particular purpose: to carry His love in a way only you can. Finding that mission lights a fire that sloth can't survive.

### Diligence: love in motion

The tradition also names the cure **diligence**, from the Latin *diligere*, "to love." Diligence isn't just staying busy; it's **love in motion**. The Catechism describes acedia as *"a form of depression due to lax ascetical practice, decreasing vigilance, carelessness of heart,"* and prescribes *"humility, trust, and perseverance"* (Catechism 2733).

- **The desert rule: stay.** When the noonday devil said "leave, quit, go somewhere else," the monks' answer was simple: *don't leave your cell.* Stay at the prayer, stay at the duty, especially when it feels dry.
- **Look at God more deeply.** Aquinas says sloth is sadness about God's goodness because we don't really *see* it. One real glimpse of how good God is, and the heaviness lifts.
- **St. Benedict:** *"Idleness is the enemy of the soul."* His cure was a steady rhythm of **prayer and work** (*ora et labora*). Structure carries us when feelings fail.

### Mary went in haste

On Dante's mountain, the slothful have to **run**, nonstop, like on a treadmill. As they run, they hear the words of Luke's Gospel about Mary: right after the angel's message, *"Mary set out and went with haste into the hill country"* to help her cousin Elizabeth (Luke 1:39). She knew her mission, and she hurried to do it. That's zeal.

🕯 **Reflect:** What spiritual practice am I tempted to give up because it feels dry? Can I return to it today, not for the feeling, but out of love?

## How to fight sloth

### Starve the sin

- **Don't run away.** When sloth says "skip it, later," stay at the prayer or the duty.
- **Keep your appointment with God**, even when it's dry. Faithfulness in dryness is the whole battle.
- **Name lukewarmness in Confession.** Sloth likes to hide as "I'm just tired" (Catechism 2733).
- **Close the escape hatches:** the scrolling, the noise, the busyness that runs from stillness.
- **Recognize the "why bother?"** as the noonday devil, and do the opposite.

### Grow the virtue

- **Pray for your mission.** Ask every day: *"Lord, what do you want me to do? Send me."* Then watch. God usually answers through people, opportunities and signs.
- **Read Scripture with that question.** Find a good spiritual director and ask it together.
- **Go to Mass.** Feeling lost is no surprise if we stay away from the Eucharist, the Word and the Church. Make the Eucharist the center of your week.
- **Practice the works of mercy.** Dorothy Day said everything a Christian does each day should connect to them:
  - *Corporal:* feed the hungry, give drink to the thirsty, shelter the homeless, visit prisoners, care for the sick, clothe the naked, bury the dead. Make dinner for someone, bring cookies to a nursing home, invite a lonely neighbor to supper, volunteer at a shelter, visit someone who's sick.
  - *Spiritual:* pray for the living and the dead, teach, counsel the doubtful, correct sinners gently, bear patiently with difficult people, comfort the sorrowful, forgive offenses. Spend time with that difficult or lonely person; maybe that's part of your mission.
  - Barron's promise: do these regularly, and God will show you your mission.
- **Build a simple rule of life:** set times for prayer and Scripture (*lectio divina*).
- **Start small and finish.** One completed act of devotion beats the paralysis of "it's too much."

🎯 **The one move that matters most:** when sloth whispers *"skip it, why bother,"* do the very thing right away, before the feeling decides. Show up. The feeling follows faithfulness, never the other way around.

**Sloth sits bored in the middle of the mountain; zeal hears "Go!" and runs like Mary into the hill country.**
