# Pride & Humility

*The first of the seven deadly sins and the lively virtue that heals it — the root of every other sin, and the door to a bigger, freer, more joyful life.*

**In plain words:** pride is putting yourself in God's place: *my plan, my rules, my life.* It is the oldest sin and the root of all the others. It promises greatness but leaves us small, anxious and, as Bishop Barron says, *bored*. Humility is not thinking badly of yourself. It is seeing the truth: everything good in you is a gift, and God has a great adventure for your life. The humble person is free, light and fully alive.

## Where pride sits

Pride is **the first and worst** of the seven capital sins — the "root and queen" of them all. 🟡 St. Thomas Aquinas calls it "the beginning of all sin": inside every sin is a heart turning away from God toward itself, and that is pride in seed form.

The Church calls these seven sins **capital** — from the Latin *caput*, "head" — *"because they engender other sins, other vices"* (Catechism 1866). The Catechism lists them: pride, avarice, envy, wrath, lust, gluttony and sloth. Pride comes first.

Bishop Barron pictures the seven sins the way the poet Dante did, as seven terraces on a holy mountain we climb toward God. Pride is at the very bottom: the heaviest place, where it is hardest to move.

## What pride is

### Making yourself God

The very first temptation was *"you will be like God"* (Genesis 3:5). The Catechism says Adam and Eve wanted to be like God, *"but without God, before God, and not in accordance with God"* (Catechism 398). Seven words sum up pride: **like God, but without God.**

- **Lucifer**, one of God's greatest angels, said *"Non serviam"* — *"I will not serve."* I will decide what is good and evil, not God.
- **St. Michael** answered him. His name means *"Who is like God?"* When we are tempted to run the universe ourselves, we can hear Michael's question gently laughing at us.
- **Adam and Eve** reached for the fruit of the knowledge of good and evil: the power to decide right and wrong, which belongs only to God. Every time we say *"It's my life, I'll decide what's right,"* we are reaching for the same fruit.
- As G. K. Chesterton put it, we're all in the same boat, and we're all seasick. Every one of us struggles with this.

### Four steps down

St. Gregory the Great, as Aquinas explains, describes four steps of pride, each worse than the last:

1. Thinking your goodness comes **from yourself**.
2. Admitting God gave it, but thinking you **earned** it.
3. Boasting of good things you **don't actually have**.
4. **Looking down on others**, wanting to be above everyone.

Pride starts as a small lie about where our gifts come from and ends in contempt for other people.

> *"The beginning of pride is when one departs from God."* — Sirach 10:12
> *"God resists the proud, and gives grace to the humble."* — 1 Peter 5:5

### It even hides in good deeds

St. Augustine said all of history is the story of two loves: *love of self, even to contempt of God*, and *love of God, even to contempt of self* (City of God XIV.28). He also warned that pride is sneaky: *other sins attach to bad deeds, but pride attaches even to good deeds, so that they are lost.* You can do something kind and then spoil it by needing everyone to notice.

### Pride makes life boring

This is Bishop Barron's surprising point. We are made to let God's love flow through us into the world: *"the high adventure of the spiritual life."* Pride shrinks life down to *me, mine, my little projects.* That is why Dante put **ice**, not fire, at the very bottom of hell: Satan frozen to the waist, unable to move, weeping. Proud people can look like they're on top of the world, but inside they are stuck, sad and bored.

**Think of a baseball field full of kids** with bats, balls and bases. Say "play any way you want," and you'll get arguing, then boredom. Bring in a good coach who teaches them how to hit, catch and win as a team, and suddenly it's an adventure. *"I will not serve"* is the most boring sentence in the world. *"I will serve"* opens the door to the adventure God has planned for you.

### Pride inside your head

Compare two moments:

1. You're in a great conversation with a friend, so caught up in it that you forget yourself. That's joy.
2. You're watching yourself talk: *Do I sound smart? What does she think of me? Why did I say that?* That's misery.

In the first, you're living in the real world. In the second, you're locked in the little box of your own ego. Pride always pulls us away from what's real.

🕯 **Reflect:** Which of the four steps is closest to my heart right now? Where am I living "like God, but without God," taking credit for gifts I only received?

## The cure: humility

### What humility really is

Humility is **not** "poor little me, I'm worthless." That's false humility, and it is often pride in disguise. The word comes from the Latin *humus*: **the earth, the ground.** Humility means being grounded in **reality**: seeing the truth about God, about things and about yourself. As St. Teresa of Ávila put it, *"Humility is truth."*

And the deepest truth is wonderful: God is love, everything good in you is His gift, and He has a mission for your life bigger than you can imagine.

The Catechism calls humility *"the foundation of prayer,"* because before God we come as beggars with open hands (Catechism 2559). Pride is a clenched fist that can't hold anything God offers. Humility is an open hand.

### Humble people are strong

Think of **Mother Teresa** or **St. John Paul II**. Nobody would call them shy or weak. They knew exactly who they were and what they were called to do, because they were rooted in God. The humble have a *great soul* (*magna anima*), connected to the source of all life. The proud live with a *small soul* (*pusilla anima*), curled up around themselves.

### Dante's heavy stones

On Dante's mountain, the proud carry huge boulders on their backs. The stone is the weight of the ego we all drag around: *How am I doing? What do they think of me?* But the stone also bends them down to the ground, to the *humus*, to reality. It is a "lovely burden" that teaches them humility.

### The models: Jesus and Mary

**Jesus.** God answered the pride of Eden with the humility of Bethlehem and the Cross. *"He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant... and humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross"* (Philippians 2:7-8). Adam reached up to grab; Christ bent down to serve.

**Mary.** When the angel told her she would be the Mother of God, she said: *"Be it done to me according to your word"* (Luke 1:38). Not *my* word, *Your* word. Her *Magnificat* sings: *"He has looked upon the humility of his servant"* (Luke 1:48). That's not the voice of someone weak. It's the voice of a young woman ready for the greatest adventure in history.

### Saints to remember

- **St. Augustine:** *"Do you wish to rise? Begin by descending."* The taller the tower, the deeper the foundation must be.
- **St. Thérèse of Lisieux:** her "Little Way" is to become small, trust like a child, and let God lift you up instead of climbing on your own.

🕯 **Reflect:** Can I accept one correction today without defending myself? Where is God asking me to "begin by descending," to take the lower place He took first?

## How to fight pride

### Starve the sin

- **Go to Confession** often, and name pride specifically, not vaguely (Catechism 1456-1458).
- **Don't defend yourself** the next time someone corrects you. Let them have the last word.
- **Do one hidden good deed** that nobody will ever find out about.
- **Catch the voice** in your head that compares you with others, call it pride, and let it go.
- **Stop announcing your good deeds.** When you want to mention something nice you did, don't.

### Grow the virtue

- **Take the lower place on purpose.** Give someone else the better seat, the credit, the spotlight. Recommend someone else for the job you think you deserve. Bishop Barron borrows Aristotle's image: to straighten a bent stick, you bend it the other way.
- **Learn to love simple things.** Watch a small child crouched on the floor, fascinated by a bug. That's humility: close to the ground, close to reality, and full of real joy.
- **Start each day with thanks.** Name three gifts before you make a single plan.
- **Pray the Litany of Humility** (Cardinal Merry del Val) slowly, once a week.
- **Think about Christmas and the Cross** (Philippians 2:7-8). With God, the way up is down.
- **Ask someone you trust** where they see pride in you, and just listen.

🎯 **The one move that matters most:** when pride whispers *"you earned this,"* answer right away: *"I received this."* Make that switch a hundred times a day, and you're winning the whole battle.

**Pride says "I will not serve" and ends up frozen; humility says "Be it done to me" and sets out on God's great adventure.**
