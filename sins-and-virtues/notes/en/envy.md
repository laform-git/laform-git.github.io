# Envy & Admiration

*The second deadly sin, the sadness that someone else is doing well, and the lively virtue that turns it into joy.*

**In plain words:** envy is feeling sad when someone else does well, and secretly glad when they fail. It grows from fear: forgetting that we are loved by God just as we are. It hurts nobody more than the envious person. The cure is **admiration**: seeing every good thing in others as God's gift, praising it, and celebrating it as if it were our own. After all, in God's family it is.

## Where envy sits

Envy is the **second** deadly sin, right next to pride at the bottom of Dante's mountain, where things are heaviest. Bishop Barron calls it **pride's closest daughter**. 🟡 St. Thomas Aquinas ranks it among the gravest sins because it attacks love itself: love rejoices in another's good, and envy grieves over it.

## What envy is

### Sad at someone else's good

The Catechism defines envy as *"sadness at the sight of another's goods"* (Catechism 2539). Aquinas says it in five words: *sorrow at another's good.* It isn't just wanting what someone has. It's being unhappy that **they** have it, as if their good took something away from you.

The novelist Gore Vidal admitted it honestly: *"When a friend of mine succeeds, something in me dies."* Notice that it's usually not strangers across the world who bother us. It's the brother, the coworker, the neighbor, the friend: people close to us.

### Its twin: secret delight in failure

The Germans have a word for the other side of envy: *schadenfreude*, pleasure at someone else's trouble. When we hear good news about a rival, we say, "That's great, I'm so happy for him." When we hear bad news, we lean in: "Really? Sit down, tell me everything." We'd never say it out loud, but we've all felt it.

### The saddest sin

Every other sin at least *promises* some pleasure: gluttony has its feast, lust its thrill. Envy gives nothing. It only gnaws. *"A sound heart is the life of the body, but envy rots the bones"* (Proverbs 14:30). St. John Chrysostom said envy eats a person *"as a moth eats a garment."*

### Where it comes from: fear

Bishop Barron says the root is **fear**. We forget that we are valuable because God loves us into existence, not because of our trophies and praise. So we feel we *need* the attention, and when someone else gets it, something in us dies. Envy is pride's daughter: we keep looking at ourselves and comparing. "If I stopped looking at me for five seconds," says Barron, "I wouldn't be envious."

### Envy drives the story of the world

- **Scripture:** envy led **Cain to kill Abel**, the first death in human history (Genesis 4). It set **Jacob** against Esau, **Joseph's brothers** against Joseph (they threw him in a well and sold him), **King Saul** against young David, and even the apostles against James and John.
- **Literature:** in Shakespeare, Iago destroys Othello out of jealousy, and Macbeth murders King Duncan.
- **The devil's own sin:** *"Through the devil's envy death entered the world"* (Wisdom 2:24).

That's why these sins are called **capital**, from the Latin *caput*, "head": they are the heads of many other sins. The Catechism, quoting the saints, says envy gives birth to *"hatred, detraction, calumny, joy caused by the misfortune of a neighbor, and displeasure caused by his prosperity"* (Catechism 2539).

### Envy's favorite game: scapegoating

What do we do with someone we envy? We find fault, blame them, talk behind their back, and try to "bring them down a peg." Barron points to the morning coffee group: first sports, then the weather... and then someone gets torn apart, very often someone the group envies.

In Herman Melville's story *Billy Budd*, a kind, gifted, generous young sailor comes aboard a ship, and slowly the crew turns against him until he is killed. Barron calls it the "Billy Budd syndrome": when someone with real gifts shows up, something in us wants to cut them down.

### Dante's picture: sewn-shut eyes

On Dante's mountain, the envious sit along the cliff with their **eyelids sewn shut**, the way falconers once closed the eyes of hunting hawks. All their lives they kept looking around: *Is he doing better than me? Worse? Good.* Now they must stop looking at others that way.

🕯 **Reflect:** Whose success quietly stings me? When I hear good news about someone, is my first reaction joy, or a small inner flinch? That flinch is envy, and it's asking to be named.

## The cure: admiration and brotherly love

### We are one body

The deepest truth, says Barron, is that we are all connected. The same God who is loving you into existence right now is loving every person around you. St. Paul says we are one body: *"If one member is honored, all the members rejoice together"* (1 Corinthians 12:26).

Imagine the liver being jealous of the stomach and trying to sabotage it. The whole body would fall apart! In a healthy body, every part rejoices in the others. The saint says: **when you succeed, I succeed; when you suffer, I suffer.**

### Admiration: everything is a gift

Bishop Barron calls the lively virtue against envy **admiration**. Every gift anyone has comes from God. "I earned this with hard work!" Wonderful, but where did your ability to work hard come from? Your mind? Your willpower? It's all gift. So why compete? Look around and **admire** what God has done in others, and yes, in yourself too: *Thank you, Lord. Use this gift through me.*

Barron's favorite verse for this, worth putting on the refrigerator: *"It is you, O Lord, who have accomplished all we have done"* (Isaiah 26:12).

### Love that rejoices

St. Paul's famous description of love begins with this: *"Love is patient, love is kind. Love does not envy"* (1 Corinthians 13:4). The Catechism says we fight envy *"through good-will, humility, and abandonment to the providence of God"* (Catechism 2554).

**St. Thérèse of Lisieux** saw the Church as God's garden: He wants big roses and tiny wildflowers, each beautiful in its own place. Wanting to be someone else's flower means missing the beauty God planned in **you**.

### God made us different on purpose

Are we all equally talented, beautiful or smart? Of course not. Barron says God made a beautifully *unequal* world **because each of us has a different mission**. Envy wants to "level everyone down." Instead, celebrate the differences and focus on your own unique role.

### Mary at Cana

At the wedding in Cana, the wine ran out, an embarrassing disaster for the hosts. An envious guest might whisper, "Did you hear? They ran out of wine! Our party was better." Mary did the opposite. She told Jesus, *"They have no wine,"* and told the servants, *"Do whatever he tells you"* (John 2:3-5). No gossip, no secret pleasure: just love that hurries to help.

🕯 **Reflect:** Can I praise, out loud and sincerely, someone whose gift I envy? Praising the very thing I envy is how love breaks envy's grip.

## How to fight envy

### Starve the sin

- **Name the specific sting.** Envy likes to hide; bring it into the light in Confession (Catechism 2540).
- **Catch comparison** the moment it starts. Comparison is envy's fuel.
- **Bless what you envy.** When the sting comes, say a quick prayer *for* that person.
- **Cut back on what feeds comparison**, like social media feeds and status-watching.
- **Stop the subtle complaints** about other people's advantages. Envy often speaks through "innocent" criticism.

### Grow the virtue

- **Praise the person you envy, on purpose.** Write a note, make a call, tell others about what they've accomplished.
- **Break up the gossip.** When a group starts tearing someone down, throw a wrench in it by praising that person.
- **Rejoice by choice.** When good news comes, make an act of the will: *"Thank you, God, for their good."*
- **Pray for the people you envy by name**, every day. You can't keep resenting someone you pray for.
- **Do a hidden kindness** for the very person you envy.
- **Read 1 Corinthians 12.** We're one body, so their gift makes us all richer.

🎯 **The one move that matters most:** the instant envy stings, *bless that person*, out loud or in your heart. You can't envy and bless at the same time.

**Envy closes its eyes to everyone's gifts but its own; admiration opens them and finds God's goodness everywhere.**
