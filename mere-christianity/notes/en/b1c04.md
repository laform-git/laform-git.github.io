## The Insight

Two options, once you take the Moral Law seriously: the universe is blind matter that accidentally produced minds, or something mind-like stands behind the universe. Lewis's move is quiet but decisive — a rule that judges matter cannot itself just be another fact matter produced. Weather does not judge you. You do not feel guilty when it rains. But the Moral Law does judge you, constantly, which means whatever it's coming from has intentions, not just physics.

Science cannot adjudicate this one, and it's worth being precise about why: science watches how matter behaves. It has no instrument for detecting whether anything stands behind matter, issuing it commands. Asking science whether a mind is behind the universe is like asking a thermometer whether the room is beautiful — wrong tool, not a harder version of the same question.

## Why It Matters

This is the chapter where the argument stops being comfortable. You cannot hold "the Moral Law is real" and "there's nothing behind it with a stake in how I act" at the same time without contradicting yourself. Most people manage it anyway, quietly, by never putting the two beliefs in the same room.

## Evidence / Support

Materialism says mind showed up by accident in blind matter. Theism says a mind is what's behind matter. Lewis sets aside vague "Life-Force" views as an unstable middle — comfort without the actual claim.

We cannot inspect the universe from outside — except through one window: ourselves. Inside, we do not just find behavior, we find a persistent sense we ought to behave differently. That "ought" is smuggled evidence of something that issues real commands.

Not mathematical proof — inference to the best explanation, same reasoning used everywhere else. A law that judges matter fits better with something behind matter than with matter alone.

## Meditations (3)

- You cannot climb outside the universe to see what's behind it — except through the one window you were given: yourself.
- A thermometer can tell you the temperature. It was never built to tell you if the room is beautiful.
- The gap between how you act and how you know you ought to act is not silence. It's a voice.

## Socratic Question

"If the universe is just matter obeying blind laws, why does it feel like something inside you is being judged, not just observed, every time you fall short?"
