## The Insight

Lewis will not let you land soft. Most people who follow "there's probably something behind the universe" assume that's automatically good news. He blocks the assumption on purpose: a real, demanding standard is only good news for someone actually meeting it. Four chapters just established that's nobody. So the honest first response to this argument is not comfort — it's unease, and Lewis wants you to actually feel that before he will let you move on.

Two facts, held at once without flinching: you know what you ought to do, and you do not do it. Most people quietly soften one side or the other — "morality's basically made up" (denying the first), or "I slip sometimes, but I am good overall" (softening the second). Lewis refuses both exits.

## Why It Matters

Check yourself here honestly: did this chapter leave you comfortable or unsettled? Comfort at this exact point in the argument usually means the logic got nodded along with, not actually felt. That's worth noticing before moving on, not after.

## Evidence / Support

Lewis blocks the vague, soft cosmic-force option — a being clever enough to invent "two and two makes four" and strong enough to make the universe was never obligated to also be undemanding. His word: we have been "cheeky" to assume otherwise.

No relief offered here on purpose — no gospel yet, no forgiveness. Christianity only lands as good news for someone who's understood the bad news first. Lewis's comparison: you do not want a doctor who reassures you before telling you what's wrong.

"Atheism is too simple" — cannot explain the Moral Law — but the honest alternative is not cozy either. This is the hinge into Book 2, where the same demanding Power does something about the mess it judges.

- A being clever enough to invent the universe was never obligated to also be soft.
