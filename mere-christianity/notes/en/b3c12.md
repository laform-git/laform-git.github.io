## The Insight

A tree is not judged fruit-first — the fruit follows from what the tree actually is. Lewis's deeper claim: you are not saved by an accumulation of good works, yet real faith reliably produces them, the way a healthy tree just does what healthy trees do. The handover this chapter describes is not one more belief added on top of the moral effort you were already making. It's ceasing to run that effort as a self-contained project at all, and relying on something outside yourself for the part the project could never finish alone.

Every virtue and rule since the Cardinal Virtues chapter was genuinely demanding. Lewis is not retracting any of it here — he's revealing that all that moral effort was always meant to end in this handover, not in successful self-completion. The rules were real. None of them were ever meant to be finished solo.

## Why It Matters

Take honest inventory across your business, your marriage, your health: where are you still quietly running the self-sufficiency project this chapter says has to be handed over rather than finished alone?

## Evidence / Support

Genuine faith — total reliance on Christ — produces good works as a natural consequence, not as the thing being judged first.

The deeper act of faith is not intellectual assent to more propositions — it's giving up the project of being good enough on your own steam.

Sets up Book 4 directly: giving up the separately-maintained self anticipates the claim that real spiritual life means a kind of death of the self-sufficient old self.
