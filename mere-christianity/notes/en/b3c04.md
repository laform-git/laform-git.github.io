## The Insight

"That's just how I am wired" answers a real question and then gets used to close a different one. Lewis splits the two apart: psychoanalysis can genuinely diagnose and sometimes repair the raw material a person starts with — temperament, complexes, disordered instinct. But it cannot make the choice about what to do with that material. That choice is the will's job alone, and it's the choice, not the finished psychological state, that gets judged.

Two people resist the identical temptation. One barely feels it. The other is fighting enormous internal pressure the whole time. If both simply comply, they are not doing equal moral work — the second may deserve more credit for a smaller, uglier external result. The ledger the world can see is not the one that actually counts.

## Why It Matters

Next time you explain away a choice with a psychological label, check what the explanation is actually doing — real diagnostic work, or cover for a decision you'd otherwise have to own.

## Evidence / Support

Two separate questions: what's the raw material like (psychoanalysis answers this), and what do you choose to do with it (only the will answers this).

Treatment and repentance are not rivals — fixing genuinely disordered material is compatible with, and can serve, the moral work of choosing rightly.

Judged on effort against your own starting material, not on a finished-character comparison to anyone else.

## Meditations (3)

- Two people can perform the identical act and be doing entirely different amounts of moral work.
- Fixing the material and choosing rightly with it are two different jobs, not one.
- The verdict compares you to what you started with, not to your neighbor's finish line.

## Socratic Question

"Where have you used 'that's just how I am' to end a conversation about a choice you are actually still free to make?"
