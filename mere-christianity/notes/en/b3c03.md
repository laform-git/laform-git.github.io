## The Insight

No five-point plan for taxes or government. Christianity hands over raw material instead — charity, honesty, work done for the community rather than personal gain — that could get built into very different social arrangements. That's not a gap Lewis is embarrassed by; it's what you'd expect from a teaching aimed at producing a certain kind of person rather than a certain kind of system. A specified political program can be followed by people whose hearts never changed. Lewis is betting on the slower, harder route: change the person, and the systems follow.

Three principles, almost nobody actually practices: work for the community, not just for money. Give until it actually costs you something. Treat authority — parent over child, employer over employee — as morally real, not an oppressive fiction. Put those three together and you get something economically Leftist and socially old-fashioned at once — which fits no party cleanly, and that's the point, not an oversight.

## Why It Matters

This confronts a live assumption on both sides of the political spectrum: the demand that Christianity endorse a specific economic program as "the Christian one." It is not running either play. Check where you have substituted a debate about the right system for the smaller, costlier personal change actually required of you.

## Evidence / Support

No detailed political blueprint exists — clergy who claim one in Christ's name are overstepping.

The work test: if it produces nothing of real use — pure salesmanship manufacturing wants rather than meeting needs — it fails, regardless of legality or profit.

The giving test, stated bluntly: if it never pinches your own comfort, you are probably giving too little.

The reason none of us live this way is not unclear principles — we have quietly exempted ourselves and redirected the energy into debating systems we do not control instead.

## Meditations (3)

- A system can be perfectly legal and still produce nothing anyone actually needed.
- If the giving never costs you anything, it probably is not giving yet.
- Old-fashioned about authority, radical about money — Lewis fits no party, and is not trying to.

## Socratic Question

"Where in your business or your own finances have you substituted a debate about the right system for the smaller, costlier change actually required of you personally?"
