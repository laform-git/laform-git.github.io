## The Insight

A child playing at being an adult is not lying. He's rehearsing what's coming. Lewis's test for telling the two kinds of pretending apart: a lie aims to make someone believe something false about the present. Rehearsal aims to make something true about the future. Same outward behavior, opposite direction of intent — which is why praying "Our Father" before you fully feel it can be either dishonest or genuinely formative, depending entirely on what it's aimed at.

This generalizes the Charity chapter's mechanism into the whole spiritual life: action tends to produce the corresponding inner state, not the other way around. "Putting on Christ" through consistent practice, even before the interior change feels complete, is the actual method — not a workaround for people who have not arrived yet, the method itself.

## Why It Matters

Name one specific virtue you have been waiting to feel before practicing — patience with an employee, generosity you do not feel like extending, forgiveness that has not landed emotionally yet — and test the rehearsal instead of the wait.

## Evidence / Support

Two kinds of pretending: deceiving versus practicing toward becoming the thing pretended — Christian "pretending" belongs to the second.

Praying "Our Father" is initially more aspiration than settled reality for most people — the act of praying it anyway is part of the mechanism by which the relationship becomes more real over time.

Lewis does not fully map where good rehearsal ends and empty performance begins — left for your own honesty to track.
