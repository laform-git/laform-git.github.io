## The Insight

The question assumes a scale. Lewis is questioning whether the demand belongs on one at all. Christianity was never offering a moderate, manageable self-improvement plan you could rate for difficulty — it demands the entire self at once. That sounds harder than partial reform. Lewis's actual claim is that it's simpler, because partial reform by your own effort was already shown, back in Book 3's Faith chapters, not to work at all.

Here's the counterintuitive mechanism: a partial, managed effort requires constant negotiation — deciding daily how much to give, tracking where the line sits, defending the boundary. Total surrender removes that negotiation entirely. Not by asking for less internal management — by ending the ongoing argument with yourself about where the limit should be. Giving everything, oddly, might require less ongoing upkeep than giving most things.

## Why It Matters

Check honestly: where has faith been budgeted as one more moderate category alongside diet, exercise, business discipline — rather than treated as the total demand Lewis is actually describing?

## Evidence / Support

Ranking Christianity hard-to-easy assumes a quantity of moral effort is being requested — Lewis rejects the premise entirely.

Total surrender is not a harder version of the partial-reform project — it's a different project, replacing rather than intensifying the failed one.

Whether total surrender is genuinely simpler in practice is asserted from testimony and argument, not independently demonstrable.
