## The Insight

"I do not feel love for this person" sounds like a dead end. Lewis turns it into an instruction instead: act as if you already did, and the feeling tends to follow the action rather than wait to precede it. Charity is primarily an act of will, not a feeling — which reverses the assumption that authentic action has to wait for authentic feeling to show up first.

The traffic runs both directions, and this is the part worth actually sitting with: every small unkindness toward someone does not just express dislike you already had, it manufactures more of it. You are shaping the direction of that traffic constantly whether or not you notice — which means the feeling you have toward someone right now is partly downstream of choices you have already made.

## Why It Matters

Name a specific person you are waiting to feel differently about before acting differently toward them. Reverse the order deliberately for a set period and watch what actually happens to the feeling.

## Evidence / Support

Doing someone a good turn tends to increase your liking for them; an ill turn increases dislike — action to feeling more reliably than the reverse.

Distinguished from natural affection — charity is a distinct, willed commitment to someone's real good that operates even where natural liking is thin.

Universal scope without equal fondness: loving everyone was never a demand to feel identical warmth toward all — it's a demand to will the real good of all.

## Meditations (3)

- Do not wait to feel it. Act as if you already do, and watch what the feeling does.
- Every unkind act you do to someone makes them a little harder to love tomorrow.
- Willing someone's good does not require finding them easy to be around.

## Socratic Question

"Is there someone you are waiting to feel differently about before you will act differently toward them — and what would change if you reversed the order?"
