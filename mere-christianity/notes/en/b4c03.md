## The Insight

An author is not rationing attention across chapters. Flip to any page of a novel and the author's full attention to that scene does not diminish attention to any other page, because the author was never inside the book's timeline the way characters are, queued through it moment by moment. Lewis uses this to dissolve "how can God attend to millions of prayers at once" — the objection assumes God is inside time like we are, distributing a limited reserve across a queue. Take that assumption away and the objection has nothing left to stand on.

Not because the question was foolish — because its picture of God's relationship to time was wrong from the start. God's relationship to every moment in created time is a single, undivided relationship to the whole, not sequential allocation. Freed from time's limits, what looks within a time-bound picture like a fraction of a finite resource is actually what a time-bound mind's best guess at undivided attention looks like from outside.

## Why It Matters

Notice how often this exact mistake shows up elsewhere — assuming a claim must operate under your own limits, not just in objections to prayer.

## Evidence / Support

The author-and-story analogy: an author relates to the entire story at once, not sequentially — this is how Lewis pictures God relating to all moments of created time without being queued through them.

Not offered to make prayer impersonal — the model preserves real, particular attention; it just relocates what "undivided" means.

A model, held with humility — the best available picture for resolving the objection, not a settled metaphysical certainty about divine eternity.

## Meditations (3)

- An author is not rationing attention across chapters. Consider what that does to the "too many prayers" objection.
- The objection assumed God was inside the queue. Check that assumption before accepting the objection.
- Undivided attention is not impossible — it's just not how a time-bound being could ever picture it working.

## Socratic Question

"What other objections to faith might you be holding that quietly assume God operates inside the same limits you do?"
