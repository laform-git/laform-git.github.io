## The Insight

A fleet of ships needs three things to succeed: do not collide with each other, keep each individual hull seaworthy, and actually be headed to the right harbor. Miss any one and the voyage fails even if the other two are flawless. Lewis maps morality the same way — fairness between people, harmony inside a person, and the purpose the whole thing is aimed at. Nearly all public moral talk lives entirely on the first level.

That's the trap worth naming directly: a society can achieve perfect fairness between its members while every individual in it is inwardly fragmented and pointed at nothing — ships that never collide, quietly falling apart, going nowhere. External fairness and internal order are different measurements. Improving one tells you nothing about the other.

## Why It Matters

Take honest stock of the three levels right now — how you treat others, the order of your own inner life, what you believe any of it is actually for — and name which one has been getting the least attention while you worked hardest on the other two.

## Evidence / Support

Level one (social morality) is genuinely necessary and gets nearly all the public attention — fairness, rights, not harming others.

Level two (inner harmony) usually gets filed as private psychological quirk rather than moral matter — envy, self-pity, disproportionate fear. Lewis insists it's moral, because a malfunctioning inner life eventually damages the fairness-to-others that level one cares about.

Level three (purpose) is the one modern moral thought avoids almost entirely — you cannot evaluate whether the fleet's seaworthiness even matters without knowing if it's headed anywhere real.

## Meditations (3)

- Ships that never collide can still be falling apart, each one alone in the dark.
- A private psychological quirk and a moral matter are not always different things.
- You cannot know if the voyage matters without first asking where the fleet is going.

## Socratic Question

"Of the three levels — how you treat others, the order of your own inner life, and what you believe you are actually for — which one have you been quietly neglecting while working hardest on the other two?"
