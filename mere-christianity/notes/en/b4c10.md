## The Insight

Judging spiritual standing by pleasantness is like judging two runners by finishing position without checking their starting line. One may have covered enormous ground and still finish rougher than someone who started ten yards from the tape. Lewis separates natural niceness — pleasant temperament from good health, easy upbringing, fortunate circumstances — from actual spiritual transformation. A naturally nice non-believer is not further along than a naturally difficult believer fighting hard, because judgment tracks the direction and cost of change, not the pleasantness of the finished surface.

This is the practical payoff of the Psychoanalysis chapter's material-versus-choice distinction, applied to a specific trap: natural niceness can let a person feel no urgent need for actual conversion, precisely because their unconverted state already looks acceptable to everyone, including themselves. Meanwhile someone whose temperament makes goodness a constant visible struggle may be doing more real work than their rougher exterior suggests.

## Why It Matters

Place yourself honestly: naturally easy to get along with in a way that's let you skip real interior work, or naturally difficult in a way you have been mistaking for lack of progress rather than the actual cost of real progress?

## Evidence / Support

Niceness is raw material, not achievement — inherited circumstance producing pleasantness with little moral effort, continuous with the material-versus-choice split from Ch4's chapter.

The comparison trap: judging the nice unbeliever favorably against the difficult believer mistakes a difference in starting material for a difference in distance actually traveled.

## Meditations (3)

- Niceness inherited at birth and virtue earned through struggle can look identical from the outside and mean something very different.
- The nicest person in the room might have the least distance left to travel — or the least distance already traveled.
- Compare yourself to where you started, not to someone else's easier starting material.

## Socratic Question

"Are you resting on natural temperament in some area of your life and mistaking it for real spiritual progress?"
