## The Insight

A 2D being trying to picture a 3D solid using only 2D categories would find it exactly as paradoxical as humans find the Trinity — not because the higher reality is incoherent, but because it's richer than the vocabulary available at the lower level. Lewis climbs the ladder from line to plane to solid to make one narrow point: each added dimension produces a genuinely richer unity, unpicturable from below. One God, three Persons is not a contradiction. It's a level of unity above ordinary one-personal human experience.

Notice what the analogy actually proves and what it does not. It does not demonstrate the Trinity is true. It demonstrates that "more unified than a single person, yet genuinely one" is not automatically incoherent — richer unity at a higher level is a real category, not a euphemism for nonsense, in mathematics and, Lewis argues, in theology too.

## Why It Matters

Notice how often "I cannot picture how that holds together" gets treated as proof it does not — well beyond theology. The same shortcut shows up in arguments about people, organizations, anything complex enough to resist a simple mental picture.

## Evidence / Support

Human difficulty with the Trinity comes from trying to picture "person" using only the one-personal template we directly experience — not being asked to compute 1=3, but to accept a personal unity beyond a single human person.

Prayer, on this account, is being caught up into the interior life of the Trinity itself — the Son's own relationship to the Father, extended to include you — not a message sent to a distant, single-personed God.

An analogy, not a proof — useful for showing the doctrine is not self-contradictory, not for fully explaining how it's true.

## Meditations (3)

- What looks like a contradiction from a lower vantage point might just be a richer unity you cannot yet picture.
- Prayer, on this account, is not a message sent outward — it's being drawn into a relationship already underway.
- The difficulty is not that God is less than reasonable. It's that our template for "person" is smaller than He is.
