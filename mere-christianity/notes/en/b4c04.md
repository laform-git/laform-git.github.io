## The Insight

You do not decide to catch a cold by studying someone who has one. Infection is not voluntary the way imitation is — it spreads through actual contact. Lewis picks that image deliberately: the Son's life has always been derived from the Father, and believers receive that same derived quality of life not by copying Christ's behavior but by union with Him — proximity, not effortful imitation, is the transmission mechanism.

This changes what the spiritual project is actually asking. Trying hard to behave as Christ behaved, using only your existing resources, is a fundamentally different project from actually receiving new life through union — and on Lewis's account, an insufficient one. What you have on your own resources was never going to be enough. That was rather the point.

## Why It Matters

Check your own spiritual effort honestly: mostly imitation-by-willpower, or actually pursuing the contact and union this chapter says is the real mechanism?

## Evidence / Support

An infection transmits a real state — it does not just describe one from outside. That's why "good infection" is the operative image, not decoration.

The Son's life has always been derived from, not self-generated apart from, the Father — this same pattern extends to believers becoming "little Christs," borrowed vitality, not self-manufactured.

A claim beyond empirical verification — held as the doctrine's own internal logic, not something independently confirmable.
