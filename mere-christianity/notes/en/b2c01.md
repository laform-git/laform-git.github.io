## The Insight

Here's the test hiding inside this chapter: does your version of God have the standing to disagree with you? Pantheism — God is everything, everything is God — cannot produce that, worked out consistently. If God and the torture chamber are equally "God expressing Himself," so is the hospital next door, and the word "good" has nowhere left to stand. Theism keeps the distance an artist has from a painting, which is exactly what lets the artist judge the villain in his own story.

Pantheism is attractive precisely because it's undemanding — nothing outside the system to be held accountable to, no one who could disagree with you from outside. That's the whole appeal, and it's also the whole problem. You bought comfort by spending coherence.

## Why It Matters

Most people who reject flat atheism drift toward some version of "God is in everything" without noticing it's a real position with its own bill to pay. Worth checking the God you actually operate from day to day: does He have any real capacity to disagree with your choices, or has He quietly been reshaped into something that only ever agrees?

## Evidence / Support

Lewis's real dividing line: those who think whatever's behind the universe is beyond good and evil (pantheists) versus those who think it definitely takes sides (theists) — not atheism versus religion, but which kind of religion.

If God is simply identical with the universe, He's equally present in charity and cruelty — no way to call one more godly than the other. That dissolves the moral distinctions Book 1 spent five chapters proving real.

Theism's trade: God made the universe the way an author writes a book — distinct from it, not identical to it — which is what lets Him prefer some things in it over others without contradiction.

## Meditations (3)

- If God is equally present in the hospital and the torture chamber, the word "good" has nowhere left to stand.
- An author is not the same thing as the book he writes — that distance is what lets him judge his own villain.
- Comfort and coherence are not always the same purchase.

## Socratic Question

"If you strip away the parts of God that could disagree with you, is what's left still strong enough to have made the universe — or just strong enough to never bother you?"
