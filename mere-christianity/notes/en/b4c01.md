## The Insight

A carpenter and a chair share a workshop, not a nature. A father and son share the second. Lewis leans on that ordinary distinction to state something precise: God made the universe, but only begets the Son. Humans are made — biologically alive, what Lewis calls Bios, the kind of life that runs down by design. Through Christ, a person can become begotten instead — adopted into Zoe, God's own kind of life.

Here's the misreading this chapter forecloses: Zoe is not Bios intensified, a better version of the same life the way training improves fitness. It's categorically different. Which means "becoming a Christian" was never moral self-improvement within the life you already have — it's an offer of a genuinely different kind of life, full stop.

## Why It Matters

Check directly: has your own picture of spiritual growth quietly been "better version of the same life," when the actual claim on the table is something categorically different?

## Evidence / Support

Adoption is the mechanism — because humans are made, not begotten, Zoe is not automatic. It's an offer, received by grace, of what the Son has by nature.

The making/begetting language is a technical tool Lewis borrows to make a hard-to-state distinction precise — a clarifying analogy, not a literal biological claim about God.

## Meditations (3)

- A carpenter and a chair share a workshop, not a nature. A father and son share the second.
- Biological life eventually runs down by design. The life on offer here does not.
- Adoption is being brought into a relationship you were not born into — not merely improved circumstances.
