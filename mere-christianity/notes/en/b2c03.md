## The Insight

A thermostat cannot choose to turn on the heat. It also cannot choose to withhold it. That's the whole argument in one object: take away the possibility of choosing wrong, and you have taken away the possibility of choosing right in any meaningful sense too — you are left with mechanism wearing the language of virtue. God wanted sons, not thermostats. Getting real sons meant accepting they might rebel, which is exactly what happened.

So why does not God just force the rebellion to stop right now? Because forcibly overriding every wrong choice the instant it happens just rebuilds the automaton world through the back door. Removing free will retroactively defeats the entire reason it was granted. The patience looks like weakness or indifference from outside. It's actually the cost of the only project — love, not mere obedience — that was ever worth running.

## Why It Matters

Apply this somewhere you have wanted to force an outcome instead of letting it be freely chosen — with an employee, a spouse, a kid. Compliance you engineered was never the same as loyalty freely given, and the gap between the two does not close just because the engineered version was more convenient to obtain.

## Evidence / Support

A world of automatons incapable of wrong would also be incapable of love or genuine goodness, since neither means anything unless freely chosen.

Evil is not a separate created thing — it's free will, first in angels and then in humans, misused. Given Ch2 already ruled out evil as a co-equal power, this is where it actually came from.

The "shocking" part: taking the Fall as a real event, not decorative metaphor, which modern ears resist. Lewis's answer to the resistance itself: the discomfort may be the same self-flattery Book 1 already diagnosed, not evidence against the claim.

## Meditations (3)

- A thermostat cannot choose to turn on the heat. It has no choice to withhold.
- Building the automaton world back by force is not victory. It's surrender by another name.
- The discomfort you feel at the word "fallen" may be evidence, not refutation.

## Socratic Question

"If God removed your ability to choose wrong tomorrow, would what remained still be love — or just compliance wearing love's name?"
