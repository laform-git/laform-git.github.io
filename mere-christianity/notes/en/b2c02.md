## The Insight

Lewis tells on his own younger atheist self here. His argument against God used to be that the universe is cruel and unjust. Then the obvious question hit him: where did he get "unjust" from, if the universe is all there is and has no meaning? A line only looks crooked to someone who already knows what straight looks like. The outrage itself — not mild disappointment, actual outrage — was smuggling in a real standard atheism has no way to supply.

Simple dualism does not save you either. Two equal powers, one good, one bad, forever deadlocked — but the moment you call one of them "good," you are using a third standard above both to rank them. That third thing, not either power, turns out to be the real ultimate reality, and dualism just quietly reintroduced the single higher order it claimed did not exist.

## Why It Matters

"If God is good, why is there evil" is the objection everyone eventually has to sit with. Lewis does not dodge it or soften it into "evil is an illusion." He uses it as the doorway into what Christianity actually claims: not a neat universe with no conflict in it, but a real occupied territory under a real rebellion.

## Evidence / Support

Calling the universe unjust assumes a real standard of justice exists — precisely what pure atheism cannot supply and theism can.

Evil is not its own independent thing — it's goodness gone wrong, the way rust is not a substance separate from iron but a corruption of it. Cruelty is courage twisted; badness is always parasitic on some good being misused.

Because evil depends on good to exist (a parasite needs a host; the reverse is not true), evil cannot be co-equal and opposite to good. That kills dualism on its own terms.

The actual claim: not a fair fight between equals, but a rightful God and a real rebel enemy holding much of the world as occupied territory — a resistance operating inside enemy lines, not a draw.

## Meditations (3)

- A line only looks crooked to someone who already knows what straight looks like.
- Rust is not iron's equal opposite. It is iron, corrupted.
- Calling the universe unjust is not proof there is no God. It may be proof there is.

## Socratic Question

"The last time you were genuinely outraged at real injustice — where did the standard you were measuring against actually come from?"
