## The Insight

A wiring diagram describes electricity. Only an actual wire carries it. That's the whole distinction Lewis is drawing here — believing Christianity is true is the diagram: necessary, accurate, and completely inert on its own. Baptism, belief, and Communion are the wire. You can have a flawless diagram and still no current running through the house.

Lewis refuses the tidy exclusivist reading too. He will not say everyone who never hears the message, or who only meets a distorted version of it, is automatically shut out of whatever Christ's work ultimately accomplishes. What he does say: there's no other source of the rescue, whether or not a given person ever consciously plugs into it. He leaves the edge cases to God's judgment rather than claiming certainty he does not have.

## Why It Matters

Run the honest check: is belief functioning as the diagram only — correct, filed away, inert — or is current actually running through the ordinary channels available to you: prayer, sacrament, practice? Knowing the diagram is accurate was never the same as flipping the switch.

## Evidence / Support

Belief is necessary but not sufficient — agreeing Christianity is true is the doorway, not the destination. The aim was never a slightly better philosophy in your head; it was actually changing what you are.

Baptism, belief, Communion — named as the ordinary given channels, the way eating is the normal channel for nourishment, even though God could theoretically work another way.

That some people have survived without the ordinary means does not make it wise to skip them yourself, any more than rare survival without food makes starvation a reasonable plan.

## Meditations (3)

- Electricity spreads through a wire. It is not merely described on a diagram of one.
- That some men have survived without food does not make it wise for you to stop eating.
- The doorway was never meant to be mistaken for the house.

## Socratic Question

"Have you been treating Christian belief as the destination — or have you actually walked through the door it opens into?"
