## The Insight

A coward can perform one brave act. That does not make him brave. Lewis's real subject here is not the four virtues themselves — prudence, temperance, justice, fortitude — it's the line between doing a just thing once and being a just person. Adrenaline, social pressure, a lucky circumstance can produce isolated right action from almost anyone. What actually reveals character is what happens when none of that pressure is present — the quiet, unwitnessed repetitions, not the one dramatic moment anyone would perform for an audience.

"Cardinal" means hinge, and Lewis picks the word deliberately: pagan philosophers recognized these same four as what everything else in a good life turns on, long before Christianity said anything about them. That's not incidental — it's the same cross-cultural evidence from Book 1, showing up again at the level of virtue instead of law.

## Why It Matters

Pick one of the four — prudence, temperance, justice, fortitude — and test it against your actual record: does it show up reliably when nothing's at stake and no one's watching, or only when circumstances make it easy?

## Evidence / Support

Temperance is not abstinence — it's proportion in any pleasure, not just drink. The person who exercises compulsively or reads to the exclusion of everything else is just as intemperate as a drunk.

The goal is not a scorecard of isolated good deeds — it's becoming, through repeated practice, the kind of person for whom right action is now natural rather than a fluke.

These four function as the load-bearing structure for the rest of Book 3 — social morality, psychology, chastity, pride are all tests of whether this settled character is actually forming, not separate topics.

## Meditations (3)

- Temperance was never about wine alone. It is proportion in every pleasure, including the ones that look respectable.
- A coward can perform one brave act. That does not make him brave.
- A hinge is not decoration. Everything else in the room depends on it holding.

## Socratic Question

"Think of a virtue you have displayed once, under the right conditions. Has it become who you are — or is it still just something you did that one time?"
