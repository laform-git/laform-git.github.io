
# Barron — Thomas Aquinas, Ch.2: Approach to God
*How Aquinas approaches God: why he rejects Anselm's ontological argument yet grants an inchoate self-evidence through the desire for happiness, and how three of his Five Ways (motion, contingency, design) work as "paths" rather than proofs.*

*Source: Bishop Robert Barron, Peterson Academy course "Thomas Aquinas," Lecture 2 (transcript). Second of eight lectures; follows Ch.1 Life, Times, and Culture and hands off to Ch.3 The Attributes of God. Transcript confirmed complete: it opens with two leftover items from Ch.1 and closes by announcing the next lecture.*

## Why This Matters
This is the hinge of the course. Ch.1 introduced the man; this lecture introduces the one subject he never stopped asking about. Barron sets two traditions side by side: the a priori, Anselmian route (God's existence follows from the very idea of God) and the a posteriori, Thomist route (look at the world, find what needs explaining, follow the chain to God). He then walks through three of the Five Ways in detail, with homely examples (a cooling cup of coffee, a lamp on a chain, a studio that will not exist in a few hours). The key interpretive move is that Thomas calls these *viae*, paths, not proofs: they lead toward God without claiming to capture him. The lecture only establishes *that* something like an unmoved mover, a necessary being and a governing intelligence exists; *what* that reality must be like is deferred to Ch.3.

## "What Is God?" — Theology's Single Subject
- Dominican story: as a boy of about seven, Thomas asked his teacher, **"But master, what is God?"** and never stopped asking. ✅ (as Barron tells it)
- For Thomas, theology is **talk about God, and everything else in relation to God**. A physicist studies being under one aspect; the theologian studies God and how all things relate to him. ✅
- God runs through every page of his writings, right up to the late experience after which everything he had written "seems like nothing" (the Ch.1 story). ✅

## Is God's Existence Self-Evident? The Ontological Argument
Thomas first entertains the possibility that God's existence is self-evident. Barron makes the case sympathetically:
- **Consensus gentium** ("consensus of the peoples"): nearly every culture across space and time has believed in God. Barron attributes the phrase to Carl Jung 🟡 (the phrase itself is much older; Barron is citing Jung's use of it).
- **Charles Taylor, *A Secular Age*:** around 1500 it was almost unthinkable in Europe not to believe in God; we may be the first culture developing without a keen sense of the transcendent. ✅

### Versions of the argument (a priori: the idea of God implies his existence)

| Thinker | Formula for God | Key step | Barron's note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Anselm** (d. 1109), *Proslogion* | *Id quo maius cogitari nequit*: "that than which nothing greater can be thought" | Existing in the mind and in reality is greater than existing in the mind only; therefore God exists | Accepted by Descartes and by Bonaventure, Thomas's contemporary |
| **Alvin Plantinga** (modal logic) | The maximally perfect being | A maximally perfect being exists in all possible worlds; the actual world is a possible world; therefore God exists | Contemporary restatement of Anselm |
| **Kurt Gödel** | That which has every possible positive quality | Necessary existence is a positive quality; so God necessarily exists | Gödel was reluctant to share it ("they'll think I'm crazy") |

- On all versions, the atheist (Barron's foil: Christopher Hitchens) would be in a **formal contradiction**: denying existence to the one whose idea includes existence. ✅ (as the argument's defenders claim)
- **Kant's objection:** the argument treats existence as a **predicate**. "Big, red, seven rooms, slate roof" versus the same list "and it exists": existence adds nothing to the concept; it is a category error. ✅
- Barron ranks Gödel with Bertrand Russell and Alfred North Whitehead as the greatest theoretical mathematicians of the 20th century 🟡 (his ranking).

### Thomas's verdict: closer to Kant
- The ontological argument makes **an illegitimate transition from the intentional to the real**: however impressive the idea of God, you cannot slide from idea to reality. Thomas quarrels with Anselm here. ✅
- **But a partial yes:** everyone wants to be happy (*beatitudo*); our ultimate happiness is God, the Supreme Good; you cannot desire what you do not know in some way; so in an **inchoate** way, everyone intuits that God exists. ✅
- **The caution:** in a fallen world most of us are wrong about happiness most of the time (we look for it in pleasure, money, power, honor, knowledge). So de facto we lack a clear grasp of God's existence and need **evidence drawn from the world**. ✅

## A Priori vs. A Posteriori

|  | A priori (Anselmian) | A posteriori (Thomist) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Latin meaning | "Prior to" experience | "From afterwards," after experience |
| Starting point | The idea of God alone | Features of the world that need explaining |
| Patron image | Plato pointing up (Raphael's *School of Athens*) | Aristotle pointing out at the world |
| Examples | Anselm, Descartes, Gödel, Plantinga | The Five Ways |

Barron likes that **both traditions live inside the great tradition**.

### Barron's reading: the experience of the unconditioned 🟡
His own interpretation of what all versions of the ontological argument witness to:
- We live among **conditioned** things (things in categories, related to other things, studied by the sciences). Sometimes, "through, above, under" all of that, we sense **the unconditioned**: not a being but being itself, not a true proposition but truth itself, not a beautiful thing but beauty itself.
- What is truly unconditioned cannot be **only in the mind** (that conditions it) nor **only out there** like a thing; it is beyond the subject–object split. That is exactly Anselm's "both inside and outside the mind."
- Anselm says in the *Proslogion* that after long failed effort he **surrendered, and the formula "was given to me."** Barron reads the argument as rooted in a spiritual experience, not just a logical trick. 🟡 (interpretation; Barron's language is Kantian)

## Viae, Not Rationes — Paths, Not Proofs
- Thomas does not call these arguments *rationes* (demonstrations); he calls them ***quinque viae***, "five ways" or paths. ✅
- Why it matters: God cannot be "caught in the net of our minds" or be the tidy conclusion of a syllogism. **Augustine:** ***si comprehendis, non est Deus*** ("if you understand it, it is not God"). Barron adds the Buddhist parallel, "If you find the Buddha on the road, kill him." ✅
- Thomas himself: **"Whatever can be known or understood is less than God Himself."** ✅ (quoted by Barron; exact text not cited)
- Thomas's other word: ***manuductio***, "a leading by the hand" (*manu* + *ducere*), like leading a child to show him something. Because of original sin, our minds and perceptions are fallen; we need to be led. ✅

## The First Way — Motion (*prima et manifestior via*)
"The first and more manifest way," because its starting point is so obvious.

**The bones:** (1) things change; (2) nothing changes itself; (3) there is no infinite regress of changed changers; (4) therefore there is a first unchanged changer, ***et hoc omnes dicunt Deum*** ("and this all people call God"). ✅

- ***Motus*** **means change of any kind**, not just local motion: speed, temperature, knowledge ("I didn't know Russian, now I do"). ✅
- **Potency and act (Aristotle):** potency is real possibility (not yet, but could be); change is "the reduction of something from potency to act." ✅
- **Why nothing changes itself:** what is changed must be in potency; what causes the change must be in act ("you can't give what you don't have": the heater must be hot, the teacher of Russian must know Russian). Nothing is potential and actual **in the same respect** at once. Animals "moving themselves" are really one part moving another (brain, nerves, muscles, bones). ✅
- **No infinite regress, but not back in time:** the series in question is ***per se* subordinated**, a here-and-now dependency of cause on cause. Remove the first and you remove all later motion, including the change in front of you. A series going back in time (father, grandfather...) Thomas says might be infinite and he cannot disprove it. ✅
- So the argument is not "God started things long ago" but **God operative now** in all motion and change. ✅
- The first mover has no potency: it is ***actus purus***, pure act, "pure energy," the "unenergized energizer" (developed in Ch.3). ✅
- Barron warns that billiard-ball illustrations get the argument wrong: they picture a temporal chain. ✅

### Barron's examples
- **The coffee cup:** his roughly 200-degree coffee during his morning holy hour cools over about 15 minutes (potency to act). The room changes it; the room depends on outside weather (pressure systems, winds, Earth's motion) and the house heating; the heating depends on the Rochester grid, the state grid, and so on, "like a Russian doll," all here and now. Stop at an infinite regress and you violate the **principle of sufficient explanation**. In prayer, he says, the cooling cup makes him aware of the pure act sustaining it. ✅ (example; 🟡 the details are his illustration, not Thomas's)
- **The lamp on a chain:** each link holds the lamp now; an infinite chain would leave the lamp on the floor. Since it hangs, there must be something that is not one more link. ✅
- **Second Way** (*ex causalitate*, efficient causality) is mentioned as very similar and not developed. ✅

## The Third Way — Contingency (*ex contingentia mundi*)
1. **Things come into being and pass out of being** (plants, people, this studio set-up "that didn't exist this morning" and will be gone in hours). ✅
2. So they **do not contain within themselves the reason for their existence**: if they did, they would exist necessarily. "They are, but they don't have to be." ✅
3. A contingent state of affairs **requires a cause**. Barron argues this intuition is the root of all physical science: what is but need not be calls for explanation. 🟡 (his claim)
4. If the cause is itself contingent, keep looking: floor, ground, planet, solar system; cameras, makers, room temperature; oxygen, gravity, the Earth's size and position. Or psychologically: why are we here? Barron wants to teach; truth is worth serving; why? ✅
5. No infinite regress of contingent causes; so there must be **a being that exists necessarily, whose very nature is to be**. "This all people call God." ✅

- **Teilhard de Chardin's lock of hair:** as a three-year-old he watched a lock of his own hair burn in the fire and was shocked that part of himself was simply gone; he dedicated his life to finding what does not pass away. Barron: much of philosophy, poetry and drama is born of this desire. ✅ (Barron's anecdote)

## The Fifth Way — Teleology (Design)
Barron calls it "the most intuitively obvious, but also maybe the weakest" in strictly demonstrating God.

### Aristotle's four causes

| Cause | Meaning | Which Way uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material | The stuff something is made of | — |
| Formal | Its structure, pattern, intelligibility | Grounds the fifth way (Barron) |
| Efficient | What brings it about, "pushes" it | Ways 1–3 (motion, causality, contingency) |
| Final | Its purpose or goal, what "pulls" it (*telos*) | Way 5 |

- **Thomas's statement:** non-intelligent things act purposefully for an end; that is possible only if they are directed by an intelligence, as **the arrow is directed by the archer**. "This all people call God." ✅
- **Obvious objection:** why God and not many designers, or aliens? 🟡 Barron's answer pushes it deeper: final causality rests on **formal causality**. A tree predictably sends out roots and branches because of its intelligible structure. ✅
- **Where does intelligibility come from?** Every science presupposes the world is intelligible and that our minds will correspond to it. "It's just there" does not satisfy Barron. ✅
- **Joseph Ratzinger, *Introduction to Christianity*:** objective intelligibility can only be explained by recourse to a subjective intelligence. Our language shows it: we **"re-cognize"** a pattern, rethinking what was already thought into the thing. ✅
- **Eugene Wigner, "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences":** a secular Jewish theoretical physicist, younger friend of Einstein, who wondered why the highest mathematics describes physical reality so well that we can build bridges and reach the moon; he repeatedly called it "miraculous" without invoking God. Barron reads this as a modern form of the same intuition. 🟡 (Barron's reading of Wigner)

### Three Ways at a glance

| Way | Starting observation | Conclusion | Barron's name for God |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Motion | Things change | First unmoved mover, pure act | The unchanged changer, unenergized energizer |
| 3. Contingency | Things come and go | Necessary being whose nature is to be | The non-contingent source of contingency |
| 5. Teleology | Non-intelligent things act for ends | A governing intelligence | The supreme intelligence behind intelligibility |

**His closing frame:** atheists can quarrel with every one of these ("welcome to the history of philosophy"), and more work is needed to show these realities are God. But they are at least *viae*, doors opened in God's direction, always remembering *si comprehendis, non est Deus*. ✅

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "The Five Ways are airtight proofs that capture God": Thomas calls them *viae* and *manuductio*; whatever we understand is less than God.
- 🔴 "The first way says God started the universe long ago": it concerns a here-and-now (*per se*) series; Thomas says he cannot disprove an infinite series back in time.
- 🔴 "*Motus* means physical motion": it means any change from potency to act, including temperature and knowledge.
- 🔴 "Aquinas accepted the ontological argument": he rejected it, closer to Kant, while granting an inchoate knowledge of God through the desire for happiness.
- 🔴 "Billiard balls illustrate the first way": they picture a temporal chain; the lamp-on-a-chain or the cooling cup fit better.
- 🔴 "The design argument is about pretty order only": in Barron's reading it rests on the intelligibility every science presupposes.

## Practical Takeaway
Study-level takeaways: (1) when you meet an argument for God, ask first whether it is a priori (from the idea) or a posteriori (from the world); Thomas only trusts the second; (2) explain the first and third ways with a **here-and-now** dependency (lamp, coffee), never a timeline; (3) the desire for happiness is itself a pointer to God, which is why misplaced happiness (pleasure, money, power, honor) dims that pointer; (4) present the Ways as paths with humility, not as a trap for the other person.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Why does Thomas reject Anselm's ontological argument?**
A. Because he denies that God is that than which nothing greater can be thought
B. \* Because it makes an illegitimate transition from the intentional (an idea) to the real, much as Kant later objected that existence is not a predicate
C. Because he thinks God's existence cannot be known by reason at all
*Why B: Thomas accepts the high idea of God but denies you can slide from the concept to the reality; he still holds God can be known from the world.*

**2. In the first way, why is an infinite regress ruled out?**
A. Because the universe must have had a beginning in time
B. Because Aristotle said the world is finite
C. \* Because the series is a here-and-now (*per se*) dependency: without a first mover, nothing would actualize the change you can see right now, like a lamp on an endless chain lying on the floor
*Why C: Thomas explicitly allows that a series back in time might be infinite; the argument concerns simultaneous dependence, not a timeline.*

**3. How does Thomas grant a limited "self-evidence" to God's existence?**
A. \* Everyone desires happiness, true happiness is found only in God, and you cannot desire what you do not know in some way, so all people know God inchoately, though sin clouds it
B. Every culture has believed in God, so the consensus proves it
C. The idea of a maximally perfect being includes existence in all possible worlds
*Why A: Thomas's concession runs through beatitudo; B is the consensus gentium Barron mentions and C is Plantinga's modal version.*

**4. What does Barron add to the fifth way to answer "why not aliens or many designers"?**
A. A claim that Thomas proved there is exactly one designer by counting effects
B. \* A deeper grounding in formal causality: purpose rests on intelligibility, which every science presupposes, and objective intelligibility points to a primordial intelligence (Ratzinger, Wigner)
C. A retreat to faith alone, since reason cannot address design
*Why B: Barron moves from "things act for ends" to "the world is intelligible at every level," which a local designer would not explain.*
