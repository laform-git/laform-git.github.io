
# Barron — Thomas Aquinas, Ch.3: The Attributes of God
*Aquinas's attributes of God as Barron presents them: three ways of naming God, divine simplicity (essence equals existence, ipsum esse subsistens, not in any genus), the non-competitive God, perfection and goodness, transcendence and immanence, immutability as fidelity, eternity vs. everlastingness, and God's creative knowledge.*

*Source: Bishop Robert Barron, Peterson Academy course "Thomas Aquinas," Lecture 3 (transcript). Third of eight lectures; follows Ch.2 Approach to God (the five ways) and leads into Ch.4 The Trinity. Transcript complete: Barron closes the lecture himself and previews the next one.*

## Why This Matters
Lecture 2 gave the five ways; this lecture is where Barron says the real work starts. The proofs only "open the door"; the bulk of Aquinas's treatise on God is filling in what can be said about the God those arguments reach. Barron walks through the opening questions of the *Summa theologiae* (Part I): the three ways of naming God, divine simplicity (which he calls Aquinas's single most important teaching), perfection and goodness, God's presence in the world, immutability, eternity and knowledge. The thread that ties it together is his signature theme: because God is not a being among beings, God does not compete with creatures. That idea answers the New Atheists, frames the Incarnation, and turns metaphysics into a spirituality of surrender. Its vocabulary (*ipsum esse subsistens*, *actus purus*, *nunc stans*) is the toolkit for the rest of the course.

## How We Speak About God — Three Ways
Aquinas starts from a striking admission: about God we know "not what God is, but what God is not." Barron says that is not entirely true even in Thomas, "but there's a lot of truth to it." ✅

| Way | Rule | Barron's example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Via negativa** (negative way) | Whatever is imperfect or finite in creatures is denied of God | "Eternal" does not mean everlasting; it means *not temporal*. Like Michelangelo removing everything that is not David |
| **Via positiva** (positive way) | Any perfection in creatures (good, true, beautiful) is in God, because it came from God as first cause | Beauty, goodness |
| **Via eminentiae** (way of eminence) | The positive perfection is in God in so eminent a way that we cannot grasp it | "God is supremely good. What does that mean? I don't really know." |

- Barron's verdict: the via negativa keeps "a primacy in theology." Even language that sounds positive often works negatively. ✅
- Recurring motto: *si comprehendis, non est Deus*, "if you understand, that isn't God". ✅

## Divine Simplicity — "His Most Important Teaching"
Asked for Aquinas's most important teaching, Barron answers: **the simplicity of God** (*simplicitas*, *Summa* I, q.3). Everything in our experience is metaphysically complex: it exists, but as a certain kind of thing, so it can be categorized, defined and named. God simply *is*. The mind wants traction and gets none: Barron compares it to a blank white wall or a Minnesota whiteout where you lose all reference points. ✅

### Article 1: Is God a body?
- Not a silly question: Greek and Roman gods were idealized bodies, Mormon theology gives God a body, and the Old Testament has bodily manifestations. ✅
- Answer from the first way: God is the unchanged changer, so **actus purus** (pure act, no potency). Matter in Aristotle is supremely potential and malleable. So God cannot be a body. ✅
- **The atheist's fallback** ("fine, there's a ground of contingency, but why not just matter or energy?"): matter never exists "as such." It is always in a particular form, temperature, speed or configuration, which invites the question *why this and not that?* So matter cannot be the final explanation of contingent existence. ✅
- "Spirit" is not Casper the Friendly Ghost, a vaporous thing among things. It is via negativa: whatever God is, He is not a configuration of matter, and He transcends every mode of existence, so He is not *less* than embodied. ✅
- Bodies can be avoided; God cannot (Psalm 139, "Where can I run from your love?"). ✅

### Article 4: In God, essence and existence are the same
- Barron calls this the most important discussion in Aquinas and "one of the most important in all Christian theology." ✅
- **Essence** = *quidditas*, "whatness," the ontological structure of a thing. **Existence** = *quo est*, "that by which something is," the *actus essendi* (act of existing). Barron credits the distinction to the Arab (Muslim) philosophers who passed it to the West 🟡 (no names given).
- In every creature the two are distinct: existence is "poured into the receptacle" of an essence. I exist *as human*; the podium exists *as a podium*. Essence is a limiting, potential principle. ✅
- In God they coincide. David Burrell (Notre Dame Thomist), quoted by Barron: "To be me is to be human... **To be God is to be to be.**" ✅
- **Exodus 3:14.** Moses, a prince used to control, must first take off his sandals. Asked for a name among many local gods, God answers "I am who I am." Aquinas reads this as "my essence is my existence." Barron's gloss: shorthand for "you're asking the wrong question, pal." ✅ (the gloss is Barron's 🟡)
- Link back to the third way: contingent being must end in something that needs no explanation, whose nature is to be. ✅

### Not in Any Genus — Not Even "Being"
- No genus at all, or God would be a *type* of being. And, pushed further, not even in the genus of **being**. "God is not a being." Barron calls this a "mind-blowing" admission from the supreme categorizer. ✅
- Thomas occasionally slips into *ens summum* (highest being), but his usual name is **ipsum esse subsistens**, "the subsistent act of to be itself." In Barron's phrase: **God is not a noun but a verb** (*esse*). Anything that is a noun could be categorized and caught. ✅

## The Non-Competitive God — Burning Bush
- Finite things are **ontologically competitive**: I cannot invade the podium's being without compromising it. ✅
- When God comes close, things are not destroyed but become "more radiant and more beautifully themselves." The bush burns but is not consumed, because *ipsum esse* is not a being "jostling for position on the same playing field." ✅
- Barron's diagnosis: the picture of God as a rival to human freedom is "the lie that gave rise to atheism," from Marx and Feuerbach to Christopher Hitchens, who said belief in God is living in "a permanent North Korea." 🟡 (Barron's interpretive thesis about atheism)
- The Incarnation is the test case: God becomes human without ceasing to be God and without overwhelming the humanity; the two natures unite "without mixing, mingling, or confusion." Jesus is "radiantly human" because God is so close. ✅

## Perfection and Goodness
- **Perfection (q.4):** what is fully actual has realized the full perfection of being. *Perfectum* means "made through and through," complete. ✅
- **Goodness:** used in the Aristotelian sense, the good is what is attractive. Every desire seeks perfection, so the ultimate desire of the heart is what is perfect in every sense: God. God is "the most enticing, fascinating, alluring reality." ✅
- Against **deism** (the clockmaker who "went to write his memoir"): God is not only efficient cause but **final cause**, constantly luring and drawing the world back. ✅
- Teaser: "In every act of the will, God is implicitly co-willed." Follow any desire to its end and you finally want God. (Promised for later.)
- **Exitus–reditus:** all things come out from God and return to Him, not as a pointless round trip but "enhanced and perfected and realized," culminating in the beatific vision. ✅

## God in the World — Transcendence and Immanence (q.8)
- The sed contra Barron loves: Isaiah, **"Lord, you have accomplished all we have done."** We did it, and God did it. Between creatures that would be presumptuous; with God it is coherent because God is not on our "grid." ✅ (Barron says "Article 8"; context suggests Question 8 🟡)
- **Geometry analogy:** point, line, square, cube. Each higher dimension contains infinitely many of the lower without erasing them. God likewise includes countless creatures who are realized *through* His activity. ✅ (Barron's analogy)
- Thomas: God is present the way an agent is present to what it moves. **"God is in all things by essence, presence, and power, and intimately so"** (*intime*). ✅
- Summary line: "God is above all things by the excellence of His nature; nevertheless, He is in all things as the cause of the being of all things." **His transcendence makes His immanence possible.** ✅
- Augustine: God is *interior intimo meo et superior summo meo*, closer to me than I am to myself and higher than my highest. (Transcript spells it *intimior*.) ✅
- Where is God in this room? Nowhere, as one thing among things. Everywhere, as the act of being through which everything is. The Baltimore Catechism's "Where is God? Everywhere" means this, not a gas. ✅
- Barron's own formula: **"God is that reality which can be neither grasped nor avoided."** Biblical failures go one way or the other: Adam and Eve grasp, Jonah runs (told to go east by land, he goes west by sea). ✅
- What remains is love and surrender. Irenaeus: "the glory of God is a human being fully alive." Contrast Gore Vidal on jealousy: "When a friend of mine succeeds, something in me dies," which Barron reads as creaturely competitiveness. ✅

## Immutability
- God is the unmoved mover, *actus purus*, so He does not change. Sed contra: Malachi, "I am the Lord, and I change not." 🟡 Transcript slip: Barron says the answer to "Is God unchangeable?" is "no," but everything that follows shows he means God does not change.
- Malachi is not doing metaphysics; he is talking about **covenant fidelity**. Barron puts the metaphysics "in service of the theology of God's fidelity." We do not actually want a God who changes. ✅
- Thomas's argument Barron likes best (the third): whatever moves acquires something it lacked (coffee cooling); God already holds "all the plenitude and perfection of being," so He cannot acquire anything new. ✅
- Not the Rock of Gibraltar, which is being "at a very low level of actualization." Immutability does not mean cold or unresponsive: compassion and identification with us exist in God "to the supreme degree," just not by creaturely change. 🟡 (Barron's pastoral gloss)

## Eternity (q.10)
- **Everlasting ≠ eternal.** Everlasting is being stuck in time forever: "Vampires are everlasting." Barron has no wish to live in this body forever through technology. ✅
- Argument: Aristotle defines time as the measure of motion; God does not change, so God is outside time. God exists in the **nunc stans**, the "standing now," and is present to every moment of history because He is in none. ✅
- Paul Tillich (Barron's doctorate compared Aquinas and Tillich) calls it *Überzeitlichkeit*, "over-time-ness." ✅
- **Objection 2 (Matthew 25, "eternal fire"):** Thomas answers that hell's fire is called eternal only because it never ends: **"in hell, true eternity does not exist, but rather endless time."** Heaven is outside time. ✅
- Notre Dame's North Rose window meditates on heaven through the number eight: Christ at the center, then rings of 8, 16, 32 and 32 (88 total, as Barron describes it). Seven is the full cycle of time, so eight is what lies beyond it. ✅

## God's Knowledge
- "Knowledge" here carries a biblical sense of intimacy (Mary's "I know not man"), not a Cartesian, mathematical one. ✅
- Immateriality is the root of knowing: a rock is closed in on itself; the immaterial mind is *quodammodo omnia*, "in a certain sense all things." God is supremely immaterial, so His capacity to know is infinite. ✅
- God knows Himself first (Aristotle agreed). Aquinas adds: in knowing Himself, God knows His power and everything that power reaches, which is creation. God knows us "in and through Himself," which is how we are most truly known; sin is understanding myself apart from God. ✅
- Objection: the object known perfects the knower, so does the world perfect God? Answer: our knowledge is derivative; **God's knowledge is antecedent and creative**. "He knows you into being." The world is perfected by His knowing, not the reverse. ✅

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "God is the supreme being": for Aquinas God is not *a* being and not in the genus of being; He is *ipsum esse subsistens*.
- 🔴 "Matter or energy could be the uncaused ground": matter always exists in some form and so always invites a further why.
- 🔴 "Spirit means a ghost-like thing": spirit is a negation (not a body), not a vaporous object among objects.
- 🔴 "God competes with human freedom": finite things compete; God does not, which is why the Incarnation and grace enhance rather than crush.
- 🔴 "God is a distant clockmaker": He is final cause, drawing everything, and present in all things intimately.
- 🔴 "Immutable means cold": it means fidelity and fullness, not the stillness of a rock.
- 🔴 "Eternal means everlasting": eternity is outside time; endless time is closer to hell.
- 🔴 "God learns about the world": His knowledge causes the world, not the reverse.

## Practical Takeaway
Study takeaways: (1) when talking about God, test every statement against the via negativa and the way of eminence; (2) keep the chain clear: pure act → not a body → essence equals existence → not in any genus → non-competitive presence; (3) read immutability and eternity as statements about fidelity and fullness, not coldness. Spiritual takeaway Barron explicitly draws: stop grasping and stop hiding; neither works, and what remains is surrender and love.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Given that in God essence and existence coincide, what is the best Thomistic reading of "I am who I am" (Exodus 3:14)?**
A. A refusal to reveal anything at all about God
B. \* God's very nature is to be, so asking "which being are you?" is the wrong question
C. A claim that God is the highest-ranking of the many local gods
*Why B: Aquinas reads the name as "my essence is my existence"; God is not one god among others but the act of being itself.*

**2. Why can Isaiah coherently say "Lord, you have accomplished all we have done" without cancelling human agency?**
A. \* Because God is not a being on our ontological grid, so divine and human action do not compete
B. Because humans actually do nothing and God does everything
C. Because it is pious exaggeration that should not be taken literally
*Why A: finite agents compete, so between creatures the line would be presumptuous; God, as the ground of being, acts in and through our action, like a higher dimension containing the lower.*

**3. How does Aquinas answer the objection that Matthew 25's "eternal fire" creates a second eternity next to God's?**
A. Hell is eternal in exactly the same sense God is
B. Hell will eventually end, so the word is metaphorical
C. \* Hell's fire is called eternal only because it never ends; it is endless time, while true eternity is beyond time
*Why C: eternity is the nunc stans outside time; hell is everlastingness, which is why Barron links vampire-style endless time to hell, not heaven.*

**4. Answering the objection that the object known perfects the knower, how does God's knowledge of the world differ from ours?**
A. God learns about the world gradually, as we do
B. \* God's knowledge is antecedent and creative: things exist because God knows them
C. God knows only Himself and not particular creatures
*Why B: our knowledge is derived from things; God, knowing His own power, knows everything that power produces, so He "knows you into being" and depends on nothing.*
