# Barron — Thomas Aquinas, Ch.4: The Trinity
*How Aquinas, following Augustine, reads the Trinity through the mind's self-knowledge and self-love: one God, two processions, three persons as subsistent relations, four immanent relations, plus his account of God's will and love and of faith as supra-rational.*

*Source: Bishop Robert Barron, Peterson Academy course "Thomas Aquinas," Lecture 4 (transcript). Fourth of eight lectures; follows the divine attributes (Ch.3) and precedes the doctrine of creation (Ch.5). Transcript confirmed complete: it closes on Dante's "love that moves the planets and the other stars" and Barron's "probably a good place to end this lecture."*

## Why This Matters
Up to this lecture Barron has kept Aquinas on purely philosophical ground: the five ways, simplicity, immutability, eternity, knowledge. A pious Jew, a Muslim or a secular philosopher could follow most of it. Here he crosses into properly theological territory, the distinctively Christian claim that the one God of Israel is Father, Son and Holy Spirit. He does it in three moves: (1) he finishes the attributes with God's will and love; (2) he explains why faith is above reason, not below it; (3) he walks through Aquinas's "image-making" argument from the *Summa contra Gentiles*, Book 4, ending in a compact 1-2-3-4 summary you can memorize. It is the densest lecture so far, and the one that ties the abstract "unchanged changer" to "God is love."

## The Missing Attribute — God's Will and Love
- **Will is a modality of intellect.** Aquinas is an intellectualist: the mind comes first. When the mind knows something *as good*, will (*voluntas*) arises automatically. ✅
- Will has two forms: **resting** in a good possessed, or **seeking** a good that is absent. Barron's examples: a great conversation you get lost in (rest), Tiger Woods's golf swing (an absent good he seeks). ✅
- **First object of God's will is God's own being.** God rests in and savors the good that He is. Not selfishness: His good is perfect and never absent to Him. *Requiescat in pace* means resting (*quies*) in the good possessed, not "a great hammock in the sky." ✅
- **"What does God do all day?" "He enjoys Himself."** Barron calls this precise philosophical language. 🟡 The anecdote is told as a story about Aquinas; no source is given in the lecture.
- Because God loves Himself, He loves all that participates in His being: Genesis, "God found it very good." Spiritual corollary Barron stresses: the fact that you exist means you have been **loved into being** and are sustained moment by moment. ✅
- **Love = to will the good of the other.** Whatever exists, "from a worm to an archangel," has been willed some good; an angel more, a dog less (degrees of ontological perfection, to be developed later). ✅

### Objection: isn't love a passion, and God has no passions?
| Objection | Aquinas's reply as Barron gives it |
| --- | --- |
| Love is a passion; the changeless God has no passions; so God cannot love | In us (body and soul), bodily passions *accompany* acts of the soul. God has no body, so no passion in that sense. But love is not the bodily reaction; it is willing and savoring the good of the other, which God does fully. ✅ |

Barron's "good news" framing: the Greek and Roman gods are all passion and mood, mollified by the right sacrifice. The Bible instead promises the **reliability** of God's covenant love. "God can't not love Himself, and by the same token, He cannot but love us." 🟡 The pastoral application is Barron's emphasis; the doctrine itself is standard Thomism.

## Faith and Reason — Infra-Rational vs. Supra-Rational
- Barron's target: the New Atheists (and Old) who call faith childish superstition. His reply: that *is not* faith. Superstition and credulity are **infra-rational** (below reason) and the Church opposes them as much as any rationalist. ✅
- Authentic faith is **supra-rational**: it stands on reason, acknowledges its limits, and glimpses what reason alone cannot reach. ✅
- **The friendship analogy:** you come to know someone by reason (observation, conversation, "a Google search"). As intimacy grows, she reveals something about her deepest longing that reason could never have reached. If it is not repugnant to what you know, you decide: "I believe you." That is faith, "a belief on the far side of reason." ✅
- The Trinity is exactly this kind of truth: philosophers do not reach it naturally; "God spoke and we came to believe." ✅

## Where the Doctrine Comes From — The Biblical Data
| Datum | Texts Barron cites |
| --- | --- |
| Jesus is **sent** by the One He calls Father | "The One who sent me"; doing the will of the Father (like prophets and patriarchs) |
| Yet He **speaks and acts as the God of Israel** | Forgiving sins ("only God can forgive sins"); "You have heard it said, but I say" (authority over the Torah); "Unless you love me more than mother, father, your very life" |
| John makes it **explicit** | "I and the Father are one"; "Whoever sees me sees the Father"; "In the beginning was the Word... and the Word was God... and the Word became flesh" |
| He **rose from the dead** | Convinced the first Christians "He is who He says He is" |
| The **Spirit** rushes in | Not a creature; the one who "christifies" them; sent by Father and Son |
| **"God is love"** (1 John) | Not just "God loves": if love is His very nature, there must be lover, beloved, and the love shared |

- All of this lands on Jewish ears committed to the **Shema** (Deut 6, "Hear, O Israel, the Lord your God is Lord alone"). Barron says every word of Aquinas on simplicity is "a fancy way of saying" the Shema. ✅
- The biblical writers "didn't have this all figured out"; they took in the data of revelation. Working it out took centuries: Origen, Irenaeus, the Cappadocians, above all Augustine's *De Trinitate*. ✅

### The two errors the Fathers oscillated between
| Error | What it says | Why it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monarchianism / modalism** | One God in different modes: imperious God of the OT, gentle Jesus, the Spirit in the Church | Doesn't honor the real distinction of sender, sent and shared love. Barron's illustration: himself in bishop's suit, golf shirt, dinner clothes, same man, different modes. |
| **Tritheism / subordinationism** | A high God, a lesser Son, a third-level Spirit | Ends with three gods; violates the Shema |

## Aquinas's Problem — Generation in a Simple God (SCG IV, ch.10)
Barron switches from the *Summa Theologiae* to the earlier, more philosophical *Summa contra Gentiles* (1250s), Book 4, which he finds "especially illuminating." Scripture speaks of a **generation** (*generatio*): the Son comes forth from the Father. But every generation we know seems to involve three things impossible in God:
1. **Change** (a seed from a plant, a child from a mother); God is the unchanged changer.
2. **Numerical differentiation** (one generator here, one generated there); God is one and simple.
3. **Diminution** (the generated is less than the generator); there is no more or less in God.

## The Resolution — The Ladder of Image-Making (SCG IV, ch.11)
**Principle:** the higher the reality, the more **interior** and **perfect** its capacity to reproduce itself, to form an *imago*. ✅

| Level | How it forms an image of itself | Interior? | Perfect? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rock** | Impression in mud; a crack in a window when thrown | Exterior (needs an outside agent) | A vague outline |
| **Plant** | Forms a seed inside itself; seed grows into a replica | More interior, but must fall to the ground | Like, not identical |
| **Animal** | Mammalian reproduction | More interior still | Higher, still imperfect |
| *Quantum line* | — | — | — |
| **Human mind** | The mind poses itself to itself as an object: *mens* (mind), *notitia sui* (self-knowledge; Aquinas's "interior word"), *amor sui* (self-love) | Utterly interior: "I ask myself," still one person | Remarkably accurate |
| **Angel** | Pure intelligence; knows and loves itself immediately, without depending on the outside world | More interior than us | More perfect |
| **God** | Mind, interior Word, self-love, utterly perfect and utterly interior | Totally | The Son is a complete, unrestricted image of the Father, so they are one in being |

- **Language clue:** *me pregunto*, *je me demande* ("I ask myself" = "I wonder"). Self-reflection does not split you into two people. ✅
- **The house of fire** (Aquinas's image as Barron gives it): heat on the porch, hotter in the front room, hotter deeper in: there must be a pure fire at the center. From rock to archangel the capacity for self-replication "heats up" until it reaches God. ✅
- **Mapping:** Father = *mens*, the mind of God; Son = *notitia sui*, the Father's interior Word; Holy Spirit = *amor sui*, the "white-hot" love that arises automatically when Father and Son know each other as good. ✅
- **Imago Dei:** Augustine (and Aquinas after him) locate the image of God in the **mind**, not the body, since God has no body. The mind's knowing and loving reflects the inner life of the Trinity. ✅
- **Fulton Sheen's image of the Spirit:** Father and Son look at each other and "exhale"; *spiritus* means breath, so the Spirit is the holy breath they share. ✅

### How the three objections are answered
| Objection | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Change | No change: the procession obtains from all eternity; there was no time when it was not |
| Numerical difference | Not two beings; the image is so perfect the Son is one in being with the Father |
| Diminution | The Son is the perfect image; "the Word was with God... and the Word was God," not greater, not less |

- **Statue, son, Son:** a statue reminds you of a great person; a human son is a much better likeness; the eternal Son is utterly one in being with the Father. ✅
- **"Begotten, not made"** (Nicene Creed): what is *made* is a creature, less than its maker, and comes by a free act of will. The Son is *begotten*: from the Father, perfectly imaging Him, and **not** by an act of will. Creation is willed; the Son's procession "belongs to the very to be of God." ✅

## Count to Four — Barron's Schematic Summary
| # | Claim | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | One God | Everything from the earlier lectures stands: simplicity, essence = existence, the Shema. Christians are monotheists. |
| **2** | Two processions | The Son (Word) proceeds from the Father; the Spirit proceeds from the Father **and the Son**, because He is their shared love |
| **3** | Three persons | Father, Son, Spirit, but not "persons" as in three separate people in a room. Augustine: we say *personae* "so we have something to say." Anselm: three *nescio quid*, "I don't know whats." Aquinas: **subsistent relations** |
| **4** | Four immanent relations | Active generation (Father to Son); passive generation (Son to Father); active spiration (Father and Son to Spirit); passive spiration (Spirit to Father and Son) |

### Why "subsistent relation" is a deliberate paradox
- In Aristotle, **substances** subsist (exist through themselves); **relations** are secondary, obtaining *between* substances. "The one thing relations aren't is subsistent." ✅
- Aquinas knows this and says it anyway: Father, Son and Spirit differ not in being, mind or substance (they share one divine essence) but in **sheer relationality**: the one who begets, the one begotten, the one spirated (*spirare*, to breathe). ✅
- **Ratzinger (Benedict XVI):** the Trinity signals "the absolute nature of the relative": the ground of being is a set of relations. ✅
- ***Si comprehendis, non est Deus*** ("If you understand it, it is not God"). Barron's images: incense is both prayer rising and smoke in your eyes; at the edges of physics ordinary laws become anomalous too, so why expect otherwise at the ground of being? 🟡 Barron does not attribute the Latin maxim in the lecture (it is usually credited to Augustine).

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 4)
- **"What does God do all day?" "He enjoys Himself."** Rest in the good possessed.
- **"The very fact that you are is an indication that you've been loved into being."**
- **Love is to will the good of the other.** From a worm to an archangel, everything has been willed some good.
- **Faith is never infra-rational, always supra-rational.** Superstition is below reason; faith stands on it.
- **"God is love," not merely "God loves."** So there must be lover, beloved and love shared.
- **The higher the being, the more interior and perfect its self-imaging.** Rock, plant, animal, human mind, angel, God.
- **Mens, notitia sui, amor sui:** Father, Word, Spirit.
- **Begotten, not made:** creation is an act of will; the Son's procession is not.
- **1-2-3-4:** one God, two processions, three persons (subsistent relations), four immanent relations.
- **"The absolute nature of the relative"** (Ratzinger).

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "Faith means giving up on reason": Barron (and Aquinas) place faith *above* reason, not below; superstition is the infra-rational thing.
- 🔴 "An unchanging God can't really love": love is willing the good of the other, not a bodily passion; God's love is steadier precisely because He does not change.
- 🔴 "The Trinity is one God in three modes or masks": that is modalism; the distinctions are real.
- 🔴 "The Son and Spirit are lesser gods": that is subordinationism/tritheism; there is one God, and the Son is a perfect, undiminished image.
- 🔴 "Three persons = three individuals like you and me": in God, "person" means subsistent relation, not three separate beings.
- 🔴 "The Son was made by the Father, like creation": begotten, not made; eternal, not willed into being.
- 🔴 "The image of God is in our body": for Augustine and Aquinas it is the mind, which knows and loves itself.

## Practical Takeaway
Study-level takeaways: (1) memorize the 1-2-3-4 schema; it is the fastest way to state the doctrine without falling into modalism or tritheism; (2) use the mind analogy (I know myself, I love myself, I am still one) as an entry point, while remembering Barron's point that the persons are "I don't know whats" and no analogy captures them; (3) keep the faith/reason distinction ready for apologetics conversations: the question is never "reason or faith" but "below reason or above it"; (4) the "loved into being" line is a usable prayer starting point.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Given Aquinas's account of will as Barron presents it, why does God necessarily love creatures?**
A. Because God feels an emotional attraction to them that changes over time
B. \* Because will follows intellect: God knows Himself and all that participates in His being as good, and to know something as good is to will it
C. Because creatures earn God's love by their good actions
*Why B: Barron's principle is that will is a modality of intellect; the moment the good is known as good, willing (resting or seeking) follows, and God knows all that participates in Him.*

**2. Given Barron's friendship analogy, which statement best captures authentic faith?**
A. Faith replaces reason once a question becomes difficult
B. Faith accepts any claim, whether or not it conflicts with what reason knows
C. \* Faith accepts, on another's self-revelation, something congruent with reason that reason alone could never have reached
*Why C: the friend's revelation is not repugnant to what you know, yet unreachable by investigation; believing it is "a belief on the far side of reason," which is supra-rational, not infra-rational.*

**3. Given the ladder from rock to God, how does Aquinas answer the objection that generation implies diminution?**
A. \* The higher the being, the more perfect its self-image; in God the image (the Son) is utterly perfect, so He is one in being with the Father, neither less nor greater
B. He concedes the Son is slightly less than the Father but still divine
C. He denies there is any generation in God at all
*Why A: the whole ladder is built to show self-replication becoming more interior and perfect, culminating in a perfect image: "the Word was God," not greater, not less.*

**4. Given Aquinas's term "subsistent relations," what distinguishes the Father from the Son?**
A. They have different minds that agree perfectly
B. \* Their relation alone: the Father is the one who begets, the Son the one begotten, while both share the one divine essence
C. They are different modes in which the one God appears to us
*Why B: difference in being or mind would make two gods (tritheism), and mere modes would be modalism; Aquinas locates the distinction purely in relation.*
