
# Barron — Thomas Aquinas, Ch.5: Doctrine of Creation
*Why God creates (not from need), what His power can and cannot do, creation from nothing as a continual relation rather than a past event, and why the world's beauty and variety display God's glory.*

*Source: Bishop Robert Barron, Peterson Academy course "Thomas Aquinas," Lecture 5 (transcript). Fifth of eight lectures; turns from God in Himself (Ch.2 to Ch.4) to the world in relation to God, and sets up Ch.6 on providence and evil. Texts used: Summa Theologiae I, q.44 onward, and the disputed question De Potentia Dei, which Barron studied for his Paris doctorate. Transcript reads as complete: it ends on a clean segue to the next lecture.*

## Why This Matters
Barron calls creation "as puzzling as the Trinity in some ways," and here Aquinas's metaphysics turns directly into spirituality. Four ideas carry it: (1) God creates out of overflowing goodness, never need; (2) God's power is unlimited but never cut loose from His mind (the case against voluntarism); (3) creation from nothing means nothing stands between us and God, and creation is happening now; (4) a creature is a relation to God more than a substance over against Him. Each has a spiritual consequence Barron draws from years of spiritual direction.

## Opening Coda on the Trinity
Before the new topic Barron adds two notes to Ch.4:
- **"Person" is built for relation:** *persona* (*per-sonare*, the actor's mask that sounds toward an audience) and Greek *prosopon* ("looking toward"). The three subsistent relations are "a breathing in and a breathing out," Ratzinger's "absoluteness of the relative." ✅
- **Trinitarian language is meant to stymie the mind,** like Moses told to take off his shoes at the burning bush: "you're not in command here." It is not arbitrary, but it confounds the mind that wants to master everything. 🟡 (his spiritual reading)

## Why Is There a World at All? — The Good Diffuses Itself
If God is perfect, the unmoved mover, essence and existence coinciding, knowing, willing and loving Himself, why create? Aquinas answers from **Plato, not Aristotle** here.

|  | Aristotle | Plato |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The good is... | What is **sought** (the perfect, the goal) | What **gives** (diffusive of itself) |
| Image | God as the perfect object of desire | The sun outside the cave giving light to everything; the Form of the Good "beyond being" because it gives being |
| Use in Aquinas | God's goodness and perfection (Ch.3) | Why God creates |

- **Bonum diffusivum sui:** "the good is diffusive of itself." Barron's everyday image: in a bad mood you hide in your room; when life is good you want friends, conversation, a party. ✅
- **God has no needs.** Barron calls this "very, very important, theologically and spiritually." A needy god with all power would manipulate us, as the Greek and Roman gods needed sacrifices and homage and got angry without them. Psalm 50: "You think I need the blood of your bulls? I made everything." ✅
- So the world exists "purely as an act of goodness and an act of love": God creates to manifest His glory and share His life, not to fill a lack. ✅
- **Spiritual consequence:** many people in spiritual direction treat God as a power to be mollified. God does not need our worship or moral perfection; He **wants** them because they make us rightly ordered and happy. ✅
- **Irenaeus:** *Gloria Dei homo vivens*, "the glory of God is a human being fully alive." The model is parents who want to share their life with their children. ✅
- **Meister Eckhart** (German Dominican who held Aquinas's Paris chair a generation later): instead of only "climbing the Holy Mountain" to a distant supreme being, "sit and sink" into God, the ground of our being. Barron does not reject the mountain image, only its danger. Ron Rolheiser's version: much of prayer is "looking at pictures of water" when we are already in the water. 🟡 (Barron reads Eckhart as drawing out Aquinas's implications; Eckhart is "an edgy figure" to many)

## God's Power — Unlimited, but Never Arbitrary
Barron flags power as a live question: modern philosophy for some 200 years, and "almost any teenager in America," asks who has power and what it is for.
- **Sed contra:** "God is able from these stones to raise up children to Abraham." Barron draws two points: the **extent** of God's power, and its **purpose**, which is salvation. God's power is "always united to the love that He is," never domination. ✅
- In the third part of the Summa, Aquinas argues nothing is more powerful than that God became a human being: the Incarnation is the greatest expression of God's power, because of how extraordinary it is and because its purpose is to save. ✅ (Barron gives no question number)
- **Respondeo:** God's *potentia* is absolute and unlimited because He is simple, the sheer act of being, with every perfection. ✅

### What "can God do anything?" really means

| Kind of "impossible" | Example from the lecture | Can God do it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naturally impossible (beyond creatures) | Virgin birth; raising children from stones | Yes, if logically consistent |
| Strictly (logically) impossible | Square circle; 2 + 2 = 5; affirmation and negation true at once | No, because these are non-entities, a kind of non-being |
| Contrary to His own nature | Sin | No; sin is a kind of non-being, a perversion |

- Aquinas: God "cannot make affirmation and negation simultaneously true or any of those things which involve any such impossibility." ✅
- The schoolboy objection "then I'm more powerful than God because I can sin" fails: sinning is not a power but a lack. ✅

## The Case Against Voluntarism
- **Voluntarism** (Latin *voluntas*, "will") is the primacy of will over intellect, and so of power over being. ✅
- **Barron's modern example, Descartes:** 2 + 2 = 4 because God decreed it; God could decree 2 + 2 = 5; adultery is wrong because God willed it and could will the opposite. 🟡 (Barron's summary of Descartes)
- **Aquinas's answer, rooted in divine simplicity:** intelligence, will, power, immutability, eternity and perfection are one in God. Barron's image is a **prism**: our finite minds see the one white light of God broken into a spectrum of attributes. Voluntarism wrongly separates God's will from His being and mind. ✅
- So 2 + 2 = 4 because it reflects "the integrity of the being that God is," and adultery is a sin because it is out of step with the congruence of God's mind, will and reality. This does not limit God's power; it preserves its integrity. Without it God would be "a really dangerous devil"; His reliability rests on this congruence. ✅
- **Benedict XVI, Regensburg:** notorious for a comparison about Islam ("probably unfortunate the way he did it"), but its real burden was voluntarism: Christianity embodies the primacy of *logos* over *voluntas*. If will has primacy, "that's a hammer and everything else will be a nail." ✅
- **Nietzsche and Foucault:** Barron agrees with Jordan Peterson that "God is dead, and we killed him" was said in desperation. Without God, truth and morality lose their anchor and only the **will to power** remains: "a bunch of hammers," and the biggest wins. Foucault, reducing truth claims to plays of power, is a 20th-century Nietzsche; today's society is Nietzsche's victory mediated by Foucault. 🟡 (Barron's cultural diagnosis)

## Creation from Nothing (Creatio ex Nihilo)
- **The contrast:** ancient myths build the world from pre-existing stuff, often the bodies of defeated gods. Plato has the gods shape eternal matter; Aristotle's prime mover is absorbed in self-thinking thought while pre-existing matter, attracted to it, shapes itself into the world. ✅ (as Barron summarizes them)
- **Why that cannot be the real God:** God is *ipsum esse*, the sheer act of to-be. Nothing can exist independently of Him, so He is not like a painter with canvas, a carpenter with wood, or Michelangelo with marble. He does not draw the world from His own substance (He cannot change), nor from anything pre-existing. ✅
- **Spiritual principle:** what stands between us and God? **Nothing.** We are "always already" in relation to God in the most intimate way possible. ✅

| Question | Aquinas's answer (per Barron) |
| --- | --- |
| Is creation a motion (potency to act)? | No; there is nothing already there to be changed |
| Is it in time? | No; time is the measure of motion and is itself a creature |
| "What was God doing before He created?" | A "dumb question": there is no "before" |
| Is it in space? | No; space is a creature, not an empty container God filled |

- Psalm 139 ("Where can I run from your love?") fits: the Creator is inescapable because He makes from nothing. ✅

## Creatio Continua — Creation Is Now
- Creation is not an event "way back then." It is **continual creation**: the here-and-now rapport between *ipsum esse* and beings, God informing the totality of a creature's being at every moment. ✅
- **The Big Bang is not creation.** Whatever it was (a hyper-concentration of matter that expanded), it is for physicists to describe; creation is the ongoing relationship of Creator to creatures. ✅
- **Jean-Pierre de Caussade:** everything that happens is directly or indirectly the will of God, willed or permitted. Barron calls him a 17th-century Jesuit 🟡 (he is usually dated to the early 18th century).
- **Teilhard de Chardin:** "There's nothing profane for those who know how to see." The sacred/profane distinction has truth, but ultimately God touches everything. ✅

## A Creature Is a Relation to God
- Creatures are not substances "standing over and against God" who then have a relationship with Him. Barron: "You are a relationship to God." ✅
- **De Potentia Dei, q.3:** asking whether creation is something really in the creature, Aquinas answers that creation is "not an approach to being nor a change brought about by the Creator, but only the beginning of being and the relation to the Creator." Conclusion: creation is *quaedam relatio ad Creatorem, cum novitate essendi*, "a kind of relation to the Creator, with newness of being." ✅ Barron notes *quaedam* ("a kind of") is a deliberately soft word, and calls the line "almost like a Zen statement." 🟡
- Barron's name for the consequence: a **"metaphysics of the gospel."** The more you lose yourself in God, the more you find yourself; clinging to independence over against God loses who you are. ✅
- **Images for novitas essendi:** the woman at the well (water welling up to eternal life) and Hopkins: "There lives the dearest freshness deep down things." ✅

## The Ethical Payoff — Ontological Siblings
- If every creature is coming forth here and now from God, everything is connected at the deepest level. My deepest center, where I am being created, is also yours. ✅
- **Francis of Assisi's** "brother sun and sister moon" is "pretty good metaphysics": sun, moon, stars and planets are our "ontological siblings" (Barron also gestures to Dante). ✅
- **Sermon on the Mount:** love of enemies, turning the other cheek, going the extra mile only make sense if the enemy, truly an enemy on the surface, is an ontological sibling at depth. These gestures signal a deeper bond. 🟡 (Barron's interpretation)
- **Thomas Merton** on contemplative prayer: "Finding the place in you where you are here and now being created by God." Barron calls it "right out of Thomas Aquinas" and says Aquinas influenced Merton's conversion and monastic vocation. ✅

## Beauty, Variety, and God the Artist
- **Joyce, A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man:** the young artist explains beauty to a friend using a basket on a woman's head, in Aquinas's categories. ✅

| Aquinas's term | Meaning | Joyce's basket | Barron's golf swing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Integritas* | Wholeness | Seen as one thing, set apart from the rest | Holds together as one (amateur swings are "all over the place") |
| *Consonantia* | Harmony | Parts fitted together for their purpose | Shoulders, hips and downswing in harmony |
| *Claritas* | Radiance; the deeper sense is *claritas formae*, clarity of form | Exulting in its form | "That is a golf swing" |

- Aquinas often calls God an **artist** (and also the general of an army ordering it to its purpose). Genesis: everything good, the ensemble very good. God wants the world to display His beauty. ✅
- **Why so much variety** (fish, birds, shells, stars "by the gazillion")? What one creature cannot display, another does, so over space and time the whole becomes a "relatively adequate display" of God's glory. ✅
- **Segue:** the world is not always beautiful. Creation is the most elemental way God relates to the world; providence, how He orders it to its end, and why He permits great evil, comes in Ch.6. ✅

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 5)
- **"Bonum diffusivum sui."** God creates because the good gives itself, not because He lacks anything.
- **"God has no needs."** "He doesn't need my moral perfection. He wants my moral perfection... because it makes me happy."
- **"Sit and sink."** Eckhart's alternative to climbing toward a distant God.
- **"Nothing is more powerful than that God became a human being."** The Incarnation as the peak of divine power.
- **A square circle is not a limit on God; it is nothing.** Logical impossibilities are non-being.
- **"What stands between us and God? Nothing."** The spiritual meaning of ex nihilo.
- **"Quaedam relatio ad Creatorem, cum novitate essendi."** Creation is a kind of relation to the Creator, with newness of being.
- **"Finding the place in you where you are here and now being created by God."** Merton's definition of contemplative prayer.

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "God made the world because He needed something (worship, company, glory)": He has no needs; creation is pure gift.
- 🔴 "Omnipotence means God can do contradictions": logical impossibilities and sin are non-being, not tasks.
- 🔴 "Right and wrong are whatever God arbitrarily decrees": that is voluntarism; for Aquinas God's will is one with His mind and being.
- 🔴 "Creation happened once, long ago (the Big Bang)": creation is continual, the present dependence of everything on God.
- 🔴 "God shaped pre-existing matter": nothing pre-exists; time and space are themselves creatures.
- 🔴 "I am a self-standing thing that happens to relate to God": a creature is a relation to the Creator, with newness of being.

## Practical Takeaway
As Barron frames them: (1) when practice feels like appeasing a needy God, recall *bonum diffusivum sui*: He wants, not needs; (2) test any claim about power by asking whether it is tied to truth and the good, or is a hammer; (3) try Merton's definition literally, a few minutes of "sit and sink"; (4) use the ontological-siblings idea when an enemy is hard to love; (5) use *integritas, consonantia, claritas* to notice beauty.

## Questions to Learn From (Socratic) — with Answer Options
**1. Given Barron's contrast between Aristotle and Plato, why does Aquinas say God creates?**
A. Because God, like Aristotle's prime mover, needed something outside Himself to know and love
B. \* Because the good is diffusive of itself: God shares His life and manifests His glory out of overflowing goodness, not need
C. Because creation is a necessary emanation God cannot hold back
*Why B: Barron stresses that God has no needs; creation follows the Platonic model of the good as what gives (bonum diffusivum sui).*

**2. A student asks whether God can make a square circle and, if not, whether His power is limited. Which answer matches Aquinas?**
A. Yes, God can, because His will is above logic
B. No, so God's power is limited by an external law of logic
C. \* No, but that is no limit: a square circle is a non-entity, and God's power extends to everything consistent with being and with His own nature
*Why C: logical impossibilities and sin are forms of non-being. Saying God "cannot" do them names nothing God lacks.*

**3. Given creatio continua, what is the right way to relate creation and the Big Bang?**
A. \* The Big Bang, whatever it was, is a physical event for physicists; creation is God's ongoing, here-and-now giving of being to everything
B. The Big Bang is the moment of creation, after which God steps back
C. Creation is a motion in time that began with the Big Bang
*Why A: creation is not a motion, not in time and not in space; it is the continual rapport between ipsum esse and creatures.*

**4. Why, on Barron's reading, does the metaphysics of creation make sense of "love your enemy"?**
A. Because enemies are not really enemies at all
B. \* Because at the deepest level every creature is coming forth here and now from God, so even a real enemy is an "ontological sibling," and loving him signals that deeper bond
C. Because morality is whatever God commands, whether or not it fits reality
*Why B: Barron grants the enmity is real on the surface but grounds the Sermon on the Mount in a shared relation to the Creator. Answer C is the voluntarism the lecture rejects.*
