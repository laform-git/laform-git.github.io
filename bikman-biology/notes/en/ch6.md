
# Bikman — Basics of Biology, Ch.6: Metabolic Mechanisms
*The cell's full energy-extraction pipeline — glycolysis, beta-oxidation, the citrate cycle, the electron transport system and ATP synthase — with insulin as the master switch deciding whether acetyl-CoA is burned, stored as fat, or turned into ketones.*

*Source: Dr. Benjamin Bikman, Peterson Academy course "Basics of Biology," Lecture 6 (transcript) — the final lecture of the course, followed by a Q&A on statins and cancer metabolism. Same lecturer as the "Why We Get Sick" and "Essentials of Endocrinology" series; this lecture is the energy-chemistry foundation underneath both.*

## Why This Matters
Every hormone, receptor and transporter covered in the other Bikman series ultimately cashes out as a question about energy: is the cell breaking fuel down to make ATP, or building something up and storing it? This lecture lays out the machinery behind that choice and names the one signal that largely decides it — **insulin**.

## Scope-Setting Claim — Metabolism = Catabolism + Anabolism, Brokered by ATP
**Metabolism** is the sum of every reaction in every cell — hundreds of thousands of reactions across trillions of cells. It is the *balance* of two processes, not just "breaking things down" as the buzzword implies. ✅
- **Catabolism:** breaking things down — releases energy.
- **Anabolism:** building things up (nucleic acids, carbohydrates, proteins, lipids) — consumes energy supplied by catabolism.
- **ATP is the mediator between them** — adenosine triphosphate, the cell's "currency." It is built on a ribose sugar and a double-ring base (like the nucleic acids), with **three phosphates** on its tail; breaking those high-energy bonds lets an enzyme do work it could not otherwise do. The cell "buys" everything with it: synthesis, active transport against a gradient, nerve signals, muscle contraction. ✅
- **The fire analogy:** a campfire takes in carbon fuel plus oxygen and releases CO₂ and heat. A cell takes in carbon fuel (carbohydrate or lipid) plus oxygen and releases CO₂, a little heat, and — the key difference — **a lot of ATP**. ✅
- **Energy lineage:** all energy ultimately comes from the sun; plants bind atmospheric CO₂ into carbon bonds, and animals eat those bonds. Bikman's thought experiment: the fuel in your body was once CO₂ in the air. ✅

The lecture runs three steps in order: **(1) nutrient catabolism → (2) the citrate cycle → (3) the electron transport system + ATP synthase.**

## Punchlines & Quotable Facts (Lecture 6)
- **"Animals do not make lactic acid. It does not exist."** — we make **lactate**; lactic acid applies only to bacteria. The "lactic acid burn" blamed by the old track coach is the wrong molecule *and* the wrong cause.
- **Insulin "reigns supreme"** — its main effect is to make a cell store energy, "always, always." Understanding how a cell uses energy requires understanding insulin.
- **Acetyl-CoA is the nexus of all nutrient metabolism** involving the mitochondria — glucose, fat, ketones and lactate all funnel into it, and it can be turned into anything else.
- **One turn of the citrate cycle is worth about 10 ATP** (via NADH and FADH₂ downstream) — so an 18-carbon fatty acid, split into 9 acetyl-CoAs, yields about **90 ATP from the citrate cycle alone**.
- **Insulin as the "fat shot"** — type 1 diabetics describe insulin this way: untreated, they eat voraciously and waste away; once on insulin, intake drops and fat is gained.
- **"You're not burning fat in the fat cell"** — lipolysis only *moves* fat out of the fat cell; the burning (beta-oxidation) happens in mitochondria elsewhere.
- **Dietary carbohydrate is a non-essential nutrient** — the body can make all the glucose it needs.
- **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor and ends up as water** — metabolic water, which is how camels get much of their water from the fat in their humps.

## The Three-Step Pipeline at a Glance

| Step | Where | Input | Output | Main control point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1a. Glycolysis** | Cytosol | Glucose (6C) | 2 pyruvate (3C), NADH, a little ATP | Reactions 1, 3, 9 (one-way enzymes) — insulin on, glucagon off |
| **1b. Lipolysis → beta-oxidation** | Fat cell (lipolysis); mitochondria of any cell (beta-oxidation) | Triglyceride → free fatty acids | 2-carbon acetyl-CoA units | Insulin guards the lipid droplet; CAT1/CAT2 gate entry to mitochondria |
| **2. Citrate cycle** | Mitochondrial matrix | Acetyl-CoA (2C) | 1 ATP, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 2 CO₂ per turn | Citrate synthase inhibited by its own product (citrate) |
| **3. Electron transport + ATP synthase** | Inner mitochondrial membrane | NADH, FADH₂, O₂ | ~2.5 ATP per NADH, ~1.5 per FADH₂; water | Supply of electron carriers; oxygen as final acceptor |

Bikman stresses that **NADH and FADH₂ are not ATP** — they are loaded electron carriers. Whenever a reaction produces one, read it as "future ATP, and a lot of it." ✅

## Glycolysis — Nine Reactions, a Buy-In and a Payoff

| Reaction | What happens | Energy | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Glucokinase/hexokinase phosphorylates glucose — locks it in the cell and commits it to glycolysis | Costs ATP (buy-in) | Irreversible |
| **2** | Minor structural change | None | Reversible |
| **3** | Second buy-in phosphorylation | Costs ATP | Irreversible |
| **4** | The 6-carbon molecule is **split into two 3-carbon molecules** — everything after happens in duplicate. End of the preparatory phase | None | Reversible |
| **5** | NADH produced (payoff phase begins) | Gives NADH | Reversible |
| **6** | ATP produced | Gives ATP | Reversible |
| **7–8** | Minor rearrangements | None | Reversible |
| **9** | ATP produced; final product is **pyruvate** | Gives ATP | Irreversible |

- ✅ The two ATP spent at reactions 1 and 3 are paid back by reaction 6 alone (two molecules, one ATP each) — "you already got your money back." 🟢 Adding reaction 9 (also in duplicate), the arithmetic implies a **net of 2 ATP** per glucose from glycolysis itself; Bikman does not state the net figure in this lecture, so treat that number as my tally rather than a quote.
- ✅ Glycolysis is a "choose your own adventure" at pyruvate — **anaerobic / non-oxidative / non-mitochondrial** (pyruvate → lactate, stays in the cytosol) versus **aerobic / oxidative** (pyruvate enters the mitochondria). Aerobic = with oxygen.

**Pyruvate → lactate (anaerobic end):** uses up one NADH (the "reducing power"). ✅ When it is the only option:
- **Red blood cells** — no mitochondria, no nucleus; they only ever make lactate (and live roughly 2–3 months).
- **Vigorously working muscle** — demand outruns oxygen supply.
- **Cancer cells** — rely almost exclusively on anaerobic glycolysis. 🟡 Bikman says cancer "abhors" mitochondria; this is his emphasis, and he frames it as an active research area.

**Pyruvate → mitochondria:** requires the multi-part enzyme **pyruvate dehydrogenase**, which makes another NADH and converts pyruvate to **acetyl-CoA**. It shuts down when the mitochondria are "loaded" — too much acetyl-CoA, ATP or NADH — leaving lactate as the only exit. ✅

## Lactate Is Not the Villain — The Cori Cycle
- ✅ The burning sensation in hard-working muscle is a coincidence of state, not an effect of lactate: the same high-demand condition that produces lactate also breaks down huge amounts of ATP, and **each ATP split releases a free hydrogen**, acidifying the area and irritating nerve endings.
- ✅ **The Cori cycle** (identified by the husband-and-wife Cori team): muscle or red blood cells release lactate into blood → the liver takes it up → **gluconeogenesis** rebuilds it into glucose.
- ✅ After roughly **24 hours without food**, all blood glucose must be made from scratch by the liver, and **lactate is the primary carbon source** for it — "beautiful metabolic recycling."
- 🟡 Bikman's claim that lactate "doesn't contribute to any problem whatsoever" is stated strongly and without qualification in this lecture; carry it as his position, not a settled blanket rule.

## Gluconeogenesis and the Futile-Cycle Rule
- The three one-way glycolytic enzymes (reactions 1, 3, 9) are the **regulation points**; gluconeogenesis uses **detour enzymes** that bypass them (three steps need detours). ✅
- **Reciprocal regulation:** insulin turns the three glycolytic enzymes **on** and the detour enzymes **off**; glucagon does exactly the reverse. Insulin and glucagon are never high together, so the cell never runs both directions at once. ✅
- A cycle that builds and breaks the same thing simultaneously is a **futile cycle** — "the cell abhors the lack of efficiency." ✅
- The cell's own energy state also regulates: plenty of ATP inhibits the glycolytic enzymes. ✅
- 🟢 Practical logic: after a starchy meal, insulin is up and glucose is burned; with fasting or carbohydrate restriction, glucagon is up, the body shifts toward fat burning, and the liver makes the small amount of glucose that cells without mitochondria (like red blood cells) still require.

## Lipolysis → Beta-Oxidation → Acetyl-CoA
- **The fat cell:** one giant lipid droplet of **triglyceride** (glycerol backbone plus fatty acids) pushes the nucleus and organelles to the edge. A **phospholipid monolayer** (tails inward, heads outward) coats the droplet and guards it. ✅
- **Lipolysis** = lipase enzymes pulling free fatty acids off the triglyceride. It happens only once hormones tell the phospholipid guards to "move out of the way." **High insulin keeps the guards in place; low insulin lets lipolysis proceed.** ✅
- The fat cell has **no direct nerve link to muscle** — a working muscle changes the body's hormones, and those hormones carry the signal back to the fat cell. ✅
- **Three fates of a free fatty acid** once it reaches a cell: (1) become **phospholipids** for membranes; (2) **re-esterification** back into new triglyceride — this is what drives **fatty liver disease** when insulin-resistant fat cells leak fat and high insulin plus high free fatty acids tell the liver to store it; (3) **beta-oxidation** — the actual burning. ✅
- **CAT1 and CAT2 (carnitine acyl transferase)** on the outer and inner mitochondrial membranes escort fatty acids in, then go back out. This is a regulated checkpoint. ✅
- **Beta-oxidation** cuts the carbon chain after the beta carbon, two carbons at a time, each piece becoming **acetyl-CoA** — the same molecule glucose becomes after pyruvate dehydrogenase. ✅

## The Citrate Cycle (Krebs Cycle)
- Named after **Sir Hans Krebs** (Nobel Prize); "citrate cycle" is more precise because **citrate is the first molecule made**. It is "cyclic" because its components are reused again and again, though not arranged in a physical circle. ✅
- **Every** oxidative (mitochondrial) fuel passes through it — glucose, fat, **ketones**, even **lactate** — almost always after conversion to acetyl-CoA. ✅
- **Per acetyl-CoA, one full turn yields:** **1 ATP, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 2 CO₂.** ✅ The two CO₂ are the carbons that came in with acetyl-CoA; this is where most exhaled CO₂ originates.
- **The CO₂ loop:** we exhale CO₂ → plants take it up → plants release O₂ → we inhale it for the electron transport system. ✅

## The Fork in the Road — Lipogenesis vs. Ketogenesis (Insulin Decides)
The first reaction (acetyl-CoA → citrate, via citrate synthase) is inhibited when citrate builds up. Acetyl-CoA then needs another fate:

|  | **Insulin HIGH** | **Insulin LOW** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Acetyl-CoA goes to** | **Lipogenesis** (de novo lipogenesis — building fat from scratch, two carbons at a time) | **Ketogenesis** (ketones, fuel for every cell with mitochondria) |
| **Mechanism** | Citrate leaves the mitochondria into the cytosol and is turned back into acetyl-CoA; intermediates of lipogenesis **block CAT1/CAT2**, so fat cannot enter to be burned | No signal to stop lipolysis; fat flows out and is burned or turned to ketones |
| **Net direction** | Build and store | Break down and burn |

✅ A cell does not inherently "know" what to do — it relies on hormonal signals about the body's overall state, and here insulin is the dominant one.

**The no-insulin experiment — type 1 diabetes:**
- ✅ Without insulin there is no brake on lipolysis and no ability to make new fat: a person can overeat enormously and still not gain fat. Untreated type 1 diabetics eat voraciously, waste away, run a high metabolic rate, and lose ketones in breath and urine.
- ✅ On starting insulin, intake falls and fat gain begins — hence "fat shots," and the eating disorder **diabulimia** (skipping insulin to stay thin), which produces runaway glucose and **ketoacidosis — explicitly not the same as nutritional ketosis** from fasting or a ketogenic diet.

## Electron Transport System and Oxidative Phosphorylation
- ✅ **Oxygen is extremely electronegative** — it pulls electrons harder than almost anything in the cell. That pull is what powers the whole system.
- Bikman prefers **"system" over "chain"** because **Complex I and Complex II are not linked**: Complex I takes electrons from **NADH**, Complex II takes electrons from **FADH₂** (its own "direct deposit"). Both hand electrons to **ubiquinone (CoQ10)**, which carries them to **Complex III**, then **Complex IV**. ✅
- **Proton pumping** into the intermembrane space (between inner and outer membranes): Complex I = 4, Complex II = 0, Complex III = 2, Complex IV = 4. ✅

| Electron source | Enters at | Protons pumped | ATP equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **NADH** | Complex I | 4 + 2 + 4 = **10** | **~2.5 ATP** |
| **FADH₂** | Complex II (skips I) | 0 + 2 + 4 = **6** | **~1.5 ATP** |

- ✅ At Complex IV, **oxygen** finally receives the electrons — and picks up protons so it stays neutral rather than becoming a damaging **superoxide / reactive oxygen species** — forming **water**.
- ✅ **ATP synthase** (Bikman refuses to call it "Complex V") is the only way protons flow back into the matrix; **every 4 protons through it make 1 ATP** by attaching a free phosphate to ADP. That is why 10 protons ≈ 2.5 ATP and 6 ≈ 1.5.
- ✅ The ATP tally per citrate-cycle turn: 3 × 2.5 + 1 × 1.5 + 1 = **10 ATP**.
- **CoQ10 comes from cholesterol.** ✅ Cholesterol is converted into ubiquinone, the electron carrier. Reduced cholesterol synthesis means reduced ubiquinone, so the electron transport system suffers.

## Plants in Reverse — Chloroplast vs. Mitochondrion
- Mitochondria are long and stringy ("mitos" = thread; textbook bean shapes are slices through a string). Chloroplasts look like **stacked contact lenses**. Plant cells have a little mitochondria too, but also chloroplasts. ✅
- The chloroplast runs the mirror image: it takes in **CO₂ plus solar energy** and binds carbons into chains, releasing oxygen. The two systems keep each other in balance. ✅
- 🟡 Bikman adds that rising CO₂ is "blossoming" plant growth because CO₂ is plant fuel; this is a side comment, not developed.

## Q&A Addendum — Statins and Cancer Glucose Use
**Statins:** inhibit **HMG-CoA reductase**, the enzyme that makes cholesterol; every cell can make cholesterol, so dietary cholesterol does not set blood cholesterol. ✅ Per Bikman, consequences include muscle pain/breakdown (likely mitochondrial damage via low ubiquinone), possible effects on the heart, and reduced sex-hormone production; hence he says people on statins should also take CoQ10. 🟡 These are Bikman's stated positions; the lecture does not quantify any of them.
- 🟡 **His own paper:** statin efficacy was not predicted by LDL level alone — in people with high LDL, statins appeared to help only if **triglycerides were also high**. He says people on low-carb, ketogenic or carnivore diets may have high LDL with low triglycerides and gain no benefit.
- 🟡 He also asserts high LDL is a persistent feature of long-lived people. Worth carrying as a claim to verify, not a conclusion.

**Cancer and glucose:** the source of the glucose (liver-made vs. dietary) does not matter. ✅ Cancer cells use glucose at **about 200× the normal rate** (the basis of PET scans, which use radiolabeled glucose to find tumors) 🟡. Bikman describes **Thomas Seyfried's "glucose ketone index"** — aim for glucose and ketones near 1:1 — with reported shrinkage in some brain cancers like glioblastoma and improved efficacy of chemotherapeutics. 🟡 Presented as emerging evidence from the lecturer, not as clinical guidance.

## Key Corrections to Carry Forward
- 🔴 "Metabolism" means only burning calories: it is the balance of catabolism **and** anabolism, linked by ATP.
- 🔴 "Lactic acid builds up in muscle and causes the burn": animals make **lactate**, and the burn tracks ATP breakdown and acidity from released hydrogen, not lactate.
- 🔴 "Lipolysis is fat burning": lipolysis only releases fatty acids; **beta-oxidation in mitochondria** is the burning.
- 🔴 "Excess calories make fat regardless of hormones": with no insulin, fat storage is impossible however much is eaten; **insulin determines lipogenesis vs. ketogenesis**.
- 🔴 "Ketoacidosis and ketosis are the same": ketoacidosis (uncontrolled type 1) is not nutritional ketosis.
- 🔴 "The electron transport chain is a linear chain": Complexes I and II feed in separately via NADH and FADH₂.

## Practical Takeaway
Whether your cells are building or burning depends on one signal you can influence: insulin. High insulin: store, make fat, block fat entry to mitochondria. Low insulin: release fat, burn it, make ketones.

*Why A: ATP synthase makes one ATP per four protons, so 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 and 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5; Complex II skips Complex I's four protons.*
