# LDS-04 Men Can Become Gods

> **Error 4 of 10.** The other side of the coin. If God was once a man, then a man can become a god.

## What they teach

The goal of the plan of salvation is exaltation: to become a god. Faithful Latter-day Saints who are sealed in the temple and keep their covenants *"shall be gods, because they have no end"* (D&C 132:20). They will have *"a continuation of the seeds forever"*, which their leaders explain as eternal increase: having spirit children of their own and ruling over worlds, as Heavenly Father does.

Joseph Smith, in the King Follett Discourse (1844): *"You have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves... the same as all Gods have done before you."*

**They will quote the Church Fathers at you.** Many Latter-day Saints know that St. Athanasius wrote, *"He was made man that we might be made God."* They take it as proof that the early Church believed what they believe. It did not, and the difference is the whole point of this note.

## Why it is an error

- **It erases the line between Creator and creature.** God is uncreated; we are created. No amount of progress turns a creature into the uncreated. That line is eternal, even in heaven.
- **It repeats the oldest temptation.** The first lie in Scripture is the serpent's: *"You will be like God"* (Gen 3:5). Lucifer fell saying *"I will make myself like the Most High"* (Is 14:14).
- **It multiplies gods without end.** If every exalted couple becomes a god with its own world, the universe is full of gods, and Isaiah's "besides me there is no god" (Is 44:6) is false.
- **It mistakes the goal.** Heaven is not running our own world. It is seeing God face to face and loving him forever, with all the saints.

## Response

### Scripture ✅

- *"I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god"* (Is 44:6). God does not say "besides me and my children."
- *"You will be like God, knowing good and evil"* (Gen 3:5). This is the serpent's promise, and it brought death.
- Scripture does promise something real: we become *"partakers of the divine nature"* (2 Pet 1:4). Partakers, sharers, not owners. Iron in the fire glows with the fire's heat, but it is still iron.
- *"We shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is"* (1 Jn 3:2). Like him by seeing him, not by replacing him.
- In heaven, the twenty-four elders *"cast their crowns before the throne"* and say *"Worthy art thou, our Lord and God"* (Rev 4:10–11). The saints in glory worship; they are not worshipped.

### Catechism ✅

- CCC 300: God is infinitely greater than all his works. He is present to his creatures, but transcends them.
- CCC 460: the Word became flesh to make us "partakers of the divine nature." The Catechism quotes Athanasius and Aquinas here, and both mean sharing God's life by grace.
- CCC 1721: God put us in the world to know, love and serve him, and so to come to paradise.
- CCC 1996–1997: grace is a participation in the life of God, a free gift.

### Councils ✅

- **Lateran IV (1215):** *"Between Creator and creature no similarity can be found so great that a greater dissimilarity cannot be seen between them."*
- **Vatican I, *Dei Filius*** ch. 1 (1870): God is really and essentially distinct from the world.

### Doctors 🟢

- **St. Athanasius** is the very Father the missionaries quote. But read him whole. The same Athanasius spent fifty years insisting that only the Son is God by nature. We become sons, he wrote against the Arians, not as the Son is, by nature, but by grace and adoption. "Made God" means taken into God's life, not made a second God.
- **St. Thomas Aquinas** (*ST* I-II q.112 a.1): *"It is necessary that God alone should deify, bestowing a partaking of the Divine Nature by a participated likeness."* We receive divine life; we never become its source.

### Saints 🟢

- In a vision early in her life, **St. Catherine of Siena** heard the Lord tell her: *"Do you know, daughter, who you are and who I am? You are she who is not; I am He who is"* (Raymond of Capua, *Life* I.10). Catherine became one of the greatest mystics in the Church's history, united to God as closely as a creature can be. She never stopped being the one "who is not."

## One line at the door

> *"In the garden, the serpent promised Eve, 'You will be like God.' Who else has made that promise?"*

Then stop talking and let the question do its work.

🕯 **Reflect:** Is my hope to be like God on my own terms, or to be with God on his?

## From the full reference

### Human Destiny

**LDS teaching:** Faithful humans can become gods, with eternal increase of spirit children (D&C 132:19–20).

**Catholic teaching:** ✅ The Creator–creature distinction is eternal (CCC 300). Divinization means sharing in God's life by grace (2 Pet 1:4; CCC 460), never becoming a separate deity. No creature crosses that line, even in glory.

### PSALM 82:6 / JOHN 10:34 — "YOU ARE GODS"

- **Claim:** Humans can become gods.
- **Response:** Psalm 82 rebukes unjust human judges, and the next verse says "you shall die like men." Jesus cites it to argue from the lesser to the greater: if even judges were called "gods," how much more the Son of God. It says nothing about exaltation.
