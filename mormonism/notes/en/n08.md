# LDS-08 Salvation Through Temple Ordinances

> **Error 8 of 10.** The temple is the heart of Latter-day Saint life. It is also where their gospel parts most clearly from Christ's.

## What they teach

They distinguish two things. **Salvation** in the general sense, meaning resurrection, comes to everyone through Christ, and nearly everyone will receive some kingdom of glory. **Exaltation**, the highest glory where one becomes like Heavenly Father, requires a chain of ordinances performed by their priesthood: baptism, confirmation, the priesthood for men, the temple **endowment**, and **sealing** in eternal marriage. To enter the temple, a member needs a recommend, granted after an interview on tithing, the Word of Wisdom, chastity and loyalty to church leaders.

The Book of Mormon puts it this way: *"It is by grace that we are saved, after all we can do"* (2 Nephi 25:23). Some recent leaders read "after all we can do" more gently. But the ordinances and the worthiness interview are still required for exaltation.

The same ordinances are performed by proxy for the dead. Members are baptized, endowed and sealed in the name of deceased people whose names come from genealogical records. This is why the church gathers family records worldwide.

**Two facts worth knowing.** In 1995 the church agreed with Jewish leaders to stop proxy-baptizing Holocaust victims, after the practice caused deep offense. In 2008 the Holy See asked Catholic dioceses not to give parish registers to LDS genealogical projects, because of these proxy baptisms.

## Why it is an error

- **It puts grace last.** In the Gospel, grace comes first. It moves us to every good work, carries us through it, and crowns it. Grace that arrives only "after all we can do" has the order backwards.
- **No one can do "all he can do."** A system that makes exaltation depend on worthiness leaves sincere people anxious and the honest ones hopeless.
- **It replaces Christ's sacraments with Smith's rites.** The endowment and sealing appear nowhere in Scripture or in nineteen centuries of Christian worship.
- **It treats death as negotiable.** Scripture sets judgment after death, not a second chance by proxy.
- **It contradicts Jesus on marriage.** He said plainly there is no marrying in the resurrection.

## Response

### Scripture ✅

- *"For by grace you have been saved through faith; and this is not your own doing, it is the gift of God, not because of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works"* (Eph 2:8–10). Good works come after grace, as its fruit.
- *"He saved us, not because of deeds done by us in righteousness, but in virtue of his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal in the Holy Spirit"* (Ti 3:5).
- *"God is at work in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure"* (Phil 2:13). *"Apart from me you can do nothing"* (Jn 15:5).
- The Catholic answer is not "faith alone." It is *"faith working through love"* (Gal 5:6), all of it moved by grace.
- *"In the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven"* (Mt 22:30).
- *"It is appointed for men to die once, and after that comes judgment"* (Heb 9:27).
- *"I have spoken openly to the world... I have said nothing secretly"* (Jn 18:20).

### Catechism ✅

- CCC 1996–2005: justification and grace are God's free gift; grace precedes, prepares and elicits our free response.
- CCC 2007–2011: we have no strict right to any merit before God. Our merits are themselves God's gifts.
- CCC 1257: God has bound salvation to Baptism, but he himself is not bound by his sacraments. The Church entrusts children who die without Baptism to his mercy (CCC 1261, 1283), and teaches that those who seek God sincerely without knowing the Gospel can be saved (CCC 1260). She prays for the dead and offers Mass for them (CCC 1032). She does not baptize them.
- CCC 1603, 1617, 1640: God is the author of marriage; Christian marriage is a sign of the covenant of Christ and the Church, a bond that only death ends (cf. Mt 22:30).

### Councils ✅

- **Orange (529):** even the beginning of faith, the first desire to believe, is a gift of grace.
- **Trent**, Session VI, ch. 8 (1547): *"None of those things which precede justification, whether faith or works, merit the grace itself of justification."* Ch. 16: the merits of the just are themselves the gifts of God.

### Doctors 🟢

- **St. Augustine** fought the Pelagians, who taught that man takes the first step and God rewards the effort. His prayer became the Church's answer: *"Give what you command, and command what you will"* (*Confessions* X.29). And: *"When God crowns our merits, he crowns nothing other than his own gifts"* (*Letter* 194).

### Saints 🟢

- **St. Thérèse of Lisieux**, who died at twenty-four and is now a Doctor of the Church, offered herself to God's merciful love in 1895: *"In the evening of this life, I shall appear before you with empty hands, for I do not ask you, Lord, to count my works. All our justice is stained in your sight."* No recommend, no interview, no list. Empty hands, filled by Christ.

## One line at the door

> *"Your book says we are saved by grace 'after all we can do.' Have you done all you can do? How would you know?"*

Then stop talking and let the question do its work.

🕯 **Reflect:** Do I receive grace as God's gift, or treat my practices as a checklist?

## From the full reference

### Salvation

**LDS teaching:** Exaltation, the highest glory, requires faith, repentance, baptism, and temple ordinances (endowment, celestial marriage) performed under LDS priesthood authority.

**Catholic teaching:** 🟢 Salvation is by grace, received through faith working in love (Gal 5:6; CCC 1987–2029), in sacraments Christ himself instituted. (The Catholic answer is not "faith alone," which Trent rejected.) LDS ordinances rest on a priesthood the Church does not recognize, and they aim at becoming a god, not at holiness in Christ.

### TEMPLE ENDOWMENT AND GARMENTS

- **Claim:** Secret temple rites and sacred undergarments are needed for exaltation.
- **Response:** Christ taught openly: "I have said nothing secretly" (Jn 18:20). The rites closely track the Masonic ritual Smith joined in 1842, weeks before introducing the endowment. The Christian sacraments are public and instituted by Christ.

### ETERNAL (CELESTIAL) MARRIAGE

- **Claim:** Marriage sealed in the temple lasts forever and is required for the highest glory.
- **Response:** "In the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage" (Mt 22:30). Marriage is a holy sacrament for this life, pointing to the eternal union of Christ and the Church (CCC 1603, 1617).

### BAPTISM FOR THE DEAD

- **Claim:** Members are baptized on behalf of the dead so they can accept the gospel.
- **Response:** "It is appointed for men to die once, and after that comes judgment" (Heb 9:27). The Church prays and offers Mass for the dead, which Scripture supports (2 Macc 12:45), but does not baptize for them.
