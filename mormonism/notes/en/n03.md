# LDS-03 Three Gods, Not the Trinity

> **Error 3 of 10.** They name the Father, the Son and the Holy Ghost, just as we do. But they count three gods.

## What they teach

They call it the Godhead: three separate beings. The Father has a body of flesh and bones, the Son has a body of flesh and bones, and the Holy Ghost is a personage of spirit (D&C 130:22). They are one in purpose, the way three men can be one in a plan, but not one God.

Joseph Smith said it without hedging in a sermon of June 1844, days before his death: *"I have always declared God to be a distinct personage, Jesus Christ a separate and distinct personage from God the Father, and that the Holy Ghost was a distinct personage and a Spirit: and these three constitute three distinct personages and three Gods."*

Their account of the First Vision supports this: in 1820, they say, the Father and the Son appeared to the young Smith as two separate personages.

**Their own book disagrees.** The Book of Mormon, published in 1830, speaks of one God. *"Is there more than one God? And he answered, No"* (Alma 11:28–29). It ends a sermon with *"the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, which is one God, without end"* (2 Nephi 31:21). Smith's teaching moved from one God toward many gods over fourteen years.

## Why it is an error

- **It is polytheism.** Three gods who agree are still three gods. That is what the pagans believed, with a smaller pantheon.
- **It breaks the first commandment.** Israel's creed is *"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD"* (Dt 6:4). Jesus called it the first of all commandments (Mk 12:29).
- **It changes baptism.** Their baptism uses the right words, "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost," but means three gods. In 2001 the Church ruled that LDS baptism is invalid, chiefly for this reason.
- **It misreads the Trinity.** The Trinity is not three beings sharing a plan. It is one God, one divine substance, in three Persons who are really distinct by their relations.

## Response

### Scripture ✅

- *"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD"* (Dt 6:4).
- *"Before me no god was formed, nor shall there be any after me"* (Is 43:10). *"I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god"* (Is 44:6). *"Is there a God besides me? There is no Rock; I know not any"* (Is 44:8).
- *"There is no God but one... for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things... and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things"* (1 Cor 8:4–6). One God, and the Lord Jesus is placed inside that one God's work of creation.
- *"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"* (Mt 28:19). One name, not three names.
- *"I and the Father are one"* (Jn 10:30). The crowd took up stones, because he was making himself God (Jn 10:33), not because he claimed to share a plan.

### Catechism ✅

- CCC 253: *"We do not confess three Gods, but one God in three persons."*
- CCC 254: the divine Persons are really distinct from one another, by their relations of origin.
- CCC 255: the Persons are relative to one another; the Father is wholly in the Son and wholly in the Spirit.
- CCC 202, 228: the first commandment and Israel's faith in one God.

### Councils and Creeds ✅

- **Athanasian Creed** (5th–6th c.): *"The Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God; and yet they are not three Gods, but one God."*
- **Lateran IV (1215):** the Father, Son and Holy Spirit are three Persons and one absolutely simple essence, substance or nature.
- **Florence (1442):** in God *"everything is one where there is no opposition of relationship."*

### Doctors 🟢

- **St. Gregory of Nazianzus**, called "the Theologian," preached in Constantinople when the Church there was small and besieged: *"No sooner do I conceive of the One than I am illumined by the splendor of the Three; no sooner do I distinguish them than I am carried back to the One"* (*Oration* 40.41).
- **St. Augustine**, *On the Trinity*: Father, Son and Spirit are not three gods but one God, because they share one and the same divine substance, equal in everything.

### Saints 🟢

- Late in the fourth century, a man named Ablabius asked **St. Gregory of Nyssa** the very question the missionaries raise: if Peter, James and John are three men, why aren't Father, Son and Spirit three gods? Gregory answered in a short letter still read today, *On Not Three Gods*. Three men act separately; Father, Son and Spirit never do. Every work of God comes from the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit, as one act of one God.

## One line at the door

> *"Your own Book of Mormon asks, 'Is there more than one God?' and answers, 'No' (Alma 11:28–29). Why did Joseph Smith later teach three Gods?"*

Then stop talking and let the question do its work.

## From the full reference

### The Trinity

**LDS teaching:** The Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are three separate gods, united in purpose only.

**Catholic teaching:** ✅ One God in three Persons, one divine substance (CCC 253–256). Baptism is in the one "name" of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit (Mt 28:19). This is the chief reason the Church ruled LDS baptism invalid in 2001: the words are Trinitarian, but the God invoked is not.
