# DEPRESSION — 03 Inflammation and Sickness Behaviour

*Why depression can feel exactly like having the flu — and why, for a subgroup of people, that is not a metaphor.*

## Sickness behaviour: a program, not a side effect

When the body fights an infection, immune cells release signalling proteins called **cytokines** — chiefly IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α. These reach the brain through the vagus nerve, through leaky spots in the blood–brain barrier, and by activating the brain's own immune cells (**microglia**). 🟢

The brain then switches on a coordinated program the physiologist Robert Dantzer called **sickness behaviour**: fatigue, loss of appetite, sleepiness, withdrawal from others, loss of interest and pleasure, slowed thinking. 🟢

It is adaptive. Withdrawing saves energy for the immune fight and keeps an infectious animal away from the group. The problem: the list is almost identical to the symptoms of depression ([[DEPRESSION — 01 What Depression Is]]). If the inflammatory signal stays on for months without an infection, the program keeps running.

## The evidence that inflammation can cause depression

- **Interferon therapy.** Patients given interferon-alpha (an immune drug once standard for hepatitis C) developed major depression in roughly **30–50%** of cases within months. Pre-treating with an antidepressant cut that rate sharply. 🟢
- **Experiments in healthy people.** A small dose of bacterial endotoxin or typhoid vaccine raises cytokines and, within hours, produces low mood, social disconnection and reduced reward responses on brain scans. 🟢
- **Blood levels.** Meta-analyses find higher IL-6, TNF-α and C-reactive protein (CRP) in depressed people on average. Roughly **one in four** depressed patients has low-grade inflammation (CRP above 3 mg/L). 🟢
- **Prospective data.** Higher inflammatory markers in childhood or mid-life predict later depression. 🟡
- **Arrows both ways.** Depression raises inflammation too — through stress, poor sleep, inactivity and diet. 🟢

Edward Bullmore (*The Inflamed Mind*) argues medicine missed this because of a "Cartesian" split: mind treated by psychiatry, body by everyone else, with the immune system crossing the line unnoticed.

## How inflammation changes brain chemistry

- **Tryptophan diverted.** Inflammation switches on an enzyme (IDO) that sends tryptophan — serotonin's raw material — down the **kynurenine pathway** instead. One end product, quinolinic acid, overstimulates glutamate receptors and can be toxic to neurons. 🟡
- **Dopamine starved.** Inflammation depletes a co-factor (BH4) needed to make dopamine, and reduces activity in reward circuits. This links inflammation specifically to **anhedonia and low motivation**. 🟡
- **Plasticity blocked.** Cytokines reduce BDNF and neurogenesis ([[DEPRESSION — 05 Neuroplasticity and Why Antidepressants Take Weeks]]). 🟡
- **Cortisol resistance.** Chronic stress makes immune cells deaf to cortisol's anti-inflammatory signal ([[DEPRESSION — 02 Stress, Cortisol, Genes and Early Adversity]]). 🟡

## Does treating inflammation treat depression?

- **Only in the inflamed.** In a trial of infliximab (an anti-TNF drug), the overall result was negative — but people with **high baseline CRP improved**, while those with low CRP did slightly worse than placebo. 🟡
- The same pattern appears with omega-3 (EPA): benefit concentrated in people with raised inflammatory markers. 🟡
- Anti-inflammatory add-ons in general (NSAIDs, minocycline, statins) show modest, inconsistent benefit across meta-analyses. 🔴
- Antidepressants work less well in people with high CRP; some evidence suggests different drug choices for this subgroup. 🟠
- The lifestyle levers that lower inflammation — exercise, sleep, weight loss, Mediterranean-style eating, not smoking — are also antidepressant ([[DEPRESSION — 10 Treatments Compared]]). 🟡

The emerging picture: **inflammatory depression is a subtype**, not the whole of depression. A CRP test can help identify it. 🟡

## Example case

Grief is a physical event as well as an emotional one. In the months after a death, sleep breaks up, exercise drops, eating changes, and the stress system runs hot — each of which raises inflammation. If the low mood is dominated by heaviness, exhaustion, wanting to withdraw and a flu-like flatness, an inflammatory component is plausible. A high-sensitivity CRP test, alongside the usual labs, is one way to check. The practical levers are the same ones that lower inflammation generally: protected sleep, daily movement, regular meals with whole foods and oily fish.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Sickness behaviour and depression share almost the same symptom list.
- Inflammation can cause depressive symptoms in healthy people — this is experimentally proven.
- About one in four depressed people has measurable low-grade inflammation.
- Inflammation steals tryptophan from serotonin and starves dopamine — anhedonia is the signature.
- Anti-inflammatory treatment helps the inflamed subgroup, not everyone.
- Sleep, movement, abdominal fat and diet are the main everyday dials.
