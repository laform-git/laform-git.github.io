# DEPRESSION — 10 Treatments Compared

*An honest comparison: what each option does, how big the effect is, what it costs, and where medication clearly earns its place.*

> **Never stop or change a psychiatric medication abruptly. Any change is planned with the prescriber.** Thoughts of suicide → call or text **988** (US) or go to an emergency department.

## How big are the effects?

Depression scales make effects hard to feel. A useful anchor: on the 52-point Hamilton scale, a difference of about **3 points** is often cited as the smallest change a clinician would notice.

- **Antidepressants vs placebo:** in the largest network meta-analysis (Cipriani 2018, 522 trials, ~116,000 people), all 21 drugs beat placebo, with an average standardised effect of about **0.3** — roughly **2 points** on the Hamilton scale. 🟢 that they beat placebo; 🔴 whether the average difference is clinically meaningful.
- **Severity matters:** the drug–placebo gap is small in mild depression and larger in severe depression. 🟢
- **Averages hide a subgroup:** FDA data analysed in 2022 suggest about **15%** of patients get a large benefit beyond placebo, while most get little extra. 🟡 No one can yet predict who.
- **Placebo response** in depression trials is large — often 35–40%. Psychologist Robert Pihl describes the drug-specific effect as roughly 10% on top. 🟡
- **Psychotherapy** (CBT, behavioural activation, interpersonal therapy) has effects of similar size to medication in mild–moderate depression; effects look bigger against waiting-list controls. 🟢 **Combined** treatment beats either alone. 🟢

## STAR*D: the real-world trial and its correction

STAR*D (~4,000 patients, US, 2000s) tested up to four sequential treatment steps.

- **Originally reported:** remission rates of about 37%, 31%, 14% and 13% at steps 1–4, and a cumulative remission of **67%**. 🟡
- **2023 reanalysis** (Pigott and colleagues) using the trial's own pre-specified outcome and excluding ineligible patients: cumulative remission about **35%**. 🔴 The original figure was inflated by protocol changes.
- Many who remitted later relapsed or dropped out during follow-up. 🟡
- Lesson: each additional medication step helps fewer people; switching strategy (therapy, combination, ECT, other approaches) matters after two failures.

## Antidepressants: the full downside list

| Effect | What is known | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sexual dysfunction** | Low libido, delayed or absent orgasm, erectile problems; 25–70% depending on how it is asked; lower with bupropion, mirtazapine, vortioxetine | 🟢 |
| **Persistent sexual dysfunction after stopping (PSSD)** | Recognised on EU labels since 2019; how common is unknown | 🔴 |
| **Emotional blunting** | Feeling flat, less able to cry or feel joy; reported by roughly 40–60% of SSRI users in surveys; dose-related | 🟡 |
| **Sleep and REM** | REM suppression, vivid or disturbing dreams, more night awakenings, restless legs, teeth grinding; REM rebound on stopping ([[DEPRESSION — 06 Sleep and Emotion REM as Overnight Therapy]]) | 🟢 |
| **Weight gain** | Mainly long-term; worst with mirtazapine and some tricyclics | 🟢 |
| **Withdrawal (discontinuation) symptoms** | Dizziness, "brain zaps", irritability, flu-like feelings, insomnia, mood swings; worst with paroxetine and venlafaxine | 🟢 that it happens |
| **How common is withdrawal?** | One review claimed ~56%; a 2024 meta-analysis found ~31% with symptoms vs ~17% after stopping placebo — about **15% attributable** to the drug, ~3% severe | 🔴 estimates conflict |
| **Bleeding** | Higher risk combined with aspirin, NSAIDs or blood thinners, including GI bleeding | 🟢 |
| **Low sodium, falls** | Especially in older adults | 🟢 |
| **Suicidal thoughts in under-25s** | Boxed warning; mainly first weeks | 🟢 |
| **Mania switch** | In undiagnosed bipolar disorder ([[DEPRESSION — 12 Seasonal Depression and Bipolar Caution]]) | 🟢 |
| **Serotonin syndrome** | Agitation, fever, sweating, tremor, muscle jerks — emergency; risk from combining serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tramadol, triptans, dextromethorphan, St John's wort, SAMe, linezolid) | 🟢 |
| **Urinary retention** | Anticholinergic drugs (tricyclics, paroxetine, diphenhydramine sleep aids) and SNRIs can worsen urination — relevant with an enlarged prostate | 🟢 |

**Tapering:** a slow, proportional taper (reducing by a fraction of the *current* dose, with smaller steps at low doses — "hyperbolic tapering") reduces withdrawal. 🟡 Always with the prescriber.

## Where medication clearly earns its place

- **Severe** depression, especially melancholic (early waking, weight loss, slowed movement). 🟢
- **Psychotic** depression (antidepressant plus antipsychotic, or ECT). 🟢
- **Suicidal risk** with inability to engage in other treatment. 🟢
- **Recurrent** depression: staying on an effective antidepressant roughly **halves** relapse compared with switching to placebo. In the ANTLER trial, relapse was 56% after stopping vs 39% after continuing. 🟢
- **When nothing else is possible** — when depression prevents therapy, exercise or routine, medication can restore enough function to start them. 🟡
- **ECT** remains the most effective treatment for severe, treatment-resistant or psychotic depression (remission often 60–80%), with short-term memory side effects. 🟢

| Class | Examples | Notes |

| SSRIs | sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine | First-line; sexual side effects, blunting |
| SNRIs | venlafaxine, duloxetine | Also for pain; blood pressure, sweating, urinary hesitancy; venlafaxine withdrawal |
| Bupropion | Wellbutrin | Activating; few sexual effects; no REM suppression; lowers seizure threshold |

| Rapid-acting | esketamine (nasal) | Treatment-resistant depression; supervised dosing |

## Non-drug treatments compared

| Approach | Effect | Evidence | Costs and cautions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Exercise** — walking or jogging, strength training, yoga; more intense = more effect | Moderate; in a 2024 network meta-analysis (218 trials) comparable to psychotherapy and antidepressants | 🟢 that it helps; 🟡 for size (many trials low quality) | Hardest to start when depressed — start small |
| **CBT** | Moderate; lower relapse after stopping than medication | 🟢 | Access, cost, effort |
| **Behavioural activation** — scheduling rewarding and meaningful activity | About as effective as CBT, simpler to deliver | 🟢 | Same |
| **Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy** | Reduces relapse in people with 3+ past episodes | 🟢 | Prevention, not acute treatment |
| **CBT-I** for insomnia | Improves depression and prevents new episodes | 🟢 | See [[DEPRESSION — 07 The Insomnia–Depression Loop and CBT-I]] |
| **Morning bright light** (10,000 lux, ~30 min soon after waking) | Strong for seasonal; moderate for non-seasonal; light plus an SSRI beat either alone in one trial | 🟢 seasonal / 🟡 non-seasonal | Can trigger mania; caution with eye disease and photosensitising drugs |
| **Dim light at night** | Evening light suppresses melatonin and delays the clock; bedroom light at night linked to depressive symptoms in older adults | 🟡 human observational, 🟠 animal | Free |
| **Fixed wake time** | Stabilises the circadian rhythm | 🟡 | Free |
| **Mediterranean-style diet** | SMILES: 32% vs 8% remission | 🟡 small trials | [[DEPRESSION — 04 Brain Energy Mitochondria and Insulin]] |
| **Social connection** | Isolation predicts onset; connection protects | 🟡 observational | Hardest when depressed |
| **TMS** (magnetic stimulation) | Moderate in treatment-resistant depression; accelerated protocols faster | 🟡 | Time, cost, rare seizures |

## Supplements

Not regulated like drugs: look for third-party verification (USP, NSF). Several interact with antidepressants. Doses below are those **studied in trials**, not recommendations.

| Supplement | Studied dose | Evidence | Cautions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Omega-3, EPA-predominant | ~1–2 g/day EPA, ≥60% EPA | 🟡 as add-on; best in inflamed subgroup | Mild bleeding risk with blood thinners |
| Saffron | 30 mg/day | 🟡 small trials, many from one region | Short-term data only; toxic at high doses |
| St John's wort | ~900 mg/day extract | 🟢 for mild–moderate (Cochrane) | **Many dangerous interactions**: serotonin syndrome with SSRIs; weakens blood thinners, statins, others |
| L-methylfolate | 15 mg/day (prescription) | 🟡 as SSRI add-on | Mostly augmentation data |
| Vitamin D | Per blood level | 🟡 only if deficient | Don't megadose |
| SAMe | 400–1,600 mg/day | 🟠 | Serotonin risk with SSRIs; can trigger mania |
| Creatine | 3–5 g/day | 🟠 add-on, small trials | Generally safe |
| Magnesium | 200–400 mg | 🟠 | Diarrhoea; caution in kidney disease |
| Probiotics | Varies by strain | 🟠 inconsistent | Low risk |
| Inositol | 12–18 g/day in mood trials | 🔴 no clear benefit for depression | Possible mania in bipolar |

## Voices worth reading — with caveats

- **Johann Hari** (*Lost Connections*) is right that loss of connection, meaning and status drives depression. He overstates the case against medication and has been criticised for misrepresenting some research. 🔴 Use for the social causes, not the drug verdict.
- **Andrew Huberman** popularises useful mechanisms (morning light, the physiological sigh, exercise). Treat specific protocols as supporting, not primary, evidence. 🟡

## A severity-based map

- **Mild:** behavioural activation or CBT, exercise, sleep and light, diet, social contact; medication not usually first-line. 🟢
- **Moderate:** therapy **or** medication, ideally both, plus the lifestyle base. 🟢
- **Severe, suicidal or psychotic:** medication and/or ECT urgently, with therapy and lifestyle supports added as function returns. 🟢
- **Recurrent:** plan for prevention — continued medication, MBCT, sleep protection. 🟢

## Example case

A bereaved adult with poor sleep and depressive episodes of moderate severity, no suicidal thinking, still functioning at work. The evidence points first to the foundations — fixed wake time, morning light, dim evenings, CBT-I for the sleep, daily exercise, regular meals, connection — plus a talking therapy (behavioural activation or grief-focused therapy). Medication becomes a stronger option if episodes deepen, recur, or prevent the other steps; its trade-offs (sexual effects, blunting, REM suppression, withdrawal) are part of an informed choice with the prescriber, and so is the evidence that in severe or recurrent depression it substantially lowers risk.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Antidepressants beat placebo, modestly on average and more in severe depression.
- STAR*D's real cumulative remission was closer to 35% than 67%.
- Downsides are real: sexual effects, blunting, REM suppression, withdrawal. Never stop abruptly.
- Medication clearly earns its place in severe, psychotic, suicidal and recurrent depression; ECT is the most effective option for the most severe cases.
- Exercise, CBT, behavioural activation, CBT-I and light have solid evidence; combined treatment beats any single one.
- Supplements: omega-3 (EPA) and saffron have the best data; St John's wort works but interacts dangerously.
