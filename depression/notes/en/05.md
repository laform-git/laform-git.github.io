# DEPRESSION — 05 Neuroplasticity and Why Antidepressants Take Weeks

*If antidepressants raise serotonin within hours, why does mood take weeks to move? The answer is the leading modern explanation of how depression treatments work.*

## The delay that broke the old theory

An SSRI blocks serotonin reuptake within **hours** of the first dose. Mood improvement, when it comes, typically takes **2–6 weeks**. 🟢 If depression were simply low serotonin ([[DEPRESSION — 01 What Depression Is]]), the effect should be immediate. The gap means the drug is starting a slower process — and the best candidate is **neuroplasticity**: the brain's ability to grow, prune and rewire connections.

## Depression as a loss of plasticity

- Chronic stress and depression are associated with **fewer dendritic spines** (the contact points between neurons) in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus. 🟢 in animals, 🟡 in human post-mortem and imaging studies.
- **BDNF** (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) is the main growth signal for this rewiring — often described as fertiliser for neurons. Stress, inflammation and sleep loss lower it; depressed patients have lower blood BDNF on average. 🟡
- The **neurotrophic hypothesis** (Ronald Duman and colleagues): depression is partly a state in which mood circuits have lost connections and the ability to remake them. 🟡

## How the main treatments converge on plasticity

| Treatment | Plasticity link | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SSRIs and SNRIs | BDNF rises over weeks; in mice, antidepressant effects need new hippocampal neurons; a 2021 study suggests antidepressants bind the BDNF receptor (TrkB) directly | 🟡 / 🟠 |
| Ketamine and esketamine | Block NMDA glutamate receptors → glutamate burst → BDNF release → new synapses within **24 hours**; mood lifts in hours, typically fades within a week or two | 🟢 for speed, 🟡 for durability |
| Psilocybin | Rapid growth of dendritic spines in animals; in trials, large drops in depression scores after one or two supervised sessions | 🟠 blinding is nearly impossible |
| ECT | Strong increases in hippocampal volume and BDNF | 🟡 |
| Exercise | Raises BDNF reliably; increases hippocampal volume in older adults | 🟢 for BDNF rise |
| Sleep | Sleep loss lowers BDNF and impairs synaptic remodelling | 🟡 |

Whether adults keep making new neurons in the human hippocampus is itself disputed — two major 2018 studies reached opposite conclusions. 🔴 Synapse growth, not new neurons, is the safer core of the theory.

## The window, not the cure

A refinement from Eero Castrén and others: antidepressants do not choose the direction of change; they **reopen a window of plasticity**. 🟡 What fills that window is experience — what the person does, thinks, practises and is exposed to while the brain is malleable.

This explains several observations:

- **Medication plus therapy beats either alone.** 🟢 Therapy supplies the direction; the drug supplies the malleability.
- **Environment matters during treatment.** In mice, an antidepressant given in an enriched environment helps; the same drug in a stressful environment can make things worse. 🟠
- Psychologist Catherine Harmer's work shows SSRIs shift **emotional bias** — noticing faces as less threatening — within hours, before mood changes. Mood follows as the person accumulates more neutral or positive experiences. 🟡

## Practical consequences

- **Early signal:** some improvement in the first 2 weeks predicts eventual response; no improvement at all by 2–4 weeks at an adequate dose makes response less likely. 🟢 This is a conversation for the prescriber, not a reason to stop.
- **Full effect:** judge an antidepressant at 4–8 weeks at an adequate dose. 🟢
- **Use the window:** activity, social contact, therapy, exercise and sleep during the early weeks of any treatment are not extras — they decide what gets wired in. 🟡
- **Ketamine's speed** comes at a cost: dissociation during treatment, blood-pressure rises, abuse potential, and effects that need repeated dosing. 🟢

## Example case

Grief itself is a form of learning: the brain has to rewrite a map in which the dead person was always present ([[DEPRESSION — 09 Grief Normal and Stuck]]). That rewriting needs plasticity. Months of broken sleep, inactivity and stress lower BDNF and slow the rewrite — which can turn a passing grief into a stuck one. Anything that raises plasticity (exercise, sleep, novelty, connection, and medication if prescribed) works best when paired with new experiences that let the map update.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Serotonin rises in hours; mood moves in weeks. The gap is plasticity.
- Depression involves lost connections in mood circuits; BDNF is the main regrowth signal.
- Drugs, ketamine, ECT, exercise and sleep all converge on plasticity.
- Antidepressants open a window; experience decides what fills it.
- Judge a medication at 4–8 weeks; early partial response is a good sign.
