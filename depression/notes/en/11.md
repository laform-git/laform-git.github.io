# DEPRESSION — 11 Anxiety, Burnout and Chronic Stress

*Anxiety, burnout and chronic stress share depression's machinery and often travel with it. This note separates them and covers what is specific to each.*

## Fear and anxiety are different circuits

- **Fear** responds to an immediate, specific threat; it is driven by the amygdala and fades when the threat passes. 🟢
- **Anxiety** is anticipatory — a sustained state of alertness to uncertain, possible threats. It relies more on a nearby region, the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST). 🟡
- About **half** of people with major depression also have an anxiety disorder; anxiety usually comes first. 🟢 They share genes, the stress system ([[DEPRESSION — 02 Stress, Cortisol, Genes and Early Adversity]]) and treatments.

## The main anxiety disorders

| Disorder | Core feature |
| --- | --- |
| Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD) | Excessive, hard-to-control worry about many things, 6+ months, with tension, fatigue, poor sleep, irritability |
| Panic disorder | Recurrent unexpected panic attacks plus fear of the next one |
| Social anxiety | Fear of scrutiny and embarrassment |
| Health anxiety | Persistent fear of serious illness despite reassurance |

**Why worry persists:** worry feels like problem-solving and briefly reduces physical arousal, so it is reinforced — but it keeps the threat alive and prevents learning that the feared outcome is unlikely. Intolerance of uncertainty is a core driver. 🟡

**Treatment:** 🟢 CBT, especially **exposure** (approaching what is avoided until the alarm learns it is safe); SSRIs and SNRIs; exercise (🟡). **Benzodiazepines** work fast but bring tolerance, dependence, rebound anxiety, falls and memory effects — short-term only. 🟢 **Caffeine** at high doses and **alcohol** (rebound anxiety the next day) make anxiety worse. 🟡 Slow breathing with a long exhale — the "physiological sigh" promoted by Andrew Huberman — improved mood and lowered arousal in a small 2023 trial. 🟠

## Burnout

- The WHO classifies burnout as an **occupational phenomenon**, not a medical condition: exhaustion, mental distance or cynicism about work, and reduced effectiveness (Christina Maslach's three dimensions). 🟢
- Overlap with depression is large; some researchers argue burnout is largely work-located depression. 🔴 The practical difference: burnout improves with time off and changed work conditions; depression follows the person into the rest of life.
- Drivers: workload, **lack of control**, insufficient reward, poor community, unfairness, value conflict (Maslach's six areas). 🟡 Jobs combining high demand with low control, or high effort with low reward, predict depression. 🟡
- Cortisol is often **blunted** in burnout rather than high. 🟡
- Recovery depends on **psychological detachment** from work in off-hours, sleep and exercise. 🟡

## Chronic stress and allostatic load

Bruce McEwen's concept: the body adapts to stress (allostasis), but repeated or unrelenting activation leaves cumulative wear — **allostatic load** — visible as higher blood pressure, abdominal fat, insulin resistance, inflammation, poor sleep and a shrinking hippocampus. 🟡 This is Sapolsky's point in physiological terms: the stress response is built for short emergencies.

**Loneliness** belongs here: chronic loneliness raises mortality risk to a degree comparable with well-known risk factors and predicts depression. 🟢 (observational, large)

## Example case

After a death, anxiety often arrives alongside sadness: worry about one's own health or other loved ones, a startle at the phone, difficulty with uncertainty. Practical responsibilities — paperwork, a business, a household — can add a burnout layer. Distinguishing the three helps: anxiety responds to exposure and reducing avoidance; burnout to workload, control and real time off; depression to the approaches in [[DEPRESSION — 10 Treatments Compared]].

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- Fear is about now; anxiety is about maybe. Half of depressed people also have an anxiety disorder.
- Worry is reinforced because it feels useful; exposure, not reassurance, retrains the alarm.
- Benzodiazepines are short-term tools only.
- Burnout is work-located and responds to control and time off; depression follows the person everywhere.
- Unrelenting stress leaves physical wear; loneliness is a health risk.
