# DEPRESSION — 13 Trauma, Substances and Screens

*Three things that commonly ride alongside depression: trauma, the substances people use to cope, and the screens that fill empty hours. General science only.*

## PTSD (brief)

- After trauma, most people recover; about **6%** of US adults develop PTSD at some point. 🟢 Core features: intrusive memories and nightmares, avoidance, negative changes in mood and beliefs, hyperarousal, lasting more than a month.
- Mechanism: a fear memory that fails to be updated as "past" — an overactive amygdala with weaker hippocampal (context) and prefrontal (calming) control; noradrenaline stays high, including during REM ([[DEPRESSION — 06 Sleep and Emotion REM as Overnight Therapy]]). 🟡
- About half of people with PTSD also have depression. 🟢 Sudden or violent deaths can produce PTSD plus grief ([[DEPRESSION — 09 Grief Normal and Stuck]]).
- First-line treatment: **trauma-focused therapies** — prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy, EMDR. 🟢 SSRIs help modestly. 🟡 MDMA-assisted therapy was not approved by the FDA in 2024. 🟢

## Alcohol

- Alcohol enhances GABA (calming) and blocks glutamate (excitatory). As it wears off, the brain rebounds into an **excitatory, anxious state** — the "hangxiety" and 3am waking. 🟢
- It suppresses REM in the first half of the night, then fragments the second half. 🟢 Mechanism in [[PERFECT SLEEP — 08A Sedative Mechanisms THC, Alcohol and CBD]].
- Alcohol use disorder roughly **doubles** depression risk; long-term data suggest heavy drinking causes depression more than the reverse, though both directions exist. 🟡
- Many alcohol-associated depressions **lift within 2–4 weeks of abstinence**. 🟡
- Drinking to cope with grief or low mood (self-medication) is common, especially in men, and can mask depression. 🟡
- Dangerous with sedating medications; worsens depression on any antidepressant. 🟢

## Cannabis, nicotine and caffeine

- **Cannabis:** heavy and early-onset use is associated with higher rates of depression, bipolar disorder and psychosis. 🟡 Withdrawal brings insomnia and vivid dreams (REM rebound). 🟢
- **Nicotine:** smokers are more often depressed; quitting is associated with **improved** mood and anxiety, not worse. 🟡
- **Caffeine:** high doses worsen anxiety and sleep; moderate coffee intake is associated with slightly lower depression risk observationally. 🟡

## The reward system and screens

**Wanting vs liking.** Dopamine drives **wanting** — anticipation and pursuit — more than pleasure itself ("liking" uses opioid and other signals). 🟢 This is why cues and unpredictability are so powerful.

**The pleasure–pain balance.** Anna Lembke (*Dopamine Nation*) applies the opponent-process idea: a large reward is followed by a counter-swing below baseline; with repetition the brain adapts, the high shrinks and the low deepens — tolerance and between-dose anhedonia. 🟡 for drugs of addiction; extending it to everyday digital habits is plausible but less tested. 🟠

**Variable rewards.** Notifications, feeds and pull-to-refresh deliver rewards on an unpredictable schedule — the reinforcement pattern that produces the most persistent behaviour. 🟢 (behavioural principle)

**Screens and mood — what the evidence shows.**

- Population correlations between social media use and depression are **small** on average. 🔴 Researchers disagree on whether they matter, especially for adolescents.
- Experiments: limiting social media to about 30 minutes a day for three weeks reduced loneliness and depressive symptoms in students; deactivating Facebook for four weeks produced small well-being gains in adults. 🟡
- The likeliest mechanisms are **displacement** (less sleep, movement and in-person time), **evening light and arousal** delaying sleep, **social comparison**, and doomscrolling feeding rumination. 🟡

## Example case

In the evenings after a loss, the empty hours are the hardest. A drink takes the edge off, then returns as 3am waking; scrolling fills the time, pushes bedtime later and replaces calls or walks. Neither causes grief, but both can quietly deepen depression and break sleep. Taking the evening drink out and the phone out of the bedroom are low-cost tests: if mood and sleep improve over two to four weeks, they were part of the problem.

## WHAT TO HOLD ONTO

- PTSD is a fear memory stuck in the present; trauma-focused therapy is first-line.
- Alcohol calms, then rebounds into anxiety and broken sleep; many alcohol-linked depressions lift within weeks of stopping.
- Quitting smoking improves mood.
- Dopamine is about wanting; unpredictable rewards are the stickiest.
- Screen effects on mood are small on average; sleep displacement and evening use are the likeliest harms.
