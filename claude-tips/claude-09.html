CLAUDE CLASS · CLAUDE-09 · ADVANCED

Let Claude Work While You're Away

Stop chatting step by step. Hand over a whole job, or a job that repeats, and come back to the result.

In this lesson
  • The difference between chatting and delegating
  • How to write a brief that Claude can finish on its own
  • How to set up a scheduled task and check that it worked
The big idea

Chatting

You ask, read, ask again. You stay at the screen. Good for thinking together.

→

Delegating

You give one complete brief, Claude works through the steps, and you get a finished result. Good for jobs you already know how to describe.

The secret is the brief. Talk to Claude like a capable assistant on their first day: they are smart, but they don't know what you didn't say. Use the five parts:

1 · Goal

What done looks like.

2 · Sources

Where to look: which emails, folder, pages.

3 · Output

What to make, and where to save it.

4 · Rules

Limits: don't send, don't delete, ask before paying.

5 · Report

What to tell you at the end, in how many lines.

Step by step

A. One big job now

1Write the brief with the five parts in one message.
2Claude makes a task list and works through it. You can leave.
3Come back, read the result, and correct what's off in one message.

B. A job that repeats (scheduled task)

1Say when: "Every Monday at 8 am…" or "Tomorrow at 6 pm…", then the brief.
2Claude sets it up and tells you it exists. You'll find it in your list of scheduled tasks.
3Each run starts as a fresh chat that remembers nothing, so the brief must stand on its own.
4Check the first run. If a run is waiting for your approval, approve it, or allow that task to run on its own in its settings.
Recipes to copy and paste
Morning brief (scheduled)
Every weekday at 7:30 am: read my calendar for today and my unread Gmail since yesterday 6 pm. Write a short brief: my meetings with times, the 3 emails that most need me, and anything due today. Keep it under 12 lines. Do not send or change anything.
Weekly report (scheduled)
Every Friday at 4 pm: read the pages in my Notion "Orders" database that changed this week. Make a one-page summary: total orders, top 3 products, anything late. Save it as a new page called "Week of [date]" inside "Reports", then tell me in 3 lines what you saved.
One big job now
Goal: a clean list of every subscription I pay for. Sources: my Gmail receipts from the last 12 months. Output: a table with name, price, how often, last charge date; save it as a new Notion page "Subscriptions". Rules: read only, do not cancel anything. Report: total per month and the 3 I use least.
Mistakes to avoid

✕ "As we discussed"

A scheduled run starts blank. It never saw your earlier chat. Put every detail in the brief.

✕ No place to save

If you don't say where the result goes, it may only exist in a chat you never open.

✕ Never checking the first run

Read the first result. Most problems show up once and are fixed with one sentence.

✕ Too big a job

Split "run my business" into small jobs with clear ends: one report, one list, one cleanup.

Practice (10 minutes)
  1. Write a five-part brief for a job you do every week. Don't run it yet: ask Claude "What is missing from this brief?"
  2. Turn it into a scheduled task for tomorrow, at a time you'll be free to check it.
  3. When it runs, read the result and send one sentence to improve it next time.
Check yourself
Why must a scheduled task's instruction be complete?

Each run starts as a new chat with no memory of the conversation where you made it.

Name the five parts of a good brief.

Goal, sources, output (and where to save it), rules, report.

A run keeps waiting and never finishes. What is the likely cause?

It is waiting for your approval. Approve it, or turn on automatic approval for that task in its settings, if your account allows it.

REMEMBER
Brief like a new assistant: goal, sources, output, rules, report. Check the first run.
